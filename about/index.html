    About The Filing Desk

    The Filing Desk turns US-listed companies' filings with the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) into plain-language pages: a page of figures for each company, reports on their results, stories on the week's business news, and tools for projecting a company's future yourself. Every figure comes from a filing or another named source and is checked by code before it is published. When a figure can't be checked, the page leaves it blank and says why, instead of estimating it.

    What we publish

    • Company pages, for more than 1,700 US-listed companies: ten fiscal years and the last 12 quarters of revenue, margins, returns, cash flow and debt, compared with similar companies. Most also have "What the company does", summarized from its latest annual report, and "What the company says to expect", its own outlook quoted from its latest earnings release (shown for 150 days after the release). Pages are updated within hours of each new 10-Q or 10-K and checked against the SEC's data every Monday.
    • Reports on one company's results: a company's latest annual results every Friday, and a much-read company's new quarter on weekdays in earnings season.
    • Stories: the week's biggest business or markets topic every Sunday, and, between Sundays, stories on big companies' earnings on the day they report and previews of what to watch for.
    • Projections, the screener and side-by-side comparisons of companies, for working with the figures yourself.

    Where the numbers come from

    • Annual and quarterly reports (Forms 10-K and 10-Q) filed with the SEC, taken from the structured XBRL data on EDGAR. Foreign companies file an annual report on Form 20-F or 40-F and no quarterly reports, so their pages have fiscal years only.
    • Company earnings releases, for the company's outlook and for items the filings don't break out.
    • Form 4 filings, for insider trades.
    • Closing share prices: consolidated US end-of-day prices from Databento, updated after each trading day.

    When a later filing restates an earlier year, the pages use the latest figure the company filed.

    How figures are calculated

    • Free cash flow is net cash from operating activities minus additions to property and equipment.
    • Margins are the item divided by revenue for the same period.
    • Years are the company's fiscal years, which may not match calendar years. Companies don't file a report for their fourth quarter, so it is the full year minus the first nine months.
    • After a stock split, per-share figures and share counts for earlier years are put on today's basis.
    • The share-price move after an earnings release compares two closes: for a release before the market's 4pm close, that day's close with the close before; for one after it, the next trading day's close with the last close before. It is the whole day's move, the news and the market together, so it says how the shares moved, not why.

    Each report lists any other definitions it uses.

    Projections

    On the projections page you pick a company and work out where its figures could go in the next five years, in a bull, a base and a bear case. Each case starts from the company's latest fiscal year in its 10-K, today's diluted share count and the last closing share price. Every projected figure is one you enter: no box is filled in for you, so no share price appears that you didn't work out.

    • The P/E model: you enter revenue growth and net income growth for each year (a net margin, for a company with a loss), and a low and a high price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple. The page works out revenue, net income and earnings per share, the share prices your multiples give, and the yearly change from the last close they would mean. For reference, it shows the company's own revenue growth and its P/E today, and the median P/E of its peers and of its sector.
    • The discounted cash flow (DCF) model: you enter revenue growth and free cash flow growth for each year (a free cash flow margin, for a company whose free cash flow was negative), and a discount rate and a growth rate after year five for each case. The page works out each year's cash flow worth today, a value per share and how it compares with the last close. Banks get the P/E model only, because their cash flow is mostly the money they lend and take in.

    You can copy a link to your projection, or save as many as you like under names you choose. They are kept in your own browser and never sent to us. Foreign companies aren't covered, because their US share price is per depositary share and their figures per ordinary share. The results are the arithmetic of the figures you enter: they are not forecasts, price targets or recommendations, and The Filing Desk doesn't publish its own.

    What the reports don't do

    Reports describe what companies reported. They don't value companies or forecast share prices, and they don't rate stocks or recommend buying, selling or holding anything. A report may say how the shares moved on the first trading day after the results, from our own closing prices, with no reason or prediction attached. A company's outlook appears only as its own words, quoted, and analysts' expectations only as an exact quotation from an SEC filing.

    Stories

    Stories are different from the reports. They explain a topic using news coverage, public posts, market data and the filings, with analysis of what it could mean next. Every fact is attributed, every figure is checked against its source before publishing, and passages that look ahead are marked as analysis. Stories may report what analysts expect or what investors think, always saying whose view it is. Like everything here, they never recommend buying or selling anything.

    Insider trades

    Company pages and the insider trades page list purchases and sales of a company's shares on the open market by its officers, directors and owners of more than 10%, from the Form 4 filings they must make with the SEC within two business days. History comes from the SEC's quarterly insider transaction data sets, and newer filings are read every weekday. Grants, option exercises, shares withheld for tax and gifts aren't counted, the same trade reported by related owners is counted once, and prices that can't be right (a total typed into the price box) are left out. Insiders trade for many reasons, so a trade on its own isn't a signal, and we don't treat it as one.

    Photos

    Photos come from Wikimedia Commons or, when it isn't available, from other sites through Openverse, a catalogue of openly licensed images, always under licences that allow anyone to reuse them, including commercially. Each one is credited under the photo with its author and licence. They show the companies, places and people in a story; they aren't taken at the events described unless the caption says so.

    Corrections

    If a figure doesn't match the filing, write to thefilingdesk.net@gmail.com. Confirmed errors are corrected on the page, with a note saying what changed.
