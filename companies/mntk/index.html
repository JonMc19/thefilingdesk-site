    What Montauk Renewables does

    Montauk Renewables captures biogas, mostly from landfills and also from livestock farms, and turns it into renewable natural gas (RNG) or electricity. RNG is pipeline-quality gas that is chemically the same as fossil natural gas. Nearly all of Montauk's RNG is sold as a fuel for vehicles such as city fleets, local delivery trucks and waste haulers.

    Montauk makes money in two ways. It sells the gas or electricity itself, at prices tied to wholesale natural gas markets or under power contracts. It also sells the environmental credits that government programs award for renewable fuel and power, such as renewable identification numbers (RINs). It builds, owns and runs the plants, and gets the gas through long-term agreements with landfill and farm hosts. It has eleven RNG projects and two renewable electricity projects in seven states.

    Its revenue is concentrated: 67.7% of 2025 operating revenue came from five project sites. Montauk had 189 employees at the end of 2025, and its shares are also listed in Johannesburg.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its plants may produce less gas than expected, because landfill gas output and equipment can change.
    • A few sites bring in most of its revenue, so an outage at one would hurt more.
    • A small number of customers account for much of its revenue.
    • Cuts to government incentives and credits for renewable fuel could lower its income.
    • Its projects depend on landfill hosts and operators whose actions it does not control.
    From Montauk Renewables's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 11, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
