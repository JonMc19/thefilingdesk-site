    What Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals does

    Arrowhead develops medicines that silence the genes that cause disease. Its drugs use a natural process called RNA interference (RNAi) to turn down the activity of a target gene. It says about 20 drug candidates that it discovered are in clinical trials, from early to late stage, some with partners and some wholly owned.

    In 2025 the U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved REDEMPLO (plozasiran) for adults with familial chylomicronemia syndrome, a condition of very high blood triglycerides. This was Arrowhead's first commercial launch. The drug has also been approved for this use in the European Union, Canada, Australia, China and the United Kingdom. Arrowhead has asked the FDA to approve it for severe hypertriglyceridemia as well, and is preparing for a possible launch there in 2027.

    Arrowhead has also signed license and collaboration agreements with drug companies, including Novartis, Sarepta, Amgen, Takeda, GSK and Madrigal, for programs it does not plan to sell itself. It says these deals aim to bring in money that does not dilute shareholders.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its near-term prospects depend heavily on the commercial success of one product, REDEMPLO.
    • Clinical trials of its drug candidates may fail, take longer than planned or show safety problems.
    • Its drug candidates may not win regulatory approval, or may not be commercialized successfully.
    • Its reliance on license and collaboration partners for programs it does not plan to sell itself.
