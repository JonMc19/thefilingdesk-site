    What Williams-Sonoma does

    Williams-Sonoma is a retailer of home goods. Its brands include Williams Sonoma (cookware and kitchen items), Pottery Barn, Pottery Barn Kids, Pottery Barn Teen, West Elm, Rejuvenation, Mark and Graham and GreenRow. It sells to consumers and businesses through websites, mailed catalogs and stores in the United States, Puerto Rico, Canada, Australia and the United Kingdom. Franchisees run stores in other countries.

    The company makes money by selling furniture, kitchenware, lighting, bedding and decor, mostly designed in-house. It buys most of its goods from outside makers and manufactures some upholstered furniture and lighting itself. About 19% of its products were made in the U.S. in fiscal 2025. Of the rest, it sourced about 19% from China, 16% from Vietnam and 15% from India. It says it competes with other online sellers, department stores, discount retailers and specialty home retailers.

    Sales peak from October through January, the holiday season. At the start of February 2026 the company had about 19,800 employees, which it calls associates, including part-time and seasonal workers.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Weaker consumer spending on home furnishings and kitchen products.
    • Misjudging changing tastes, or failing to manage inventory and deliver goods on time.
    • Heavy reliance on foreign suppliers, with tariffs and trade policy changes raising costs.
    • Strong competition, including from online sellers and discount retailers.
    • Problems running its online, catalog and store businesses together across many brands.
    From Williams-Sonoma's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
