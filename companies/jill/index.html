    What J.Jill does

    J.Jill sells women's clothing, footwear and accessories under its own brand, through its website, catalogs and stores. As of January 31, 2026 it operated 256 stores in 42 states, about half in lifestyle centers and half in malls. All of its stores are leased, and the average store is approximately 3,700 square feet.

    It orders from suppliers order by order and has no long-term merchandise supply contracts. A sourcing team negotiates costs and inspects goods. It says the women's apparel industry is highly competitive and that it competes with retail chains, department stores, specialty and discount stores, catalogs and internet businesses.

    J.Jill says it sees room to open profitable new stores, aiming at a fleet of approximately 300 stores. Its main shareholder is the private equity firm TowerBrook Capital Partners.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Changes in consumer spending and the economy, and picking the right styles for its customers.
    • Intense competition, and the need to run a store, website and catalog business that works together.
    • Its debt and the nearness of its repayment dates, including options to refinance after any default.
    • Long-term store leases and falling mall traffic.
    • Reliance on third-party vendors for merchandise, and tariffs and shipping costs.
    From J.Jill's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 31, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
