    What Snap does

    Snap makes Snapchat, a mobile app for visual messaging. The app opens straight to the camera, so people can send photos and short videos, called Snaps, that are deleted by default. It also has Stories, a map that shows friends who choose to share their location, a Spotlight feed of videos, augmented reality (AR) effects called Lenses, and an AI chatbot called My AI. It also makes wearable AR glasses called Spectacles, or Specs.

    Snap makes most of its money from advertising. Advertisers, from small businesses to well-known brands, pay to reach people on the app, and advertising was about 87% of total revenue in 2025. It also sells subscriptions, including Snapchat+, Lens+ and Snapchat Platinum, which adds an ad-free experience, plus cloud storage plans for saved photos and videos. Snap had 474 million daily active users on average in the fourth quarter of 2025.

    Snap says many of its competitors have more resources and larger market shares. It depends on Google Cloud and Amazon Web Services for most of its computing and storage, and on Google, Apple and Amazon for the mobile systems that run the app.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Losing users or seeing them use Snapchat less, since its user growth has slowed in the past.
    • Its heavy dependence on advertising, and the chance that advertisers leave or cut their spending.
    • Changes by mobile operating system and device makers that it does not control.
    • Its reliance on Google Cloud and Amazon Web Services for most of its computing and storage.
    • Whether its new products and its physical products, such as Spectacles, will succeed.
    From Snap's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 5, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
