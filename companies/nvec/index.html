    What NVE does

    NVE develops and sells devices built on spintronics, a technology that uses the spin of electrons rather than their charge to sense, store and send information. Its main products are magnetic sensors and couplers, which move data between electronic parts. It also works on spintronic memory, called MRAM.

    Sensors detect things like the position or speed of robot parts, or communicate with implanted medical devices. Couplers send data between machines, for example between robots and central controllers in factory automation. NVE says its spintronic sensors are smaller, more precise and more reliable than competing devices, and that its couplers move more data faster and over longer distances. It sells these parts to other manufacturers, and some end up inside medical devices.

    NVE was founded in 1989 and is based near Minneapolis, Minnesota. It has been awarded more than $50 million in government research contracts, which it says helped it develop products and build its patents. It is very small: it had 42 employees as of March 31, 2026. Its fiscal year ends March 31.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Loss of supply from its few single-source wafer suppliers, or of key chemicals and packaging.
    • Losing business to competitors, or failing to meet strict customer requirements.
    • Failing to renew customer agreements.
    • Product liability claims and recalls because some of its products go into medical devices.
    • Failure of critical production equipment, or losing important certifications.

    Piotroski F-score: how many of nine simple tests of profitability, funding and efficiency the company passed in the year (Piotroski, 2000); 8 or 9 is strong, 0 to 2 weak.
