    What Smart Sand does

    Smart Sand mines and sells sand. Most of it is frac sand, used to prop open cracks in rock when oil and natural gas wells are hydraulically fractured. It also sells sand for industrial uses such as glass, foundry and building products. It operates three mines with processing plants, in Oakdale and Blair, Wisconsin, and Ottawa, Illinois.

    Its core customers are oil and gas producers and oilfield service companies. It sells under short- and long-term contracts, where customers must take minimum volumes or pay for a shortfall, and on the spot market. It also earns money from logistics: it runs rail terminals near drilling regions and rents wellsite equipment for storing and delivering sand. In 2025 EQT was 27.7% of its revenue and EOG Resources was 10.9%.

    It names Badger Mining, Iron Oak Energy Solutions, U.S. Silica, Capital Sand, Source Energy Services and Solaris Energy Infrastructure as main competitors. Its demand follows how many wells customers drill and complete.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Swings in oil and gas prices and drilling activity that cut demand for frac sand.
    • Depending on a few large customers for much of its revenue.
    • Competition from other sand producers that could cost it market share.
    • Customers shifting from long-term contracts to spot sales at market prices.
    • Permit, environmental and safety rules that could raise costs or limit mining.
    From Smart Sand's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
