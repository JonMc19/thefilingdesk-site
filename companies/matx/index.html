    What Matson does

    Matson provides ocean shipping and logistics services. It has two segments, Ocean Transportation and Logistics. Through Matson Navigation, founded in 1882, it carries freight to Hawaii, Alaska, Guam and other Pacific islands. It also runs expedited services from China to Long Beach, California, and an export service from Alaska to Asia. Its subsidiaries provide stevedoring (loading and unloading ships), refrigerated cargo and inland transport.

    The Logistics segment, Matson Logistics, extends its reach across North America and Asia. It arranges rail, truck and other freight, consolidates and forwards containers, offers warehousing and distribution, and manages purchase orders and bookings for customers.

    Matson's owned fleet represents an investment of approximately $2.4 billion, and most of its owned ships are U.S.-flagged and qualify under the Jones Act, which limits some domestic shipping to U.S.-built and U.S.-crewed vessels. It also owns 35 percent of SSA Terminals, a joint venture that runs terminals on the U.S. West Coast. It competes with other shipping companies, and it depends on a few large customers for much of its revenue.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Economic, geopolitical and tariff developments that can hurt it and its customers.
    • Competition, including new rivals or added shipping capacity on its routes.
    • Losing or damaging relationships with its largest customers.
    • Higher fuel prices, or trouble collecting fuel surcharges.
    • Dependence on the Alaskan and Hawaiian economies, including effects of oil prices.
