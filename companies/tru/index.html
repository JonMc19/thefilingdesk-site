    What TransUnion does

    TransUnion is a credit reporting and data company. It collects information on consumers from banks, lenders, private databases and public records, and sells it to businesses that need to judge credit risk, find customers, confirm identities and spot fraud. Its customers include banks, credit card issuers, online lenders, auto lenders, insurers, telecom companies, retailers and government agencies.

    It also sells directly to consumers, who use its tools to view their credit profiles and watch for identity theft. It says other companies now offer similar services free, and that it launched a direct-to-consumer product with free and paid tiers in 2025.

    TransUnion works in over 30 countries and territories across North America, Latin America, Europe, Africa, India and Asia Pacific. It is moving its data onto a platform it calls OneTru, which uses AI to match records to the same person. It says it usually gets updated information from its data contributors at little or no cost.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its revenue is concentrated in US financial services and consumer credit, so a downturn there hurts demand.
    • Strong competition, including free or low-cost consumer credit information.
    • Losing or weakening its relationships with key long-term customers.
    • Losing access to the data sources it depends on.
    • Broad powers of the CFPB over its business, and a CFPB matter described in its filing.
    From TransUnion's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
