    What JD.com does

    JD.com is a Chinese online retailer that also runs its own delivery network. It says it is a supply chain-based technology and service provider. Customers shop through its mobile apps and website, and it also lets partners, brands and other industries use its technology and logistics infrastructure.

    It runs two kinds of e-commerce business. In online retail, it buys products from suppliers and sells them directly to customers, in categories such as electronics, home appliances and other general merchandise. Its online marketplace, launched in October 2010, lets third-party merchants sell to its customers. It delivers a majority of its orders itself through its nationwide fulfillment network.

    It reports three segments: JD Retail, JD Logistics and New Businesses. JD Logistics worked as an internal logistics department from 2007 and became a stand-alone segment in April 2017. It sells logistics and supply chain services to outside customers as well as to JD Retail. The net product revenues come mainly from the JD Retail segment.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to manage its growth or carry out its strategies.
    • Failing to give customers a good experience, which could hurt its brand and reputation.
    • Failing to run its nationwide fulfillment network efficiently.
    • Intense competition that could cost it market share and customers.
    • Mistakes in managing inventory, and the cost of expanding into new product categories.
    From JD.com's annual report on Form 20-F, filed April 16, 2026, in our words; the 20-F has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
