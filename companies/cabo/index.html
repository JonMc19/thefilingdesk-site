    What Cable One does

    Cable One sells internet, video and phone services under the Sparklight brand over a network that uses fiber-based infrastructure. It serves non-metropolitan, secondary and tertiary markets in 24 Western, Midwestern and Southern states. At the end of 2025 it served about 1.0 million residential and business customers out of about 2.9 million passings (homes and businesses its network reaches). About 999,000 subscribed to data, 88,000 to video and 94,000 to voice.

    Substantially all of its revenue comes from three product lines. By share of 2025 revenue, residential data is 60.1%, business data is 15.3% and residential video is 12.5%. Video earns much lower margins because programming costs and retransmission fees (what it pays to carry channels) take a large share of video revenue, so it focuses on growing residential and business data.

    It says it faces competition from other service providers and from new entrants in video and data, and from local telephone companies and regional overbuilders in fiber services for businesses.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Strong competition from other service providers and well-funded new entrants in video and data.
    • Rapid technology change, and risks in how it uses artificial intelligence.
    • Rising programming costs and retransmission fees that squeeze video margins or cut demand.
    • Risks as it puts more focus on selling to business customers.
    • Getting the hardware, software and support it needs from vendors, with trade policy and tariffs adding risk.
    From Cable One's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
