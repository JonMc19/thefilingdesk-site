    What GitLab does

    GitLab sells a software platform that software teams use to plan, build, test, secure and release their code. This kind of work is called DevSecOps. The platform brings these steps together in one product, and GitLab adds artificial intelligence (AI) features that can carry out some tasks for the team.

    GitLab uses an open-core model. Its core product is open to outside contributors, and it sells paid versions with more features. Customers can run GitLab themselves on their own servers or in their own cloud, use a hosted software-as-a-service version run by GitLab, or use a single-tenant hosted version, GitLab Dedicated, for organizations with strict security needs. It counts a customer as a "Base Customer" once the customer pays more than $5,000 in annual recurring revenue.

    GitLab says it faces strong competition and that it has a history of losses. Its 10-K also lists risks from the code it runs for customers and from the AI features in its products.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A history of losses, with higher operating costs expected, and no promise of lasting profit.
    • Strong competition that could cost it market share.
    • Security breaches, made more likely by running customers' code and using open source parts.
    • Growth that may slow, and difficulty managing the growth it has had.
    • AI features that could give wrong or unsafe output and lead to harm to its reputation or to claims.
    From GitLab's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 17, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
