    What Interface does

    Interface, Inc. is a flooring company founded in Georgia in 1973. It sells carpet tile, luxury vinyl tile (LVT), nora rubber flooring and FLOR area rugs. Its carpet tile and LVT carry the Interface and FLOR brand names, and its rubber flooring is sold as noraplan and norament. The company says it has grown from mostly modular carpet into a maker of several kinds of flooring.

    It sells mainly for commercial and institutional spaces: corporate offices, education, healthcare, public buildings, retail, transportation and residential living. It reports two segments. Americas, which covers the United States, Canada and Latin America, made up 61% of 2025 net sales. Europe, Africa, Asia and Australia made up 39%.

    Interface says it competes with a large number of flooring makers, some with more money than it has. It adds that its business follows the cycle of commercial building and renovation. It bought nora systems, the rubber flooring maker, in 2018, and began selling LVT in late 2016.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competition from many flooring makers, some with more financial resources, on price, design and sustainability.
    • Swings in office, new construction and renovation markets that drive demand for its flooring.
    • Higher raw material, shipping and tariff costs it may not be able to pass on to customers.
    • Reliance on a few suppliers of synthetic fiber and of luxury vinyl tile.
    • Debt that takes a lot of cash to service, and currency and political risks from its worldwide operations.
    From Interface's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 25, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
