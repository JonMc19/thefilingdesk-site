    What Cooper-Standard does

    Cooper Standard makes sealing systems and fluid handling systems (fuel and brake delivery systems and fluid transfer systems), mainly for passenger vehicles and light trucks built by global automakers, and for replacement markets. It has about 22,000 employees, including 4,000 contingent workers, across 108 facilities in 20 countries. Its products appear on more than 430 vehicle nameplates.

    It reports two segments, Sealing Systems and Fluid Handling Systems. About 86% of 2025 sales went to automakers. Its largest customers are Ford, General Motors, Stellantis, Volkswagen Group, Mercedes-Benz and Renault-Nissan. Chinese customers include BYD, Geely and Chery. The rest of its sales went mostly to other automotive suppliers, non-automotive customers and replacement market distributors.

    It says it is the largest global producer of sealing systems and the second and third largest in the fuel and brake delivery and fluid transfer products it makes. The company was set up in 2004 when it bought the automotive business of Cooper Tire & Rubber. It also runs an Industrial and Specialty Group serving transportation and industrial markets.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Changes in U.S. or foreign trade policy, including tariffs and other trade restrictions.
    • Inflation, higher raw material and component costs, and supply chain disruptions.
    • Work stoppages or other disruptions to its operations.
    • Problems with the timing, quality and cost of new vehicle program launches.
    • Product liability, warranty and recall claims, and failing to develop products customers need.

    Income statement

    From fiscal 2016 to 2019, earnings per share fell 19.2% a year and net income 21.4% a year, while buybacks cut the share count by 8%. Cooper-Standard then lost money in fiscal 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025.
