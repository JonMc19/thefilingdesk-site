    What Flex does

    Flex is a contract manufacturer. Other companies hire it to design, build, deliver and manage their products, and it also sells a line of power and cooling products. It has a workforce in about 30 countries and serves data center, healthcare, industrial, automotive, communications and consumer lifestyle customers.

    It reports three segments. Integrated Technology Solutions covers communications and networking systems and consumer lifestyle products. Regulated Manufacturing Solutions covers industrial, automotive and healthcare products, such as medical devices and drug delivery equipment. The new Cloud and Power Infrastructure segment makes computing systems, liquid cooling and electrical power equipment for cloud, data center and AI-related buildouts.

    Customers are concentrated: in fiscal 2026 its ten largest customers made up 45% of net sales. In May 2026 Flex announced plans to split into two public companies, one for Cloud and Power Infrastructure and one for the other two segments, targeted for the first quarter of calendar 2027 and subject to conditions. It spun off Nextracker to its shareholders in January 2024.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Customer order cancellations, production changes and demand that swings.
    • A large share of sales comes from a small number of customers.
    • Heavy spending on cloud and power infrastructure that may hurt margins.
    • Losses if equipment bought for specific customers becomes obsolete or unused.
    • The planned spin-off may not happen on the planned terms or timeline, or bring the intended benefits.
    From Flex's annual report on Form 10-K, filed May 20, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
