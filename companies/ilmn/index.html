    What Illumina does

    Illumina makes machines and supplies that read DNA. Its sequencing systems determine the order of the chemical letters in a DNA sample, and its array products test samples for known genetic variations. Customers include genomic research centers, universities, government labs, hospitals, drug and biotechnology companies, clinical testing labs and consumer genetics companies. They use the tools for research and, increasingly, for medical uses such as cancer testing, rare disease research and prenatal testing.

    Most sales are consumables, the kits, reagents and chips customers buy to keep running their instruments. In 2025 consumables were 74% of revenue. Instruments and services and other made up the rest, and sequencing products were about nine-tenths of the total. Illumina also sells software for analyzing genomic data and runs a certified lab that does whole-genome sequencing for customers. It sells directly in North America, Europe, Latin America and Asia-Pacific, and through distributors in some other regions.

    It has one reportable segment. In June 2024 it spun off GRAIL, its cancer-detection company, into a separate public company. At the end of 2025 it had about 8,600 full-time employees.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Slower growth in the markets for genetic analysis, or cuts to research budgets and government funding.
    • Intense competition that could force price cuts or make its products obsolete.
    • A growing share of sales coming from a few large laboratory customers.
    • Being placed on China's List of Unreliable Entities, which could bring fines or limits on doing business there.
    • Tariffs and trade limits, and the risks of launching new products on time.
    From Illumina's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
