    What KinderCare Learning Companies does

    KinderCare provides early education and child care in the United States for children from six weeks to 12 years old. It runs community-based centers, employer-sponsored programs and before- and after-school sites, using its own curriculum and third-party tools that measure school readiness.

    It operates under three brands. KinderCare Learning Centers had 1,555 centers at January 3, 2026, and made 88% of fiscal 2025 revenue. Crème School, a higher-priced brand, had 46 schools in 15 states and made 4% of revenue. Champions runs before- and after-school programs and summer camps at 1,153 sites, mostly under contract with schools and districts, and made 8% of revenue. It also provides family care benefits to employers.

    The company says center-based child care is highly fragmented, and it estimates that the top five providers, including KinderCare, held approximately 6% of total capacity.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Attracting and keeping families in its centers, schools and programs, since families can cancel enrollment at any time.
    • Changes in demand for child care from demographic trends, the economy and workplace shifts.
    • Hiring and keeping qualified teachers.
    • Harm to its brands and reputation from adverse publicity.
    • Depending on key management and key employees.
    From KinderCare Learning Companies's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 13, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
