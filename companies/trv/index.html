    What Travelers Companies does

    Travelers is a holding company whose subsidiaries sell property and casualty insurance to businesses, government units, associations and individuals. It dates back to 1853. It sells mainly in the United States, and also in Canada, the United Kingdom, Ireland and Brazil (through a joint venture).

    It has three business lines. Business Insurance sells a broad range of coverages, such as workers' compensation, commercial auto, general liability and property, to small, mid-sized and large businesses, mostly through independent agents and brokers; it also owns Simply Business, a small business insurer in the United Kingdom. Bond & Specialty Insurance sells surety bonds, management liability and professional liability coverage. Personal Insurance sells car and home insurance to individuals. It earns money from premiums and from investing them until claims are paid. It competes with domestic and foreign insurers on price, service, product range and its relationships with agents and brokers.

    In May 2025 Travelers agreed to sell its Canadian personal insurance business and most of its Canadian commercial business to Definity Financial Corporation for approximately US$2.4 billion. The sale closed on January 2, 2026.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Catastrophes such as storms, other natural disasters and terrorist attacks can cause large insurance claims.
    • Claims reserves are estimates, and the full extent of its liability on policies may not show for some time.
    • The value of its investment portfolio, which holds many municipal and mortgage-backed bonds, could fall.
    • It may not be able to collect what reinsurers owe it, and reinsurance may not stay available at reasonable cost.
    • Claims over the actions of its agents, and competition from other insurers on price and service.
    From Travelers Companies's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
