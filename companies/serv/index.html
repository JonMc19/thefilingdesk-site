    What Serve Robotics does

    Serve Robotics builds and runs small autonomous robots that deliver goods on sidewalks. Food delivery is its main commercial use. Its robots connect to delivery platforms such as Uber Eats and DoorDash, which send them delivery requests and receive their status. It says it is expanding into other uses where robots can ease labor shortages and cut emissions.

    The technology began in 2017 as a project inside Postmates, and Uber, which bought Postmates, contributed the related assets to Serve in 2021. At the end of 2025 its fleet was over 2,000 sidewalk robots. The company calls itself early-stage, with minimal revenue and a history of losses, and says a significant part of its revenue comes from a small number of customers.

    In January 2026 it bought Diligent Robotics, which makes AI-powered robot assistants for hospitals. This moves Serve's technology indoors and into healthcare.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • An early-stage business with minimal revenue and a history of losses.
    • Depending on a small number of customers and on delivery platform and merchant partners.
    • Needing large amounts of money to grow, which may be hard to raise or may dilute shareholders.
    • Acquisitions, such as Diligent, that could distract management or add debt.
    • Limited experience selling its products at large scale, and use of AI that brings new risks.
    From Serve Robotics's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
