    What CrowdStrike does

    CrowdStrike, founded in 2011, sells cybersecurity software over the internet by subscription. Its Falcon platform uses one lightweight program, called a sensor, installed on customers' computers, servers and cloud systems. The sensor collects data that the platform uses to detect and stop attacks. CrowdStrike says the platform replaces older, separate security products.

    The company offers 33 cloud modules that customers can add to their subscription. They cover protecting laptops and servers, cloud workloads and employee identities, managing software vulnerabilities and IT operations, collecting and searching security logs, threat intelligence, data protection, and security for artificial intelligence systems. It also sells managed security services, in which its own staff watch for threats and respond for the customer.

    CrowdStrike recognizes revenue from subscriptions over the length of the subscription. It sells through its own sales force and through channel partners, and it hosts the platform at third-party data centers such as Amazon Web Services as well as its own. Its 10-K says the July 19 Incident has hurt its business and customer relationships and is expected to keep doing so.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • The July 19 Incident has affected, and is expected to keep affecting, its sales and its relationships with customers and partners.
    • Customers might not renew subscriptions or add more modules.
    • A history of losses, with no assurance of lasting profit.
    • Intense competition and pricing pressure.
    • Its products failing to detect or prevent attacks, and its own exposure to cyberattacks.
