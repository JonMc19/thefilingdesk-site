    What Marvell Technology does

    Marvell designs semiconductors that move, process and store data in data centers and networks. It is fabless, which means it designs chips and pays other companies to manufacture them. Its products include custom chips built to a single customer's specification, optical and electrical connection chips, Ethernet switches, processors, security processors and storage controllers.

    Marvell sells to equipment makers, design manufacturers and distributors, and it reports one segment. It groups sales into two end markets: data center, which includes chips for AI systems, cloud servers and storage, and communications and other, which covers networking, carrier and consumer equipment. Data center is the larger of the two and has grown as a share of sales. Marvell says one distributor and one direct customer each account for at least a tenth of its net revenue. It competes with companies that make their own chips and, in many cases, run their own factories.

    In August 2025 Marvell sold its automotive ethernet business to Infineon for $2.5 billion in cash. In early 2026 it bought Celestial AI, which makes optical connection technology for AI data centers, and XConn Technologies, which makes switching chips.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Weak or uncertain conditions in the data center and communications markets that can slow its growth.
    • Large customers that may start designing their own chips instead of buying Marvell's.
    • Strong competition from companies with longer histories, more resources and their own factories.
    • Trade policy changes and export limits, especially involving China and Chinese customers.
    • Acquisitions and sales of businesses that may not work out, and debt that could limit its choices.
    From Marvell Technology's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 11, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
