    What Dexcom does

    Dexcom makes continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) systems. A small sensor worn on the body measures a person's blood sugar all day and sends readings to a receiver or a phone, so users do not need finger sticks for most decisions. Its customers are people with diabetes, their caregivers and their clinicians, around the world.

    Dexcom sells sensors and related equipment. Its main products are the G7 system, launched in 2023, and the G7 15 Day system, launched in late 2025, whose sensor is worn for about 15 days. In August 2024 it launched Stelo, a sensor sold over the counter in the US for adults with prediabetes or Type 2 diabetes who do not use insulin.

    In the US, Medicare, Medicaid and commercial insurers cover the G7 for many users, so insurer payment rules matter a great deal to what Dexcom earns. The company also offers Dexcom Clarity, its cloud-based reporting software, and its products connect to insulin pumps, smart insulin pens and the Apple Watch. Dexcom says it sold its first product in 2006 after FDA approval.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Falling prices for its products as Medicare reimbursement changes and insurers push back on pricing.
    • Failing to meet ongoing FDA requirements, including its response to an FDA warning letter.
    • Manufacturing problems or too little production capacity, and its reliance on third-party suppliers.
    • Competition from large, well-established companies, including makers of other CGM devices now covered by Medicare.
    • Research that does not lead to products that sell well or earn enough.
    From Dexcom's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
