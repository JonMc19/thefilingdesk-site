    What CG Oncology does

    CG Oncology is a late-stage clinical biopharmaceutical company, which means it is testing a medicine in large trials and has no approved product yet. It has one product candidate, cretostimogene grenadenorepvec, a treatment made from a virus that is designed to kill cancer cells directly and to prompt the immune system to attack the tumor.

    The target is bladder cancer that has not yet grown into the muscle wall (non-muscle invasive bladder cancer). Its main Phase 3 trial, BOND-003 Cohort C, covers high-risk patients whose cancer did not respond to the standard BCG treatment. For these patients, the next step is usually removal of the whole bladder. CG Oncology began submitting an application to the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in late 2025 and expects to finish it in 2026. A second Phase 3 trial, PIVOT-006, tests the treatment in intermediate-risk patients after tumor removal.

    It relies on a partner, Biovire, and other third parties to make the drug and to run its trials.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It depends entirely on cretostimogene, its only product candidate.
    • Its approach to treating cancer is new, so the time and cost of development and approval are hard to predict.
    • Trial results may be unfavorable, or the drug may cause side effects that delay or block approval.
    • It relies on third parties, including Biovire, to run trials and make and ship the drug.
