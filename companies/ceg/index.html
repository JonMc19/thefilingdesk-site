    What Constellation Energy does

    Constellation Energy generates electricity and sells it to utilities, businesses and households. It says it is the largest nuclear power operator in the U.S., with ownership interests in 14 nuclear stations. It also owns natural gas, hydro, wind and solar plants, and serves about 2.5 million customer accounts as a retail energy supplier.

    It makes money by selling the power its plants produce, in the wholesale markets and under long-term contracts, and by supplying power and energy services to retail customers. It reports five segments, based on where its plants are and where it serves customers: Mid-Atlantic, Midwest, New York, ERCOT (Texas) and Other Power Regions. It competes with other power producers and retail energy suppliers.

    In January 2026 Constellation bought Calpine, a large producer of power from natural gas and geothermal sources, which adds plants in Texas, California and the Northeast. Unless noted, the 10-K's numbers leave Calpine out. Constellation also has a 20-year agreement to sell power to Microsoft from Three Mile Island Unit 1, which it plans to restart, subject to regulatory approvals.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Swings in power, natural gas and nuclear fuel prices, since part of its output is not sold in advance.
    • Nuclear risks: regulator actions, the cost of waste disposal and of decommissioning plants.
    • Power purchase agreements that expire or end could cut its revenue.
    • Changes to wholesale electricity market rules by regulators or lawmakers.
    • Operating power plants carries risks, and the Calpine purchase may bring integration and accounting charges.
    From Constellation Energy's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 24, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
