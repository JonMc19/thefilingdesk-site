    What Ivanhoe Electric does

    Ivanhoe Electric explores for and develops minerals in the United States, mainly copper and other metals used in electric power, manufacturing, infrastructure and technology. It does not run a mine and has no history of mineral production. Its own properties are in the United States, and it has exploration joint ventures and alliances in Saudi Arabia, Chile and the United States.

    It uses a geophysical surveying system it calls Typhoon, together with data analysis from its subsidiary Computational Geosciences, to look for deposits that older exploration methods may miss.

    Its one material mineral project is the Santa Cruz Copper Project in Arizona, on private land with water rights. In June 2025 it finished a preliminary feasibility study for an underground copper mine with a heap leach plant, and it is now working on detailed engineering, readiness for operations and permitting. Its other U.S. projects include Tintic in Utah and Hog Heaven in Montana.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It operates no mines, and turning its projects into working mines is speculative and may never succeed.
    • It has no history of mineral production and may never begin producing.
    • Past net losses and negative operating cash flows, and it may never reach profit.
    • Resource and reserve estimates that may be wrong or change for the worse.
    • Falling metal prices, and title to some rights that may be uncertain, plus debt secured on its assets.
