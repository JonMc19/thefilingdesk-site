    What Willis Towers Watson does

    WTW (Willis Towers Watson) is an advisory and insurance broking firm that helps companies manage people, risk and capital. It has about 47,000 employees, which it calls colleagues, serving clients in more than 140 countries and markets. Its clients range from large multinationals to mid-sized companies, government agencies and not-for-profit groups, and no single client was more than 10% of revenue.

    It reports two segments. Health, Wealth & Career, its larger segment by revenue, gives advice, broking and technology for employee benefit plans, investors, pay and career programs and the employee experience, including a private Medicare marketplace in the U.S. Risk & Broking helps clients assess and control risk and acts as an insurance broker: it is the go-between for clients and insurance companies, advising on risk and placing coverage with carriers through its global network.

    WTW says it is not an insurance company and does not take on insurance risk for its own account. It places insurance with approximately 2,500 insurance carriers, and says none of them accounted for a significant share of the premiums it placed. It competes with other insurance brokers and with firms in human capital consulting.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its results depend on a strategy that may not work as planned, and on whether demand for its services holds up.
    • Heavy dependence on the insurance industry, and on good relations with insurance companies.
    • Losing key employees, which could also cost it client relationships.
    • Data breaches or improper release of confidential company or personal data.
    • Claims and lawsuits over its work, and investigations by regulators.
    From Willis Towers Watson's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 25, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
