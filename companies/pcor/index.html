    What Procore does

    Procore sells construction management software. Builders, property owners, general contractors and specialty contractors use it to share project information, manage workflows and communicate from any connected device. Its products fall into four groups: Preconstruction, Project Execution, Resource Management and Financial Management. Since February 2026 it has also sold them in four bundled packages. It also offers AI features that automate steps in a construction project.

    Substantially all of its revenue comes from subscriptions. A customer pays a fixed fee, set mostly by which products it uses and by the dollar volume of construction work it plans to run on the platform each year. Customers range from small businesses to global enterprises. Procore says customer support is free to all users.

    Customers in about 160 countries ran projects on the platform at the end of 2025, but customers outside the U.S. produced only 15% of 2025 revenue. Procore had 4,421 full-time employees at that date.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A history of losses and no assurance it will become or stay profitable.
    • Slowdowns in construction spending and the wider economy.
    • A fast-changing software industry that may not develop as it expects.
    • Failing to build up its sales and marketing so as to add customers and grow their use.
    • Relying on subscription revenue booked over time, which delays the effect of new business.
    From Procore's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 24, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
