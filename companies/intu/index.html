    What Intuit does

    Intuit sells tax, accounting, money-management and marketing software to consumers, small and mid-sized businesses and accountants. It says it serves approximately 93 million customers. Its main brands are TurboTax for tax returns, QuickBooks for bookkeeping, payroll and payments, Credit Karma for credit scores and personal finance, and Mailchimp for email marketing.

    It reports two segments. Global Business Solutions, the larger one, sells QuickBooks, payroll, payment processing, business loans and Mailchimp to small and mid-sized businesses and the accountants who serve them. Consumer sells TurboTax, Credit Karma and professional tax software for accountants in the U.S. and Canada. TurboTax sales are concentrated between November and April, so revenue is higher in the second and third fiscal quarters. International revenue was approximately 8% of total net revenue in each of the last three fiscal years.

    Intuit says its strategy is to combine AI with human tax and bookkeeping experts so the software does more of the work for customers. It also offers money products, such as early access to tax refunds and loans, through bank partners.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Strong competition that may hurt its results, and the need to keep adapting to fast technology change.
    • Security breaches, fraud and outages that could harm customers' trust and its business.
    • Uncertainty over how AI is developed and used in its products, which could harm its business and reputation.
    • Sharp seasonality in its tax business, which makes quarterly results swing.
    • Credit losses and other risks from lending money to small businesses and consumers.
    From Intuit's annual report on Form 10-K, filed September 9, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
