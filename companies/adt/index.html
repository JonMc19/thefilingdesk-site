    What ADT does

    ADT sells and installs home security systems and monitors them for homes and small businesses in the United States. Customers pay monthly for monitoring, and ADT also sells interactive and smart home products. Its sales and service offices, monitoring and support centers and a large network of installers and service technicians serve them nationwide.

    At the end of 2025 ADT had about 6.1 million security monitoring subscribers. It does business mainly under the ADT brand, which it links to home security since 1874. It is building around its ADT+ app, which it designs to work with both professionally installed and self-installed systems.

    In February 2026 ADT bought Origin Wireless, which makes AI-based sensing technology that detects people in a home without cameras, for $170 million in cash. ADT says it competes with large technology companies and other service providers. Its largest shareholders include State Farm, Apollo and, through a separate class of shares, Google.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Keeping pace with fast technology changes through partnerships, in-house work and acquisitions.
    • Intense competition from large technology companies and other service providers.
    • Rising customer attrition, since it relies on customers staying for long periods.
    • Insurers ending the rate discounts they give homeowners who have monitored security systems.
    • Older telecom technologies being retired, which could force costly changes to its systems.
    From ADT's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 2, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
