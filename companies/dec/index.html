    What Diversified Energy does

    Diversified Energy produces, transports and markets natural gas, natural gas liquids (NGLs) and oil in the United States. It focuses on mature, long-life wells and tries to keep them producing longer rather than drilling many new ones. Its assets are in the Appalachian Region, which spans seven states from Ohio to Alabama, the Central Region, which includes Texas, Oklahoma, New Mexico, Louisiana and Arkansas, and a smaller area in Florida and Wyoming.

    It earns its revenue by selling what its wells produce, and it uses a hedging program to reduce the effect of price swings. It also runs a plugging company in Appalachia that permanently closes old wells. In 2025 its average daily production rose 37% from 2024, helped by buying Maverick Natural Resources in March 2025 and Canvas Energy in November 2025.

    In November 2025 it completed a U.S. domestication, so a Delaware corporation became its publicly traded parent. It also launched a well plugging fund with West Virginia to retire oil and gas wells.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Changes in natural gas, NGL and oil prices that it cannot control.
    • Costs of plugging and retiring its large number of older wells that could exceed what it expects.
    • Reserves and production that turn out lower than estimated.
    • Reliance on outside pipelines and infrastructure that it does not control.
    • Hazards in producing gas and oil, and weather events that can disrupt its operations.
    From Diversified Energy's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
