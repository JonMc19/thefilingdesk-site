    What Murphy USA does

    Murphy USA sells gasoline and convenience store items through a network of 1,800 retail stores in 27 states. Of these, 1,649 are Murphy USA or Murphy Express stores and 151 are QuickChek stores. The company was split off from Murphy Oil in 2013, and bought the QuickChek chain in 2021. It also sells fuel to unbranded wholesale customers through terminals and pipeline positions.

    Most of its Murphy stores sit close to Walmart stores, in 25 states in the Southeast, Southwest and Midwest. It says the Walmart traffic helps its stores, and it works with Walmart on fuel discount programs such as Walmart+. QuickChek stores are in New Jersey and New York and offer more food and drink. The company sells fuel at low prices in high volume and sells snacks, drinks and tobacco through small, low-cost store formats.

    Its stores buy most of their merchandise from one main supplier, which provides over 78% of it. The company also earns money from Renewable Identification Numbers (RINs), credits tied to renewable fuel rules, and says that income can swing a lot. It runs loyalty programs, Murphy Drive Rewards and QuickChek Rewards, that give customers discounts.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Volatile oil and fuel prices and economic conditions it cannot control.
    • Relying on one main supplier for most of its merchandise.
    • Its close ties to Walmart, which keeps certain rights under its agreements.
    • Revenue from RINs, which can be highly variable.
    • Debt obligations and possible write-downs of goodwill and other assets.
    From Murphy USA's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 18, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
