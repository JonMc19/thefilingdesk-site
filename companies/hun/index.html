    What Huntsman does

    Huntsman makes organic chemicals. It sells them around the world, mostly to industrial and building product manufacturers. Its products go into adhesives, aerospace parts, cars, coatings, construction products, insulation, electronics and consumer goods.

    It has three segments. Polyurethanes makes MDI, polyols and thermoplastic polyurethane, used for foams, insulation, coatings and adhesives. Performance Products makes amines and maleic anhydride, used in foams, fuel additives, paints and fiberglass resins. Advanced Materials makes epoxy and other polymer formulations for aerospace, electronics and electrical uses. The company lists competitors such as BASF, Dow, Covestro, Wanhua and Evonik.

    Revenues were $5,683 million in 2025. The company was formed in 2004 to hold businesses founded by Jon M. Huntsman, and has grown through acquisitions and sales of businesses. In 2023 it sold its textile chemicals and dyes business.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competition from rivals, including large and state-owned producers, with more money to spend.
    • Production disruptions at its manufacturing plants.
    • Cyclical, volatile markets for many of its products.
    • Swings in the price and supply of raw materials and energy that it may not be able to pass on to customers.
    • Environmental, health and safety rules, and rules to cut greenhouse gas emissions, that raise costs.
    From Huntsman's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 18, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Income statement

    From fiscal 2016 to 2023, earnings per share fell 11.7% a year and net income 15.4% a year, while buybacks cut the share count by 26%. Huntsman then lost money in fiscal 2024 and 2025.
