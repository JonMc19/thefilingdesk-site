    What S&P Global does

    S&P Global sells data, benchmarks, analytics and software to people in finance, energy and commodities, and the car industry. Customers include asset managers, banks, insurers, exchanges, energy traders and automakers. Most products are sold by subscription.

    It runs five businesses. Market Intelligence sells data, analytics and workflow software to finance professionals. Ratings issues credit ratings, which are opinions on whether a borrower can repay its debts, and earns fees from new debt issues and from ongoing monitoring. Energy sells price data and benchmarks for oil, gas, metals and other commodities. Mobility sells data and software to automakers, dealers and lenders. Indices (S&P Dow Jones Indices) maintains stock and bond indexes and earns fees tied to funds that track them and to trading in derivatives based on them.

    At the end of 2025 S&P Global had about 44,500 permanent employees, most of them in Asia. In April 2025 its board decided to spin off Mobility as a separate public company, a deal it expected to finish in mid-2026.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competitors with new or better products, and free or low-cost sources of information that reduce demand.
    • Growing regulation of its credit ratings business in the U.S., Europe and elsewhere.
    • The planned separation of its Mobility business may not finish on time, or at all, or bring the intended benefits.
    • Legal and regulatory proceedings, and liability for or reputational harm from its ratings and products.
    • New risks from artificial intelligence and from its heavy reliance on networks and the Internet.
    From S&P Global's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 11, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
