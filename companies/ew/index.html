    What Edwards Lifesciences does

    Edwards Lifesciences makes medical devices to treat heart disease, mainly problems with the heart's valves. Its customers are hospitals, whose surgeons and heart specialists use its products. Some are implanted through open-heart surgery, such as tissue heart valves and rings that repair a valve. Others, called transcatheter products, are placed through a catheter, a thin tube, without open-heart surgery while the heart keeps beating.

    Edwards sorts its products into three groups: Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement (TAVR), Transcatheter Mitral and Tricuspid Therapies (TMTT), and Surgical Structural Heart. It makes money by selling these devices. It says it plans to grow by treating heart conditions beyond valve disease, such as heart failure.

    It competes in highly competitive markets, and its sales depend on clinical evidence, approvals and payment from government and other payors. It has sold its Critical Care product group. The company is based in Irvine, California.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to develop and market new products on time, or clinical trials that do not succeed.
    • Manufacturing, supply or quality problems at the company or its suppliers and logistics partners.
    • Competition in highly competitive markets.
    • Government or other payors refusing to reimburse hospitals for its products or cutting what they pay.
    • Risks tied to the sale of its Critical Care product group.
