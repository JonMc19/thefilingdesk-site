    What Baker Hughes does

    Baker Hughes sells equipment, technology and services to energy and industrial companies in more than 120 countries. Oil and gas producers use its drilling tools, well equipment, pumps and chemicals. Gas plants, pipelines, power producers and factories use its turbines, compressors, inspection and sensor products and software.

    It has two segments. Oilfield Services & Equipment (OFSE) serves oil and gas operators from exploration through production. Industrial & Energy Technology (IET) covers gas turbines and compressors, aftermarket service, testing and monitoring products, and newer lower-carbon work such as carbon capture and geothermal. In 2025 OFSE booked $14.7 billion of new orders and IET booked $14.9 billion. It names SLB, Halliburton, NOV, Weatherford and TechnipFMC as OFSE competitors, and Siemens Energy and Mitsubishi Heavy Industry among IET's.

    Baker Hughes has agreed to buy Chart Industries, a maker of equipment for handling gases and liquids, and expects to close in the second quarter of 2026. It has also sold its sensor business and put its surface pressure control business into a joint venture with Cactus, both completed on January 1, 2026.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Strong competition, and new technology and facility investments that may not earn good returns.
    • Losing GE Vernova or GE Aerospace as suppliers, or its joint venture contracts with GE Vernova.
    • The proposed Chart purchase brings business, regulatory and reputational risks and may not deliver the expected benefits.
    • Changes in oil and natural gas prices and its customers' spending, since its customers are concentrated in the energy industry.
    • Fixed-price and turnkey contracts that make it take on extra cost and risk.
    From Baker Hughes's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 5, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
