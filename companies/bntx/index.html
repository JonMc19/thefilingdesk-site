    What BioNTech does

    BioNTech is a German biotechnology company, founded in 2008, that develops immunotherapies. These are medicines that use the body's immune system to fight cancer, infectious diseases and other serious diseases. Its primary focus is oncology, the treatment of cancer, and it works with messenger RNA (mRNA) medicines and protein-based therapeutics, including antibodies and antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs).

    It has one marketed product, its COVID-19 vaccine Comirnaty, which it develops and sells with Pfizer under a collaboration agreement. Pfizer provides estimates of the costs and profits to be shared in the countries where Pfizer commercializes the vaccine. BioNTech says its revenue currently depends heavily on COVID-19 vaccine sales, and that demand is expected to keep falling in the near future.

    Most of its work goes into product candidates that are still in development, particularly BNT327 and its ADC candidates. It says its model combines research, manufacturing, clinical development and commercial capabilities. It bought Biotheus in January 2025 and CureVac in December 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its business depends on developing, getting approved and selling new medicines, particularly BNT327 and its ADC candidates.
    • Revenue that depends heavily on a COVID-19 vaccine whose demand is expected to keep falling.
    • Clinical trials that are lengthy, costly and uncertain, and may be delayed or fail.
    • Competitors, most of which have significantly greater resources.
    • Challenges to its intellectual property, and claims by others that could block its products.
    From BioNTech's annual report on Form 20-F, filed March 10, 2026, in our words; the 20-F has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Income statement

    From fiscal 2020 to 2023, earnings per share grew 299.7% a year and net income 294.1% a year, as the share count fell by 2%. BioNTech then lost money in fiscal 2024 and 2025.
