    What Target Hospitality does

    Target Hospitality rents and runs lodging for workers in remote locations. It owns relocatable modular accommodation units, with 16,991 beds in 29 communities, and runs two more that it does not own or lease. It also supplies meals and hospitality services on site. It works in the United States and Canada, mainly in the Southwest, Nevada and the Midwest.

    Its customers are mostly companies in natural resource development and government agencies. It says it also depends on critical minerals and data center building. For the year ended December 31, 2025 it generated revenues of approximately $321 million. Most of that came from specialty rental with hospitality services, and the rest from leasing lodging facilities and from construction fees.

    Nearly all its revenue comes from signed customer contracts, and some of them set minimum revenue commitments. It says it expects such commitments to make up a larger share of its revenue. It reports segments including Workforce Hospitality Solutions, HFS - South and Government.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Losing one of its largest customers could hurt its results.
    • Its customers, in natural resources and emerging industries, face their own operating risks.
    • Falling occupancy at its communities would reduce revenue and profit.
    • Procurement rules that allow the U.S. government to end contracts.
    • A contract-heavy business, with possible disputes or late payments from customers.
    From Target Hospitality's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 11, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
