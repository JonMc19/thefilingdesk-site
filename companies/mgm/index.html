    What MGM Resorts does

    MGM Resorts owns and runs casino resorts with hotels, restaurants, shows, shops and convention space, and it also runs online gaming and sports betting. At the end of 2025 it operated 16 casino properties in the United States and two in Macau, China. Its Las Vegas Strip resorts include Bellagio, Aria, MGM Grand and Mandalay Bay. It also holds a stake in a resort under development in Osaka, Japan.

    It reports four segments: Las Vegas Strip Resorts, Regional Operations, MGM China and MGM Digital. Over half of the Las Vegas Strip segment's revenue usually comes from non-gaming sources such as hotel rooms, food and entertainment, while most of the regional segment's revenue comes from gambling. MGM China earns most of its revenue from gambling under a gaming concession in Macau. MGM Digital is mainly the online company LeoVegas, based in Sweden and Malta. The company also owns half of BetMGM, a U.S. online betting and gaming venture.

    MGM owns about 56% of MGM China. It leases the land and buildings of its U.S. properties from landlords under long-term lease agreements, which it calls an asset-light approach. It says it competes with other casinos and hotels, with travel destinations such as Hawaii, with other Macau concession holders, and with other online gaming companies.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competition from other casinos, hotels, travel destinations and online betting and gaming companies.
    • Its substantial financial commitments, including debt and lease payments.
    • Not being able to collect gambling debts that customers owe it.
    • Lost revenue and disruption while rooms, restaurants and meeting space are closed for renovations.
    • Insurance that may not cover losses from war or terrorism, because that coverage is unavailable.
    From MGM Resorts's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 11, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
