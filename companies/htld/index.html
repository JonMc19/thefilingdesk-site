    What Heartland Express does

    Heartland Express is a trucking company. It hauls truckloads of freight, mainly in dry van trailers, for major shippers across the United States. It also offers temperature-controlled trucking and logistics in Mexico, but says these are not significant. Its average haul is under 400 miles, and it focuses on customers with a lot of freight in the areas where it operates.

    It earns money from the number of miles per load it delivers and the rate it is paid per mile or per load. It runs under the brands Heartland Express, Millis Transfer and Smith Transport, plus CFI for services in Mexico. At the end of 2025 it folded the U.S. operations of CFI, a carrier it bought, into the Heartland Express name. Its headquarters are in North Liberty, Iowa.

    It says it competes by keeping service and safety high, which depends on having experienced drivers and late-model trucks. Its workforce shrank during the year: it averaged about 4,560 drivers and non-driving staff in 2025 and had about 3,630 by the end of January 2026.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Freight demand, fuel and other costs that it cannot control, and may not be able to pass on.
    • Higher driver pay, or trouble hiring and keeping enough qualified drivers.
    • Depending on a few major customers.
    • Problems from joining CFI, its largest acquisition, into its own business.
    • Strong competition in trucking that limits its prospects for growth.
    From Heartland Express's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 3, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
