    What Adobe does

    Adobe sells software for creating, editing and sharing documents, images, video and marketing content. Its customers are business professionals, consumers, creators, creative professionals and marketing professionals. Its products include Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere Pro, Lightroom, Acrobat and Express. It is also adding AI features, including its own Firefly AI models, across these products.

    Adobe had three reportable segments in fiscal 2025: Digital Media, Digital Experience, and Publishing and Advertising. Digital Media sells the creative and document products, mostly by subscription, and had revenue of $17.65 billion. Digital Experience sells software and services, such as Adobe Experience Platform and GenStudio, that help businesses manage and personalize their marketing and customer experiences, and had revenue of $5.86 billion. Publishing and Advertising is the third segment.

    Adobe sells through its own website, a direct sales force, distributors and other partners, and it offers free versions of some products, such as Acrobat Reader, to bring in new users. It says it competes in rapidly changing, intensely competitive markets.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to innovate quickly enough as technology and customer needs change, including in AI.
    • Intense competition in fast-moving markets, which could hurt its business and results.
    • Issues around how AI is built and used in its products that could cause reputational harm and legal liability.
    • Problems with its sales channels, including its direct sales force, distributors and partners.
    • Long and unpredictable sales cycles for some of its enterprise products.
    From Adobe's annual report on Form 10-K, filed January 15, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
