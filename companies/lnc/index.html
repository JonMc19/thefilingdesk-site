    What Lincoln Financial does

    Lincoln National is a holding company whose subsidiaries sell insurance and retirement products in the United States under the marketing name Lincoln Financial. It reports four segments: Annuities, Life Insurance, Group Protection and Retirement Plan Services. A fifth group, Other Operations, holds activities not tied to those segments.

    Annuities sells variable, fixed (including indexed) and registered index-linked annuities, which give customers tax-deferred growth and income for life. Life Insurance sells individual life policies and also corporate-owned and bank-owned life policies. Group Protection sells insurance through employers. Retirement Plan Services sells retirement plan products and services.

    Its wholesale distributor, Lincoln Financial Distributors, sells the individual life, annuity and retirement products through consultants, brokers, planners, agents, financial advisers and other intermediaries. It had about 450 internal and external wholesalers at the end of 2025. Group Protection sells mainly through employee benefit brokers. Lincoln sold its wealth management business in 2024.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Weak capital markets and a weak economy.
    • Changes in interest rates, both low rates that squeeze spreads and high rates that cause withdrawals and surrenders.
    • Equity market moves that affect products with guaranteed benefits, and hedges that may not offset them.
    • Heavy regulation that may raise capital needs or cut profit.
    • Rules on the use of artificial intelligence that could raise compliance costs.
    From Lincoln Financial's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 19, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Piotroski F-score: how many of nine simple tests of profitability, funding and efficiency the company passed in the year (Piotroski, 2000); 8 or 9 is strong, 0 to 2 weak. Rule of 40: revenue growth plus free cash flow margin; software investors treat 40% or more as the bar.
