    What Johnson Controls does

    Johnson Controls, headquartered in Cork, Ireland, makes, installs and services equipment that heats, cools, secures and protects commercial buildings. Its products include commercial heating, ventilation and air-conditioning (HVAC) equipment, industrial refrigeration, building controls, security systems and fire detection and suppression systems. Customers include owners of offices, data centers, hospitals, schools and government buildings. Its brands include York, Metasys and Simplex.

    The company sells mainly through its own sales and service offices, and also through distributors. In fiscal 2025 products and systems were about 68% of sales from continuing operations and services were 32%. Services include maintenance, repair and replacement of equipment, plus energy consulting and an OpenBlue software platform for managing buildings. It has three regional segments: Americas, EMEA (Europe, Middle East and Africa) and APAC (Asia Pacific).

    On July 31, 2025 it sold its residential and light commercial HVAC business to Bosch. It names Honeywell, Siemens, Schneider Electric, Carrier, Trane and Daikin among its larger competitors.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Falling short in developing or buying new products and technology that customers accept at acceptable margins.
    • Problems with product quality, on-time delivery or supplier shortages.
    • Demand that rises and falls with cyclical industries such as construction.
    • Trade and tariff changes, and the risks of operating in many countries.
    • Asbestos-related product lawsuits and environmental contamination liability.
    From Johnson Controls's annual report on Form 10-K, filed November 14, 2025, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
