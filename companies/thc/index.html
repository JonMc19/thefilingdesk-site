    What Tenet Health does

    Tenet is a healthcare services company based in Dallas. It runs 50 acute care and specialty hospitals in eight states, mostly in urban and suburban areas, along with employed physicians and outpatient sites such as urgent care and imaging centers. Patients are people who need hospital care, surgery and emergency treatment.

    It has two segments. Hospital Operations includes the hospitals and Conifer, which provides billing, collections and related services to hospitals, health systems, physician practices and employers. Ambulatory Care is USPI, which holds ownership interests in 533 ambulatory surgery centers (outpatient surgery sites) and surgical hospitals. Tenet is paid for patient care by government programs such as Medicare and Medicaid and by private insurers, and it competes with other hospitals and outpatient providers for patients, physicians and insurer contracts.

    Tenet employs nearly 100,000 people. It also has a business center in the Philippines that supports administrative work. In 2025 it opened a new 54-bed hospital in Port St. Lucie, Florida.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Changes to Medicare and Medicaid and other government payment programs that could cut what it is paid.
    • Trouble keeping or renewing contracts with insurers on good terms, or lower rates from them.
    • Higher labor costs and shortages of nurses and other healthcare workers.
    • Its ability to attract and keep the physicians its hospitals and surgery centers need.
    • Its large debt and the chance it cannot pay or refinance it on good terms.
    From Tenet Health's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 17, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
