    What Penske Automotive Group does

    Penske Automotive Group runs car and truck dealerships. In 2025 it earned $31.8 billion in revenue. Most of that came from retail automotive dealerships in the United States, the U.K., and other countries. The rest came from retail commercial truck dealerships and from commercial vehicle distribution and other operations.

    Its car dealerships offer over 40 vehicle brands. Premium brands such as Audi, BMW, Land Rover, Lexus, Mercedes-Benz and Porsche accounted for 71% of its retail franchised dealership revenue in 2025. It operated 365 franchised dealerships, 148 in the U.S. and 217 elsewhere, mainly in the U.K. It also runs used vehicle dealerships under the CarShop and Sytner Select names. Besides selling new and used vehicles, it makes money from maintenance and repair, financing and insurance products it sells for outside companies, extended service contracts and parts.

    Its truck business sells trucks and provides service and parts. The company also owns 28.9% of Penske Transportation Solutions, which leases, rents and maintains trucks. It has over 27,700 employees. In some U.K. and European dealerships it uses an agency model, where it earns a fee for helping the manufacturer sell a new vehicle and does not hold the vehicle in inventory.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Reliance on vehicle makers, who exercise significant control over its dealerships.
    • Changes in how vehicles are sold, including direct sales by makers and digital retailers.
    • Tariffs and trade rules that affect vehicle prices and supply.
    • Heavy debt and other commitments.
    • Its two largest stockholders, Penske Corporation and Mitsui, together own most of its stock.
