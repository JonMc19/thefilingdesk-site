    What Joby Aviation does

    Joby is developing a piloted, all-electric air taxi that takes off and lands vertically, known as an eVTOL. It is designed to carry a pilot and up to four passengers, and the company hopes it will fly cities' short routes faster than driving. Joby has not yet started a commercial service, and the aircraft is still going through certification with the US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA).

    Joby names three possible routes to market: air taxi service that Joby owns and operates, service run by affiliates, and direct aircraft sales, including to defense customers. It plans to manufacture, operate and sell its aircraft itself, as a vertically integrated company. It also builds Elevate OS, its own software that links data from building, flying and maintaining the aircraft.

    Joby is working with regulators in other countries, including the United Kingdom, Japan, South Korea, Australia, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates, where it says foreign certification may open a path to flying passengers before US certification.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Not getting the regulatory approvals it needs to sell its aircraft or run its service, in the US or abroad.
    • Delays in launching commercial service.
    • A market for air taxis that is not yet established, with customers who may not adopt it or pay its projected prices.
    • Airspace rules that may need to change, which could limit how many flights it can run.
    • Competitors reaching the market before it does.
