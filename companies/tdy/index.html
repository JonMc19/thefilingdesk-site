    What Teledyne Technologies does

    Teledyne makes sensors, cameras, instruments and electronic parts for customers that need equipment to work reliably in demanding settings. Its markets include aerospace and defense, factory automation, environmental monitoring, oceanographic research, offshore oil and gas, medical imaging and pharmaceutical research. Products range from infrared and X-ray cameras to underwater vehicles, air and water quality monitors, and electronic test equipment.

    It has four segments: Digital Imaging, Instrumentation, Aerospace and Defense Electronics, and Engineered Systems. Digital Imaging is the largest. Engineered Systems does systems engineering and complex manufacturing for defense, space, environmental and energy customers. The U.S. Department of War, its main government customer, accounted for $1,203.6 million of net sales in 2025.

    Teledyne grows partly by buying other companies: it completed four acquisitions in 2025 and one more after year end. It had about 15,800 employees in 38 countries at the end of 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A recession could reduce demand for its products across the many industrial markets it serves.
    • Component and raw material shortages or price increases could stop it from making and shipping products.
    • Trade tensions, tariffs, sanctions and export limits could hurt its sales abroad, including in China.
    • Downturns in cyclical markets such as offshore oil and gas could reduce its sales.
    • Breaking rules on U.S. Government contracts or export controls, including FLIR's past export practices, could harm it.
    From Teledyne Technologies's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 20, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
