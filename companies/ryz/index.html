    What Ryerson does

    This company, which filed the report as Ryerson, buys industrial metals in bulk, processes them and sells them in smaller quantities. It carries about 75,000 products in stainless steel, aluminum, carbon steel and alloy steels, plus a limited line of nickel and red metals. It also offers cutting and fabrication, and says nearly 80% of what it sells is processed to meet customer needs.

    It works through service centers in the United States, Canada and Mexico, and has a smaller operation in China. Its customers range from local fabricators and machine shops to large equipment makers. It delivers with its own fleet of tractors and trailers, often the day after an order. It says metals service centers act as go-betweens that let metal producers sell in large lots while buyers get smaller orders and faster delivery.

    In February 2026 it completed a merger with Olympic Steel, another metals service center, which it says makes it North America's second largest. It also grows by buying smaller companies.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Changing metals prices, which affect its sales, margins and cash needs.
    • Weak customers and a weak economy, and strong competition in metals service.
    • Loss of a main supplier, or product shortages, or imports and global overcapacity that push down U.S. metal prices.
    • Not integrating the Olympic Steel merger or other acquisitions as planned.
    • Underfunded pension and retirement benefit plans that could need large unplanned payments.
    From Ryerson's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 23, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
