    What Casey's does

    Casey's runs convenience stores that sell gasoline, prepared food, drinks, groceries and other items. At the end of April 2026 it operated 2,944 stores in 19 states, about half of them in Iowa, Missouri and Illinois. Approximately 71% of its stores were opened in towns with fewer than 20,000 people, where national convenience chains are often absent. Almost all stores have a full kitchen that makes pizza, sandwiches, donuts and other hot food, and nearly all sell fuel.

    It earns most of its revenue from retail fuel and from the products sold inside its stores. Over the last three fiscal years, prepared food, drinks, groceries and general merchandise brought in about 36% of total revenue but about 63% of revenue less cost of goods sold, so the inside sales matter more to profit than their share of revenue suggests. A small wholesale fuel business supplies dealer sites and made up approximately 3% of fiscal 2026 revenue.

    Casey's runs three distribution centers that supply most stores and also delivers most of its own fuel. It had about 23,490 full-time and 26,358 part-time employees, and over 10 million members in its Rewards program. It competes with local grocery and convenience stores, dollar stores, restaurants and other fuel sellers.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • The volatility of wholesale fuel costs, and falling fuel demand from efficiency gains and changing consumer habits.
    • Rising food ingredient costs, since prepared food is a key part of its profit.
    • Disruption to its distribution network or distribution centers that supply its stores.
    • Government action and tax increases that cut tobacco and nicotine sales.
    • Hazards in storing and moving fuel, which could cause disruptions and large losses or liabilities.
    From Casey's's annual report on Form 10-K, filed June 22, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    What Casey's says to expect

    • Fiscal 2027: sales, margin, expenses and EBITDAThe Company expects inside same-store sales to increase 2% to 5% with an inside margin above 42%. The Company expects same-store fuel gallons sold to be negative 1% to positive 1%. Total operating expenses are expected to increase approximately 5% to 7%.
    • Fiscal 2027: EBITDA growthThe Company expects EBITDA to increase 8% to 10%, which would imply 35% on a two-year stack basis at the midpoint of the range.
    • Fiscal 2027: store openings and capital spendingThe Company expects to open at least 120 stores in fiscal 2027 through a mix of M&A and new store construction. Net interest expense is expected to be approximately $95 million. Depreciation and amortization is expected to be approximately $490 million and the purchase of property and equipment is expected to be approximately $800 million.
