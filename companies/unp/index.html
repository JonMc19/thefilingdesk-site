    What Union Pacific does

    Union Pacific is a freight railroad. Its main operating company, Union Pacific Railroad, connects 23 states in the western two-thirds of the United States. It runs trains from all major West Coast and Gulf Coast ports to Eastern gateways, links with Canada's rail systems, and says it is the only railroad serving all six major Mexico gateways. Its network has 32,889 route miles.

    It earns money by hauling freight for customers in three groups. Bulk covers grain and grain products, fertilizer, food and refrigerated goods, and coal and renewables. Industrial covers construction materials, chemicals, plastics, forest products, metals and ores, and petroleum products. Premium covers finished automobiles, auto parts and goods shipped in intermodal containers, and generated 30% of total freight revenue in 2025. The company treats the railroad as one reportable segment.

    Union Pacific had an average of 29,287 employees during 2025. It faces competition from other railroads, trucking companies, ships, barges and pipelines. It has agreed to acquire Norfolk Southern, a deal that still needs approvals, and it paused its share buyback program because of it.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • The pending Norfolk Southern merger may be delayed, cost more than planned, or fail to close.
    • Competition from other railroads, trucks, ships, barges and pipelines.
    • Strikes or work stoppages by its workforce.
    • Fluctuating fuel prices, which are a large part of its costs.
    • Carrying hazardous materials, which pose risks if there is a release or fire.
    From Union Pacific's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 6, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
