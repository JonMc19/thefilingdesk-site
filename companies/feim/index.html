    What Frequency Electronics does

    Frequency Electronics makes precision clocks and timing equipment. Its technology keeps time and generates stable radio frequencies, and it ends up in satellites, military communications and command systems, and electronic warfare equipment. Founded in 1961 as a research firm working mainly on U.S. government contracts, it now designs and builds products for both government and commercial use.

    It has two main markets. For satellites, it supplies master timing systems, power converters and frequency equipment for satellite payloads. For secure ground, sea and air communications, it supplies clocks and software that support military command and control systems. It points to threats such as jamming or faking GPS signals as a reason for demand. It reports two segments: FEI-NY, which includes its government systems and communications subsidiaries, and FEI-Zyfer.

    The company relies heavily on U.S. government programs, as a prime contractor or subcontractor. It derived approximately 91% of its sales from them in fiscal 2026. It has 242 employees, almost all in the United States.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Relying heavily on U.S. government programs, whose funding can be delayed, cut or ended.
    • Government shutdowns or budget fights could delay payments or cancel contracts.
    • Contracts typically are funded only in stages, so more funding must keep coming.
    • It may not win new work on programs where it competes.
    From Frequency Electronics's annual report on Form 10-K, filed July 17, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
