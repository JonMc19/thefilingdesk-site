    What Nasdaq does

    Nasdaq operates stock exchanges and sells the technology and data that markets and financial firms use. It was founded in 1971 as a subsidiary of FINRA and became an independent registered exchange in 2007. In 2008 it combined with OMX AB, which brought exchanges in the Nordic and Baltic region. It has since added Verafin in 2021 and Adenza (with its AxiomSL and Calypso products) in 2023.

    It reports three segments. Capital Access Platforms covers data and listing services, indexes, and workflow and insights products. Financial Technology covers software for financial crime management, regulatory technology and capital markets technology, which it sells to banks, exchanges and other clients. Market Services covers equity derivatives trading and clearing, cash equity trading, and fixed income, currency and commodities trading.

    Nasdaq says it uses a single "One Nasdaq" go-to-market strategy and is adding AI to products such as Verafin, which supports monitoring of transactions for financial crime. It faces intense competition in North America and Europe.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • System failures or delays on its exchanges and technology that could harm its markets and customers.
    • New products and trading initiatives, such as tokenized securities and longer trading hours, that may not succeed.
    • Losing trading volume or market share, which could make its exchanges look less liquid.
    • Credit and counterparty risks from its clearinghouse operations.
    • Strict regulation of its exchange businesses and conflicts between its regulatory and for-profit roles.
    From Nasdaq's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
