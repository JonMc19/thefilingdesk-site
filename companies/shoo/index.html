    What Steven Madden does

    Steve Madden designs, sources and markets fashion footwear, handbags, apparel and accessories. It sells to department stores, mass merchants, off-price retailers, shoe chains, online retailers and specialty stores in the United States, the United Kingdom, Europe, Canada and Mexico, and through joint ventures and distribution arrangements in many other markets. It also sells directly to shoppers through its own stores, concessions and websites.

    It reports three segments. Wholesale Footwear, which sells brands such as Steve Madden, Kurt Geiger, Dolce Vita and Betsey Johnson, made up 40.9% of 2025 revenue. Wholesale Accessories/Apparel, mainly handbags and apparel, made up 25.3%. The Direct-to-Consumer segment runs full-price and outlet stores, concessions and e-commerce sites under the Steve Madden, Kurt Geiger, Carvela, Dolce Vita and ATM names.

    The company bought Kurt Geiger and took on debt to pay for it. It describes its edge as design creativity and quick delivery of trend-driven products at accessible prices.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to guess changing fashion trends correctly.
    • Competition from established companies and newer entrants.
    • Losing one or more big retail customers, or their failure to pay for goods bought on credit.
    • Trouble combining Kurt Geiger with the rest of the business.
    • Debt taken on to buy Kurt Geiger, which limits its flexibility.
    From Steven Madden's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 2, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
