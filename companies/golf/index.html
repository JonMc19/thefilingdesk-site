    What Acushnet does

    Acushnet designs, makes and sells golf products for what it calls dedicated golfers, meaning avid players who spend time and money improving their game. Its two main brands are Titleist, known for golf balls and clubs, and FootJoy, known for shoes, gloves and apparel. It also owns Vokey Design wedges, Scotty Cameron putters, Club Glove and Links & Kings gear, and KJUS outerwear. Acushnet reported net sales of $2,558.7 million for 2025.

    It has three reportable segments: Titleist golf equipment, FootJoy golf wear and Golf gear. They made up about 62%, 22% and 10% of 2025 net sales. Balls and gloves, which golfers use up and replace, were nearly 40% of sales, and longer-lasting items like clubs, shoes, apparel and gear were over 60%.

    Acushnet sells mainly through on-course golf shops and golf specialty retailers, and also to other retailers and directly to consumers on its websites. It makes or controls the design, sourcing and production of most of its products. Its marketing strategy is for its products to be the most played by the best professional and amateur players.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Fewer rounds of golf or golf players, including from bad weather or weaker consumer spending.
    • Tariffs and other trade policies that raise its costs.
    • Changes to the Rules of Golf for equipment that could limit what it can sell.
    • Reliance on sole or limited suppliers for raw materials and parts.
    • Professional golfers could stop using its products, or receive negative publicity.
    From Acushnet's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
