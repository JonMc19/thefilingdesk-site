    What Teradyne does

    Teradyne, founded in 1960, makes automated test equipment and robots. Its test systems check semiconductors, wireless products, data storage devices and complex electronics for makers in industries such as consumer electronics, automotive, computing and aerospace. Its robots are mainly collaborative robotic arms and autonomous mobile robots, sold to factories, warehouses and other industrial users.

    It reports three businesses. Semiconductor Test sells chip testers to chipmakers, foundries and companies that assemble and test chips, with demand from data centers and AI chips a recent driver. Robotics sells robots, and most of its revenue is in foreign currencies. Product Test, set up as a segment in 2025, covers circuit-board, wireless, photonic chip and defense and aerospace test systems. Teradyne says it faces intense competition in each segment.

    Sales of its test products are concentrated: its five largest direct customers made up 44% of revenue in 2025. It had about 6,600 employees at the end of 2025. In 2025 it bought Infineon's automated test equipment technology and team in Germany.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Swings in the semiconductor and electronics industries that cut customers' equipment spending.
    • Dependence on a few large customers for a big share of its test equipment sales.
    • Intense competition, including from customers' in-house efforts and new Asian rivals.
    • Trade restrictions and tariffs that limit where it can sell and raise costs.
    • Shortages of electrical components and higher costs that make it hard to meet orders.
    From Teradyne's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 19, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
