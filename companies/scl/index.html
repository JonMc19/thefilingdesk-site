    What Stepan does

    Stepan makes specialty and intermediate chemicals that it sells to other manufacturers, which use them in a wide range of end products. It does not sell directly to shoppers. It reports three segments: Surfactants, Polymers and Specialty Products.

    Surfactants are the cleaning agents in laundry and dish detergents, shampoos, body wash, fabric softeners and toothpaste. They are also used in farm chemicals, floor polishes, wallboard manufacturing and oilfield products. Polymers include polyols, used in rigid foam insulation for buildings, and polyester resins used in coatings, adhesives and sealants. Specialty Products are chemicals used in food, flavoring, nutritional supplements and medicines.

    Stepan says it competes on product performance, price, technical help, meeting each customer's needs and having enough capacity. Its competitors range from large global producers to small specialty firms. Its plants run mostly on electricity and natural gas, and its raw materials are petroleum-based or plant-based. Because it handles chemicals and hazardous materials, it is subject to many environmental and safety laws. Compliance involved capital spending of $9.8 million in 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Accidents or unplanned shutdowns at its manufacturing plants.
    • Volatile costs and disrupted supply of raw materials, natural gas and electricity.
    • Customers changing their formulas or new technologies that cut demand for its products.
    • Tariffs, trade policy and currency swings in its international business.
    • Environmental, health and safety rules, and cost overruns on new plant projects.
    From Stepan's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
