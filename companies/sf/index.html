    What Stifel does

    Stifel Financial is a US financial services firm that serves individual investors, institutions and companies. Its main activities are private client services such as securities transactions and financial planning; sales, trading and research in stocks and bonds, and municipal finance; investment banking, including mergers and acquisitions, public offerings and private placements; and retail and commercial banking, including personal and commercial loans.

    It reports three segments: Global Wealth Management, Institutional Group, and Other. Its subsidiaries include broker-dealers such as Stifel Nicolaus and Keefe, Bruyette & Woods, an independent contractor broker-dealer firm, banks and trust companies, and an asset manager. It earns money from client fees, commissions, investment banking and advisory fees, asset management fees, and interest from its banks.

    Stifel says it has a 135-year history and has grown through acquisitions. In 2024 and 2025 it bought Finance 500 and CB Resource, part of the wealth management business of B. Riley Financial, and Bryan, Garnier & Co., a European investment bank focused on technology and healthcare companies.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Not having enough funding, liquidity or access to capital.
    • Swings in client cash sweep and bank deposit balances, and higher costs to get such deposits.
    • Market and interest rate risk, and risks to its own capital from underwriting and trading.
    • Losing senior professionals and financial advisors.
    • Reliance on fees from managing client accounts, and strong competition and pricing pressure.
    From Stifel's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 24, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
