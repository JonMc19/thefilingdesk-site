    What EZCORP does

    EZCORP runs pawn stores in the United States and Latin America. At its pawn stores it lends cash against the value of items customers leave as collateral, and it sells merchandise to shoppers looking for good value. The merchandise is mostly pre-owned items that were forfeited when customers did not repay their loans, or that the store bought from customers.

    At September 30, 2025, it operated 1,360 locations: 545 in the U.S. (mainly EZPAWN and Value Pawn & Jewelry), 622 in Mexico (mainly Empeño Fácil and Cash Apoyo Efectivo), and 193 in Guatemala, El Salvador and Honduras (GuatePrenda and MaxiEfectivo). By store count, EZCORP says it is the second-largest pawn store owner and operator in the U.S. and one of the largest in Latin America. Customers can also manage pawn loans, layaways and rewards through its EZ+ web applications.

    EZCORP earns money from fees and interest on pawn loans and from selling merchandise. It also holds a stake in Cash Converters International, a company listed in Australia that franchises and runs stores and offers personal finance.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Changes in laws that affect pawn lending, or failing to comply with them.
    • Negative portrayals of the pawn industry that could lead to tighter rules.
    • Its U.S. business is concentrated in Texas and Florida.
    • A sharp fall in gold values or in gold transactions.
    • Risks from selling firearms, and from robberies and other crimes at its stores.
    From EZCORP's annual report on Form 10-K, filed November 13, 2025, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
