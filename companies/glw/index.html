    What Corning does

    Corning is a materials company, tracing back to a glass business started in 1851. It makes glass, ceramics and optical fiber for other companies' products. Its markets include optical communications, displays, mobile electronics, cars and trucks, life sciences, semiconductors and solar. It makes products in 14 countries.

    It reports five segments. Optical Communications sells optical fiber, cable and connection hardware to telephone and cable carriers and to businesses, including data centers. Display makes precision glass for screens. Specialty Materials makes specialty glass, including damage-resistant cover glass for mobile devices. Automotive combines technical glass for vehicles with clean-air products for cars and trucks. Life Sciences makes lab products used in drug discovery and delivery.

    Corning says demand for fiber and connection products inside and between data centers is rising with artificial intelligence. Corning says its Optical Communications and Display segments produce a large part of its profit and cash flow. In 2025 it started managing its auto glass and environmental technologies businesses together as the Automotive segment, and it renamed Display Technologies as Display.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Optical Communications and Display bring in much of its profit and cash, so a drop in either would hurt.
    • Prices and supply of raw materials, energy and other inputs it relies on.
    • Disruptions at its factories, or those of its contract manufacturers.
    • Trouble enforcing its patents and other rights, and no guarantee its research spending will pay off.
    • Trade policies that could limit what it can sell or make outside the U.S.
    From Corning's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
