    What Gevo does

    Gevo makes renewable fuels and other carbon-based products for sectors that are hard to run on electricity or hydrogen, such as jet fuel, specialty and on-road fuels, chemicals and materials. It also makes protein and feed as co-products. It was founded in 2005. Its main focus is a process that turns alcohol into jet fuel, which it calls alcohol-to-jet.

    Besides selling physical products, Gevo plans to earn money from what it calls carbon abatement value, by selling carbon credits, federal and state fuel credits and similar items. It also hopes to license its plant designs to others. It operates renewable natural gas facilities in Iowa.

    On January 31, 2025, Gevo bought the Red Trail Energy ethanol plant in North Dakota for a purchase price of $210 million, subject to adjustments. The plant already makes ethanol, protein and feed and corn oil, and captures and stores carbon. Gevo says it intends to build a unit there to turn that ethanol into sustainable aviation fuel.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A history of net losses, and no guarantee it will reach or keep a profit.
    • Needing a lot more financing to build its growth projects.
    • Growth projects that may be late, cost more than planned or not perform as expected.
    • Swings in corn and other feedstock prices and in oil prices and fuel demand.
    • A fall in the value of the carbon and fuel credits it hopes to sell.
    From Gevo's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 5, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
