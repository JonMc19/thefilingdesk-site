    What Tronox does

    Tronox makes titanium dioxide (TiO2) pigment, the white pigment used in products such as paints, coatings and plastics. It is vertically integrated, which means it also mines and processes the raw materials. It operates titanium-bearing mineral sand mines and smelting operations in Australia and South Africa, and it has seven pigment plants in the United States, Australia, Brazil, the UK, France and Saudi Arabia.

    The company sells to approximately 1,200 TiO2 customers around the world. Mining the mineral sands also yields co-products that it sells, including zircon, pig iron and monazite, a mineral that contains rare earths. It also makes high purity titanium chemicals and ultrafine TiO2 for specialty uses. Its stated aim is to become the low-cost TiO2 producer and to make as much of its own feedstock as possible.

    In 2025 the company started two major mining projects in South Africa to replace mines nearing the end of their lives, and launched a cost improvement program. It says it competes on innovation, reliability, product quality, customer service and price. It also says it is working on rare earth initiatives that need financing.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Weaker demand for its end-use products when the global or regional economy slows.
    • Competition and excess production capacity in the TiO2 industry, particularly in China.
    • Higher energy and raw material prices, or interruptions in their supply.
    • Industrial accidents, equipment failures and production stoppages at its mining and chemical sites.
    • Heavy exposure to South Africa, where it has large mining assets and gets a significant share of its revenue.
    From Tronox's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 20, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
