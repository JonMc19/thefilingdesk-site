    What Huckleberry.ai (formerly Domo) does

    Huckleberry.ai, which its annual report calls Domo, sells a cloud-based data and AI platform to businesses. Customers use it to pull data from across the company, prepare and organize it, analyze and display it in dashboards, automate tasks, and build apps. It also includes AI features that can generate insights and trigger alerts, run within the customer's own permission and governance controls.

    The platform works on top of cloud data systems such as Snowflake, Databricks and Google BigQuery, and it has more than 1,000 connectors to other data sources. Domo says customers can adopt it piece by piece, and that it combines tools that often come from several separate vendors. As of January 31, 2026, it had more than 2,400 organizations as customers.

    Customers pay for subscriptions, and the company recognizes that revenue evenly over each subscription's term. It sells to large organizations through long sales cycles, and it sells outside the United States, where some customers pay in other currencies. It has a credit facility with restrictive covenants.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It has a history of losses and may not reach profitability.
    • It must manage its growth and may not be able to raise the capital it needs.
    • Subscription revenue is recognized over time, so near-term sales changes show up in results only slowly.
    • Long, costly and uncertain sales cycles with large customers.
    • Its credit facility has restrictive and financial covenants that limit its flexibility.
