    What Wynn Resorts does

    Wynn Resorts builds and runs large resort hotels that combine casinos with luxury rooms, shops, restaurants, entertainment and convention space. It has four resorts: Wynn Palace and Wynn Macau in Macau, China, Wynn Las Vegas and Encore in Nevada, and Encore Boston Harbor in Massachusetts. Guests are gamblers, vacationers and groups attending meetings.

    Its four resorts are its reporting segments, and it gets its money from casino gaming, hotel rooms, food and drink, retail space and entertainment. It owns about 72% of Wynn Macau, Limited, the company that runs its Macau resorts. Macau operators pay a special gaming tax of 35% of gross gaming revenue. Wynn Resorts competes with the other casinos in each market, including the 18 other casinos in Macau.

    Its right to run casinos in Macau comes from a gaming concession (a government contract) that runs from 2023 to the end of 2032. It also owns a 40% stake in an unconsolidated resort, Wynn Al Marjan Island, which is under construction in Ras Al Khaimah, United Arab Emirates, and which it expects to open in 2027.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Weaker spending by guests if the economy slows, because casino resorts rely on discretionary spending.
    • Depending on a small number of resorts for all of its cash flow.
    • The Macau government could end its gaming concession without paying compensation if the terms are not met.
    • Strict gaming regulation and licensing in Macau, Nevada and Massachusetts.
    • Heavy debt and the chance that cash flow will not cover what it owes.
    From Wynn Resorts's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 2, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
