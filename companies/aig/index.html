    What American International Group does

    American International Group, known as AIG, is a global commercial and personal insurer. It sells property, casualty, financial and specialty insurance to businesses, and accident and health, auto, home and warranty insurance to individuals. It sells in about 45 countries.

    AIG reports three segments: North America Commercial, International Commercial and Global Personal. Together they make up its General Insurance business. Commercial products include property, general liability, workers' compensation, professional liability such as directors and officers cover, and marine, aviation and political risk insurance. Personal products include group accident and health cover, personal auto and homeowners policies, device protection and extended warranties, and cover for high net-worth individuals. A fourth group, Other Operations, holds items such as investment income on the parent company's liquidity portfolio, dividend income from Corebridge Financial, corporate expenses and interest expense.

    AIG earns money mainly from insurance premiums and from income on its investments. Its costs are mostly claims, the costs of settling them, commissions and other costs of selling and servicing policies. It says it competes with global, national and local insurers, reinsurers and underwriting syndicates, on risk acceptance, price, service and terms. At the end of 2025 it had about 22,100 employees, with 47 percent in the Asia Pacific region.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Claims that arrive later or cost more than the reserves it has set aside for them.
    • Natural and man-made catastrophes and mass torts, which can hurt its results, liquidity and ratings.
    • An investment portfolio that is concentrated in certain parts of the economy, and some investments that are hard to sell quickly.
    • Risk management policies that may fail to catch risks it did not foresee or expect.
    • Heavy regulation that can raise capital requirements for its insurance subsidiaries or cut its profit.
    From American International Group's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
