    What Intellia Therapeutics does

    Intellia Therapeutics develops gene-editing medicines based on CRISPR, a tool that can cut and change DNA. It has no approved product. Its approach is to deliver the editing tool into the body using lipid nanoparticles, tiny fat bubbles, and aim for a one-time treatment given by a single intravenous infusion in an outpatient setting.

    Its two lead candidates are in Phase 3 trials. Lonvo-z is for hereditary angioedema, a rare inherited condition that causes swelling attacks, and the company is preparing for a planned launch in the first half of 2027. Nex-z is for transthyretin amyloidosis, a disease in which a misfolded protein builds up in organs. Intellia says these are the first in vivo genome editing candidates in Phase 3.

    Intellia works with partners, mainly Regeneron, and licenses technology from others. It plans to build its own sales and manufacturing capability.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • CRISPR gene editing has only recently been tested in people and may never lead to products it can sell.
    • Clinical development is long, costly and uncertain, and trials could show safety problems.
    • Negative public opinion and tighter regulation of gene editing could hurt it.
    • Its products may not be accepted by doctors, patients or insurers.
    • Patents of others or loss of licensed rights could hurt it.
