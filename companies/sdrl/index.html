    What Seadrill does

    Seadrill is an offshore drilling contractor. It owns and operates drillships and semi-submersible rigs, which are floating platforms, and a jackup rig, that drill oil and gas wells in shallow to ultra-deep water around the world. Its customers include large oil companies, state-owned national oil companies and independent oil and gas producers.

    It earns money by contracting each rig to a customer at a set daily rate, called a dayrate. It also earns fees for managing drilling units owned by affiliated companies. At the end of 2025 it owned 15 drilling units. Ten were operating, one was being upgraded for a contract starting in the second quarter of 2026, one was in repair, and three were cold stacked, meaning idle and taken out of active service. It also managed two units owned by Sonangol.

    The company is based in Bermuda and had about 3,000 employees. It came out of Chapter 11 bankruptcy in February 2022 and has been listed on the New York Stock Exchange since October 2022.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Offshore drilling demand that is cyclical, with price competition and volatility.
    • Customers that may cancel or renegotiate contracts or accept lower dayrates, and a backlog that may not be realized.
    • Not renewing or finding new contracts for its rigs.
    • Delays and cost overruns when upgrading, repairing or surveying rigs.
    • Operating hazards that insurance may not fully cover.
    From Seadrill's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
