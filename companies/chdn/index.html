    What Churchill Downs does

    Churchill Downs runs horse racing, betting and casino businesses in the United States. It owns Churchill Downs Racetrack in Louisville, Kentucky, home of the Kentucky Derby, and operates historical racing venues, where people bet on the outcomes of past races on machines, in Kentucky, Virginia and New Hampshire. It also operates regional casinos and online wagering businesses.

    It reports three segments: Live and Historical Racing, Wagering Services and Solutions, and Gaming. The racing venues earn commissions on bets, fees for sending races to other betting sites, and money from admissions, seating, sponsorships, television rights, and food and beverages. The Gaming segment earns money from its casino properties. Churchill Downs competes with other racing, gaming and wagering businesses and expects that competition to grow.

    Much of its business depends on licenses and approvals from state regulators, and on agreements with horsemen and other racetracks in some states. The company has spent heavily in recent years to rebuild seating and guest areas at Churchill Downs Racetrack, and says it pursues acquisitions to grow.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Fewer visitors and less betting if consumers cut back spending or lose confidence in the integrity of its racing and gaming.
    • Strong competition that it expects to increase.
    • Changes in demand for the Kentucky Derby and Churchill Downs Racetrack, such as attendance, wagering and sponsorships.
    • Depending on slot and historical racing machine makers for popular games at acceptable prices.
    • Acquisitions and building projects that may cost more, take longer or fail to work out.
    From Churchill Downs's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 25, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
