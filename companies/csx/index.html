    What CSX does

    CSX is a railroad company based in Jacksonville, Florida. Its main subsidiary, CSX Transportation, runs a rail network of about 20,000 route miles serving 26 states east of the Mississippi River, the District of Columbia and two Canadian provinces. It carries freight for manufacturers, farmers, energy producers, retailers and others, and connects to ports on the coasts, rivers and the Great Lakes.

    In 2025 the company's services generated $14.1 billion of revenue across four lines of business. Merchandise, which covers chemicals, farm products, autos, minerals, forest products and metals, brought in 62% of revenue. Intermodal, which moves containers and trailers by rail and then truck, was 15%. Coal was 13%, and trucking was 6%. The rest comes from other revenue, such as fees for storing containers and regional railroads.

    Its other subsidiaries include Quality Carriers, which it calls the largest hauler of bulk liquid chemicals by truck in North America, and terminal and auto distribution businesses. CSX says it competes with other transportation providers.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • New laws or rules that could change its earnings or limit its freedom to set prices.
    • A train accident involving hazardous materials, which it is required by law to carry.
    • Network or supply chain limits that slow service or cut shipments.
    • Competition from other transportation providers.
    • Severe weather and other events that interrupt service and cost more than its insurance covers.
    From CSX's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
