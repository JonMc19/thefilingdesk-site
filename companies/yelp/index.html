    What Yelp does

    Yelp runs a website and app where people find local businesses and read reviews of them. It says its platform holds more than 300 million ratings and reviews, covering a broad range of business categories. Its stated mission is to connect consumers with local businesses.

    Yelp says it earns substantially all of its revenue from advertising. Businesses pay to be shown in sponsored search results, on the Yelp pages of related businesses, and in other places on the platform. The price of each ad click is set in an auction for every placement. It also sells subscription services that help businesses run day to day, such as Yelp Guest Manager, a set of front-of-house tools for restaurants and nightlife venues, and AI-powered phone assistants called Yelp Host and Yelp Receptionist.

    Yelp says its reviews come from users and that this content is why consumers come to it, which keeps its cost of getting traffic low. It relies on Internet search engines and app stores to send people to its platform, and it faces competition that it expects to increase.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Weak local economies that lead businesses to cut their advertising.
    • Depending on advertising for nearly all revenue, so it must keep and grow its advertisers.
    • A fall in traffic to its platform or in how much people use it.
    • Relying on Internet search engines and app stores to send people to it.
    • Failing to gather enough reliable content from users, or to protect its brand.
    From Yelp's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
