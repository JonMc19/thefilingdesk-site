    What Agilent Technologies does

    Agilent sells instruments, software, services and supplies that laboratories use to analyze samples. Its customers include pharmaceutical and biotech companies, clinical and diagnostic labs, and labs that test chemicals, food, the environment and materials.

    It reports three segments. Life Sciences and Diagnostics Markets covers liquid chromatography and mass spectrometry instruments, cell analysis, cancer-diagnostic products for pathology, genomics products, and contract manufacturing of ingredients for a class of drugs built on nucleic acids. Agilent CrossLab covers services, consumables, software and lab automation across the whole lab. Applied Markets covers gas chromatography, spectroscopy and vacuum instruments and software used to identify and measure the properties of substances.

    Agilent sells mainly through its own sales force, and also through distributors, resellers and online. As of October 31, 2025, it employed about 18,100 people worldwide. It reorganized its segments in November 2024, and past segment results were restated to match.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Demand that depends on customers' capital spending, research budgets and government funding.
    • Failing to launch new products in time against fast-changing technology and competitors.
    • Risks from selling and operating in many countries, including currency swings.
    • Strict regulation of its diagnostics products, including by the Food and Drug Administration.
    • Acquisitions, investments and divestitures that may not turn out as expected.
    From Agilent Technologies's annual report on Form 10-K, filed December 22, 2025, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
