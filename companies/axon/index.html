    What Axon Enterprise does

    Axon sells technology to police, other public safety agencies and some private customers. It describes itself as a company that sells both hardware and software that work together. Its products include TASER stun devices, body cameras, in-car and fixed cameras, drones, and cloud software for storing and sharing video evidence, writing reports and running real-time operations. Many of its software tools use artificial intelligence.

    It reports two segments. Software and Services sells cloud subscriptions, such as Axon Evidence, Axon Records and the Draft One report-writing tool, along with professional services and some on-premise software licenses. Connected Devices sells TASER devices, body and other cameras, drone and counter-drone products, accessories and extended warranties. Its revenue comes from hardware sales, multi-year software subscriptions, professional services and extended warranties.

    Axon is based in Scottsdale, Arizona, and is expanding in Europe, Asia and the Americas. Its largest customers are law enforcement agencies, so its sales depend on their budgets and their acceptance of its products.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Depending on law enforcement agencies around the world to keep buying its products and services.
    • Depending on sales of its TASER devices, which may lose acceptance.
    • Selling cycles are long and it spends money before it knows whether a sale will follow.
    • A security breach at Axon or its cloud providers, which hold sensitive evidence.
    • Uncertainty over how AI in its products will be developed, regulated and accepted.
    From Axon Enterprise's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 25, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
