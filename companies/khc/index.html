    Two ways to value Kraft Heinz yourself, both starting from its filings: a discounted cash flow model and the price-to-earnings method.

    1Discounted cash flow (DCF)

    A discounted cash flow (DCF) model values a business as the cash it may generate in future, discounted back to today. The starting figures come from Kraft Heinz's 10-K (or, for net cash and shares, its latest 10-Q) and the assumptions are yours: start from one of three example scenarios, then change any figure to see what it implies for a share, and how that compares with the last share price.

    Value per share on three example scenarios, from Kraft Heinz's fiscal 2025 free cash flow of $3.7B, net cash of −$18.6B at December 27, 2025 and 1,187 million diluted shares, from its filings. The scenarios are fitted to Kraft Heinz: the base scenario's growth in years 1 to 5 is its revenue growth over the last 3 years, -2.0% a year, kept between 0% and 20%; the cautious and optimistic ones are lower and higher, and growth in years 6 to 10 is halfway to the long-run rate. The base scenario's discount rate is 8%: 7% for a stable business (Packaged Foods & Meats), plus 1 point for unsteady results (a loss in fiscal 2025); the cautious and optimistic ones use a point more and a point less.     How the model works. Free cash flow grows at the first rate for five years and the second rate for five more, and each year is discounted at the discount rate. Everything after year 10 is valued as year-10 cash flow × (1 + growth after year 10) ÷ (discount rate − growth after year 10), discounted back ten years. Net cash is added and the total is divided by diluted shares. The result depends entirely on the assumptions. It is not a forecast, a price target or a recommendation.     2Price-to-earnings (P/E) method

    Another common way to value a company is to multiply its earnings per share by a price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple: the number of years of today's earnings an investor pays for a share. A higher multiple usually means investors expect earnings to grow. Choose a multiple, for example from similar companies, to see the value it gives.

    The P/E method needs positive earnings, and Kraft Heinz's latest diluted earnings per share were negative or zero.
