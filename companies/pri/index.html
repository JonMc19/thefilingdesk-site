    What Primerica does

    Primerica sells financial products to middle-income households in the United States and Canada. It does this through a large force of independent sales representatives, who are licensed to sell life insurance and number 151,524. They help clients buy term life insurance, which Primerica itself underwrites. They also sell mutual funds, annuities, managed investments and other products, which Primerica distributes mainly for other companies.

    It has three segments. Term Life Insurance earns premiums from policies written by its insurance companies. Investment and Savings Products earns commissions and fees on sales and on client assets, along with recordkeeping and custodian fees. Corporate and Other Distributed Products covers the rest, including products it distributes for others. It sold its Senior Health business in 2024.

    Primerica says its annuity and investment products compete with those of many other companies. Its sales representatives are independent contractors, not employees. The company had 2,833 employees at the end of 2025. It became a public company in 2010 after Citigroup transferred the business to it.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to recruit and keep licensed independent sales representatives.
    • Laws that could force changes to its independent contractor sales model, or reclassify its representatives as employees.
    • Claims experience on its life insurance business that turns out worse than it expected.
    • Insurance and securities regulation, and the risk that representatives break the rules.
    • Losing or changing its relationships with the fund and annuity companies whose products it sells.
    From Primerica's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
