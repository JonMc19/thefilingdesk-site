    What Corteva does

    Corteva sells seed and crop protection products to farmers. It has its headquarters in Indianapolis, Indiana, and says its products reach farmers in approximately 110 countries. It became an independent company in 2019, when it was separated from DowDuPont.

    The company runs two segments. The Seed segment develops and supplies commercial seed, combining genetics with added traits meant to raise yields, and includes brands such as Pioneer. The Crop Protection segment supplies products that protect crops from weeds, insects and disease. It says it works face-to-face with farmers to learn what they need.

    On October 1, 2025, Corteva announced that it intends to split its Seed and Crop Protection businesses into two separate publicly traded companies, in a transaction meant to be a tax-free spin-off for US federal income tax purposes. It employed approximately 21,500 people worldwide at the end of 2025. It says it competes in an industry that is highly competitive and has consolidated.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Not getting or keeping the regulatory approvals it needs to sell some seed and crop protection products.
    • Its growth depending on the successful development and commercialization of pipeline products.
    • Public doubts about biotechnology and other farm technologies, which can affect approvals and purchases.
    • Unfavorable changes in the farm policies of governments and international bodies.
    • Climate change and unpredictable weather and seasons that could reduce its sales and earnings.
    From Corteva's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
