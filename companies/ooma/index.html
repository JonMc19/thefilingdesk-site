    What Ooma does

    Ooma sells phone and communications services over the internet, to businesses and to households. For businesses it offers cloud phone service with messaging, virtual receptionists and video meetings. It also sells a product that replaces old analog phone lines, such as Ooma AirDial, which keeps systems like alarms and elevator phones connected through the cloud. For consumers it offers home phone service.

    Most revenue comes from subscriptions and related services. The rest comes from selling the on-premise and end-point devices customers use to connect. Total revenue was $273.6 million in fiscal 2026. Customers are mainly in the United States and Canada, and a large share of revenue comes from small and medium-sized businesses.

    Ooma sells through its own channels and through retailers, resellers and technology services distributors. It buys its devices from manufacturers in Asia. In December 2025 it bought FluentStream.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Attracting new customers at a reasonable cost, while customers can cancel in most cases without penalty.
    • Service interruptions or security breaches that hurt its reputation and sales.
    • Relying on small and medium-sized businesses, which may struggle in a downturn.
    • Its debt, and its ability to draw more under its credit agreement.
    • Depending on Asian manufacturers, and the effect of tariffs on imported devices.
