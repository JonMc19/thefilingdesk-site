    What Morgan Stanley does

    Morgan Stanley is an investment bank and wealth manager that serves companies, governments, financial institutions and individual investors in the United States and around the world. It advises on mergers and other deals, helps clients raise money, trades stocks and bonds, manages people's savings and investments, and lends to clients. It is regulated by the Federal Reserve as a bank holding company. Its net revenues were $70.6 billion in 2025.

    Morgan Stanley reports three segments. Institutional Securities, its investment banking, trading and lending business for large clients, had net revenues of $33.1 billion. Wealth Management, which serves individuals through financial advisors, a self-directed online brokerage and workplace services such as stock plan administration, mainly in the United States, had $31.8 billion; it earns fees on client assets, trading commissions and interest on loans and deposits. Investment Management, which runs stock, bond and alternative funds for pension plans, endowments, insurers and other investors, had $6.5 billion. It says it competes with commercial and investment banks, brokers, private banks, investment advisers, digital investing platforms, asset managers and financial technology firms.

    Morgan Stanley had about 83,000 employees in 42 countries at the end of 2025, about 54,000 of them in the Americas.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Market swings and economic conditions that could cut its trading, deal-making and wealth management results.
    • Losses if borrowers or trading partners fail to pay, or from large concentrated positions.
    • Its reliance on outside funding, which depends on its credit ratings and on market conditions.
    • Extensive and changing regulation, lawsuits and investigations that could limit its business or bring penalties.
    • Strong competition and the shift to automated electronic trading, which put pressure on its fees.
    From Morgan Stanley's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 19, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
