    What RH does

    RH sells high-end home furnishings: furniture, lighting, textiles, bathware, décor, outdoor and garden items, and furnishings for babies, children and teens. It sells through its own stores, which it calls Galleries, as well as its websites and printed catalogs it calls Sourcebooks. It operates in the United States, Canada and Europe.

    RH treats the Galleries as showrooms for its brand and the websites and Sourcebooks as extensions of them, so revenue comes from one integrated retail business across all of these channels. It also serves design professionals and business customers through its Trade and Contract channels, and runs Outlets. Its customer brands include RH Interiors, RH Modern, RH Outdoor, RH Baby & Child, RH Teen and Waterworks.

    A distinctive part of the model is hospitality: RH has restaurants and wine bars inside 25 of its Design Gallery locations and says it plans to add them to many new Galleries. It also develops real estate for new Galleries, including a planned second RH Guesthouse in Aspen. Many products are made with artisan partners and specialty manufacturers.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Taking on many new projects at once, including international growth, real estate development and hospitality.
    • Customers may not respond well to new Galleries, products or markets.
    • Spending changes among the affluent customers who buy its products.
    • Its dependence on foreign manufacturers and imports for its merchandise.
    • Delays or cost overruns in opening new Galleries, and trouble running its delivery network.
    From RH's annual report on Form 10-K, filed April 1, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
