    What Universal Health Services does

    Universal Health Services owns and runs hospitals and other health care facilities through its subsidiaries. They fall into two groups: acute care hospitals, free-standing emergency departments and outpatient centers, and behavioral health facilities that treat mental health and addiction. As of February 25, 2026, it owned or operated 375 inpatient facilities and 168 outpatient and other facilities in 40 states, Washington, D.C., the United Kingdom and Puerto Rico.

    Acute care and the company's commercial health insurer made up about 57% of net revenues in 2025, and behavioral health and the insurer about 43%. The company earns its revenue from the care its facilities provide, which includes surgery, internal medicine, obstetrics, emergency care, radiology and cancer care. Its behavioral health business in the U.K. brought in about $1.001 billion in 2025.

    The parent company also provides its facilities with money and shared services such as central purchasing, information systems, finance, planning and recruiting of physicians. A large share of its business sits in three states: Texas, Nevada and California.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Heavy concentration of revenue in Texas, Nevada and California, so local rules or events there can hit it harder.
    • Changes in government payment programs and health care rules.
    • Legal and regulatory scrutiny of how its hospitals and behavioral health facilities operate.
    • Hiring and keeping enough nurses, doctors and other staff.
    • Severe weather such as wildfires and storms that can disrupt facilities.
    From Universal Health Services's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 25, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
