    What Clover Health Investments does

    Clover Health is a Medicare Advantage insurer, which means it offers health plans to older Americans through the government's Medicare program, and it also sells software. Its software platform, Clover Assistant, is licensed to outside customers as Counterpart Assistant. It gives doctors data and suggestions at the point of care to help find and manage chronic diseases earlier.

    It earns money mainly from the premiums for its Medicare Advantage plans and pays the medical costs of its members. It also hopes to earn software fees by providing the platform to other insurers and physician groups. The software combines data from over 100 sources. Clover says it measures how much of premiums go to medical costs with a benefits expense ratio, a non-GAAP measure.

    It builds networks of physicians, and its members are concentrated in certain geographic areas.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It has lost money in the past and may not become or stay profitable.
    • Doctors may use Clover Assistant less than it expects, which would slow growth.
    • It may misjudge, price for or manage members' medical costs.
    • Government risk adjustment payments make its revenue hard to predict and can be adjusted back in time.
    • Its members are concentrated in certain areas, exposing it to local cost and competition changes.
