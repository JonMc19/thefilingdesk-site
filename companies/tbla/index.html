    What Taboola does

    Taboola is an advertising technology company. It places ads, and recommended content, on websites, mobile apps and devices run by other companies, which it calls digital properties. Its partners include news sites and large media and technology brands. It says it works outside the big search and social media platforms such as Meta, Google and Amazon.

    Advertisers pay Taboola when people click on or buy from their ads, or in some cases when they view them. Taboola then shares the revenue with the digital property that showed the ad. Its artificial intelligence engine predicts which ads and articles a visitor is likely to want, without search queries or social media profiles. It began operations in 2007.

    The company says it has about 14,000 digital property partners and thousands of advertisers. It has also formed a partnership with Yahoo and offers a product called Realize, and it says both need to succeed for it to meet its goals.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to attract new digital properties and advertisers, or to sell more to existing ones.
    • Contracts with digital properties that require guaranteed minimum payments, whatever revenue the ads bring in.
    • The Yahoo partnership may not bring the long-term financial benefits it expects.
    • Its Realize product may not win acceptance or deliver the expected benefits.
    • Large swings in operating results that make its future results hard to predict.
