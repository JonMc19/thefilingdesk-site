    What McGraw Hill does

    McGraw Hill sells learning materials and software to schools, colleges and professionals. Its products combine content, software and data, in digital and print form, and cover kindergarten through 12th grade (K-12), higher education and professional learning. It says it has over 100 million active student and educator curriculum licenses worldwide.

    It reports four segments. K-12 sells core, supplemental and intervention curricula to school districts, and the company says it is a top two provider in that U.S. market. Higher Education sells digital and print course materials to colleges. Professional offers medical and engineering learning products, including subscription services. International sells K-12 and higher education products in more than 100 countries outside the United States.

    For the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026, revenue was $2,102.8 million. The company completed its initial public offering in July 2025. It competes with large publishers, new entrants, and free or cheaper materials, and it says generative AI could make competing materials easier to create.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competition from large publishers and new entrants, and from free or low-cost materials.
    • Generative AI making it easier to create competing instructional materials.
    • Losing new adoptions in key U.S. states, where school districts pick materials.
    • Less federal, state or local education funding for instructional materials.
    • Falling college enrollment, which reduces demand for its higher education products.
    From McGraw Hill's annual report on Form 10-K, filed June 11, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
