    What Build-A-Bear Workshop does

    Build-A-Bear Workshop sells stuffed animals that customers make themselves in its stores, by stuffing, dressing and naming them. It also sells pre-stuffed plush, gifts and licensed and collectible items, and develops original stories through a media unit. It says its appeal now reaches teens and adults as well as children.

    It reports three segments: direct-to-consumer, commercial and international franchising. As of January 31, 2026, it had 662 locations worldwide. Of these, 375 were run by the company (333 in the United States and Canada and 42 in the United Kingdom and Ireland), 178 were run by partners that buy its products wholesale and run the store experience, and 109 were international franchises. It also sells online through its own sites and third-party marketplaces, and other companies sell its products online under wholesale agreements.

    The company was formed in 1997 as a mall-based store for children. It is adding more stores in tourist spots and in new formats, and is expanding abroad mainly through partners and franchisees.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Changing consumer interests, and whether its marketing and online efforts keep up with them.
    • Dependence on shopping malls and tourist locations for visitors to its stores.
    • Tariffs and trade policy affecting the products it imports, and inflation that may force price changes.
    • Competitive threats, and demand for its interactive in-store experience and products.
    • Risks from its use of artificial intelligence technologies, and swings in petroleum product prices.
    From Build-A-Bear Workshop's annual report on Form 10-K, filed April 16, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
