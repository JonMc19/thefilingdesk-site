    What SunCoke Energy does

    SunCoke Energy makes coke, a fuel made by heating metallurgical coal in an oven. Steelmakers burn it in blast furnaces, and iron foundries also use it. It says it is the largest independent coke producer in the Americas. Most of its sales come from long-term contracts under which customers must pay for coke whether or not they take it, and it sells extra capacity to other buyers.

    It reports two segments. Domestic Coke owns and runs five plants in the United States, with capacity to produce about 3.7 million tons of coke a year. The plants use waste heat from the ovens to make steam and electricity, which it also sells. It also runs a plant in Brazil for ArcelorMittal. Industrial Services runs coal and bulk-material terminals and handles materials for steel mills, including molten slag.

    Its biggest customers are steelmakers such as Cliffs, U.S. Steel and Algoma Steel, so a few customers account for most of its sales.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Depending on a small number of steelmaker customers, including a contract dispute with Algoma Steel.
    • Equipment failures or accidents at its coke plants and terminals.
    • Weak demand for coke if global steel capacity is in excess or coke exports rise.
    • Strict environmental, health and safety rules that raise its costs and could limit its permits.
    • Disruptions to its coal supply or to rail transport.
    From SunCoke Energy's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 20, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
