    What GSK does

    GSK, based in the United Kingdom, is a global biopharma group. It develops, makes and sells medicines and vaccines to prevent and treat disease. It organizes its sales into three groups: specialty medicines, vaccines and general medicines.

    Specialty Medicines is its largest business, accounting for over 40% of sales. GSK expects it to be a major driver of growth and to account for over 50% of sales by 2031. Vaccines include Shingrix for shingles and Arexvy for respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) in adults. General Medicines covers established treatments for conditions such as asthma, and its sales fell in 2025.

    GSK's plans rely on launching new products, among them Blenrep for multiple myeloma, which received approvals in the US, Europe and Japan in 2025, and Exdensur, a new biologic for asthma. Total employee pay for 2025 is based on 68,307 employees, the average number employed during the year.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Delays or failures in delivering its pipeline of new medicines and vaccines.
    • Price controls and healthcare reforms in the US, UK and EU that change how drugs are bought and paid for.
    • Losing patent protection on products as their patents expire.
    • Legal claims over its products, such as the Zantac litigation it has settled in part.
    • Complex regulation and growing scrutiny from payers that slow product launches.
    From GSK's annual report on Form 20-F, filed March 6, 2026, in our words; the 20-F has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
