    What Schlumberger does

    SLB, formerly called Schlumberger, is a technology and services company for the energy industry. It works in more than 100 countries. Its customers are mostly oil and gas producers, and it also serves drilling contractors. It is organized in four divisions: Digital, Reservoir Performance, Well Construction and Production Systems.

    Digital sells cloud software, data and exploration data libraries. Reservoir Performance provides services to measure, stimulate and work on wells. Well Construction sells drilling services, drilling fluids, drill bits and drilling equipment. Production Systems provides equipment and chemicals that help get oil and gas from wells to the surface, including subsea systems and valves. Smaller businesses, such as data center solutions, sit in an All Other category.

    In 2025 SLB completed its acquisition of ChampionX in an all-stock deal. It reported global revenue of $35.7 billion for the year. It says demand for its products depends on customer spending, which swings with oil and gas prices. It has a workforce of about 109,000 people.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Demand depends on customer spending, which falls when oil and gas prices fall.
    • It may fail to get the expected benefits from the ChampionX acquisition.
    • Trade sanctions and other risks of working in many countries.
    • Competition, and the need to keep up with fast-changing technology.
    • Cyber incidents and severe weather that could disrupt its operations.
    From Schlumberger's annual report on Form 10-K, filed January 23, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
