    What Microchip Technology does

    Microchip designs, makes and sells chips that act as the small control computers inside other products. Its main products are microcontrollers, which are a whole computer on one chip, plus microprocessors, analog chips, FPGAs (chips a customer can reprogram), and data center, networking and memory products. It sells them together with software and services.

    Its chips go into cars, aircraft and defense equipment, communications gear, home appliances, data centers, industrial machines, medical devices and many other products. Customers are the manufacturers that build those products. Microchip says that in July 2024 it entered the 64-bit microprocessor market, beyond its earlier 32-bit chips.

    It sells partly through distributors, who resell to end customers. Sales to distributors were about 47% of net sales in fiscal 2026. Microchip relies on outside wafer foundries and other contractors for part of its production, and it says it faces intense competition, which can push down prices.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Key suppliers failing to provide raw materials, parts or equipment, including from tariffs or export controls.
    • Its dependence on wafer foundries and other contractors.
    • Intense competition that can cut prices, sales or market share.
    • Heavy reliance on distributors to reach its end customers.
    • Foreign political and economic risks, since it relies heavily on foreign sales, suppliers and operations.
    From Microchip Technology's annual report on Form 10-K, filed May 21, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
