    What Columbia Sportswear does

    Columbia Sportswear designs and sells outdoor, active and lifestyle clothing, footwear, accessories and equipment. It has four brands: Columbia, SOREL (boots and lifestyle shoes), Mountain Hardwear (technical gear for climbers and skiers) and prAna (yoga and active clothing). The company was founded in Portland, Oregon, in 1938 and sells in 115 countries.

    It reports four geographic segments: the United States (its largest), Latin America and Asia Pacific, Europe, Middle East and Africa, and Canada. Sales go through two channels. In wholesale, it sells to outdoor and sporting goods stores, department stores, internet retailers and international distributors. In direct-to-consumer, it sells through its own stores and websites. It also earns licensing revenue. Sales are weighted toward the third and fourth quarters.

    Columbia does not own or run factories. Contract manufacturers outside the US make almost all of its finished goods, and in 2025 factories in Vietnam produced about 80% of its footwear. It competes with large companies, small local brands, new brands that sell mostly direct to consumers, and its own wholesale customers' private-label products. It had about 9,620 employees at the end of 2025, more than half of them in retail.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • New or changing US trade policy and tariffs on goods it imports.
    • Misjudging consumer demand, leaving it with too much or too little inventory.
    • Depending on contract manufacturers, a few raw material suppliers and third-party logistics providers.
    • Fixed costs of its own stores and websites that may not earn a return.
    • Seasonal sales that make fixed costs hard to manage across the year.
    From Columbia Sportswear's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 25, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
