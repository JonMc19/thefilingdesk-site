    What Macy's does

    Macy's, Inc. is a retailer that runs 665 stores, websites and mobile apps under three brands: Macy's, Bloomingdale's and Bluemercury. It sells apparel, accessories, cosmetics, home furnishings and other consumer goods, to shoppers from price-conscious value seekers to luxury buyers. It says it serves a base of nearly 40 million active customers a year.

    Macy's is a department store chain for middle- to higher-income shoppers. It runs full-size and smaller stores, plus off-price stores called Macy's Backstage. Bloomingdale's is the upscale department store brand, with full-line stores, smaller Bloomies stores and an outlet format. It also has licensed stores in Dubai and Kuwait. Bluemercury sells premium skincare, fragrance and beauty treatments from neighborhood stores.

    Most revenue comes from selling merchandise. The company also earns money from its credit card program, which shares profits with the card issuer, and from Macy's Media Network, which sells advertising to brands across its stores and websites. Macy's has run department stores since 1830 and calls its employees colleagues.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to spot and answer changing customer tastes, which could hurt demand for its products.
    • Managing its inventory and merchandise selection, and losses from inventory shortage.
    • Competition in retail and the need to keep its stores attractive to shoppers.
    • Falling revenue from its private label and co-branded credit cards.
    • Disruption at its distribution and fulfillment centers, or in its websites and apps.
    From Macy's's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
