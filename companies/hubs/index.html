    What HubSpot does

    HubSpot sells a software platform that helps marketing, sales and customer service teams manage their relationships with customers. The platform includes a customer relationship management (CRM) system, which holds customer data, and tools for marketing, sales, service, operations, content and commerce. It adds artificial intelligence (AI) agents that do some of this work.

    HubSpot focuses on mid-market business-to-business companies, which it defines as companies with between 2 and 2,000 employees. It mainly sells the platform as a subscription. Outside partners resell and set up the software, and the company runs a marketplace of integrations and templates. In 2025 its total revenue was $3.1 billion. It had 288,706 customers in more than 135 countries and 8,882 full-time employees at year-end.

    HubSpot is based in Cambridge, Massachusetts. It was formed in 2005 and became a corporation in 2007.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Customers not renewing subscriptions, or HubSpot not adding enough new ones.
    • Competition from large and small software companies.
    • Depending on third-party data centers to deliver the platform.
    • Needing to keep up with fast-changing technology, including AI.
    • Relying on partners that sell and set up its platform for part of its growth.
    From HubSpot's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 11, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
