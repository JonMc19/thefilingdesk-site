    What Sherwin-Williams does

    Sherwin-Williams makes and sells paint, coatings and related products to professional painters, industrial and commercial customers, and homeowners. Most of its business is in North and South America, with operations also in the Caribbean, Europe, Asia and Australia. Its brands include Sherwin-Williams, Valspar, Dutch Boy, Krylon and Minwax.

    It has three segments. Paint Stores Group runs 4,853 company-owned paint stores in the United States, Canada and the Caribbean, serving contractors and do-it-yourself homeowners. Consumer Brands Group makes paints, stains and other products for home centers, hardware stores and dealers, and makes most of what the stores sell. About 63% of its sales are transfers to other Sherwin-Williams segments, mainly the stores. Performance Coatings Group sells industrial coatings for wood, metal and plastic, car repair paint, protective and marine coatings, and resins.

    Its main raw materials are resins, pigments, additives, solvents and containers, many derived from petrochemicals. Sales are usually highest in the second and third quarters. It competes with local, regional and international paint makers, paint stores, home centers and hardware chains. It employed 64,249 people at the end of 2025, about 73% of them in the United States.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Economic downturns, especially in cyclical parts of the economy, that reduce demand for its products.
    • Shortages and higher prices for raw materials and energy.
    • Losing one of its largest customers.
    • Trouble integrating acquisitions or expanding into South America, Asia and Europe.
    • Environmental clean-up costs and lawsuits, including lead paint claims.
    From Sherwin-Williams's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 19, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
