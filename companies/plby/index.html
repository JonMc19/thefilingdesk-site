    What Playboy does

    Playboy, Inc. (called PLBY Group until June 2025) describes itself as a pleasure and leisure company. It owns two brands. Playboy-branded products and content are available in about 180 countries. Honey Birdette sells lingerie online and in its own stores in Australia, the United States and the United Kingdom.

    Most of Playboy's income comes from licensing. Partners make and sell Playboy-branded apparel, accessories, beauty and grooming products and sexual wellness products, and work with the brand in nightlife, hospitality and online gaming. Since January 1, 2025, a license and management agreement with Byborg Enterprises has covered its older digital subscription and content businesses. It still runs playboy.com and in 2025 started publishing Playboy magazine again.

    Revenue in 2025 was $120.9 million and the net loss was $12.7 million. The loss was much smaller than in 2024, which the company credits mainly to more licensing profit from the Byborg agreement, lower costs from reworking its digital business, and lower non-cash asset write-downs.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Keeping the value and reputation of the Playboy brand, which its success depends on.
    • Sexually explicit content that can bring negative publicity, lawsuits and boycotts, and payment, advertising and other companies refusing to work with it.
    • Competition, and fast changes in the markets for its physical and digital products.
    • Failing to protect its trademarks and copyrights, or being accused of infringing others' rights.
    • Material weaknesses in its internal control over financial reporting.
    From Playboy's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 16, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
