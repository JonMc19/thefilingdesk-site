    What Navitas Semiconductor does

    Navitas designs power semiconductors, the chips that convert and control electricity in chargers and power supplies. Its products include gallium nitride (GaN) power chips, silicon carbide (SiC) devices, silicon controllers and digital isolators. It says these materials let a power supply be smaller and waste less energy than older silicon designs.

    The company now focuses on high-power markets: artificial intelligence data centers, energy and grid infrastructure, high-performance computing and industrial electrification. It is reducing its emphasis on mobile and consumer products. It designs the chips and contracts partner suppliers to make and package them. Its revenue depends on design wins, where a customer picks its chip for a new product, and the 10-K says revenue from a win often takes a year or more to appear, if it appears at all. It sells through a few key distributors.

    Legacy Navitas was founded in 2014 and went public in 2021 by merging with Live Oak Acquisition Corp. II, a blank-check company. In 2022 it bought GeneSiC Semiconductor, which added silicon carbide products. At the end of 2025 it had approximately 190 full and part-time employees, 65% of them in Asia Pacific.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its shift toward AI data centers and grid markets, away from mobile and consumer, may not pay off.
    • Winning design wins, which may take a year or more to produce revenue, if ever.
    • Failing to develop new products or keep up with fast technology change.
    • Order backlog that can be canceled, cut or delayed, and demand that is hard to predict.
    • Dependence on a few key distributors, and on partners that make and package its chips.
    From Navitas Semiconductor's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
