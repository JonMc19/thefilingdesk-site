    What Hannon Armstrong Sustainable Infrastructure Capital does

    HASI, formerly Hannon Armstrong, is an investor in sustainable infrastructure that supports the shift to cleaner energy. It puts money into income-generating real assets that have long-term recurring cash flows. It is internally managed by its own executive team.

    It invests in three markets: behind-the-meter projects (such as residential, commercial and industrial solar and energy efficiency), grid-connected projects (such as utility-scale solar, onshore wind and battery storage), and fuels, transport and nature (such as renewable natural gas, fleet decarbonization and ecological restoration). Its investments take many forms, including equity, joint ventures, real estate, receivables and securities. It earns money from the returns on these investments and from fees.

    It works over and over with U.S. clean energy developers, owners and operators, utilities and energy service companies, and says these relationships lead to repeat investments.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Falling costs of energy from traditional sources, which could cut demand for the projects it funds.
    • Reduced net metering and other policies, or utility regulation, that hurts project economics.
    • Changes in government laws, policies and support for clean energy.
    • Interest rate changes, since many of its assets pay a fixed rate.
    • Assets that are hard to sell or value.
    From Hannon Armstrong Sustainable Infrastructure Capital's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 13, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
