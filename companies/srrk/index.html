    What Scholar Rock does

    Scholar Rock is a biotechnology company developing antibody drugs for spinal muscular atrophy (SMA) and other rare muscle-wasting diseases. It has no approved product yet and no product sales. It says 2026 could be the year it becomes a commercial-stage company, if regulators approve its lead drug.

    Its technology targets growth factors in the TGF-beta family, a group of signaling proteins. Its drugs bind the inactive forms of these proteins in muscle. The lead candidate, apitegromab, blocks myostatin, a protein that limits muscle growth, in the hope of building muscle mass and strength. The company reported positive results from a late-stage trial in children and adults with SMA in October 2024 and says it expects regulatory decisions in the United States and Europe in 2026.

    The company also plans a mid-stage study of apitegromab in facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy (FSHD) and is developing a version given as an injection under the skin, rather than through a vein. A second candidate, SRK-439, is also an antibody that targets myostatin. Because it has no sales, the company relies on money it raises to pay for its work.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its lead drug may not be approved, or may be approved only with limits on how it can be used.
    • Even if approved, apitegromab may not win acceptance from doctors, patients and insurers.
    • Its clinical development depends on the continued availability of other companies' approved drug therapies.
    • Results from early trials may not predict later ones, and early data it announces may change.
    • Delays in enrolling patients could slow its trials and raise its costs.
