    What Samsara does

    Samsara sells a cloud software platform, called the Connected Operations Platform, to companies that run physical operations. These include construction, transportation, wholesale and retail, field services, logistics, manufacturing, utilities, government, healthcare and education. The platform collects data from the company's own internet-connected (IoT) devices and from other connected equipment and software, and shows it in dashboards, alerts, reports and mobile apps.

    Its applications include video-based driver safety that uses artificial intelligence (AI), telematics (vehicle data), asset tracking, routing, commercial navigation, maintenance, training, digital forms and site visibility. It also offers AI agents that can automate whole workflows. Customers pay subscriptions to use the platform, and Samsara sells them its devices, which it has made by contract manufacturers.

    Customers range from small and mid-sized businesses to state and local governments and large global enterprises. As of January 31, 2026 Samsara had over 12,000 Core Customers, which it defines as customers with subscriptions to its platform. It sells mainly through its own direct sales team.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A history of losses, and no assurance it can reach its profitability targets.
    • Fast growth that makes its future results hard to judge and hard to manage.
    • Failing to win new customers or to keep and expand sales to current ones.
    • Heavy reliance on its direct sales team and on long, unpredictable sales cycles.
    • Dependence on a few contract manufacturers and suppliers for devices and key parts.
    From Samsara's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 16, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
