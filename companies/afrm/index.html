    What Affirm does

    Affirm runs a payment network that lets shoppers pay for purchases over time. Consumers use it online and in stores to split a purchase into installments, and it also offers the Affirm Card, a debit card that can pay in full or over time. Affirm was founded in 2012 and says it has never charged late fees.

    It makes money from two sides. Merchants pay Affirm fees, which vary by deal and are generally larger on financing that charges the consumer no interest. Consumers pay interest on the interest-bearing installment loans that Affirm originates or buys from its bank partners. In fiscal 2026 these interest-bearing loans were 70% of total gross merchandise volume (GMV), the value of purchases made through its platform.

    Affirm works with its bank partners, which originate many of the loans, and relies on several funding sources. It says it relies on a small number of commercial partners for a significant portion of its revenue.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Depending on a small number of commercial partners for a large part of its revenue.
    • Needing its originating bank partners to make loans that work.
    • Loans that do not perform, causing credit losses.
    • Relying on a mix of funding sources that may not be renewed.
    • Strong competition in payments and consumer credit.
    From Affirm's annual report on Form 10-K, filed August 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
