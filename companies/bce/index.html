    What BCE does

    BCE is a Canadian communications company. Under the Bell name it sells mobile plans and devices, home and business internet, TV, phone service and cloud-based services to households, businesses and governments. It also owns media assets that provide video, audio, outdoor advertising and digital media across Canada.

    It reports three segments. Bell CTS Canada covers wireless and wireline services in Canada, and its wireline customers are mainly in Ontario, Québec, the Atlantic provinces and Manitoba. Bell CTS U.S. is Ziply Fiber, a fiber internet provider in the Pacific Northwest of the United States, which BCE bought on August 1, 2025. Bell Media is the media business. BCE also sells network services to other carriers and resellers.

    BCE's employee count at the end of 2025 fell by 1,707 from 40,390 a year earlier. It says the drop came from job cuts, normal departures and retirements, partly offset by the Ziply Fiber purchase. It has also agreed to sell its Northwestel business.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competition from other carriers, streaming and internet-based services that could draw away customers and advertisers.
    • Regulatory decisions in Canada on network access, spectrum auctions, licensing and foreign ownership.
    • Failing to integrate Ziply Fiber or to build out its fiber network as planned.
    • Network failures, outages or attacks that disrupt service.
    • Not being able to raise enough capital to fund its spending, or to maintain its dividend.
    From BCE's annual report on Form 40-F and the annual information form filed with it, filed March 6, 2026, in our words; the 40-F has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
