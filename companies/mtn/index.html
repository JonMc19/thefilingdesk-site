    What Vail Resorts does

    Vail Resorts owns and runs ski resorts. Its Mountain segment operates 42 destination mountain resorts and regional ski areas, including Vail Mountain, Breckenridge, Park City and Keystone in the Rocky Mountains. Destination resorts draw many guests who travel a long way, while regional ski areas draw mostly local skiers.

    Mountain segment revenue comes from lift tickets and pass products, plus ski school, equipment rentals, retail, dining, private clubs and summer activities. The Lodging segment owns or manages hotels and condominiums near its resorts, including the RockResorts brand, national park lodges such as Grand Teton Lodge Company, a Colorado ground transportation company and golf courses. The Real Estate segment owns, develops and sells land around its resort communities.

    In its fiscal year ended July 31, 2026, the Mountain, Lodging and Real Estate segments made up about 88%, 12% and 0% of net revenue. It lists unfavorable weather and natural disasters among its main risks.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Bad weather or natural disasters, including low snowfall, that cut skier visits.
    • Having to refund pass holders under its Epic Coverage program.
    • Weak travel and leisure demand in a poor economy or during high inflation.
    • Relying on government permits and landlord approvals to operate its U.S. resorts.
    • Not being able to hire and keep enough staff, including seasonal workers.
    From Vail Resorts's annual report on Form 10-K, filed September 28, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
