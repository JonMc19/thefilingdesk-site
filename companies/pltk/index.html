    What Playtika does

    Playtika makes and runs mobile games. Its games are free to play, and players can buy virtual items inside them. It says it runs central live operations services that study how people play and then offer players content and deals at chosen points in their game. It built its portfolio largely by buying game studios, and in some cases it applies its live operations services to the games it buys.

    Nearly all of its revenue comes from players' optional in-game purchases: 97.7% in 2025. It also earns some money from in-game advertising. It sells through third-party app stores, such as the iOS App Store and Google Play, and through its own platforms. A limited number of games bring in most of its revenue, and a small share of its users bring in most of its spending.

    In 2025, Playtika had revenue of $2,755.4 million and a net loss of $206.4 million. It was founded in Israel in 2010 and went public in January 2021. Its co-founder, Robert Antokol, has managed it since the start.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Relying on Apple's and Google's app stores, and on payment providers, to distribute its games and collect payments.
    • A limited number of games, and a small share of players, produce most of its revenue.
    • Changes to platform rules or laws on loot boxes, and lawsuits that social casino games are illegal gambling.
    • Failing to buy other game companies or combine them successfully.
    • Competition in an industry with low barriers to entry.
    From Playtika's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
