    What Stoke Therapeutics does

    Stoke Therapeutics is a late-stage clinical company that develops medicines for severe genetic diseases. It has no approved product. Its approach, which it calls TANGO, uses antisense oligonucleotides (short pieces of genetic material) to raise the amount of a protein the body makes too little of. This targets diseases caused by having only about half the normal protein level, starting with diseases of the brain and the eye.

    Its lead candidate, zorevunersen, is being tested for Dravet syndrome, a severe genetic epilepsy that starts in the first year of life and causes frequent seizures and developmental delays. The company says no approved medicine treats the underlying causes of Dravet. A global Phase 3 trial began in 2025, following earlier studies in the United States and the United Kingdom. A second candidate targets a genetic eye disease called autosomal dominant optic atrophy.

    Stoke has no product revenue. It works with Biogen, which has rights to the Dravet candidate outside the United States, Canada and Mexico, and may receive milestone and royalty payments from it.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its business depends on zorevunersen and its other candidates succeeding in trials and winning approval.
    • Early results may not hold up in later trials, and regulators may not approve its medicines.
    • Diseases that few people have are hard to enroll in trials, which can cause delays.
    • It relies on Biogen and on outside firms for trials, supply and parts of its work.
    • It has a history of losses and may need to raise more money.
