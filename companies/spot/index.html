    What Spotify Technology does

    Spotify runs an audio streaming service for music, podcasts and audiobooks. At the end of 2025 it had 751 million monthly active users and 290 million paying Premium subscribers in 184 countries and territories. People listen on phones, computers, speakers, cars and TVs.

    Spotify has two segments. The Premium segment earns subscription fees, mostly sold directly to users and also through partners such as telecom companies that bundle it with their own services. The Ad-Supported segment is free to users and earns money by selling display, audio and video advertising. Spotify says the free service is a main way it finds new paying subscribers.

    Spotify does not own most of what it streams. It licenses music from record labels, music publishers and collecting societies, and it pays royalties to them. It also licenses or produces podcasts and licenses audiobooks. It competes with other music and podcast services, radio, audiobook sellers and large online advertising companies, and for people's time with other media.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to attract, keep and earn money from users, as the free service feeds the paid one.
    • Its dependence on licenses from record labels and publishers, which it does not control.
    • Complex royalty payments and minimum payment commitments that limit its flexibility.
    • Strong competition for users, their time and advertisers.
    • Reliance on phones, operating systems and platforms it does not control.
    From Spotify Technology's annual report on Form 20-F, filed February 10, 2026, in our words; the 20-F has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
