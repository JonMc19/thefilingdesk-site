    What Intapp does

    Intapp sells cloud software to professional firms in accounting, consulting, investment banking, law, private capital and real assets. Its programs help these firms with winning and onboarding clients, checking conflicts of interest, managing the deal pipeline, reporting to investors and clients, and meeting compliance rules. It has added artificial intelligence (AI) features that use a firm's own data.

    It earns money mainly from subscriptions to its software, and recognizes that revenue over the length of each contract. Customers are large, partner-led firms in heavily regulated fields.

    Intapp says it competes with software that firms build themselves, older programs installed on their own computers, and general-purpose software that has to be heavily customized. It says it has 25 years of experience in these industries.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Data breaches at its software or at its outside cloud providers.
    • Most revenue comes from firms in a handful of professional industries.
    • Its new AI products may not work as hoped or win customers.
    • It has a history of losses and may not stay profitable.
    • Customers may not renew or expand their subscriptions.
    From Intapp's annual report on Form 10-K, filed August 14, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
