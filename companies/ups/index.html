    What United Parcel Service does

    UPS picks up and delivers packages for businesses and consumers in more than 200 countries and territories, using one network of planes, trucks and sorting hubs. It also moves freight by air and sea, runs warehouses, handles customs paperwork and sells shipping insurance. In 2025 it delivered an average of 20.8 million packages a day, and its total revenue was $88.7 billion.

    It reports two package segments, U.S. Domestic Package and International Package, and groups its freight forwarding, logistics and digital businesses as Supply Chain Solutions. Its largest customer, Amazon, made up about 10.6% of 2025 revenue, almost all in the U.S. package business, and UPS plans to cut the volume it handles for Amazon by more than 50% by June 2026 from 2024 levels. UPS is expanding in health care shipping and says that business brought in more than $11 billion in 2025. It competes with local, regional, national and international delivery and logistics firms, and with national postal services.

    UPS has about 460,000 employees, not counting seasonal workers, with 370,000 in the U.S. Nearly 80% of its U.S. employees belong to unions, many of them under contracts with the Teamsters. In 2025 it bought two health care logistics firms, Frigo-Trans and Andlauer Healthcare Group, to add cold-storage shipping.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Losing business from its large customers, including the planned drop in volume from its largest one, Amazon.
    • Strikes, work stoppages or slowdowns by its largely unionized workforce.
    • Fierce competition in a delivery industry that keeps changing quickly.
    • Rising or unstable prices for gasoline, diesel and jet fuel, and interruptions in their supply.
    • High and rising costs for employee health care and pensions.
    From United Parcel Service's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 17, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
