    What Consolidated Edison does

    Consolidated Edison, known as Con Edison, is a holding company. Its main businesses are two regulated utilities, Consolidated Edison Company of New York (CECONY) and Orange and Rockland Utilities (O&R), and Con Edison Transmission, which develops and invests in electric transmission projects and owns electric and gas assets through joint ventures. It serves customers in New York and New Jersey.

    CECONY delivers electricity to about 3.7 million customers in New York City (except part of Queens) and most of Westchester County. It delivers gas to about 1.1 million customers in Manhattan, the Bronx, parts of Queens and most of Westchester County. It also runs a steam system in parts of Manhattan, which it says is the largest steam distribution system in the United States. O&R provides electric service to about 0.3 million customers in southeastern New York and northern New Jersey, and gas to over 0.1 million customers in southeastern New York.

    The New York State Public Service Commission sets the terms of service and the rates the utilities may charge. Con Edison says it aims to provide shareholder value through continued dividend growth, supported by earnings growth in its regulated utilities and electric transmission projects.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Extensive regulation, with the possibility of substantial penalties.
    • Changes to the utilities' rate plans that could hurt the company.
    • The environmental effects of its operations, and higher costs tied to climate change.
    • Needing access to capital markets to meet its funding needs.
    • Supply chain disruptions, inflation and tariffs that raise costs.
    From Consolidated Edison's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 19, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
