    What ASML does

    ASML, based in the Netherlands, builds the machines that chipmakers use to print the tiny patterns of circuits onto silicon wafers. This process is called lithography. It sells its advanced extreme ultraviolet (EUV) machines, its deep ultraviolet (DUV) machines and measurement and software tools, and it services the machines that customers already run.

    ASML says it is currently the world's only maker of EUV lithography systems. Its customers are chipmakers, in both logic chips and memory chips. It sells to a relatively small number of them, so a few customers account for much of its net sales. It also relies on single-source suppliers for key parts, such as Carl Zeiss SMT for its optics.

    Its headquarters are in the Brainport Eindhoven region of the Netherlands, and it has a global team of more than 44,000 employees (full-time equivalents). Its principal operations are in Europe, the Middle East and Africa, North America and Asia.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A high share of its net sales comes from a few chipmakers.
    • Its dependence on a limited number of suppliers of single-source key components, such as optics.
    • Downturns in the cyclical semiconductor industry, and the chance that new products are late or fail.
    • Export controls and other geopolitical limits on shipping its systems to some countries.
    • Reliance on a limited number of manufacturing facilities.
