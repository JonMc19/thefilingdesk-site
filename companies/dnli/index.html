    What Denali Therapeutics does

    Denali Therapeutics is a drug developer with no approved product. It works on a problem in medicine: the blood-brain barrier, a lining that protects the brain but also keeps most medicines out. Denali built its own delivery technology, called the TransportVehicle, which is designed to carry large-molecule drugs such as enzymes, antibodies and oligonucleotides across that barrier.

    Its most advanced program, tividenofusp alfa, is for Hunter syndrome (MPS II), a rare inherited disease. The FDA is reviewing its license application under priority review, and the company has a target decision date of April 5, 2026. Its other programs include candidates for Sanfilippo syndrome type A and for a form of frontotemporal dementia, and it aims at common diseases such as Alzheimer's and Parkinson's.

    Denali has no product revenue. It says it has made preparations to sell a medicine if it is approved, and it runs its own manufacturing facility.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It has no approved products and may never become profitable.
    • It depends heavily on its TransportVehicle technology and the programs in its pipeline.
    • Its drugs use novel technology, so time and cost are hard to predict.
    • Clinical trials may be delayed, or show side effects or fail to show benefit.
    • It may fail to manufacture its candidates or to build sales and marketing.
