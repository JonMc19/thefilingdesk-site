    What Kaltura does

    Kaltura sells a cloud-based platform for video and other rich media to organizations. Customers use it to create, manage and deliver live and on-demand video for marketing and sales, teaching and training, workplace communication, and entertainment. It started in 2006 with enterprise video management and online video publishing, and later added virtual events, webinars and cloud-based television services.

    The platform has three layers: creating media, managing it securely, and delivering it to viewers. Kaltura is adding AI features, including conversational interfaces and workflow automation. Through its acquisition of eSelf AI it added tools for AI-generated video and avatar-based interactions.

    Its customers range from large enterprises to small and medium companies in many industries. Kaltura says its customers include about 30% of Fortune 100 companies and more than 40% of U.S. R1 universities, which are the top research universities. Because it runs on public cloud infrastructure, its costs vary with usage.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It depends on demand from existing customers, and losing a significant customer would hurt its results.
    • It has a history of losses and may not reach or keep profitability.
    • Its markets are new and may develop more slowly or differently than it expects.
    • Running on public cloud infrastructure has raised its variable costs.
    • Using AI in its products may bring new legal, regulatory and ethical risks.

    Piotroski F-score: how many of nine simple tests of profitability, funding and efficiency the company passed in the year (Piotroski, 2000); 8 or 9 is strong, 0 to 2 weak.
