    What MongoDB does

    MongoDB sells a database platform for software developers. A database stores and organizes the data an application uses. MongoDB's database uses a document model, which handles data that does not fit neatly into rows and columns, and it adds data services around the database, including tools for building artificial intelligence applications. It says it competes in the database management software market, which it describes as one of the largest in the software industry.

    Its revenue comes from a few product lines. For the fiscal year ended January 31, 2026, Atlas, its cloud database service, represented 73% of total revenue. MongoDB Enterprise Advanced, a version customers run themselves, represented 20%, and professional services represented 3%. Atlas's share has risen from 66% two fiscal years earlier.

    MongoDB also offers Community Server under a license called the SSPL. Its fiscal year ends on January 31, so this profile reflects the report for the year that ended January 31, 2026.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • More than half of its revenue comes from Atlas, so any failure of Atlas to meet customer needs would hurt it.
    • It has a history of losses and may not reach profitability as costs grow.
    • Significant, intense competition is expected to continue.
    • Its decision to offer Community Server under the SSPL may hurt adoption of that product.
    • Open source licenses and third-party open source software in its products could hurt its ability to sell.
    From MongoDB's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 11, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
