    What Ultragenyx Pharmaceutical does

    Ultragenyx is a drug company that develops and sells treatments for rare and ultra-rare genetic diseases, many of which have no approved therapy that treats the underlying cause. It uses several kinds of medicine: biologics (made from living cells), small molecules, gene therapy that uses a harmless virus to deliver genes, and nucleic acid drugs.

    It earns money from its approved products. They include Crysvita, an antibody for X-linked hypophosphatemia (a bone disease) and a related tumor-linked condition, which it develops and sells together with Kyowa Kirin, and Mepsevii, an enzyme therapy for a very rare disorder called MPS VII. It also has Evkeeza, an antibody for a rare inherited form of high cholesterol, which its partner Regeneron markets in the United States. The company mostly licenses its drug candidates from universities or other drug makers and aims to keep worldwide selling rights.

    It has its own sales teams in North America, Europe, the United Kingdom, Latin America, Türkiye and Asia, and uses distributors in smaller markets. Alongside its sold products it runs a set of clinical-stage programs, which are medicines still being tested in people.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It depends on Kyowa Kirin for the development, supply and sale of Crysvita, one of its main products.
    • Its drug candidates can fail in later trials even after promising early results, or be delayed.
    • Regulators may not approve its candidates, or may approve them for narrower uses than it wants.
    • It has a limited operating history and may need to raise more money.
    • Making gene therapies and other complex medicines is difficult, and it relies on outside makers.
