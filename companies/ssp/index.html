    What E.W. Scripps does

    E.W. Scripps, founded in 1878, is a US television company. It owns local television stations in many markets, with programming agreements with ABC, NBC, CBS and FOX, and national networks including Scripps News, Court TV, ION, Bounce, Grit, ION Mystery, ION Plus and Laff. Its stations and networks reach viewers over the air, and it has expanded onto free streaming platforms.

    It also runs the Scripps National Spelling Bee and sells Tablo, a device that lets households watch and record free over-the-air and streaming channels without a subscription. In January 2025 it announced a joint venture with Gray Media, Nexstar and Sinclair, EdgeBeam Wireless, to deliver data over broadcast spectrum. It also televises games of the Las Vegas Aces.

    The report says it has issued preferred shares whose terms limit some of its actions, and that descendants of Edward W. Scripps hold most of its common voting shares.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Losing affiliation and carriage agreements, or paying more to renew them, including retransmission consent deals.
    • Fewer pay-TV subscribers as viewers move to internet streaming.
    • Spending on programming before knowing whether it will draw enough viewers.
    • Government regulation of its stations that could change.
    • Preferred share terms that limit its actions, and control of voting shares by Scripps family descendants.
    From E.W. Scripps's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
