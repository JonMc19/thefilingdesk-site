    What SkyWest does

    SkyWest is a regional airline. Through SkyWest Airlines it flies passengers to destinations in the United States, Canada and Mexico, mostly on short and medium routes that feed larger airlines. Nearly all of its flights are sold under the brands of its big partners: United Express, Delta Connection, American Eagle and Alaska Airlines. Those partners set the schedules and sell the tickets.

    It earns money under long-term, fixed-fee code-share agreements, also called capacity purchase agreements. The partner pays SkyWest set rates based mainly on the number of completed flights, the flight time and the number of aircraft under contract, and pays for or reimburses certain direct costs, including fuel. It flies from airports that serve its partners' route networks, such as Chicago, Dallas, Denver, Los Angeles and Salt Lake City.

    Its fleet is made up of regional jets built by Embraer and Bombardier, including the E175 and several Canadair CRJ models. SkyWest has been flying since 1972. A second reporting segment, SkyWest Leasing, covers its financing of new E175 aircraft under the capacity purchase agreements.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its business depends on code-share agreements with just four major airline partners.
    • Lower use of its aircraft by partners under the fixed-fee agreements would hurt results.
    • Trouble in finding, training and keeping enough pilots and other operating staff.
    • Trouble at one of its partners' hub airports, or a partner's own financial problems.
    • Heavy government regulation, and an accident involving one of its aircraft.
