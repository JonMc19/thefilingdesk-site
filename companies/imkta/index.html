    What Ingles Markets does

    Ingles Markets runs a supermarket chain in the southeastern United States. It operates 194 supermarkets in North Carolina, Georgia, South Carolina, Tennessee, Virginia and Alabama, not counting three stores that remain closed after Hurricane Helene in 2024. Stores sell groceries, fresh meat, dairy, produce and frozen foods, plus pharmacies, fuel centers, health and beauty products, general merchandise and private label items. It also offers online ordering for pickup at its stores.

    Substantially all of its stores are within 280 miles of its warehouse and distribution center near Asheville, North Carolina. Those facilities supply approximately 58.8% of the goods it sells; the rest is bought from third parties and delivered to stores. Ingles also owns a milk processing and packaging plant that supplies milk, juice and water to its stores and also sells to other retailers, food service distributors and grocery warehouses, which gives it a second source of revenue.

    It owns the land for 174 of its supermarkets and is remodeling, enlarging and moving stores and building new ones.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its warehouse, milk plant and all its stores are concentrated in the Southeast, so storms and local events can hit it hard.
    • Operating costs, including fuel, that can rise or swing sharply.
    • Laws and regulations that may require added spending.
    • Information system failures and data security problems.
    • Competition from other grocers in its markets.
    From Ingles Markets's annual report on Form 10-K, filed November 26, 2025, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
