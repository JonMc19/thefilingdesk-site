    What Funko does

    Funko designs and sells pop culture consumer products based on licensed movies, TV shows, video games, music and sports. Its products include collectible figures, bags, wallets, apparel, plush toys, homewares, vinyl records and posters. It sells them under three brands: Funko, Loungefly and Mondo.

    Its Core Collectible products, which include the Pop! Vinyl figures, were 80% of 2025 sales. Loungefly products are mostly licensed fashion accessories. Funko sells mainly to retailers and distributors. Customers outside the United States accounted for about 40% of sales.

    Because its products rely on other companies' characters and stories, it depends on license agreements. Its top ten licensors accounted for about 63% of 2025 sales. Funko says it is adding personalized products, micro collectibles and blind boxes, which hold a mystery figure.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Dependence on license agreements, and on content that other companies create and the popularity of those properties.
    • Economic downturns and changes in retailing that hurt its retail customers.
    • Higher tariffs and trade limits, given its factories and sales outside the United States.
    • Its debt and its ability to raise or refinance financing on good terms.
    • Managing inventory and keeping its products popular with fans.
    From Funko's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
