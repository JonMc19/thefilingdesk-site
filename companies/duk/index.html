    What Duke Energy does

    Duke Energy is a utility holding company based in Charlotte, North Carolina. Through its subsidiaries it generates, transmits and sells electricity and delivers natural gas to homes, businesses and industrial customers in the United States. Its prices and returns are set largely by state and federal regulators.

    It reports two business segments: Electric Utilities and Infrastructure, and Gas Utilities and Infrastructure. The electric segment serves about 8.7 million retail customers across six states in the Southeast and Midwest, in an area of about 90,000 square miles. It also sells electricity wholesale to municipalities and electric cooperatives. Its plants run mainly on natural gas, nuclear fuel and coal. Sales are highest in summer and winter, when weather drives demand.

    In August 2025 Duke Energy agreed to take $6 billion from an affiliate of Brookfield in exchange for an eventual 19.7% indirect stake in Duke Energy Florida, to be completed in a series of closings. The 10-K says sales have grown with new homes and businesses, including data centers, while industrial sales have been soft.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Changes in laws and regulation, and rate decisions that may not let it recover its costs.
    • The cost and liabilities of owning and running nuclear generating plants.
    • Higher costs or delays building new plants, with no assurance it can recover the investment.
    • Severe weather and natural disasters that damage its systems and cut sales.
    • Needing access to debt and equity markets, and having to keep an investment-grade credit rating.
    From Duke Energy's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
