    What Kemper does

    Kemper is an insurance holding company. Through its subsidiaries it sells personal and commercial automobile insurance to consumers in targeted markets and industries, under its Kemper Auto brand. Under Kemper Life, it sells life and other insurance for people who want basic protection for themselves and their families. It operates only in the United States.

    It makes money from premiums that customers pay, and from investing those premiums until claims are paid. It has two segments: Specialty Property & Casualty Insurance and Life Insurance. The property and casualty segment sells mostly through independent agents and brokers who earn commissions, and also through direct-to-consumer and captive channels. The life segment's career agents also sell coverage for personal property and personal liability.

    Kemper is incorporated in Delaware and traded under the name Unitrin until 2011. It says it competes in a highly competitive industry and has a large share of its personal auto business in California and Florida.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Claims and benefits turning out higher than the reserves it sets aside for them.
    • Being unable to charge rates that are both competitive and profitable.
    • Catastrophe losses, and changes in the cost or availability of reinsurance.
    • A concentration of personal auto business in California and Florida.
    • A downgrade of its financial strength ratings, and strict insurance regulation.
    From Kemper's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 11, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
