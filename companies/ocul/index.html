    What Ocular Therapeutix does

    Ocular Therapeutix is a drug company focused on eye disease. It sells one approved product, DEXTENZA, a small insert placed in the tear duct that releases a corticosteroid. It is approved for eye inflammation and pain after eye surgery and for itching from allergic conjunctivitis.

    Its main development program is AXPAXLI, an implant of the drug axitinib in a hydrogel that dissolves slowly in the eye. It is in Phase 3 trials for wet age-related macular degeneration, an eye disease of older adults, and for diabetic retinal disease. A second candidate, OTX-TIC, a travoprost implant for glaucoma, finished a Phase 2 trial, and Ocular says it is evaluating next steps. All of its products use its own hydrogel technology, called ELUTYX, to release a known drug over time.

    Ocular earns revenue from DEXTENZA sales and has a history of losses. It has debt that it says may limit its cash flow.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It has a history of losses and may never be profitable.
    • It may need more funding, which could dilute shareholders or limit its operations.
    • Its significant debt could limit cash it can invest in the business.
    • Clinical trials of AXPAXLI could be delayed by slow enrollment or other unexpected events.
    • Side effects found in trials could force it to limit or drop a program.
