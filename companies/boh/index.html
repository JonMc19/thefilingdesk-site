    What Bank of Hawaii does

    Bank of Hawai'i is the main subsidiary of a bank holding company based in Honolulu. The bank was organized in 1897 and offers a broad range of financial products and services, mainly to customers in Hawai'i, Guam and other Pacific Islands. Its deposits are insured by the FDIC, and it is regulated as a bank and as a holding company.

    The company is organized into three business segments: Consumer Banking, Commercial Banking, and Treasury and Other. Like most banks, it takes in deposits and lends them out as loans, and earns the difference in interest along with fees for its services. Its loan portfolio is largely secured by real estate, with much of it in Hawai'i and the West Pacific.

    The company says its business depends heavily on the local economies of the islands it serves, and on federal government activity there, including defense spending.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A downturn in the economy of Hawai'i, Guam and other Pacific Islands, where it does most of its business.
    • A drop in real estate values, because most of its loans are secured by real estate.
    • Cuts in federal defense spending in Hawai'i and the West Pacific.
    • Changes in interest rates that squeeze what it earns on loans.
    • Setting aside too little to cover loans that are not repaid.
    From Bank of Hawaii's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 24, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
