    What Helen of Troy does

    Helen of Troy sells consumer products under a portfolio of brands, some of them licensed. They include OXO, Hydro Flask, Osprey, Vicks, Braun, Honeywell, PUR, Hot Tools, Drybar, Curlsmith, Revlon and Olive & June. It sells in about 100 countries, and U.S. sales were about 72% of net sales in fiscal 2026.

    It has two segments. Home & Outdoor covers food preparation and storage, cooking, cleaning, organization, drink service, water bottles, coolers, backpacks and travel gear. Beauty & Wellness covers hair styling appliances, grooming tools, personal care products and nail care, plus humidifiers, thermometers, water and air purifiers, heaters and fans. Both sell mainly to online and brick-and-mortar retailers and directly to consumers.

    A few retailers account for much of its sales. Amazon.com was about 20% of net sales in fiscal 2026, and the top five customers about 50%. In fiscal 2026 it made organizational changes to stabilize the business, and the board appointed a new chief executive, who is reviewing its strategic priorities.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Relying on a few large customers, whose actions or loss could cut its sales and profit.
    • Depending on third-party manufacturers, mostly in Asia, to make its products.
    • Tariffs and trade barriers on imports from China, Vietnam or Mexico.
    • Needing a steady stream of new products to meet changing consumer tastes.
    • Loss of licensed trademarks it uses, or of the royalty income it gets from licensing its own.
