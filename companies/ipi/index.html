    What Intrepid Potash does

    Intrepid Potash is a mineral company based in Denver. It produces potassium, magnesium, sulfur, salt and water products for farming, animal feed and the oil and gas industry. It says it is the only U.S. producer of muriate of potash, also called potash, a fertilizer nutrient that is also used in animal feed and in industrial processes.

    Its three main products are potash, a specialty fertilizer called Trio, and water. Trio carries potassium, magnesium and sulfur in a single particle and is mined underground at its East mine in Carlsbad, New Mexico. Potash comes from solution mines in Carlsbad, New Mexico and Moab, Utah, and a brine recovery mine in Wendover, Utah. In 2025 Trio and potash were its two biggest products by sales, with Trio ahead.

    The company also sells salt, magnesium chloride and brines. A property it calls Intrepid South in southeast New Mexico earns money from oilfield products and services such as water, brine, surface use and right-of-way agreements. All of its mining and production is in the continental United States.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Price and demand swings for potash and Trio caused by imbalances of supply and demand.
    • A decline in oil and gas drilling, which would cut its oilfield-related revenue.
    • Competitors' aggressive pricing, and new entrants or alternatives to the langbeinite in Trio.
    • Seasonal demand that makes its cash flows uneven from quarter to quarter.
    • Write-downs of its mines and other long-lived assets if prices or oil and gas activity fall.
    From Intrepid Potash's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 5, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
