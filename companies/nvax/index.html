    What Novavax does

    Novavax is a vaccine company. Its technology has two parts: vaccine particles made from proteins, and Matrix-M, an added ingredient (an adjuvant) that helps vaccines trigger a stronger and longer-lasting immune response. Its products so far are an authorized COVID-19 vaccine, Nuvaxovid, and Matrix-M for a malaria vaccine.

    The company now focuses on partnerships rather than selling vaccines on its own, and says it wants a lean operating model. The malaria vaccine, called R21/Matrix-M, was developed by the Jenner Institute at the University of Oxford and is made by Serum Institute of India, which holds the license to sell it. Under a collaboration with Sanofi signed in 2024, Sanofi has a non-exclusive license to develop and sell other vaccines that use Matrix-M. In 2025 Novavax also signed three agreements with other drug companies to test Matrix-M in their own candidates.

    Its revenue comes from its collaboration and license partners, from product sales, and from grants from government-sponsored organizations. It is also advancing a pipeline of other vaccine candidates.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A history of losses and uncertain profitability.
    • A need for significant funding to keep operating and develop its vaccine candidates.
    • Clinical trials that are lengthy and costly, may be delayed, and may not give a good result.
    • Relying on new, fast-changing technologies that may not succeed.
    • Partner agreements that may not bring the funding or milestone payments it expects.
