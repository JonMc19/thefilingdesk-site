    What Tyler Technologies does

    Tyler Technologies sells software and related services to state and local governments, schools and other public agencies in the United States. Its products run the day-to-day work of government: public safety, courts, tax and budgeting, land use, utilities, K-12 education, social services and more. It also sells platforms for online payments, data and digital services that connect residents with their governments.

    Tyler makes money from cloud-based software subscriptions and support, which give it a base of recurring revenue, and from professional services such as installing the software, converting data and training staff. Many of its customers sign long-term contracts. Tyler says it has dedicated state-level offices in many states where it holds enterprise contracts.

    Tyler says its customers pick vendors on the breadth and quality of products, industry expertise, track record in setting up systems, price and return on investment, and the vendor's financial strength. The public sector market it serves is spread across thousands of counties, cities, towns and school districts.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Cyber-attacks or leaks of sensitive client data that could harm clients and its reputation.
    • Its reliance on third-party providers such as Amazon Web Services to host its cloud products.
    • Clients ending recurring contracts to run operations themselves.
    • The unusual demands of selling to government, including open bidding that makes contract awards hard to predict.
    • Competition from other software vendors and new entrants.
    From Tyler Technologies's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 18, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
