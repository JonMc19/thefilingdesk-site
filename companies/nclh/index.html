    What Norwegian Cruise Line does

    Norwegian Cruise Line Holdings runs cruise vacations under three brands: Norwegian Cruise Line, Oceania Cruises and Regent Seven Seas Cruises. Its ships sail to Europe, Asia, Australia, New Zealand, South America, Africa, Canada, Bermuda, the Caribbean, Alaska and Hawaii. Norwegian's U.S.-flagged Pride of America runs the only itinerary entirely among the Hawaiian islands. As of December 31, 2025, it had 34 ships with about 71,400 berths, or passenger beds.

    Passengers pay a cruise fare. The company also earns onboard and other revenue from things not in the fare, such as casino play, some food and drinks, shore excursions, gift shops, spa services and communications. Cruises sell best in the Northern Hemisphere summer, so results vary by quarter. Its main competitors are Carnival and Royal Caribbean, plus MSC Cruises, Viking Ocean Cruises and Virgin Voyages. It also competes with land-based vacations such as hotels and resorts.

    The company has more ships on order and expects to add 17 to its fleet from 2026 through 2037. It is based in Bermuda, with its main executive offices in Miami, Florida.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Debt that limits its flexibility, and a possible need for more financing on terms that may not be favorable.
    • Adverse incidents involving cruise ships, or public health crises, that reduce demand.
    • Fuel price changes and the cost of its ship repairs, maintenance and delivery delays in its shipbuilding program.
    • Ports of call becoming unavailable, and its reliance on commercial airlines to bring passengers and crew to ships.
    • Overcapacity in the cruise market and competition from other vacation options.
    From Norwegian Cruise Line's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 2, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
