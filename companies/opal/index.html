    What OPAL Fuels does

    OPAL Fuels captures biogas from landfills and dairy farms and turns it into renewable natural gas (RNG) and electricity. RNG is chemically identical to the natural gas used for heating and cooking, but it comes from recycled methane from decaying organic waste instead of from the ground. It is sold to fleets of heavy trucks and to other industrial users, and put into existing gas pipelines.

    The company has three lines of business. It makes and sells RNG. In its Fuel Station Services segment it builds and maintains vehicle fueling stations for outside owners, and dispenses fuel, which also earns it environmental credits. In its Renewable Power segment it burns landfill biogas to generate power that it sells to utilities across the United States.

    OPAL says a large part of its revenue depends on environmental credits tied to RNG, such as RINs and California LCFS credits. It operates on sites owned by others, under contracts that give it the rights to the biogas. It employed 331 people at the end of 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Depending on landfill and dairy site owners for biogas rights and access, with no promise of gas quality or quantity.
    • Falling prices for the environmental credits it earns from RNG, and swings in oil and gas prices.
    • Rising prices for new or renewed biogas rights, and falling California LCFS credit prices.
    • Waste trends, such as more recycling, that could reduce the biogas available.
    • Delays or poor work by outside suppliers when building projects and stations.
