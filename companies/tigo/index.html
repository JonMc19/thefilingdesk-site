    What Millicom does

    Millicom provides mobile and home internet and TV services in eleven countries in Latin America, mostly under the Tigo and Tigo Business brands, which it has used in all its markets since 2004. It sells to consumers, businesses and governments. Its services include mobile data and voice, mobile financial services, broadband, cable and satellite TV, fixed voice, and business solutions.

    It reports its results by country. Its reportable segments are Guatemala, Colombia, Panama, Bolivia, Honduras and Paraguay, plus an Other group that holds El Salvador, Nicaragua, Costa Rica, Uruguay and Ecuador. It offers both mobile and fixed services in ten countries, and fixed services only in Costa Rica. It runs some operations through joint ventures with local partners. Uruguay and Ecuador were added through acquisitions in the last quarter of 2025.

    It also owns network assets: around 2,000 towers, 12 data centers and more than 200,000 kilometers of fiber. After deals in 2025 it has far fewer towers than the more than 9,000 it ran in the past. It had about 15,000 employees at the end of 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Fast technology change that needs large investments which may not pay off.
    • Rising costs for TV content and programming that it may not be able to pass on to customers.
    • Limited, closely regulated and costly radio spectrum.
    • Licenses that could be suspended, revoked or tied to extra obligations.
    • Network failures from disasters, sabotage or cyber attacks, and heavy regulation of the market.
    From Millicom's annual report on Form 20-F, filed March 24, 2026, in our words; the 20-F has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
