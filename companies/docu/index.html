    What Docusign does

    Docusign sells software for creating, signing and managing agreements. Its main products are eSignature, which lets people sign documents electronically, and its Intelligent Agreement Management platform, which stores a company's agreements in one place and builds workflows and AI-based insights around them. It also sells contract lifecycle management software. It says over 1.8 million customers use its products, from global enterprises down to very small businesses, and that its products connect to more than 1,100 partner applications.

    Docusign earns money mainly by selling subscriptions, usually for one to three years. Substantially all revenue is subscriptions, and the rest is fees for deployment and integration services for new customers. It sells through its own sales force, partners and online self-service. It says no single customer accounted for more than 10% of total revenue.

    Docusign says standalone eSignature will keep making up most of its revenue for the foreseeable future, because the newer platform is at an early stage of growth. It started selling the platform on a per-user basis in the second quarter of fiscal 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Slower or declining adoption of eSignature, which brings in most of its revenue.
    • The newer agreement platform not meeting customer needs or failing to win enough buyers.
    • Fast changes in AI and other technology that could reduce demand for its products.
    • Failing to keep and grow its customers, or to win new ones, in a competitive market.
    • A breach of the customer and other data it stores and processes.
    From Docusign's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 18, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
