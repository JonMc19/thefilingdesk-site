    What Genuine Parts does

    Genuine Parts sells replacement parts for cars and for industrial equipment. It serves customers from more than 10,800 locations, mostly in North America, Europe, and Australia and New Zealand. In 2025 its net sales were $24.3 billion, with revenue split about 74% in North America, 16% in Europe and 10% in Australasia.

    It has three reportable segments. North America Automotive, about 39% of net sales, operates mainly under the NAPA brand in the United States and Canada. International Automotive, about 24%, operates in Europe and Australasia. Industrial, 37% of net sales, operates through Motion Industries and Motion Asia Pacific. The two automotive segments serve over one million customer locations, including repair shops, dealerships, fleet operators and retail customers.

    In automotive parts it competes with specialty parts chains, car makers, dealers, warehouse clubs, mass merchants and online sellers, including AutoZone. On February 17, 2026 it announced plans to split into two public companies, one for automotive and one for industrial, targeted for the first quarter of 2027 and subject to conditions.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Supply chain delays, and dependence on its suppliers.
    • The failure or bankruptcy of a major supplier or customer.
    • Substantial competition, including consolidation among its competitors.
    • Geopolitical conflicts that could affect its business.
    • The planned separation into two companies may not happen as planned or may not bring the expected benefits.
