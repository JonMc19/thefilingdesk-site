    Value United Rentals yourself

    United Rentals' revenue rose 4.9% to $16.1 billion in fiscal 2025. Its operating margin was 24.7%, down from 26.5% a year earlier. It paid out $2.4 billion in dividends and buybacks. The diluted share count fell 3.0%.

    Operating margin is operating income as a share of revenue. Free cash flow is cash from operations minus capital spending. Return on capital is after-tax operating income as a share of the money invested in the business: debt plus equity, minus cash.

    About these figures. Total debt is the balance sheet's long-term debt plus its current debt line, both including finance leases, so year-end and quarterly figures are on the same basis. United Rentals' capital spending is mostly purchases of rental equipment, which its filings tag differently from year to year (in some years only its other property and equipment, in 2025 under its own label), so capital spending and free cash flow are left blank rather than shown on a basis that changes. Property and equipment is left blank: the figure its filings tag leaves out its rental fleet, its largest asset.

    How the model works. Free cash flow grows at the first rate for five years and the second rate for five more, and each year is discounted at the discount rate. Everything after year 10 is valued as year-10 cash flow × (1 + growth after year 10) ÷ (discount rate − growth after year 10), discounted back ten years. Net cash is added and the total is divided by diluted shares. The result depends entirely on the assumptions. It is not a forecast, a price target or a recommendation.
