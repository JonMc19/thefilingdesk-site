    What Tapestry does

    Tapestry owns two fashion brands, Coach and kate spade new york. Both sell handbags and other accessories, and kate spade new york also sells ready-to-wear clothing, jewelry, footwear, gifts and home décor. The company sells to shoppers around the world.

    Tapestry reports two segments. Coach is by far the larger, at 86.4% of fiscal 2026 net sales, and Kate Spade made up 13.4%. In August 2025 it sold its Stuart Weitzman business, which is no longer a segment.

    Most sales are direct to consumers, meaning its own stores, outlet stores, shop-in-shops and websites: about 87% of net sales in fiscal 2026. Wholesale sales to department stores, specialty stores and online partners were about 12%. The rest comes from licensing, where partners make and sell products under its brand names.

    Its 2028 growth plan, called Amplify, aims to win younger customers, lead with handbags and leather goods, and grow faster in Greater China and Europe.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Changing fashion trends and the need to keep its brands valued by shoppers.
    • New tariffs and changes to international trade agreements on imported goods.
    • Risks of sourcing products from around the world and operating in international markets.
    • Falling traffic in its stores, and weaker consumer spending in a downturn.
    • Whether its Amplify growth strategy is carried out successfully.
    From Tapestry's annual report on Form 10-K, filed August 13, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
