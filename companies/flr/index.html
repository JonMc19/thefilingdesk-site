    What Fluor does

    Fluor is an engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) company, and it also provides project management. Its clients are in life sciences, advanced technologies and manufacturing, data centers, mining and metals, power, infrastructure, chemicals, LNG, and oil and gas. It also works for the U.S. federal government and governments abroad. It was incorporated in Delaware in 2000 and says it and its predecessors have served clients for over 110 years.

    Fluor makes money by being paid by clients to design, build and manage large projects, often through subsidiaries and joint ventures with partners. Its work is organized into three principal segments: Urban Solutions, Energy Solutions and Mission Solutions, plus an Other segment. It competes with other EPC firms.

    The company says it now prefers contracts with reimbursable terms, where the client pays its costs, and will only consider fixed-price contracts where it has a strong position. At the end of 2025, 81% of its backlog, meaning work under contract but not yet done, was reimbursable.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Cycles in the markets it serves, since its revenue and earnings depend largely on new contract awards.
    • Intense competition in the EPC industry that can squeeze its revenue and profits.
    • Relying on joint venture partners, suppliers and subcontractors it may not control.
    • Hiring and keeping enough qualified people.
    • Cybersecurity breaches and interruptions to its computer systems.
    From Fluor's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 17, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Income statement

    From fiscal 2016 to 2024, earnings per share grew 25.5% a year and net income 28.9% a year, as new shares raised the share count by 23% and diluted each share's earnings. Fluor then lost money in fiscal 2025.
