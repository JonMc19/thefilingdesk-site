    What Jabil does

    Jabil is a contract manufacturer. It designs, builds and manages production of products for other companies, from the first design through assembly, delivery and after-sale service, and helps them manage their supply chains. It serves customers through dedicated business units that act as one point of contact. Its factories are worldwide, including in China, Malaysia, Mexico and the United States.

    In the fiscal year ended August 31, 2026, Jabil had net revenues of $36.0 billion. It has three segments. Regulated Industries serves the automotive and transportation, healthcare and packaging, and renewable and energy infrastructure industries. Intelligent Infrastructure serves capital equipment, cloud and data center, and networking and communications customers, including AI infrastructure. Connected Living and Digital Commerce, renamed Intelligent Devices and Robotics from September 1, 2026, covers areas such as warehouse automation and robotics.

    Jabil says it competes with many other manufacturing service providers and design providers. It counts temporary and contingent workers in its total workforce, which is spread across about 120 locations in 30 countries.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Customers who do not commit to long-term production schedules and may cancel or change orders.
    • Depending on a limited number of customers, so losing one could cut revenue sharply.
    • Shortages or price increases for components and materials.
    • Customer relationships with emerging companies that carry more risk than with established ones.
    • Keeping pace with technology changes and competition from many other manufacturing service providers.
    From Jabil's annual report on Form 10-K, filed October 23, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
