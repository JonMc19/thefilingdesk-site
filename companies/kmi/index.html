    What Kinder Morgan does

    Kinder Morgan owns pipelines, storage sites and terminals that move and hold energy products in North America. Most of its business is natural gas: it owns about 42,000 miles of natural gas pipelines and has stakes in entities with about 25,000 more. It also operates pipelines and terminals for refined fuels, crude oil and other liquids, and handles bulk goods such as petroleum coke, metals and ores.

    It reports four segments: Natural Gas Pipelines, Products Pipelines, Terminals, and CO2. The CO2 segment produces and sells carbon dioxide used to recover oil from mature fields, and owns some oil and gas fields. In natural gas, customers mostly pay long-term fixed fees to reserve space to ship or store gas, whether or not they use it. Contracts for natural gas transportation had about seven years left on average at the end of 2025.

    The company says it focuses on stable, fee-based assets and on growing markets, including U.S. exports. It is building several new pipelines, including some that serve liquefied natural gas export plants. In 2025 it bought a gas gathering system in North Dakota and sold its stake in EagleHawk.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its businesses depend on the supply of and demand for the products it handles.
    • Substantial debt that could hurt its financial health in a downturn.
    • Regulators may set pipeline rates that cut into its earnings.
    • A breach of information security or the failure of key systems.
    • Its dividend guidance may not be met.
    From Kinder Morgan's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 13, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
