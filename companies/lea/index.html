    What Lear does

    Lear is an auto parts supplier. It designs and builds complete car seats and seat parts, and electrical systems that carry power and signals through vehicles. Its customers are the world's major automakers. Seat parts include trim covers, leather and fabric, seat mechanisms, cushions, headrests, and heating, ventilation and cooling systems. It says its seating products work with gasoline engines and electrified powertrains alike.

    Lear reports two segments, Seating and E-Systems. E-Systems makes electrical distribution and connection systems, high-voltage power distribution products such as battery disconnect units, low-voltage power products and electronic controllers. Revenue comes from selling these systems to automakers for the vehicles they build, so sales follow vehicle production.

    Lear has 258 manufacturing, engineering and administrative locations in 36 countries. It says it supplies seat systems in sync with its customers' assembly lines, delivering them just in time. It adds to its business through its own growth and through acquisitions.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A drop in vehicle production at its major customers, or their financial trouble.
    • Higher costs of raw materials, parts, energy and labor.
    • Carmakers pushing it for price cuts that it cannot offset with lower costs.
    • Tariffs, sanctions and other trade restrictions.
    • A shift to electric vehicles and new rivals, including fast-growing Chinese automakers.
    From Lear's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 13, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
