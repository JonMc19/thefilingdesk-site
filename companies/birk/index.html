    What Birkenstock does

    Birkenstock makes and sells footwear, best known for its sandals, under a brand that dates back to 1774. It also sells some non-footwear products. It produces all its footwear in the EU, and it says that making its own products is a key difference from an industry that has largely moved production overseas since the 1980s. It owns most of its production sites.

    It sells in two ways. Business-to-business (B2B) sales go to wholesale and retail partners. Direct-to-consumer (DTC) sales go through its own stores and e-commerce sites, and in its fiscal year ended September 30, 2025, the DTC channel made up 38% of revenues. It plans to raise that share by opening more of its own stores and growing online sales.

    It reports three regional segments: the Americas, EMEA (Europe, the Middle East and Africa) and APAC (Asia-Pacific). It says the APAC segment has not reached its potential because its production capacity is limited and it has chosen to favor the Americas and EMEA.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Harm to the strength of its brand, including from negative publicity.
    • Intense competition from established companies and newer entrants.
    • Counterfeit and imitation products that may draw away demand for its brand.
    • Limited production capacity, since it makes its footwear in only a few EU sites.
    • Executing its plan to grow its own stores and online sales, and returns of merchandise.
    From Birkenstock's annual report on Form 20-F, filed December 18, 2025, in our words; the 20-F has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
