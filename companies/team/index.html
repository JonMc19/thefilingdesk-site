    What Atlassian does

    Atlassian sells software that helps teams plan, track and carry out their work. It was founded in Sydney, Australia, in 2002. Its products serve software teams, IT operations and support teams, and business and leadership teams. Its best-known apps include Jira, Confluence and Jira Service Management. They run on a shared cloud platform, and the company has added artificial intelligence (AI) features, including an AI offering it calls Rovo.

    Atlassian says it serves more than 350,000 customers, from small companies to over 85% of the Fortune 500, in over 200 countries and territories. It earns its revenue by selling subscriptions and licenses to its software. Many customers sign up on their own through self-service, and the company's sales force focuses mainly on deepening relationships with large business customers. It also runs the Atlassian Marketplace, where outside developers sell apps that work with its products.

    The company says it derives a majority of its revenue from Jira, Confluence and Jira Service Management. It is moving its business toward its cloud offerings, and it competes in markets it describes as intensely competitive.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Customers not renewing their subscriptions or buying more, which would hurt its future results.
    • Its AI offerings and investments may not be successful.
    • Challenges as it shifts its business toward cloud offerings.
    • Its reliance on Jira, Confluence and Jira Service Management for a majority of its revenue.
    • Intense competition, and the need to keep launching new apps that keep pace with technology.
    From Atlassian's annual report on Form 10-K, filed August 14, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
