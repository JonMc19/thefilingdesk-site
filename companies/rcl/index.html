    What Royal Caribbean Group does

    Royal Caribbean Group sells cruise vacations. It owns three global brands: Royal Caribbean, Celebrity Cruises and Silversea. Royal Caribbean aims at families and couples with casual ships. Celebrity Cruises sells a more upscale experience. Silversea runs smaller, luxury and expedition ships to remote places such as Antarctica. Ships call on more than 1,000 destinations on all seven continents, and the company also owns private land-based destinations for its guests.

    The company earns its money from cruise vacations and runs its three global brands as a single business. It also owns half of TUI Cruises, a joint venture with a German tourism company that sells cruises to German guests under the TUI Cruises and Hapag-Lloyd Cruises names. Together the brands run 69 ships. The company says its main cruise competitors are Carnival, Disney Cruise Line, MSC Cruises, Norwegian Cruise Line Holdings, Viking and Virgin Voyages. It also competes with resorts, hotels, theme parks and other ways to spend leisure time.

    The company has more ships on order, including Royal Caribbean, Celebrity and TUI Cruises vessels due between 2026 and 2032. It has also started a river cruise line under the Celebrity name. Most guests of its global brands come from North America. It sells in other countries through its own staff and independent representatives, and relies on travel advisors to sell its cruises.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Cruise demand and guest spending falling when the economy or travel conditions weaken.
    • Incidents on ships or at its destinations, and bad publicity, hurting its reputation and bookings.
    • Delays at shipyards, which build and repair its ships, affecting new ships and upgrades.
    • More cruise ships worldwide, or competition from other vacations, pushing down its prices and sales.
    • Disease outbreaks, bad weather and wars disrupting its voyages.
    From Royal Caribbean Group's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 11, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
