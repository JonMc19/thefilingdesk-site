    What NETSTREIT does

    NETSTREIT is a real estate investment trust, or REIT, a company that owns property and passes most of its income to shareholders. It buys and manages single-tenant retail properties across the United States, each rented to one business under a long-term net lease, where the tenant pays most property costs. It also invests in property developments and in mortgage loans backed by real estate.

    It makes money from the rent its tenants pay and the interest on its loans. It says it focuses on tenants in industries where a physical store matters to sales and profit, such as grocers, convenience stores, discount stores, home improvement, quick-service restaurants and auto parts. At the end of 2025 it owned or had investments in 761 properties with 129 tenants in 45 states. Its annualized base rent was $198.3 million, and its portfolio was 99.9% occupied.

    In 2025 it acquired 140 properties and sold 78. It paid for deals partly with new term loans. It is run in-house by 29 full-time employees and owns nearly all of the units in its operating partnership, through which it conducts its business.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Tenants that fail to run their businesses well, or that default or go bankrupt, which hits a single-tenant landlord hard.
    • Concentration in certain tenants or geographic markets, so one setback has a large effect.
    • Being unable to renew or re-lease space when leases end on good terms.
    • Borrowers defaulting on loans it makes.
    • Its view that its tenants' businesses resist online competition and recessions may prove wrong.
    From NETSTREIT's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 10, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
