    What Snap-on does

    Snap-on makes tools, equipment, diagnostics, repair information and software for professional users, such as vehicle repair technicians and people working in aerospace, the military, natural resources and manufacturing. It began in 1920 with an interchangeable socket set and later pioneered selling tools from vans that visit technicians at their workplace. It sells in more than 130 countries, and the United States is its largest market.

    It reports four segments: Commercial & Industrial Group, Snap-on Tools Group, Repair Systems & Information Group and Financial Services. Products reach customers through franchisee vans, direct sales and distributors. Its customers include industrial and commercial buyers, vehicle repair technicians who buy from its mobile tool network, and owners of independent repair shops and car dealership service departments. Financial Services offers financing to help customers buy its products and to support its franchisees.

    Its strategy is to grow its base of professional customers in vehicle repair and in other markets, where mistakes are costly. It also runs a program of continuous improvement in safety, quality, innovation and efficiency to cut waste.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Sales that depend on the health of the vehicle repair market and its changing requirements.
    • Relying on franchisees to run its mobile tool distribution business, and on its relationship with them.
    • Failing to launch new products that customers accept.
    • Competition in the tool, equipment, diagnostics and repair information industries.
    • Disruption of manufacturing, and inflation or shortages of raw materials and components.
    From Snap-on's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
