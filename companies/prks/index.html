    What United Parks & Resorts does

    United Parks & Resorts runs theme parks built around animals and rides. It owns or licenses brands including SeaWorld, Busch Gardens, Aquatica, Discovery Cove and Sesame Place. Its portfolio has 13 theme parks grouped in key markets across the United States and in the United Arab Emirates.

    It makes money mainly by selling admission to its parks and from food, merchandise and other items that guests buy inside them. Many parks show its zoological collection and offer thrill and family rides, educational presentations and shows. The company also runs seasonal events such as holiday and Halloween events. It says it competes with other entertainment and leisure options for guests' time and money.

    A large share of revenue has historically come from Florida, California and Virginia. The 10-K says the company has faced turnover and hiring challenges for some positions and more union organizing, and that Hill Path Capital LP and its affiliates could significantly influence its decisions.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Weather, attendance and other factors outside its control that change how many guests visit and what they spend.
    • Failing to hire and keep enough employees, and rising labor and benefit costs.
    • Rules on how it treats animals, scrutiny from activist groups, and bad publicity about its parks.
    • Relying on Florida, California and Virginia for a large share of revenue.
    • Hill Path Capital and its affiliates could influence its decisions in ways that conflict with other owners' interests.
    From United Parks & Resorts's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 3, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
