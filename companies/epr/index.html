    What EPR Properties does

    EPR Properties is a real estate investment trust (REIT) formed in 1997. It owns or finances venues where people spend leisure time, which it calls experiential real estate, and leases them to operators under net leases, meaning the tenant pays most property costs. Its properties include movie theatres, eat and play venues, and cultural venues, as well as lodging, ski, attractions and gaming properties.

    It reports two segments. Experiential, which made up 94% of its total investments at the end of 2025, holds the venues above. Education, a legacy investment, holds early childhood education centers and private schools. Theatres remain a large part of the Experiential portfolio, mostly modern megaplex theatres, but the company says it intends to reduce its investments in theatres and diversify into other experiential property types.

    EPR focuses its decisions on industry and property cash flow criteria and on the credit quality of its tenants and customers. Its income depends on collecting rent from tenants.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Tenants who cannot pay rent, or leases that expire and are not renewed on good terms.
    • Its concentration in theatres and the challenges that industry has faced.
    • A borrower's bankruptcy or default on financing it has provided.
    • Debt covenants, credit rating changes and high interest rates that raise financing costs.
    • Economic and trade policy uncertainty that could hurt its customers.
    From EPR Properties's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
