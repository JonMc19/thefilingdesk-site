    What AbbVie does

    AbbVie researches, makes and sells prescription medicines and related products worldwide. Its main areas are immunology (Skyrizi, Rinvoq and Humira, used for autoimmune diseases), neuroscience (including Vraylar, Botox Therapeutic, and migraine and Parkinson's treatments), oncology (blood cancers and other cancers), and aesthetics (Botox Cosmetic and Juvederm). It also sells eye care products and treatments for hepatitis C and digestive conditions.

    AbbVie reports as a single business segment. It makes money by selling its medicines. In the United States it sells mainly to three wholesale distributors, McKesson, Cardinal Health and Cencora. No single wholesaler accounted for more than 43% of its 2025 gross revenues in the United States. Outside the United States it sells to wholesalers and distributors and to government payers. Some aesthetic products are sold directly to doctors and other licensed providers. It also markets to doctors, hospitals, managed care providers and government agencies.

    AbbVie says it competes with other drug and biotech companies, and that generic and biosimilar copies of branded drugs add pressure on products without patent protection. It says Humira already faces direct biosimilar competition around the world. AbbVie became an independent public company in 2013, when Abbott Laboratories distributed AbbVie's shares to its own shareholders.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Losing patent protection on major products, and the competition from generics and biosimilars that follows.
    • Research and development that may not produce new products that can be sold successfully.
    • Pricing pressure from governments, rebate programs, and pharmacy benefit managers that influence access to its drugs.
    • Safety or effectiveness problems with its products, and product liability claims.
    • Manufacturing problems, or an interruption in supplies from single-source suppliers.
    From AbbVie's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 20, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
