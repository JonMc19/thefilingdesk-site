    What Groupon does

    Groupon runs an online marketplace that connects consumers with merchants. Consumers use its mobile apps and its websites, which are mainly local groupon.com sites in thirteen countries. It reports two segments, North America and International, and three categories: Local, Goods and Travel.

    It earns money as a commission when it sells goods or services for outside merchants. It also earns commissions when customers buy from retailers using digital coupons found through its sites and apps. The Local category covers experiences and services such as things to do, beauty and wellness, food and drink, and home and automotive services. In Goods, merchants sell items such as electronics, sporting goods, jewelry, toys, household items and apparel. In Travel, it offers hotels, airfare and package deals at discounted and market rates.

    Groupon says its strategy is to be the marketplace where customers go for local services and experiences. It plans to build long-term relationships with local merchants and to invest in its technology, including search, checkout and tools that use artificial intelligence.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its strategy to return to stable growth may not work.
    • Attracting and keeping merchants, who may stop if the service does not pay off for them.
    • Losing existing customers or failing to win new ones.
    • Declining traffic from email and search engines, which it relies on to reach customers.
    • Higher refund rates or wrong estimates of vouchers that are never redeemed.
    From Groupon's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 10, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Income statement

    From fiscal 2017 to 2021, earnings per share grew 74.2% a year and net income 70.6% a year, as new shares raised the share count by 18% and diluted each share's earnings. Groupon then lost money in fiscal 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025.
