    Operating margin is operating income as a share of revenue. Free cash flow is cash from operations minus capital spending. Return on capital is after-tax operating income as a share of the money invested in the business: debt plus equity, minus cash. Market cap is the last closing share price times the latest diluted share count, so it is approximate.     About these figures. Strategy had no borrowings before its first convertible notes in December 2020, so debt counts as zero in earlier years. From 2025 Strategy carries its bitcoin at market value, so its profits and losses swing with the price of bitcoin; its revenue is still almost all from its analytics software. Share counts and per-share figures for earlier years are restated for a stock split (10-for-1), so every year is on today's share basis.

    What Strategy does

    Strategy, formerly MicroStrategy, runs two things at once. It says it is the first and largest company built around holding bitcoin as its main treasury asset. It also sells business analytics software, now with AI features, mostly to customers it already has. It changed its name from MicroStrategy on August 11, 2025.

    On the bitcoin side, the company buys and holds bitcoin and raises money to buy more. It does this by selling Class A common stock and preferred securities through at-the-market offerings, which let a company sell shares gradually at market prices. In 2025 it issued five classes of preferred stock, each aimed at a different group of investors. As of February 13, 2026, it held approximately 717,131 bitcoins, which it bought for an aggregate purchase price of $54.5 billion. It also keeps a cash reserve, which stood at $2.25 billion on that date.

    On the software side, it is moving customers from product licenses to cloud subscriptions. That shift can change when it records revenue. Its software revenue comes from licenses, subscriptions and related services.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A fall in the value of its bitcoin, which makes up the vast bulk of its assets, could hurt its ability to meet its financial obligations.
    • Bitcoin prices are highly volatile, and the rules for bitcoin and digital assets are new and unsettled.
    • Its results can swing a lot from period to period, and it may not stay profitable.
    • Heavier regulation and tax exposure that its bitcoin strategy brings.
    • The shift of software customers from licenses to cloud subscriptions, which could raise customer losses.

    1Discounted cash flow (DCF)

    A discounted cash flow (DCF) model values a business as the cash it may generate in future, discounted back to today. The starting figures come from Strategy's 10-K (or, for net cash and shares, its latest 10-Q) and the assumptions are yours: start from one of three example scenarios, then change any figure to see what it implies for a share, and how that compares with the last share price.

    The example scenarios need a positive starting cash flow. Strategy's free cash flow was negative in fiscal 2025 and over the last three years, and so is cash from operations minus depreciation. You can still enter your own figures in the calculator.

    How the model works. Free cash flow grows at the first rate for five years and the second rate for five more, and each year is discounted at the discount rate. Everything after year 10 is valued as year-10 cash flow × (1 + growth after year 10) ÷ (discount rate − growth after year 10), discounted back ten years. Net cash is added and the total is divided by diluted shares. The result depends entirely on the assumptions. It is not a forecast, a price target or a recommendation.     2Price-to-earnings (P/E) method

    Another common way to value a company is to multiply its earnings per share by a price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple: the number of years of today's earnings an investor pays for a share. A higher multiple usually means investors expect earnings to grow. Choose a multiple, for example from similar companies, to see the value it gives.

    The P/E method needs positive earnings, and Strategy's latest diluted earnings per share were negative or zero.
