    What Boston Scientific does

    Boston Scientific develops, makes and sells medical devices used in less-invasive procedures, which are alternatives to surgery. Its products help doctors diagnose and treat heart, lung, digestive, cancer, nerve and urinary conditions. Customers are mainly hospitals and the physicians who use its devices. The company has been in the business for more than 45 years and sells around the world.

    It reports two segments, MedSurg and Cardiovascular. In late 2025 it combined its Cardiology and Peripheral Interventions businesses into a single Cardiovascular business, which did not change the two reported segments. MedSurg includes Endoscopy, whose devices diagnose and treat digestive and bile duct conditions and support weight loss in patients with obesity. The company earns revenue by selling these devices.

    Boston Scientific says it competes with other medical device makers that move fast on technology and offer lower-cost products. It has bought many companies in recent years to expand its businesses, and it says it plans to keep investing in new products and in markets outside the United States.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Intense competition and fast technology change, including lower-cost rival products.
    • Declines in market size, average selling prices or procedure volumes.
    • Failing to integrate acquired companies or to pick successful acquisitions.
    • Needing to develop new products, which takes heavy research and clinical testing.
    • Supply chain or factory disruptions, and shortages of materials, components or sterilization services.
    From Boston Scientific's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 17, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
