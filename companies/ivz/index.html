    What Invesco does

    Invesco is an independent investment management firm based on Bermuda law. It manages money for clients, offering active, passive and alternative investment products. It has about 7,500 employees and operates in more than 20 countries, serving clients in more than 120 countries. As of December 31, 2025 it managed about $2.2 trillion in assets.

    Invesco earns fees for managing client money, so its revenue rises and falls with the amount of assets it manages. It sells to retail and institutional clients in the Americas, EMEA (Europe, the Middle East and Africa) and APAC (Asia-Pacific), often through distributors, advisors and consultants. It says these groups often weigh longer-term results, such as three-year and five-year performance, when picking products.

    It competes with other investment managers on performance, service, distribution and fees. Its shares trade on the New York Stock Exchange.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A drop in assets under management from falling markets or clients withdrawing money.
    • Poor investment performance that cuts sales and assets.
    • Pressure from competitors that forces it to lower fees.
    • Losses on its seed capital and co-investments in its own products.
    • Risks in its private market products, such as private credit and real estate.
    From Invesco's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 24, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
