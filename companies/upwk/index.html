    What Upwork does

    Upwork runs an online marketplace that connects businesses with independent professionals and agencies, called talent, for freelance and other on-demand work. Clients range from small businesses to large enterprises. Work includes software and web development, design, sales and marketing, administrative support, customer service and AI-related projects.

    Most of its revenue comes from the Upwork Marketplace, which charges talent a fee based on their billings and charges clients a fee based on their payments to talent. It also earns from ads and other paid tools for talent, memberships, and currency exchange. A second business, Lifted, sells enterprise clients a way to find, hire, manage and pay contingent workers, for subscriptions and service fees.

    Upwork measures activity by gross services volume, the total value transacted through its platforms. Its platforms enabled $4.0 billion of it in 2025, with customers in over 180 countries. Upwork says it offers a direct alternative to staffing firms, recruiters and traditional agencies.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Depending on the size of its community of talent and clients, and on keeping it growing.
    • Competition from many other ways for talent to find clients and get paid.
    • Changes to its strategy, offerings and pricing that could hurt the business and are hard to judge.
    • Losing its banking and payment partners, or facing new payment rules.
    • Payment fraud and other abuse on its platforms.
    From Upwork's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 13, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
