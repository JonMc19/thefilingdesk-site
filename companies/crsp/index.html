    What CRISPR Therapeutics does

    CRISPR Therapeutics develops medicines based on CRISPR/Cas9, a technology for gene editing, which means precisely changing specific pieces of a person's DNA. It is a biopharmaceutical company based in Switzerland, with its work centered on four areas: blood disorders (hemoglobinopathies), in vivo editing (editing cells inside the body), CAR T cell therapies, and regenerative medicine.

    Its most advanced product is CASGEVY, developed with its partner Vertex Pharmaceuticals and approved in 2023 as the first CRISPR-based therapy. It treats severe sickle cell disease and transfusion-dependent beta thalassemia, two inherited blood disorders. Doctors edit a patient's own blood stem cells outside the body and then return them. The two companies work together under a collaboration agreement.

    Its other programs include gene-edited immune cells for autoimmune diseases and cancer, and an islet cell replacement candidate for Type 1 diabetes. It has funded its work through share sales and partnerships, and says it has incurred operating losses and expects more.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It has incurred significant operating losses and expects them to continue.
    • It will need to raise substantial money, which would dilute shareholders.
    • The intellectual property behind its core gene editing technology is jointly owned, which limits its rights.
    • Vertex, its partner on CASGEVY, has significant control over that lead program.
    • Its gene-editing candidates may fail in trials or fail to win regulatory approval.
