    What Gilead Sciences does

    Gilead is a biopharmaceutical company based in Foster City, California. It discovers, develops and sells medicines for life-threatening diseases, mainly HIV, viral hepatitis, COVID-19 and cancer. It operates in more than 35 countries. Its commercial portfolio in 2025 included more than 25 therapies.

    HIV is its core area. Its products include Biktarvy, Descovy, Genvoya, Odefsey, Sunlenca and Yeztugo, which is used to reduce the risk of getting HIV. It also sells Symtuza, which Janssen sells and on which Gilead receives a share of revenue. In liver disease it sells Epclusa for hepatitis C. In oncology it sells cell therapies, including Tecartus, a CAR T-cell treatment for certain lymphomas and leukemia.

    Gilead sells through its own commercial teams and through wholesalers, distributors and corporate partners. It says it competes with global pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies, specialized drug makers and makers of generic drugs. Its revenue depends on patents and other intellectual property it must defend.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to develop and sell new products or to win approval for new uses of existing ones.
    • Pricing and reimbursement pressure from governments and other payers on its existing products.
    • Competition from other drug makers, including makers of generic drugs.
    • Clinical trials that may show poor results or run late.
    • Losing patent protection or failing to defend its patents and other intellectual property.
    From Gilead Sciences's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 24, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
