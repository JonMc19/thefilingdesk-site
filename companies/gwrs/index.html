    What Global Water Resources does

    Global Water Resources owns, runs and manages thirty-nine water, wastewater and recycled water utilities, mainly in communities around Phoenix and Tucson, Arizona. It calls its approach "Total Water Management," which means reusing recycled water and planning ahead to cut demand on scarce water supplies.

    It earns money by providing water and wastewater service to homes and businesses in its service areas. It serves more than 121,000 people in about 40,000 homes. Most of its customers are connected to two utilities, GW-Santa Cruz and GW-Palo Verde, which sit within one service area. The Arizona Corporation Commission (ACC) regulates its services and sets what it can charge.

    Growth depends on new homes being built. It has large obligations under its infrastructure financing agreements (ICFAs), and the funds from them depend on development work by developers it does not control.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its profits depend on getting the Arizona Corporation Commission to let it recover its costs through rates.
    • New or stricter regulatory standards could raise its compliance and operating costs.
    • Funds from its developer agreements depend on building activity by developers it does not control.
    • Expanding into new service areas needs regulatory approval that it may not get.
    • Governments could take its water systems by condemnation and pay less than their market value.
    From Global Water Resources's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 4, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
