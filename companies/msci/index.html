    What MSCI does

    MSCI sells data, analytics and indexes to investors. An index is a measured basket of securities, used to track a market and to design funds such as exchange-traded funds (ETFs). Its products and services include indexes, tools for building portfolios and managing risk, sustainability and climate solutions, and data on private assets such as private equity and real estate.

    Its clients include asset managers, banks and brokerages, pension funds and other asset owners, hedge funds, wealth managers and insurers. It served about 6,800 clients in more than 100 countries at the end of 2025. Part of its revenue comes from fees based on assets in funds that track its indexes.

    One client is large: BlackRock accounted for 10.8% of MSCI's consolidated operating revenues in 2025. Of the revenues from BlackRock, 96.5% came from fees based on the assets in BlackRock's ETFs and similar products linked to MSCI indexes. MSCI says it uses AI to collect and check its data.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Errors or defects in its products that could bring added costs or liability.
    • Reputational and credibility concerns about its indexes and data.
    • Clients cancelling or reducing what they buy.
    • Clients becoming more self-sufficient, which could reduce demand for its products.
    • Complex relationships with clients that are also competitors, investors or distributors.
    From MSCI's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 6, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
