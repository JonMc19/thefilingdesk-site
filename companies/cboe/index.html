    What Cboe Global Markets does

    Cboe Global Markets runs exchanges and related businesses where people trade options, stocks, futures and currencies. It operates in North America, Europe and Asia-Pacific. It says it runs the largest US options exchange and the third largest US equities exchange operator, and it also owns a European equities exchange, a European clearinghouse, a US block-trading system and exchanges in Australia and Canada.

    Cboe reports five segments: Options, North American Equities, Europe and Asia Pacific, Futures, and Global FX. It earns much of its money from trading and clearing fees, and from selling market data, index licenses and access and capacity services. Some products, such as certain index options and futures, trade only on Cboe.

    In 2025 Cboe reviewed its global business. It began winding down its Japanese equities business, started a sales process for its Australian and Canadian businesses, and ended its US and European corporate listings efforts. After the end of 2025 it began winding down its European derivatives exchange.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Losing its right to list and trade certain index options and futures exclusively.
    • Falling trading volumes, since much of its revenue comes from transaction and clearing fees.
    • Price competition and special pricing offers made to win market share.
    • Falling market data and access fees if its market share or volumes decline.
    • Reliance on third parties and on technology that could suffer security breaches.
    From Cboe Global Markets's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 20, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
