    What Neighborhood Intelligence does

    The company, which called itself Bed Bath & Beyond, Inc. in this annual report, is an online retailer for the home. It owns the Bed Bath & Beyond, Overstock and buybuy BABY brands, among others, and sells furniture, bedding, bath items, rugs, cookware, décor, jewelry and more to customers in the United States through its websites and mobile apps. It also sells add-on services such as warranties, shipping insurance and installation.

    Most of its retail sales are filled by its network of partners, such as manufacturers, distributors and suppliers, who get access to its customers and its marketing, order fulfillment and customer service. It also offers a rewards membership program and a private label credit card. It describes its logistics as built for furniture and home furnishings.

    In 2025 it owned about 40% of The Brand House Collective, formerly Kirkland's, which opened Bed Bath & Beyond brand stores under a license. The two companies have a pending merger agreement expected to close in the first half of 2026. It had about 389 full-time employees at the end of 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Relying on third-party companies for functions critical to the business.
    • Failing to spot or answer changing customer tastes, or being outspent on marketing by competitors.
    • Tariffs and other measures that raise prices or limit access to products.
    • A changing business model and brands, including the planned merger with The Brand House Collective.
    • If it isn't profitable, needing to raise capital, borrow or sell assets to stay in business.
    From Neighborhood Intelligence's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 24, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
