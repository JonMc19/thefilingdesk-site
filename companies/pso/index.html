    What Pearson does

    Pearson is a British learning company. Its stated purpose is helping people realize the life they imagine through learning. It sells learning materials, online courses, tests and qualifications to schools, universities, employers and individual learners around the world.

    Its main businesses include Assessment & Qualifications, Higher Education, Virtual Learning and English Language Learning. Higher Education serves post-secondary and early-careers learners and also reaches into K12 education through programs such as AP and Dual Enrolment. Virtual Learning delivers online learning for K12 students through Partner Schools and District Partnerships. English Language Learning helps learners build and prove their English skills. Pearson also has an Enterprise Learning and Skills business, which includes its IT Pro business that was previously within Higher Education.

    Where the buyer is a school or institution, it will typically use educational funding to buy Pearson's materials or assessments. Pearson says it is adding artificial intelligence (AI) features to its offerings, and it has large strategic partnerships with technology and other companies. It had an average of 17,062 employees in 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Changes in government policy and regulation in the many countries where it works.
    • The cost of meeting new rules on its use of artificial intelligence in its products.
    • Failing to deliver the right products and services, or to respond to competitive threats and government concerns.
    • Not protecting its intellectual property well enough, which could weaken its position.
    • Failing to deliver the expected benefits from its large strategic partnerships and from moving into new markets.
    From Pearson's annual report on Form 20-F, filed March 13, 2026, in our words; the 20-F has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
