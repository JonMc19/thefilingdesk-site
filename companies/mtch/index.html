    What Match Group does

    Match Group runs dating and social connection apps. Its brands include Tinder, Hinge, Match, Meetic, OkCupid, Pairs, Plenty Of Fish, Azar and BLK. Each aims at different tastes and relationship goals, from casual discovery to serious relationships. Tinder, launched in 2012, uses its patented Swipe feature. Hinge is aimed at people looking for intentional, relationship-oriented connections.

    Match Group makes money mainly from its users. Tinder uses a freemium model: core features are free, and users pay for subscriptions or for extras such as Super Likes and Boosts, which can be bought one at a time. Tinder's subscription tiers are Plus, Gold and Platinum. Its mobile apps are almost exclusively accessed through the Apple App Store and Google Play Store, which charge fees. It reports revenue in segments by brand group, including Tinder, Hinge, Evergreen & Emerging and MG Asia.

    Azar, a video chat app, had direct revenue of $155.8 million in 2025, and 76% of it came through Apple's App Store. Apple removed Azar from the App Store on February 22, 2026. In 2025 Match Group started a company-wide effort to cut costs, and its headcount fell by approximately 12%.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Losing paying users, since Tinder's number of payers has been declining.
    • Depending on the Apple App Store and Google Play Store, which can change their rules and fees.
    • Apple's removal of the Azar app from the App Store, which could cut its Azar revenue.
    • Strong competition, and the risk that its brands lose appeal with users.
    • Laws and regulation that apply directly to online dating services.
    From Match Group's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
