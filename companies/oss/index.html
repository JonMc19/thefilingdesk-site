    What One Stop Systems does

    One Stop Systems (OSS) designs and makes rugged, high-performance computers, high-speed switch fabrics and storage systems for the "edge," meaning computing done on the machine or at the site where data is collected, not in a distant data center. Its systems handle artificial intelligence, sensor processing and autonomy tasks in places that are moving or harsh.

    It sells to makers of equipment and platforms in autonomous vehicles, medical, industrial, aerospace and defense markets. Examples include planes, drones, trucks, ships, submarines and mobile command posts. Its products must be small and light and survive shocks, vibration, heat, dust and water, and they use the latest parts from companies such as NVIDIA, AMD and Intel.

    OSS says it is focusing on rugged edge computing and on winning multi-year contracts, in both commercial and military markets. It was founded in 1998 and became a Delaware corporation in 2017 when it went public. It had about 57 employees at the end of 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Changes in U.S. government priorities, procurement rules, or cuts and delays in defense spending.
    • Relying on a limited number of customers for a significant portion of its sales.
    • Relying on a limited number of parts suppliers, and supply chain disruptions.
    • A market for its products that may not develop as expected.
    • Customers that are also competitors, along with new entrants in its markets.
    From One Stop Systems's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 18, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
