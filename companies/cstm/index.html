    What Constellium does

    Constellium is a French-based maker of rolled and extruded aluminum products. It turns aluminum into semi-finished and sometimes finished alloy products for aerospace, space, defense, packaging, automotive, commercial transportation and general industrial customers. It says its products need long and complex qualification and often close work with customers, and that they earn higher margins than plain commodity aluminum.

    It prices its products to include the cost of the metal it buys and hedges any remaining exposure, so that it is not exposed to the aluminum price. It runs 24 manufacturing plants, 3 research and development centers and 3 administrative centers. One of its segments, Aerospace and Transportation, operates five facilities in the United States, France and Switzerland. Another, Packaging and Automotive Rolled Products, makes customized rolled products.

    Constellium employed about 11,500 people at the end of 2025, plus about 500 temporary workers. It stopped qualifying as a foreign private issuer in 2025, so it now files the annual and quarterly reports that U.S. companies file.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competition from larger producers, new competitors and lower-cost producers, which could cut its market share and prices.
    • Customers switching from aluminum to steel, glass, plastics or composite materials.
    • Trade measures such as tariffs and quotas, and unstable politics, in the many countries where it does business.
    • Other risks of global operations, including currency moves, inflation and sanctions.
    From Constellium's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 25, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Piotroski F-score: how many of nine simple tests of profitability, funding and efficiency the company passed in the year (Piotroski, 2000); 8 or 9 is strong, 0 to 2 weak.
