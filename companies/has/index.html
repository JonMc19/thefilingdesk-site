    What Hasbro does

    Hasbro makes toys and games and licenses its brands to other companies. Its own brands include Magic: The Gathering, Monopoly, Play-Doh, Transformers, Dungeons & Dragons, Nerf and Peppa Pig. It also sells products based on brands it licenses from others, such as Marvel and Star Wars, which are owned by Disney. Its products range from board and trading card games to action figures, dolls, preschool toys and plush. Other companies pay Hasbro to use its brands in mobile and video games, clothing, theme parks and other products and experiences.

    Hasbro reports three segments. Wizards of the Coast and Digital Gaming develops trading card, role-playing and digital games. Consumer Products sells toys and games and licenses Hasbro brands for consumer products. Entertainment produces film, television and other content based on Hasbro brands. Hasbro sells to retailers, distributors and wholesalers, and directly to fans through sites such as Hasbro PULSE and Secret Lair. In 2025 its top five customers made up approximately 35% of net revenues; Amazon accounted for 11% and Walmart for 9%. It says its main competitors are game and toy companies and digital gaming companies, and sometimes large retailers' own private-label products.

    Most of Hasbro's products are made by outside manufacturers in China, Vietnam, India, Japan, Belgium, the United States, Mexico and Indonesia. It employed approximately 4,520 people worldwide in 2025, about 58% of them in North America. Hasbro says its cost-cutting program had delivered almost $800 million of gross savings through 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to develop, publish and sell successful digital games.
    • Children's and fans' tastes changing quickly, making it hard to keep its products popular.
    • Licensed brands selling too little to cover the minimum royalties it has guaranteed, or licenses not being renewed.
    • Tariffs that could raise its costs, and its reliance on outside manufacturers.
    • Its concentrated customer base, where trouble at major customers or changes in how they buy and promote could hit it hard.
    From Hasbro's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 25, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Income statement

    From fiscal 2016 to 2024, earnings per share fell 5.5% a year and net income 4.4% a year, as new shares raised the share count by 10% and diluted each share's earnings. Hasbro then lost money in fiscal 2025.
