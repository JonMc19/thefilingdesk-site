    What Grindr does

    Grindr runs a social networking app mainly for gay, bisexual, transgender and queer adults. Using their approximate location, people can find others nearby or anywhere, chat and share photos, whether they want dates, relationships, friends or local tips. The app is free, with paid subscriptions and add-ons for more features. In 2025 it averaged 15.0 million monthly active users in more than 190 countries and territories, and 1.3 million paying users.

    Revenue was $439.9 million in 2025. Payments from users for subscriptions and add-ons made up 83.3% of it, and advertising the other 16.7%. International revenue was 42.2% of the total. Grindr says it competes for users with larger companies and established social media firms, and it also sees possible competition from AI companion apps.

    Grindr had 165 employees at the end of 2025, 160 of them full-time. In May 2025 it launched Woodwork, a telehealth subscription that helps eligible patients get certain treatments and prescription drugs, run with a clinical partner, OpenLoop. It is also looking at new lines such as travel and events.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Losing users or failing to add new ones, or failing to turn more of them into paying users.
    • Harm to its brand from bad press or from illegal or harmful actions by people using the app.
    • Concerns about how it handles users' personal information, which could drive users away.
    • Hostile laws and attitudes toward LGBTQ people in some countries that could limit where it can grow.
    • Its reliance on mobile operating systems and app stores that it does not control.
    From Grindr's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 2, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
