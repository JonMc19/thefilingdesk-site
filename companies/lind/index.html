    What Lindblad Expeditions does

    Lindblad Expeditions sells adventure travel to remote places. Its guests travel on small expedition ships to destinations such as Antarctica, the Arctic, the Galápagos Islands and Alaska, or on land-based tours, safaris and cycling trips. Its largest group of customers is affluent people in the U.S. aged 50 or older.

    It reports two segments. The Lindblad segment runs ship-based expeditions. It operates 12 owned expedition vessels plus 10 seasonal chartered ships, with trips of five to over 30 days to over 40 destinations. The Land Experiences segment covers brands such as Natural Habitat, Off the Beaten Path, DuVine, Classic Journeys and Thomson Safaris, which offer small-group trips centered on nature and culture. Revenue comes from the trips it sells.

    Its ships carry the National Geographic name under a long-running partnership that now runs through 2040, and National Geographic experts join the expeditions. The company says it holds premium prices rather than discounting as most cruise lines do, and that it competes with other vacation options.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Wars, health scares, accidents or bad publicity that make people less willing to travel.
    • Losing its brand license agreement with National Geographic.
    • Ship repair delays, shipyard problems or large spending needs to maintain and expand its fleet.
    • A large debt load it must pay from cash flow that depends on factors outside its control.
    • Restrictions on access to places it visits, such as the Galápagos or polar areas.
