    What Immersion does

    Immersion runs two very different businesses. The first is a licensing business built on haptics, the technology behind touch feedback such as vibrations in phones and game controllers. It holds just under 300 issued or pending patents and licenses them to companies that build the technology into their own branded products. Its main markets are mobile devices, gaming and cars, and it sees possible uses in virtual and augmented reality and wearables.

    The second is Barnes & Noble Education, which Immersion took control of in June 2024. In that deal Immersion acquired 42% of its shares and appointed most of its board. Barnes & Noble Education says it is one of the largest contract operators of physical and virtual college and K-12 bookstores in the United States, and also one of the largest textbook wholesalers. It operates 1,116 physical and virtual bookstores. Immersion reports these as two segments.

    Immersion's licensing earns royalties and license fees, with a notable share from makers of gaming peripherals. The bookstore business is people-heavy: about 3,613 domestic employees, plus thousands of temporary and seasonal staff in peak periods.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to renew or sign new patent licenses on good terms, which would cut royalty revenue.
    • Customers discontinuing products that use its technology, or gaming makers cutting back.
    • Acquisitions, including Barnes & Noble Education, bring integration and management risks.
    • Tariffs and trade restrictions, and an uncertain economy, could reduce revenue.
    • Failing to file its periodic reports on time could limit its access to capital markets.
    From Immersion's annual report on Form 10-K, filed July 24, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
