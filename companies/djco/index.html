    What Daily Journal does

    Daily Journal has two parts. Its Traditional Business publishes ten newspapers and websites covering California and Arizona news, with the Los Angeles Daily Journal and San Francisco Daily Journal aimed at lawyers and judges. It also produces specialized information publications and acts as a newspaper representative for public notice advertising, the legal notices that must be published in newspapers.

    Its subsidiary Journal Technologies sells case management software to courts, prosecutor and public defender offices, probation departments and other justice agencies, as well as city and county governments. The software is licensed or subscribed to in about 37 states and abroad, and handles tasks such as e-filing and online payment of traffic citations.

    The papers earn money from subscriptions and from commercial and public notice advertising. Journal Technologies earns license, maintenance and support fees, hosting fees and consulting fees for installation and training. Its U.S. operations are based in California and Utah, and it also works in Australia and Canada.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Changes in the law that require or allow public notice advertising in newspapers.
    • Strong competition and falling commercial advertising and circulation revenue in the newspaper business, as readers move online.
    • Higher postage and paper costs.
    • Dependence of Journal Technologies on keeping its case management software up to date, against strong competitors.
    • Customers that are government bodies, which bring unique issues and risks, including payment timing.
    From Daily Journal's annual report on Form 10-K, filed December 29, 2025, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Piotroski F-score: how many of nine simple tests of profitability, funding and efficiency the company passed in the year (Piotroski, 2000); 8 or 9 is strong, 0 to 2 weak.
