    What Mastercard does

    Mastercard runs a global payments network. When someone pays with a Mastercard-branded card or another supported payment method, the network handles the transaction: it authorizes the payment, clears it and settles it between the banks and the merchant. Its customers are banks and other financial institutions, merchants, governments, digital partners and businesses. It sells under the Mastercard, Maestro and Cirrus brands.

    The 10-K reports net revenue of $32.8B for 2025. Besides switching payments, Mastercard sells services built around them, such as fraud and security tools, identity checks, data and market insights, and services that help issuers sign up and engage cardholders. It also offers account-to-account payments through automated clearing house (ACH) networks and money-transfer products under the name Mastercard Move.

    Mastercard names three priorities: consumer payments, commercial and new payment flows, and services. It says it competes worldwide with payment networks such as Visa, American Express, JCB, China UnionPay and Discover, and notes that banks can issue both Mastercard products and competing ones.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Laws and rules for the payments industry, including limits on card fees and merchant surcharges, that could cut its revenue.
    • Lawsuits and regulatory cases over card fees and competition that could lead to costs or limits on how it operates.
    • Strong competition and pricing pressure from other networks and new ways to pay that bypass cards.
    • Losing business from one or more of its largest customers, or those customers merging or siding with rivals.
    • Its role as guarantor of payments, which exposes it to losses if a customer cannot settle.
    From Mastercard's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 11, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
