    What Hallador Energy does

    Hallador Energy is an independent power producer and fuel company with operations mainly in Indiana. It works across several stages of the energy chain, from electricity capacity to coal. It reports two segments, Electric Operations and Coal Operations.

    Hallador Power owns and runs the Merom Power Plant, a coal-fired station with two steam turbine generators that started operating in 1982 and 1983. It sells accredited capacity (a guarantee that power will be available when needed) and wholesale electricity to utilities and other market participants in the Midcontinent Independent System Operator (MISO) region, mostly under power purchase agreements and also on the spot market.

    Its other subsidiary, Sunrise Coal, mines coal in Indiana and sells it to power plants in the Midwest and Southeast. Hallador also holds half interests in two other companies, Sunrise Energy and Oaktown Gas.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Customers may not honor, extend or renew their existing contracts.
    • Its results are affected by swings in electric power and coal prices.
    • Its efforts to sell accredited capacity to long-term customers may not succeed.
    • The Merom power plant faces operating risks, and growth projects may need financing it cannot get.
    • It may not recover its investments in power and mining assets and may have to record impairment charges.
    From Hallador Energy's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
