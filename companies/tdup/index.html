    What ThredUp does

    ThredUp runs an online resale marketplace for secondhand clothing, shoes and accessories. Buyers in the United States browse and buy used items, from value brands to luxury ones. Sellers order a Clean Out Bag, fill it and send it back with a prepaid label. ThredUp then sorts, photographs, prices and lists the items that it accepts.

    Because nearly every item is one of a kind, the company tracks each one as its own stock unit. It uses its own software, automation and data analysis to handle this, and to set prices, seller payouts and which items to accept. At the end of 2025 it ran distribution centers in Arizona, Georgia, Pennsylvania and Texas.

    It also sells a service called Resale-as-a-Service to brands and retailers, so they can offer their own customers a closet clean-out service or a resale shop. At the end of 2025 it had 2,132 employees and professional contractors, most of them working in its distribution centers.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Attracting new buyers and keeping existing ones.
    • Getting enough good-quality secondhand items from sellers.
    • Costs and risks in sourcing, sorting, storing and shipping its items.
    • Dependence on consumer discretionary spending, which can fall in a downturn.
    • Competition from established companies and new entrants in resale.
    From ThredUp's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 2, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
