    What Warby Parker does

    Warby Parker designs eyeglasses and sunglasses at its New York City headquarters and sells them straight to customers, with no middleman, online and in its own stores. It has expanded to eye exams, contact lenses and lens add-ons. Its pricing is simple, with glasses starting at $95 including prescription lenses. It had 323 stores at the end of 2025 and over 4,000 employees.

    It makes money by selling eyewear and contacts, and through eye exams at its stores. It calls its approach omnichannel: customers may start online, using tools like Virtual Try-On or a telehealth app that lets eligible patients renew a prescription at home, and then visit a store, or the other way round. It runs its own optical labs and also uses outside manufacturers. It says it competes in a highly competitive optical industry.

    Warby Parker is a public benefit corporation, and for every pair of glasses or sunglasses it sells, it says a pair is given to someone in need. It also works with vision insurance companies and other payors.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its retail growth and expansion into eye care may not succeed.
    • It has a history of losses and may not stay profitable.
    • It may be unable to recruit and keep the optometrists and opticians its stores need.
    • It must keep its relationships with vision insurers and other third-party payors.
    • Supply chain disruptions, and competition in the optical industry.
    From Warby Parker's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
