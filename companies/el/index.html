    What Estée Lauder Companies does

    Estée Lauder makes and sells skin care, makeup, fragrance and hair care products under more than 20 luxury and prestige brands. Its products reach shoppers in approximately 150 countries and territories. The 10-K lists moisturizers, serums and sun care in skin care, and foundations, lipsticks and mascaras in makeup, along with perfumes, colognes and related items, shampoos and hair color. An "other" group includes royalty income from licensing the TOM FORD trademark.

    The company operates mainly as a wholesaler. It sells through department stores, duty-free retailers, specialty retailers, online-only sellers, perfumeries, pharmacies, salons and spas, both in stores and online. It also runs a direct-to-consumer business through its own stores, its brands' websites and third-party online platforms.

    In February 2025 it began a strategy it calls Beauty Reimagined, which includes a simpler operating model with fewer layers of management. The Lauder family, which has controlled the company since it was founded in 1946, and some of whom are directors, officers or employees, owns shares with approximately 82% of the voting power. The 10-K says the beauty business is highly competitive.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competition in the beauty business is intense, and it may not keep up with changing consumer tastes.
    • It may not achieve its long-term strategy, and acquisitions or sales of businesses add risk.
    • Weak consumer spending on discretionary items in a downturn or business disruption.
    • Problems with the quality, effectiveness or safety of its products.
    • Risks from operating around the world, and supply chain disruptions.
