    What Iron Mountain does

    Iron Mountain stores and manages information and physical assets for other organizations. It began in 1951 storing business records in an underground facility near Hudson, New York. Today it serves more than 240,000 customers in 61 countries, including about 95% of the Fortune 1000, in fields such as legal, financial, healthcare, technology, insurance, life sciences and government.

    Its services cover records and information management, digital transformation, information security, data centers and asset lifecycle management (handling and recycling used IT equipment). It reports a Global Records and Information Management segment, which mainly stores physical records, and a Global Data Center segment, which provides data center space and capacity for customers' IT equipment, including capacity for large cloud customers. Asset lifecycle management and Fine Arts are in Corporate and Other.

    Iron Mountain is organized as a real estate investment trust (REIT), a status it has held since the tax year 2014. It says it aims to grow volume and revenue in physical storage, expand its digital services, and add data center capacity, and that it will seek to expand in markets such as Central and Eastern Europe, Latin America, Asia, the Middle East and Africa.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to carry out its strategic growth plan.
    • Less active stored records and tapes, which could slow service revenue and profit growth.
    • Customers changing how they store records, which could cut its storage revenue.
    • Attacks on its IT systems that could damage its reputation and cost it revenue.
    • Difficulty integrating acquired businesses, and relying on joint ventures and co-investment partners.
    From Iron Mountain's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
