    What Wendy's does

    Wendy's operates and franchises quick-service restaurants that sell hamburgers and other fast food. It says it is the second largest company in the U.S. hamburger sandwich segment by traffic and dollar share, with 7,397 restaurants in the U.S. and 38 other countries and U.S. territories at the end of 2025.

    Nearly all restaurants are run by franchisees, who are independent owners. Of the 5,969 U.S. restaurants, 5,546 were run by franchisees and 423 by Wendy's itself. Franchisees pay a monthly royalty of 4.0% of sales, plus a fee for each new restaurant. The company reports three segments: Wendy's U.S., Wendy's International and Global Real Estate & Development.

    Wendy's competes with other restaurant chains, grocery stores, food delivery services and other places that sell food. It announced a plan called Project Fresh in 2025, with four parts: brand revitalization, operational excellence, system optimization and capital allocation.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competition from other restaurants and grocery stores, and a poor customer experience.
    • Its mostly franchised model, which depends on franchisees' health and decisions.
    • Food safety events or health concerns that hurt its brand.
    • Whether Project Fresh and its other strategic plans succeed.
    • A large amount of debt, and rising labor and commodity costs.
    From Wendy's's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 23, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
