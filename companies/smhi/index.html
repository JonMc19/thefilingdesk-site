    What SEACOR Marine does

    SEACOR Marine provides ships and marine support services to offshore energy facilities around the world. Its customers are mainly oil and gas companies, and it also works for offshore wind farm projects. Its vessels carry cargo and people to offshore installations, support production and storage platforms, help with construction, well work-over, wind farm installation and decommissioning, and carry underwater equipment used in drilling and well repair. They also provide emergency response and housing for technicians.

    The fleet has several types of vessel. Platform supply vessels deliver cargo, drilling fluids, fuel and water to rigs and platforms. Fast support vessels have aluminum hulls and move cargo and passengers to offshore sites at higher speeds than traditional steel vessels. Liftboats are a third class that it also operates. At the end of 2025 it owned 44 vessels. It also had two new platform supply vessels under construction in China.

    It earns money by chartering its vessels to customers. It also operates vessels owned by others for a fee. It competes in the offshore marine service industry, where oversupply of vessels can pressure rates.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Falling oil and gas prices or lower offshore drilling could reduce demand for its vessels.
    • Oversupply of vessels, which could push down charter rates.
    • It relies on several customers for a significant share of revenue, and losing one could hurt.
    • Debt and credit facility limits that restrict its operating and financial flexibility.
    • Hazards of running vessels, and delays or cost overruns in building, repairing or maintaining them.
