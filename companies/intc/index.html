    What Intel does

    Intel designs and makes processors, the main chips that run personal computers and the servers in data centers, along with AI chips, networking chips and custom chips. It sells mostly to computer makers, contract manufacturers that build computers for other brands, and cloud computing companies, which put its chips in products used by consumers, businesses and governments. It makes most of its own chips in its own factories.

    Revenue was $52.9 billion in 2025. Its products business, made up of chips for PCs (the Client Computing Group) and for data centers and AI, brought in $49.1 billion. Its third segment, Intel Foundry, develops manufacturing processes and makes chips; nearly all of its work is for Intel itself, but it is trying to win outside customers. Its three largest customers made up 43% of revenue. Intel names AMD as its main rival in PC and server chips, Apple, Qualcomm and MediaTek in PC chips, Nvidia in AI chips, and TSMC and Samsung in chipmaking for others.

    2025 brought big changes. The US government took a significant stake in the company in August, SoftBank and Nvidia bought new shares, and Intel sold 51% of its Altera chip unit. It also owns most of Mobileye, which makes driver-assistance systems. Intel cut its core workforce by about 15% under a restructuring plan.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Heavy, long-term spending on research and factories that may not pay off.
    • It may pause or stop developing its next manufacturing process, Intel 14A, if it can't win a major outside customer.
    • Risks tied to the US government's large ownership stake and its changed CHIPS Act grant agreement.
    • Strong competition, and data center spending shifting to the AI chips its rivals sell.
    • Its reliance on a few large customers and on a complex global supply chain.
    From Intel's annual report on Form 10-K, filed January 23, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Income statement

    From fiscal 2016 to 2023, earnings per share fell 21.2% a year and net income 22.8% a year, while buybacks cut the share count by 14%. Intel then lost money in fiscal 2024 and 2025.
