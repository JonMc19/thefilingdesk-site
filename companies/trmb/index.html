    What Trimble does

    Trimble sells software, hardware and services to the construction, geospatial (mapping and surveying), utilities and transportation industries. Its customers include architects and engineers, contractors, surveyors, asset owners, energy and utility companies, shippers and carriers, and governments. It says its strategy centers on two cloud platforms, one for construction and one for transportation and logistics.

    It reports three segments. Architects, Engineers, Construction and Owners (AECO) serves design, engineering and construction organizations, mostly with software. Field Systems sells software and hardware products and services to commercial and government users, mainly through independent distributors. Transportation and Logistics sells tools to shippers, carriers and drivers.

    Trimble sells its software as subscriptions, term licenses or perpetual licenses, either hosted online or installed on a customer's own systems. It says software and cloud services are a growing part of its business. It competes in AECO with companies that make software for the construction process. It has operations worldwide, and it keeps a joint venture with AGCO from businesses it divested.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Operating globally exposes it to political, regulatory and economic risks in many countries.
    • Supply chain problems and long lead times for components.
    • Material weaknesses in its internal control over financial reporting that it must fix.
    • Changes in its software and subscription business that could hurt revenue.
    • Dependence on satellite positioning (GPS and GNSS) and radio spectrum that could be degraded or restricted.
    From Trimble's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 25, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
