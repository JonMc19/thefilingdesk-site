    What Aspen Aerogels does

    Aspen Aerogels makes aerogel, a very light insulating material. It sells two main kinds of products: thermal barriers that sit between battery cells in electric vehicles and energy storage systems to slow the spread of a battery fire, and insulation blankets for refineries, chemical plants, liquefied natural gas facilities and power plants.

    The electric vehicle market and the energy industrial insulation market are its two principal markets. It has production contracts with General Motors, which can end them at any time, and with Toyota, Scania, Audi, Volvo Truck and others. General Motors was 59% of total revenue in 2025. Its insulation is sold through distributors, contractors and manufacturers of equipment. It says its products cost more than traditional insulation.

    Aspen Aerogels competes with makers of other aerogel products, such as Armacell and Alkegen, and with traditional insulation makers. It had 854 full-time employees at the end of 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Depending on General Motors for most of its revenue, with a contract General Motors can end at any time.
    • Its electric vehicle business depends on carmakers' plans and on consumers buying electric vehicles.
    • Relying on outside factories in China to supply part of its aerogel.
    • Needing more money for growth and keeping to the terms of its credit agreement.
    • Competition from other aerogel and traditional insulation makers, and high prices for its products.
    From Aspen Aerogels's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 13, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
