    What News does

    News Corp is a media and information company. It owns The Wall Street Journal, Barron's and other Dow Jones news and data products, the HarperCollins book publisher, newspapers such as The Times, The Sun and The Australian, the property sites realestate.com.au and Realtor.com, and the radio brand talkSPORT. It operates mainly in the United States, Australia and the United Kingdom.

    The company says its revenue comes from subscriptions, circulation sales, advertising, sales of real estate listing products, licensing fees and other consumer product sales. It reports five segments: Dow Jones, Digital Real Estate Services, Book Publishing, News Media and Other, which holds corporate costs and costs of the U.K. newspaper legal matters. At Dow Jones, digital products made up 84% of segment revenues in fiscal 2026. News Corp also licenses its content to technology companies and AI platforms.

    As of June 30, 2026, it had approximately 21,700 employees: about 7,900 in the United States, 3,900 in the United Kingdom and 5,600 in Australia.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competition from other media, and its ability to keep up with new technology and changes in how people read, watch and shop.
    • A drop in advertising spending, or changes in the online advertising market that cut its advertising revenue.
    • Its dependence on a single or limited number of suppliers for some products, services, data and information.
    • Developments in AI, including its own use of AI, which could harm its business or reputation.
    • Higher costs to print and distribute books and newspapers, or disruption of printing and distribution.
    From News's annual report on Form 10-K, filed August 7, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
