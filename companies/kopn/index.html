    What Kopin does

    Kopin makes microdisplays, which are very small screens, and the optics, electronics and housings that go with them. Its products serve defense, enterprise, industrial, consumer and medical customers. Its display types include liquid crystal, liquid crystal on silicon, OLED and an emerging MicroLED type. Kopin was incorporated in 1984 and is based in Westborough, Massachusetts.

    It earns revenue by selling displays and complete subsystems and by performing paid research and development work. Its products go into items such as weapon-mounted thermal sights, spatial computing devices and medical headsets. Kopin has supplied microdisplays and subsystems to the U.S. Department of War for many years, for soldier-worn systems and aircraft, and it is working on products for armored vehicles and soldier-carried missile systems.

    In 2025 Kopin received a government award to research and develop color MicroLED technology. The company says its strategy is to move from selling single display parts to complete, higher-value solutions, including headsets and subsystems. It also says it is developing a fifth-generation display it calls NeuralDisplay.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A history of losses and negative cash flow, and a need to raise money that may not be available on good terms.
    • Depending on defense contracts and government funding, which may change or stop shipping.
    • Being unable to get all the components it needs, which could halt production.
    • Its new products, such as MicroLED and NeuralDisplay, may not succeed.
    • Exchange rate changes and the risks of selling in many countries.
