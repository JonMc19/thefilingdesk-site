    What BJ's Wholesale Club does

    BJ's Wholesale Club runs membership warehouse clubs, mainly in the eastern half of the United States. Shoppers pay an annual fee to buy groceries, fresh food, general merchandise and gasoline. At the end of fiscal 2025 it had 263 large-format warehouse clubs and 199 gas stations in 21 states. BJ's says it pioneered the warehouse club model in New England in 1984, and that it has far more clubs there than its closest warehouse club competitor.

    Membership fees are a core part of how BJ's makes money. A Club membership costs generally $60 a year and a Club+ membership generally $120. Membership fee income was $499.8 million in fiscal 2025, and it has more than 8 million paying members. The rest of its revenue comes from selling merchandise and gasoline. Its two private label brands, Wellsley Farms and Berkley Jensen, were approximately 27% of net sales excluding gasoline.

    BJ's buys most of its merchandise directly from manufacturers and moves it through its own distribution centers to the clubs. Members can also shop online and through its mobile app, with pickup in the club, curbside, same-day or ship-to-home delivery, and through the DoorDash and Instacart marketplaces. Its competitors include supermarkets, supercenters, online retailers and other warehouse clubs.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Harm to its relationship with members, since it depends on a large and loyal membership paying annual fees.
    • Weaker consumer spending when the economy slows.
    • Vendors not supplying quality merchandise at the right time and price, and distribution disruptions.
    • Competition, and changes to SNAP food benefit programs that some members use.
    • Changes in its sales of gasoline, which can affect revenue.
    From BJ's Wholesale Club's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
