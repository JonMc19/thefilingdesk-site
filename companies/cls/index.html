    What Celestica does

    Celestica, based in Toronto, designs and makes electronics and equipment for other companies. Its customers include cloud service providers, large data center operators, hardware makers and companies in aerospace and defense, industrial, health technology and capital equipment markets. It started as an IBM manufacturing unit and has served other customers since 1993.

    It has two segments. Connectivity and Cloud Solutions makes networking switches, optical systems, racks, servers and storage products used in data centers, including for AI work. Advanced Technology Solutions serves manufacturers in aerospace and defense, industrial, health technology and capital equipment. In 2025 the first segment's revenue rose 42% to $9.19 billion.

    Celestica also runs a Hardware Platform Solutions business, where it takes part in designing custom hardware for customers such as large cloud companies. It says this business typically earns higher margins than its traditional contract manufacturing, but it needs investment in research and development. In 2025 it was 41% of total revenue. The company says a few customers account for a large share of its business.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Reliance on a limited number of customers and end markets, and their investment cycles.
    • Changes in customer demand that make planning, supply and manufacturing harder.
    • Dependence on third parties for materials, and the risk of holding unsold inventory.
    • Expanding capacity for expected demand that may not arrive as planned.
    • Winning competitive bids to keep its business.
    From Celestica's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
