    What Appian does

    Appian sells process automation software to large companies and governments. Its single platform lets an organization design, automate and improve business processes, using rules engines, links to other systems, document processing, robotic process automation and artificial intelligence. A data fabric layer connects an organization's data without moving it, and process mining tools show where processes stall.

    Appian sells its software almost exclusively as subscriptions. It uses both direct sales and strategic partners, such as consulting and technology firms working on automation projects. It grows revenue by adding customers, adding users within existing customers, and spreading use across more processes and applications.

    Appian says most business uses of AI fall short and that it can place AI inside workflows with controls and guardrails. It cites IDC for the market for business automation platforms, and it competes with other software vendors.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Slower revenue growth, which could threaten its ability to stay profitable.
    • Depending on a single product, so weaker market acceptance of its platform would hurt results.
    • Long and unpredictable sales cycles, especially with large customers.
    • Process automation not being adopted as widely as it expects.
    • Trouble scaling the business quickly enough to meet customers' growing needs.
    From Appian's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 19, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Piotroski F-score: how many of nine simple tests of profitability, funding and efficiency the company passed in the year (Piotroski, 2000); 8 or 9 is strong, 0 to 2 weak. Rule of 40: revenue growth plus free cash flow margin; software investors treat 40% or more as the bar. Altman Z'': working capital, retained earnings, operating income and equity weighed against assets and liabilities (Altman, 1995); Altman puts above 2.6 in the safe zone and below 1.1 in the distress zone. Accruals ratio: net income minus cash from operations, over average total assets; lower means profit is backed by cash.
