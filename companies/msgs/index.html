    What Madison Square Garden Sports does

    Madison Square Garden Sports owns two professional teams, the New York Knicks of the NBA and the New York Rangers of the NHL. It also owns two development league teams: the Hartford Wolf Pack of the American Hockey League and the Westchester Knicks of the NBA G League. It is a Nevada holding company based in New York City, with a fiscal year that ends on June 30.

    The company says a significant portion of its revenue comes from media rights fees, suite rental fees and sponsorship and signage revenue, which are generally contracted on a multi-year basis. Ticket sales and playoff games also add revenue. That contract base softens, but does not remove, the effect of how the teams perform from year to year.

    On February 18, 2026, its board authorized management to explore a possible spin-off that would split the Rangers business from the Knicks business into two publicly traded companies. It formed a subsidiary called MSGS Spinco to hold the Rangers business, and says there is no assurance the spin-off will happen.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its dependence on the popularity and success of the Knicks and Rangers, including playoff runs.
    • Intense and wide-ranging competition for fans' time and money.
    • Decisions by the NBA and NHL that could hurt its business.
    • Basketball and hockey decisions on players, coaches and salaries, and injuries to players.
    • Substantial debt at some subsidiaries, and a possible need for more financing.
    From Madison Square Garden Sports's annual report on Form 10-K, filed August 13, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
