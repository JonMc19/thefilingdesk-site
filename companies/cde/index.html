    What Coeur Mining does

    Coeur Mining mines and sells gold and silver in the United States, Canada and Mexico. It says it aims to be a diversified, growing precious metals producer in mining-friendly places. It sells its metal as concentrates and doré (a mix of gold and silver) to smelters and refiners.

    It operates five mines, each reported as a segment. Las Chispas, a silver-gold mine in Sonora, Mexico, was acquired in early 2025. Palmarejo is a silver-gold complex in Chihuahua, Mexico. Rochester is an open pit silver-gold mine in Nevada. Kensington is an underground gold mine near Juneau, Alaska. Wharf is an open pit gold mine in South Dakota. It also runs Silvertip, a silver-zinc-lead exploration project in Canada.

    What it earns depends on how much it mines and on metal prices. Its plans for growth rest on exploration and on expanding mines or buying new ones.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Swings in metal prices, which also affect the price of its securities.
    • Mining hazards, some not fully covered by insurance.
    • Growth that depends on exploring, expanding and buying mines, which may not pay off.
    • Political and social risks in the countries where it operates, including Mexico.
    • Delays or disruptions at the smelters and refiners that buy its output.
    From Coeur Mining's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 18, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
