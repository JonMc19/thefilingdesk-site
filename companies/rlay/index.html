    What Relay Therapeutics does

    Relay Therapeutics is a clinical-stage drug developer. It has no approved products and no product sales. It develops small-molecule medicines, meaning drugs in pill form, for cancer and for genetic diseases.

    Its approach rests on a platform it calls Dynamo, which combines computer modeling and lab experiments to study how proteins move. The company says this helps it design drugs for protein targets that were hard or impossible to hit with older methods. It chooses targets where there is clear evidence linking a protein to a disease and where a diagnostic test can identify the patients who would benefit.

    Its lead candidate is zovegalisib, also called RLY-2608. It is an inhibitor of a protein called PI3K alpha, and the company describes it as the first of its kind to block only certain mutated forms of the protein. Its main trial is in breast cancer and other solid tumors, where it is being tested with the drug fulvestrant and, in a three-drug combination, with a drug candidate from Pfizer. Relay pays for its research with money it raises and with deals with partners. It had 192 full-time employees at the end of 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It has never completed a large pivotal clinical trial, and its drug candidates may fail in trials.
    • Its drugs use a new approach, which may bring added costs, regulatory hurdles or unexpected side effects.
    • Testing its drugs in combination with other cancer treatments makes side effects harder to predict.
    • Delays in enrolling patients or in getting regulatory approvals could hold up its drug candidates.
    • It faces strong competition, and insurers may not cover or reimburse its drugs if approved.
