    What ExxonMobil does

    ExxonMobil explores for and produces crude oil and natural gas. It also makes, trades, transports and sells crude oil, natural gas, petroleum products, petrochemicals and specialty products. Its divisions and affiliates, which go by names including Exxon, Esso, Mobil and XTO, operate or market products in the United States and most other countries. It is also pursuing lower-emission businesses, such as carbon capture and storage, hydrogen and ammonia, lower-emission fuels, carbon materials, low-carbon data centers and lithium.

    ExxonMobil reports four segments. Upstream explores for and produces oil and gas. Energy Products, Chemical Products and Specialty Products, together called Product Solutions, make and sell petroleum products and petrochemicals. Its Low Carbon Solutions business sits in Corporate and Financing until it has a material level of assets and revenue. Its earnings can be significantly affected by oil, gas and petrochemical prices and by refining margins. ExxonMobil says its industries are highly competitive, and that it competes with state-owned companies as well as private firms.

    The company was incorporated in New Jersey in 1882. It had 58 thousand regular employees at the end of 2025; over 59 percent of its global employees are from outside the U.S. It held over 8 thousand active patents at the end of 2025. Its acquisition of Pioneer closed on May 3, 2024. ExxonMobil says record production in the Permian and Guyana raised its Upstream earnings in 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Swings in oil, gas and petrochemical prices and in refining margins, since these are commodity businesses.
    • Government actions such as limits on access to resources, sanctions, new taxes, price controls or seizure of assets.
    • Government efforts to cut greenhouse gas emissions, such as carbon taxes and mandates for electric vehicles.
    • Keeping up its oil and gas output depends on finding new resources and developing them on time and on budget.
    • Its reliance on large, complex, long-term projects that need joint venture partners, governments and suppliers.
    From ExxonMobil's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 18, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
