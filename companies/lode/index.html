    What Comstock does

    Comstock is a Nevada company that is trying to turn waste and under-used natural resources into clean energy and related products. It has two main efforts: recycling solar panels into metals and minerals, and making renewable fuels from waste and woody plants.

    For metals, it runs a solar panel recycling demonstration plant in Nevada and is installing, testing and starting up a larger plant that it describes as the first of its kind at industry scale. For fuels, its subsidiary Bioleum Corporation is working on technology to make renewable fuels from waste, energy crops and woody biomass. Its assets there include two demonstration plants in Wisconsin, two pilot farms for energy crops, and a site in Tulsa, Oklahoma, for a first fully integrated biorefinery.

    It also holds older assets it wants to sell or earn from, including gold and silver mining claims, northern Nevada land and water rights, and some other investments. It describes its work as commercializing technologies, and its risk factors warn that it may never earn significant revenue.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Needing more capital, which may not be available, to continue as a going concern and fund its plans.
    • It may never earn significant revenue from its operations.
    • Trouble managing growth and carrying out its growth strategy on time.
    • A change or repeal of the Renewable Fuel Standard, a federal law that requires use of renewable fuels.
    • Losing federal and state tax incentives for producing or using renewable fuel.
    From Comstock's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 24, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
