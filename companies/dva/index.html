    What DaVita does

    DaVita provides kidney care. Its main business is dialysis, a treatment that does the work of the kidneys for people whose kidneys have failed. It operated a network of 2,657 outpatient dialysis centers in 46 states and the District of Columbia at the end of 2025. It also provides dialysis and administrative services to 585 centers in 14 other countries. Patients also receive care at home, in hospitals and in skilled nursing facilities, and the company says it supports patients from slowing kidney disease through transplant.

    Most of its US dialysis revenue comes from government programs. In 2025, Medicare and Medicare Advantage plans paid 57% of dialysis patient service revenues, and commercial insurance, including hospital dialysis services, paid 32%. Medicare sets the payment rate for much of this care, so changes in federal rules matter to it.

    The 10-K says the business is labor intensive, and lists competition as a risk. It also grows by acquiring dialysis centers and other businesses.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Fewer patients with higher-paying commercial insurance would hurt its results.
    • It may not be able to negotiate contracts with private payers on competitive terms.
    • Government health programs, mainly Medicare, set much of its pay, and rules can change.
    • Rising labor costs, or being unable to attract and keep staff, because the business is labor intensive.
    • It carries a substantial amount of debt, and its legal and regulatory compliance is complex.
    From DaVita's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 11, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
