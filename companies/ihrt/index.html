    What iHeartMedia does

    iHeartMedia owns broadcast radio stations and digital audio services in the U.S. It says it is the number one U.S. audio company by consumer reach. Its content, including radio stations and podcasts, reaches listeners over AM/FM and HD radio, its iHeart.com site and station websites, and the iHeartRadio app, which runs on 500+ platforms and thousands of devices such as car dashboards, phones and smart speakers.

    It has three reportable segments. Multiplatform Group covers its broadcast radio, networks, sponsorships and events. Digital Audio Group covers podcasting, the iHeartRadio digital service and its digital ad technology companies. Audio & Media Services covers its media representation business, Katz Media Group, and RCS Sound Software, which makes scheduling software for the industry.

    Its main source of revenue is selling advertising time on its domestic radio stations. Broadcast radio brought in $1,633.4 million in 2025, down from $1,726.9 million in 2024. It has 860+ stations in approximately 160 markets.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Weaker advertising spending when the economy is uncertain or deteriorates.
    • Intense competition, including from alternative media and entertainment platforms.
    • Depending on on-air talent, program hosts and the programming it buys.
    • A large debt load that limits its operating flexibility.
    • Government regulation, and rules or royalty audits that could raise the royalties it pays.
    From iHeartMedia's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 2, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
