    What CME Group does

    CME Group runs financial exchanges. It lets customers trade futures, options, and cash and over-the-counter products across interest rates, stock indexes, foreign exchange, farm goods, energy, metals and cryptocurrencies. Its exchanges are CME, CBOT, NYMEX and COMEX. It also runs BrokerTec for bond and repo trading and EBS for currency trading.

    Customers use its products to hedge risk, speculate and allocate assets. CME earns money mainly from clearing and transaction fees charged for trades executed and cleared through it. It also sells market data, such as real-time and historical prices, along with research and analytics tools.

    CME says liquidity, the ability of a market to absorb large trades quickly, is key to attracting and keeping customers. It is based in Chicago, and it is mainly overseen by regulators in the United States, the United Kingdom and the European Union.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competition from other exchanges and trading venues.
    • Trading volume falling if it cannot keep its customers, which would cut revenue.
    • System failures or capacity limits that stop it from running its markets.
    • Credit risk from its clearing firms and other counterparties.
    • Rules from regulators in the U.S., U.K. and EU, including rules on digital assets.
    From CME Group's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
