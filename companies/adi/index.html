    What Analog Devices does

    Analog Devices designs, makes, tests and sells chips that connect the physical world to digital systems. Its products include data converters, amplifiers, power management chips, radio frequency chips, edge processors and other sensors, along with software and subsystems. Customers build them into factory equipment, cars, phones and other consumer devices, and communications gear.

    It sells through its own sales force, third-party distributors, independent sales representatives and its website. It has sales offices, representatives or distributors in approximately 50 countries. Distributors keep an inventory of its products and can return some of it for credit under their agreements. The company says its chips can have several hundred end customers each, and that customers choose among competitors on things such as accuracy, speed, size, power use and technical support.

    Analog Devices reports its sales by end market: industrial, automotive, consumer and communications. It employs a team of approximately 13,000 engineers, and says it relies on that engineering talent and its own manufacturing as a way to stand apart.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Chip demand moves in cycles, so extra production can cause overcapacity and lower prices.
    • Tariffs and other trade restrictions could raise its costs or limit its sales.
    • Its reliance on outside suppliers for materials, wafer foundry, assembly and test services.
    • Customers usually make no long-term purchase commitments, so orders can be cut or delayed.
    • A long disruption at its own or its suppliers' factories.
    From Analog Devices's annual report on Form 10-K, filed November 25, 2025, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
