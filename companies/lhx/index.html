    What L3Harris does

    L3Harris makes defense technology: secure radios and communications networks, sensors, space systems, and rocket and missile propulsion. Its largest customers are U.S. Government departments and agencies, the prime contractors (main contractors) that work for them, and allied governments, and it serves customers in more than 100 countries. Sales to U.S. Government customers, including foreign military sales the U.S. Government funds, made up 75% of fiscal 2025 revenue. Revenue from customers outside the U.S., counting those same foreign military sales, was $4.8 billion, or 22%.

    In fiscal 2025 it reported four segments. Communication Systems makes tactical radios, night vision goggles, and radios for first responders and other public agencies. Integrated Mission Systems builds surveillance and targeting systems for aircraft, and power, sensor and autonomous systems for navy vessels. Space & Airborne Systems makes space systems for surveillance, navigation and weather monitoring, air traffic management networks, and electronics and weapons for combat aircraft. Aerojet Rocketdyne makes propulsion and armament systems for missiles and space missions. L3Harris names BAE Systems, Boeing, General Dynamics, Lockheed Martin, Northrop Grumman, RTX and Thales as competitors, along with newer defense contractors such as Anduril.

    L3Harris had about 45,000 employees as of January 2, 2026, including about 18,000 engineers and scientists, with 90% in the U.S. Its contractual backlog, work under contract still to be done, was $38.7 billion. It divested its Commercial Aviation Solutions business in March 2025, and starting in fiscal 2026 it reorganized into three segments.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its heavy reliance on U.S. Government defense contracts, which it must keep winning in competitive bidding.
    • Cuts or shifts in U.S. Government spending, or delays and shutdowns in the federal budget process.
    • Losses from cost overruns on fixed-price contracts, especially for development programs.
    • Problems at its suppliers and subcontractors, including the limited approved sources of microelectronic parts.
    • An explosion, fire or improper handling of the dangerous materials used in its business.
    From L3Harris's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
