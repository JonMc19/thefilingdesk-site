    What Labcorp does

    Labcorp runs laboratories that test samples for patients and for drug developers. Its diagnostics business serves patients, doctors, hospitals, health systems and other labs. Its drug development business, which includes a central laboratory, supports drug makers in testing and developing new medicines.

    The company says its nearly 71,000 employees served clients in about 100 countries in 2025 and performed 750 million tests. It also says it supported 85% of the new drugs and therapeutic products the FDA approved in 2025. It signed or completed 13 deals with health systems and local and regional labs that year.

    Labcorp's growth plans center on specialty testing in oncology, women's health, autoimmune disease and neurology, and on partnering with health systems. Over the longer term it targets cell and gene therapy support and consumer-focused services. In 2025 it launched tests that aid in diagnosing Alzheimer's disease and that detect molecular residual disease, and it expanded a cell and gene therapy lab in Madison, Wisconsin.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Changes in how healthcare is paid for, including insurance and government reimbursement.
    • Changes in rules for the drug, biotech and medical device industries that could cut demand for its services.
    • Price competition and the loss of customers or major contracts.
    • Failing to obtain new testing technologies, or customers switching to new technologies.
    • Trouble finding and combining acquisition targets.
    From Labcorp's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 24, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
