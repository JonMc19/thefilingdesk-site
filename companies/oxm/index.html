    What Oxford Industries does

    Oxford Industries designs, sources, markets and sells apparel under lifestyle brands: Tommy Bahama, Lilly Pulitzer, Johnny Was, Southern Tide, TBBC, Duck Head and Jack Rogers. It defines a lifestyle brand as one built around a clear point of view inspired by an attitude or way of living, which it says can earn more loyalty and higher prices.

    It sells mainly directly to consumers. In fiscal 2025, 82% of net sales came through its direct-to-consumer channels: full-price stores, websites, outlets and the Tommy Bahama restaurants. The rest comes from wholesale customers. It says apparel competition rests on brand reputation, design, consumer preference, price, quality and marketing, and that it must foresee fashion trends.

    It keeps in touch with customers through digital and print media, and says more than 2.5 million consumers transacted with it in the last year.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to anticipate changing fashion trends and consumer tastes.
    • Harm to the reputation or value of its brands.
    • Competition and price pressure in the apparel industry.
    • Not adapting its direct-to-consumer strategy as shopping habits shift.
    • Weak general economic conditions, and risks from leasing store and restaurant space.
    From Oxford Industries's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
