    What Grail does

    GRAIL sells Galleri, a blood test that screens for many types of cancer at once and points to where in the body a cancer signal may have come from. The company describes itself as commercial-stage, meaning the test is already on sale. Galleri is currently offered through partnerships with healthcare systems, employers, digital health platforms, payors and life insurance providers.

    Almost all of its revenue comes from Galleri. GRAIL says it has run what it believes is the largest clinical program in genomic medicine, and it has submitted a pre-market approval application to the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, with the last part sent in January 2026. It reported results from two large studies, PATHFINDER 2 and the NHS-Galleri trial, and included some of them in that application.

    The NHS-Galleri trial did not meet its main goal, which the company lists as a risk. GRAIL has a limited operating history and has lost money in every period since it began.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A substantial majority of its revenue comes from Galleri, so it depends heavily on one test.
    • The NHS-Galleri trial did not meet its main goal, which may hurt its business and its approval efforts.
    • Insurers and other payors may not cover Galleri or may pay too little for it.
    • The test may not perform in real-world use as it did in clinical studies.
    • It has lost money in every period since it began and expects more net losses.
    From Grail's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Piotroski F-score: how many of nine simple tests of profitability, funding and efficiency the company passed in the year (Piotroski, 2000); 8 or 9 is strong, 0 to 2 weak. Rule of 40: revenue growth plus free cash flow margin; software investors treat 40% or more as the bar.
