    What Arrow Electronics does

    Arrow Electronics sources and sells technology products to makers of equipment and to businesses that build IT systems for others. It has two segments. Global components distributes electronic parts, mainly semiconductors, to equipment makers and electronics manufacturing services providers. Global enterprise computing solutions (ECS) sells datacenter, cloud, security and analytics products to resellers and managed service providers.

    Arrow acts as a middleman between parts and computing suppliers and their customers. It also earns fees for supply chain services, engineering and design help, and integration services. In 2025 about 70% of sales came from global components and about 30% from global ECS. Within global components, about 72% of sales were semiconductor products and related services.

    The company has operations in the Americas, Europe, the Middle East and Africa, and Asia-Pacific. It says it has over 140 sales facilities and 39 distribution and value-added centers, serving over 85 countries, and about 22,230 employees.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Not having enough cost-effective funding or cash to run the business and return money to shareholders.
    • Write-downs if its goodwill and other acquired intangible assets become impaired.
    • Cyclical demand for chips and other parts, and shortages or disruptions in the supply chain.
    • The lack of long-term sales contracts with its customers.
    • The risks of operating in many countries, since a large share of its sales are outside the United States.
    From Arrow Electronics's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 11, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
