    What Supermicro does

    Supermicro, based in Silicon Valley, designs and builds servers, storage systems and related equipment for data centers. It calls its offering total information technology (IT) solutions, and it focuses on rack-scale systems for artificial intelligence (AI) and high-performance computing. Its customers include enterprise data centers, cloud service providers and edge computing uses such as 5G telecom and retail.

    It sells complete servers, storage systems, blade servers, workstations, full racks, networking devices and parts, plus support services that include liquid cooling. In fiscal 2026 it pushed into Data Center Building Block Solutions, a modular offering that ranges from single graphics processors (GPUs) and switches to full racks, site infrastructure, management software and services. Its Server Building Block Solutions use common parts across product lines so it can build new products quickly.

    Supermicro sells through its own sales force and through distributors, value-added resellers, system integrators and original equipment manufacturers. It runs operations from Silicon Valley, Taiwan, Malaysia and the Netherlands.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Sales concentrated among a few large customers, so losing one could cut revenue sharply.
    • Relying on a limited number of suppliers, and on the market for core components such as GPUs.
    • Conflicts of interest and heavy reliance on Ablecom and Compuware, companies tied to its executives' families.
    • Having previously identified material weaknesses in its internal control over financial reporting.
    • Export limits and supply risk tied to Taiwan, and the pace of change in the AI industry.
    From Supermicro's annual report on Form 10-K, filed August 31, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
