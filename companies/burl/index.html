    What Burlington Stores does

    Burlington is an off-price retailer. It sells brand-name apparel, shoes, accessories, home goods, baby items, beauty products, toys and gifts at prices it says are below those of other retailers. It opened its first store in Burlington, New Jersey in 1972, selling mainly coats. It now operates 1,212 stores in 46 states, Washington D.C. and Puerto Rico, mostly under the Burlington Stores name.

    Almost all of its sales come from its stores. Its buyers purchase some goods before the season and the rest in-season, when they find a good deal. Burlington says this gives it room to switch suppliers and categories as demand changes, and that the "treasure hunt" feel of off-price shopping brings customers back often. It competes with other retailers on price, range, quality, style, service and brand image.

    As of January 31, 2026 it employed 83,309 associates, and 79% were part-time or seasonal.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Weaker consumer spending or inflation that cuts store sales.
    • Failing to buy attractive brand-name goods at good prices, or to manage its opportunistic buying and inventory.
    • Competition from other retailers, and fewer visitors at the shopping centers where its stores sit.
    • Disruption to its distribution network, and exposure to tariffs on goods made overseas.
    • Trouble hiring and keeping enough staff, and higher labor costs across a large workforce.
    From Burlington Stores's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 19, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
