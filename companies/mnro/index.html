    What Monro does

    Monro runs retail tire and auto repair stores in the United States. Its stores sell replacement tires and tire services, brake, exhaust, steering and suspension repairs, wheel alignment and routine maintenance for passenger cars, light trucks and vans. Some stores also serve commercial customers, who buy more commercial tires.

    It earns its revenue from selling tires, parts and services at these stores, and it reports one operating segment. At March 28, 2026, it operated 1,115 company-run stores in 32 states, plus 46 franchised Car-X locations and two retread facilities. The stores operate under brands such as Tire Choice, Mr. Tire, Monro Auto Service and Tire Centers, Tire Warehouse and Car-X. The company says it serviced about 3.8 million vehicles in fiscal 2026.

    In May 2025 its board approved a plan to close 145 underperforming stores, which closed in the first quarter of fiscal 2026. The company is based in Fairport, New York, and was incorporated in 1959.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competition in the automotive repair industry.
    • Weaker consumer spending or economic conditions that reduce demand for tires and repairs.
    • Electric vehicles and other automotive technology changing the services drivers need.
    • Reliance on a few vendors for tires and parts, and tariffs and trade changes that raise costs.
    • Needing enough cash flow to meet its obligations under a credit facility with restrictive covenants.
    From Monro's annual report on Form 10-K, filed May 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
