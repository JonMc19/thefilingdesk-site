    What Orion does

    Orion makes carbon black, a fine black powder of carbon used as an additive to give products color, strength, conductivity or protection. It is based in Luxembourg, with its main executive office in Spring, Texas. Its customers make tires, rubber goods, plastics, pipes, cables, coatings, printing inks and batteries.

    It reports two segments. Specialty Carbon Black goes into coatings, polymers, printing inks and batteries, including conductive additives for lithium-ion batteries. Rubber Carbon Black goes into tires and mechanical rubber goods such as hoses and belts. Orion says it is one of the largest global producers of Specialty Carbon Black, with two other large global producers, and that it competes with regional producers and with makers of acetylene black.

    Orion has 14 wholly owned plants in Europe, North and South America, South Africa and Asia, not counting a plant under construction in La Porte, Texas, and one jointly owned plant in Dortmund, Germany. It has about 1,639 employees and four innovation centers. Its main research center is in Cologne, Germany.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Weak or uncertain economic conditions that reduce demand from its customers.
    • Reliance on major customers for a large part of its sales.
    • Swings in the cost and supply of raw materials, especially carbon black oil, and of energy.
    • Plant expansions that may be late, cost more or not bring the expected benefits.
    • Environmental, health and safety rules, and chemical regulation such as REACH, that may limit what it can make and sell.
    From Orion's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 17, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
