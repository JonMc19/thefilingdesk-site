    What Lululemon Athletica does

    Lululemon designs and sells athletic clothing, shoes and accessories, such as leggings, shorts, tops and jackets for yoga, running and training, under the lululemon name. It sells mainly through its own stores, websites and apps, and operates in 30 countries. In the year to February 2026, women's clothing made up 63% of revenue, men's 24% and accessories and other products 13%.

    It reports three regional segments. The Americas, where it opened its first store in Vancouver in 1998, brought in 71% of revenue in 2025. China Mainland brought in 16%, and the Rest of World segment covers the rest of Asia Pacific, Europe and the Middle East. Lululemon says it competes on brand, quality, style and price with global and local makers and sellers of athletic clothing and shoes, including established companies and new brands.

    Lululemon had 811 company-run stores and about 39,000 employees in early 2026. Outside manufacturers make its products: it works with about 51 of them, and 40% of them were made in Vietnam in 2025, with most of the rest from Cambodia, Sri Lanka, Indonesia and Bangladesh.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Damage to its brand, or losing shoppers to rivals and new brands, could cut its sales and profits.
    • Failing to predict what shoppers want and to keep making new products they like.
    • New US tariffs and the end of duty-free treatment for low-value imports, which have raised its product costs.
    • Its reliance on a small number of overseas manufacturers, mostly in Asia, and possible supply disruptions.
    • Its fabrics and designs are mostly not patented and can be copied and sold for less.
    From Lululemon Athletica's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 17, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
