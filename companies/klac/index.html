    What KLA does

    KLA makes equipment and software that chipmakers and electronics makers use to inspect and measure what they build, so they can find defects early and get more working chips from each wafer (the silicon discs chips are made on). It also sells to makers of photomasks, which are the stencils used to print circuits, and to makers of circuit boards and chip packaging.

    It reports three segments. Semiconductor Process Control sells inspection and measurement products and software used throughout chip making. Specialty Semiconductor Process sells tools that deposit and etch thin layers, for makers of sensors, radio-frequency chips and power chips used in cars and factories. PCB and Component Inspection serves makers of circuit boards and chip substrates. KLA also earns money from services such as maintenance, which were about 23% of revenue in fiscal 2026.

    KLA says its sales depend on how much chipmakers spend on equipment. That spending is cyclical, and it is driven by demand for chips, including for data centers and artificial intelligence. KLA says memory and foundry/logic (contract chip making) are the two main markets its chipmaker customers operate in.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its sales depend on chipmakers' spending on equipment, which rises and falls with the economy and chip demand.
    • U.S. export rules limit what it can sell to China, and the Chinese government may respond with its own rules.
    • U.S. tariffs and retaliation by other countries, and uncertainty over how they will work.
    • It may face costly claims over intellectual property.
    • Acquisitions are part of its strategy, but it may not find good targets or integrate them well.
    From KLA's annual report on Form 10-K, filed August 6, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
