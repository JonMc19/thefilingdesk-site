    What Rhythm Pharmaceuticals does

    Rhythm Pharmaceuticals sells medicines for rare diseases that cause intense, constant hunger and severe obesity. These conditions trace to faults in the melanocortin-4 receptor (MC4R) pathway, a signaling system in the brain that helps control hunger and how the body uses energy.

    Its one marketed product is IMCIVREE (setmelanotide). It is sold in the United States, the European Union, the United Kingdom, Canada and some other places for certain rare genetic obesity diseases, including Bardet-Biedl syndrome. The company makes money by selling this drug. It says it expects to launch IMCIVREE in the United States in 2026 for acquired hypothalamic obesity, which is weight gain caused by injury to part of the brain, if regulators approve it.

    Rhythm is also testing two earlier-stage drugs in the same family: bivamelagon, taken by mouth, and RM-718, designed to be given once a week by injection. It holds worldwide rights to IMCIVREE and global rights to these two. It had 414 employees on February 1, 2026, including some in 11 countries outside North America.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It has a limited history selling drugs and has not yet earned significant revenue from them.
    • It may need to raise more funding, which may not be available on acceptable terms.
    • Its revenue interest financing agreement with Healthcare Royalty Partners could limit its cash and its choices.
    • Results from earlier trials may not predict later ones, and early data it announces may change.
    • The number of patients with each rare disease it targets is small and not precisely known.
