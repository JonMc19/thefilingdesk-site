    What BioCryst Pharmaceuticals does

    BioCryst develops and sells medicines for hereditary angioedema, or HAE, and other rare diseases. HAE is an inherited condition that causes sudden swelling attacks. Its main product, ORLADEYO (berotralstat), is a once-daily capsule that prevents HAE attacks. The company says it discovered and developed it internally. It is approved in the United States and several other markets, and a pellet form for children aged 2 to under 12 is approved in the United States.

    BioCryst earns revenue from selling ORLADEYO and also sells RAPIVAB, an intravenous flu treatment. It has a partnership with Torii. Its pipeline includes navenibart, an injectable antibody for HAE that it gained by merging with Astria Therapeutics, and early-stage candidates for Netherton syndrome, diabetic macular edema and atopic dermatitis.

    The company says it wants to use its sales infrastructure to commercialize more rare disease medicines, whether it finds them itself or gets them through business deals.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It may not reach sustained profitability.
    • It may need to raise more money and could have trouble doing so.
    • The Astria merger may be harder, costlier or slower to combine than expected, and may not bring the hoped-for benefits.
    • Its drug candidates may not win or keep regulatory approval.
    • Generic or biosimilar competition could cut into the sales of its medicines.
