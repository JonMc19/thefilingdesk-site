    What Cushman & Wakefield does

    Cushman & Wakefield is a commercial real estate services firm. It advises and works for companies that rent or use office, warehouse and other commercial space (occupiers) and for the investors and owners who hold it. It has about 53,000 employees in nearly 60 countries. Its revenue in 2025 was $10.3 billion.

    It earns fees from four service lines. Services, the largest at 66% of 2025 revenue, covers property and facilities management, project management and related work, often under multi-year contracts, and includes costs passed on to clients. Leasing earns a percentage of the rent over the life of a lease, for work done for owners or tenants. Capital markets helps buyers and sellers of property and arranges financing, with fees tied to deal volume. Valuation and other provides appraisals and advice. It reports three regions: the Americas, Europe, Middle East and Africa, and Asia Pacific. The United States is its largest country by revenue.

    The firm says there are many local, regional and global competitors, and that the industry is fragmented. It also grew through acquisitions and investments.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A weak economy or low demand for commercial real estate cuts leasing and sales deals, and so its fees.
    • Its revenue and cash flow are seasonal, so results can swing from quarter to quarter.
    • It depends on recruiting and keeping its revenue-producing advisors and senior managers.
    • Losing one of its large corporate clients could hurt, because business is concentrated in some of them.
    • Its debt and the limits in its debt agreements may reduce its cash and flexibility.
    From Cushman & Wakefield's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 19, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
