    What Peabody Energy does

    Peabody, based in St. Louis, Missouri, produces coal. It makes two kinds: metallurgical coal, used to make steel, and thermal coal, burned to make electricity. At the end of 2025 it owned interests in 16 active mining operations in the United States and Australia, including a 50% stake in the Middlemount mine in Queensland, Australia.

    It reports four segments: Seaborne Thermal, Seaborne Metallurgical, Powder River Basin and Other U.S. Thermal. Its seaborne segments are centered on Australia. It sells under long-term supply agreements with large customers and ships exports through ports.

    It is developing the Centurion mine, an underground metallurgical coal mine in Queensland. Full-scale production began in February 2026. Peabody is also looking at rare earth and critical minerals, power from coal mine gas and renewable energy projects on land it has already mined and restored.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Loss or non-renewal of its long-term coal supply agreements, including those with its largest customers.
    • Take-or-pay arrangements that can hurt profit if it must pay for capacity it does not use.
    • Mining and exploration assets it may not recover its investment in, which could lead to write-downs.
    • Reclamation and mine closure costs, and the need to provide financial assurances for them.
    • Mine closures after health or safety incidents, and labor disputes.
    From Peabody Energy's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 19, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
