    What Alico does

    Alico is a Florida landowner. At September 30, 2025 it owned approximately 49,537 acres in eight Florida counties, plus oil, gas and mineral rights on substantially all of them. Until recently its main business was growing oranges and selling them to juice processors.

    It has two segments: Alico Citrus, and Land Management and Other Operations (grazing and hunting leases, conservation, rock mining royalties and farmland leasing). In January 2025 it announced a Strategic Transformation: it wound down substantially all citrus operations after the 2024-2025 harvest, citing citrus greening disease and environmental factors, and now aims to focus on land development and diversified farming. Alico Citrus still produced 93.8% of total operating revenues in fiscal 2025, and Tropicana, its largest customer, bought 87.2% of consolidated revenue until the two companies ended their contracts in May 2025. Alico also sells land: in fiscal 2025 it sold approximately 2,796 acres.

    Most of its land is now meant for diversified farming, with a smaller share set aside for development. As of September 30, 2025 it had 20 full-time employees.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Carrying out its shift from citrus to land development and diversified farming may not work as planned.
    • Weather, hurricanes and other natural events, since its land is concentrated in Florida.
    • The land development business is highly competitive, and its revenue mix is changing sharply.
    • A material weakness or other control deficiency in its financial reporting could lead to errors.
    • Its debt, much of it at variable interest rates, and its ability to keep paying dividends.
    From Alico's annual report on Form 10-K, filed November 24, 2025, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
