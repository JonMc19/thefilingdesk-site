    What General Mills does

    General Mills makes and sells packaged foods and pet food. It has more than 100 brands, sold in about 100 countries. Its main categories are snacks, ready-to-eat cereal, convenient meals, natural pet food, refrigerated and frozen dough, baking mixes and super-premium ice cream. Brands named in its 10-K include Betty Crocker, Bisquick, Annie's and Blue Buffalo.

    The company sells mainly to grocery stores, mass merchandisers, membership stores, drug and dollar chains, e-commerce retailers, foodservice distributors and restaurants. It reports four segments: North America Retail, International, North America Pet and North America Foodservice. Walmart is its largest customer, at 22 percent of consolidated net sales and 31 percent of North America Retail sales. General Mills also holds 50 percent interests in two joint ventures: Cereal Partners Worldwide with Nestlé, which sells cereal outside North America, and Häagen-Dazs Japan.

    It competes with other food makers and with retailers' own store brands, which usually sell at lower prices. As of May 31, 2026, it had about 30,000 employees, around half in the United States. Grains, meat, vegetable oils, sugar, fruit and packaging are its main raw materials.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Strong competition, including from lower-priced store brands, that could hurt its sales and profit margins.
    • Its reliance on a few large retail customers, with Walmart the biggest, as grocery chains consolidate.
    • Swings in the prices of grains, packaging, energy and other inputs it relies on.
    • Product safety or quality concerns that make shoppers lose confidence in its products or brands.
    • Failing to keep up with changing tastes and eating habits, including health concerns about ingredients.
    From General Mills's annual report on Form 10-K, filed July 1, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
