    What Bristol Myers Squibb does

    Bristol Myers Squibb discovers, develops, makes and sells prescription medicines for serious diseases. It focuses on cancer, blood disorders, immune diseases, heart disease and neuroscience. It reports a single segment. It sells mainly to wholesalers, distributors and specialty pharmacies, and to a lesser extent to retailers, hospitals, clinics, government agencies and patients directly.

    Its marketed medicines include Opdivo and Yervoy, which are cancer immunotherapies, Orencia for rheumatoid arthritis, Reblozyl for anemia, Breyanzi and Abecma, which are CAR-T cell therapies (treatments made from a patient's own immune cells), Camzyos for a heart condition, Zeposia and Sotyktu for immune conditions, Krazati for lung and colon cancers, and Cobenfy for schizophrenia. The United States made up 69% of its revenues in 2025 and international markets 29%. The company says it competes with other research-based drug companies, smaller research firms and makers of generic drugs.

    It has major manufacturing sites in the U.S., Puerto Rico, the Netherlands, Ireland and Switzerland. Its 2025 deals included buying Orbital Therapeutics and agreements with BioNTech and Philochem.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Pricing pressure and other limits on drug prices in the U.S. and abroad.
    • Losing market exclusivity for a product sooner than expected, and facing lower-priced generics.
    • Delays or setbacks in developing and commercializing new products to replace revenue from older ones.
    • Depending on a few key products for most of its revenue, cash flow and earnings.
    • Patent litigation and a significant amount of debt.
    From Bristol Myers Squibb's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 11, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
