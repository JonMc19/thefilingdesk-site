    What Cricut does

    Cricut sells connected cutting machines that cut, write, score and add decorative effects to paper, vinyl, wood, leather and other materials, so people can make their own cards, apparel, gifts and decor. It also sells accessories and materials, and a cloud-based app called Cricut Design Space, where users find images, fonts, templates and projects.

    It makes money from selling machines, accessories and materials, and from subscriptions. Cricut Access is a subscription for a wider selection of images, fonts and projects, which are also sold one by one. Some machines are bundled with accessories, materials or a subscription. It says users keep buying subscriptions, accessories and materials long after they buy a machine.

    It reports nearly 5.9 million Active Users as of December 31, 2025. It sells through brick-and-mortar and online retail partners and through its own website.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Attracting and keeping users engaged.
    • Dependence on sales of its connected machines.
    • Reliance on a limited number of key retail partners, and the need to grow online sales.
    • Keeping and renewing subscriptions.
    • Misjudging demand or user preferences, so inventory and new products miss, in a highly competitive market.
    From Cricut's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 4, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Piotroski F-score: how many of nine simple tests of profitability, funding and efficiency the company passed in the year (Piotroski, 2000); 8 or 9 is strong, 0 to 2 weak. Rule of 40: revenue growth plus free cash flow margin; software investors treat 40% or more as the bar. Accruals ratio: net income minus cash from operations, over average total assets; lower means profit is backed by cash.
