    What SharkNinja does

    SharkNinja designs small household appliances sold under two brands. Shark is best known for vacuums and floor care, and Ninja for kitchen appliances. Its products include vacuums, carpet cleaners, fans, air purifiers, beauty products, blenders, frozen drink makers, espresso machines and outdoor cooking products. It sells through most major retailers and distributors, and also directly to shoppers through its websites and infomercials, in 38 markets.

    Net sales grew by $870.5 million, or 15.7%, in 2025. Cleaning appliances brought in $2,205.8 million, cooking and beverage appliances $1,816.3 million, food preparation appliances $1,550.7 million, and beauty and home environment products $826.3 million. The US made up 62.5% of sales. The company reports one segment. Shark competes with Dyson, Hoover and Bissell; Ninja with Vitamix, De'Longhi, Breville, Hamilton Beach, Cuisinart and others. SharkNinja says most of its rivals sell at lower prices, with exceptions such as Dyson and Vitamix.

    SharkNinja doesn't make its own products. Outside suppliers, mostly in China, manufacture them, while its own engineers and designers in the US, the UK and China develop them. It had about 4,143 employees in about 13 countries at the end of 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to keep its brands strong and keep up demand for its products.
    • Failing to keep launching new products that people want to buy.
    • Its reliance on outside suppliers, many of them in China, and on getting products shipped on time.
    • Tariffs and trade policy changes that make products made abroad harder or costlier to get.
    • Its reliance on large retailers and distributors to sell its products.

    How the model works. Free cash flow grows at the first rate for five years and the second rate for five more, and each year is discounted at the discount rate.     2Price-to-earnings (P/E) method

    Another common way to value a company is to multiply its earnings per share by a price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple: the number of years of today's earnings an investor pays for a share. A higher multiple usually means investors expect earnings to grow. Choose a multiple, for example from similar companies, to see the value it gives.

    How the P/E method works. Value per share is earnings per share times the multiple. On a future year's earnings, that gives the price in that year, which is brought back to today at the discount rate: price ÷ (1 + discount rate) for each year ahead. It is quick and easy to compare across companies, but it ignores debt and cash, and earnings can swing with one-off gains and charges, so a multiple says most about steady, profitable businesses. Like the cash flow model, the result depends entirely on the assumptions you choose. It is not a forecast, a price target or a recommendation.
