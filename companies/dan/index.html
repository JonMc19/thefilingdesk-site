    What Dana does

    Dana, based in Maumee, Ohio, makes parts that move vehicles and manage their energy. Its products include axles, driveshafts, transmissions, sealing and thermal products, and electric vehicle parts such as motors, inverters and controllers. It sells to nearly every major maker of on-highway vehicles, for gasoline, diesel, hybrid and electric models.

    It runs mainly through two segments. Light Vehicle supplies makers of light trucks, SUVs, vans and passenger cars. Commercial Vehicle supplies makers of trucks and buses and also covers the aftermarket business. Its customers are carmakers, so Dana's sales rise and fall with how many vehicles they build. Sales to its ten largest customers were 76% of its sales in 2025.

    Dana sold its Off-Highway business, which served construction, mining and farm vehicles, to Allison Transmission in a deal that closed on January 1, 2026, and plans to use the proceeds to pay down debt. It had about 26,900 people in 24 countries at the end of 2025, not counting that business. It is also cutting costs.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Dependence on a few large vehicle makers: losing one or a change in what they buy could hurt sales.
    • A downturn in the global economy and in vehicle production.
    • Tariffs and trade-policy changes that raise costs or shift where it can sell.
    • Stricter fuel economy and emissions rules, and a slower-than-planned move to electric vehicles.
    • Cost-cutting plans that may not deliver the savings Dana expects.
    From Dana's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
