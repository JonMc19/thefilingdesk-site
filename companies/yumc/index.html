    What Yum China does

    Yum China runs restaurants in China. Its main brands are KFC and Pizza Hut. It also operates smaller brands such as Lavazza coffee shops, Huang Ji Huang, Little Sheep and Taco Bell. It says it has the exclusive right in China, excluding Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan, to operate and sublicense the KFC, Pizza Hut and Taco Bell brands. It owns the Little Sheep and Huang Ji Huang concepts outright, and has a joint venture with Lavazza Group for coffee.

    The company says it is the largest restaurant company in China by system sales, with $11.8 billion of revenues in 2025 and 18,101 restaurants at year-end. It reports two segments, KFC and Pizza Hut. KFC operated 12,997 restaurants and Pizza Hut 4,168.

    Yum China owns and runs many of its restaurants. It also franchises others and earns ongoing franchise fees on their sales, at an average rate of approximately 6% of system sales. It operates under a master license agreement with YUM, the owner of the KFC, Pizza Hut and Taco Bell brands.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Food safety problems and foodborne illness concerns.
    • Substantially all of its revenue comes from China, so changes in China's economy, rules or politics matter.
    • Its restaurants operate under a master license agreement with YUM and depend on YUM's brands.
    • Not being able to meet its goals for opening new restaurants profitably.
    • Rising labor and raw material costs, and supply interruptions.
    From Yum China's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
