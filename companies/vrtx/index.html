    What Vertex Pharmaceuticals does

    Vertex is a biotechnology company that develops and sells medicines for serious diseases in specialty markets. Its approved medicines treat cystic fibrosis (CF), a genetic disease that damages the lungs and other organs, as well as sickle cell disease, transfusion-dependent beta thalassemia and acute pain. Its CF medicines include Trikafta, Alyftrek, Symdeko, Orkambi and Kalydeko. Casgevy treats sickle cell disease and beta thalassemia, and Journavx is a non-opioid pain medicine approved in 2025.

    Vertex earns its money from selling these medicines. Substantially all of its net product revenue has come from its CF medicines, which it says are reimbursed or accessible in more than 60 countries. It describes its strategy as discovering, developing and selling new medicines, and it invests in research and in deals to access new technologies and drug candidates.

    It has five late-stage programs. They include povetacicept for IgA nephropathy and for primary membranous nephropathy, inaxaplin for APOL1-mediated kidney disease, suzetrigine for nerve pain from diabetes, and zimislecel, a cell therapy for type 1 diabetes whose dosing in a trial it has temporarily postponed. These are still being tested, and none is guaranteed to be approved.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Depending on its cystic fibrosis medicines for substantially all of its net product revenue.
    • Failing to develop and sell additional medicines, since drug development is highly uncertain and costly.
    • Whether Journavx and its pain programs succeed commercially.
    • Whether it can grow or keep up sales of Casgevy, which depends on complex treatment steps and payor coverage.
    • Clinical trials that fail, or approvals and reimbursement it may not get.
