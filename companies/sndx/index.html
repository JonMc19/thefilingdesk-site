    What Syndax Pharmaceuticals does

    Syndax is a drug company that sells two cancer medicines and is testing more uses for them. Revuforj (revumenib) treats certain relapsed or hard-to-treat acute leukemias, in people with a KMT2A gene change or with an NPM1 gene mutation. Niktimvo (axatilimab-csfr) treats chronic graft-versus-host disease, a condition in which donor cells attack a transplant patient's body, after other treatments have failed. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved Niktimvo in August 2024, Revuforj in November 2024, and a second use for Revuforj in October 2025.

    Syndax earns money by selling Revuforj itself in the United States. It runs the sales and further development of Revuforj, and works with its partner Incyte on Niktimvo. It licensed the worldwide rights to both medicines from other companies. It describes both as the first of their kind.

    The company is also running trials of revumenib with standard treatments in newly diagnosed patients, studying it in a bone marrow disease called myelofibrosis, and testing axatilimab in a lung disease. It plans to license or buy more drug candidates and says it may sell Revuforj outside the United States, alone or with partners.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It relies on two approved medicines, so weak sales or safety problems with either would hurt it.
    • Its trials of new uses for its medicines may fail or be delayed, and regulators may not approve them.
    • It depends on its partner Incyte for Niktimvo and on licenses from other companies for its medicines.
    • Competing medicines, including other drugs aimed at the same target, could take away patients.
    • It may need to raise more money, and it depends on outside makers and suppliers.
