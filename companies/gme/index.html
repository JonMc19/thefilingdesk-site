    What GameStop does

    GameStop sells video games, collectibles and entertainment products through its stores and online. Its products fall into three groups: hardware and accessories (new and pre-owned game consoles, controllers and headsets), software (new and pre-owned games, plus in-game currency and digital downloads), and collectibles (apparel, toys, trading cards and gadgets, and services such as authenticating and grading trading cards). Its stores and websites operate mainly as GameStop, EB Games and Micromania.

    It runs three geographic segments: the United States, Australia and Europe. At the end of its 2025 fiscal year it had 2,206 stores: 1,598 in the United States, 308 in Europe and 300 in Australia. It closed 727 US stores during the year and has left several countries, including Canada and Germany, in the past three years. It has signed an agreement about a possible sale of its French operations. It says it competes with multi-channel retailers and ecommerce businesses.

    The 10-K says the company now has two priorities: getting the most cash from its older store business, and using its cash to buy, invest in or partner with other businesses. It says it has no binding agreement for a specific deal. A committee of its board that includes its chairman and chief executive, Ryan Cohen, manages its cash and can invest in items such as Treasury obligations, listed stocks and certain crypto-currencies, including Bitcoin.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its plan to buy or invest in other businesses may not work out, and the value of its investments may fall.
    • Holding Bitcoin or other crypto-currencies exposes it to their price swings and other risks.
    • A drop in demand for Pokemon and other trading cards, which supply much of its collectibles revenue and profit.
    • Demand for games changes with new consoles and with shifts to digital downloads.
    • Possibly being treated as an investment company under US law, which would bring costly rules and limits.
    From GameStop's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 24, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
