    What Realty Income does

    Realty Income is a real estate investment trust (REIT), a company that owns property and pays out most of its income to shareholders. It buys freestanding commercial buildings, such as stores, and leases them to businesses under long-term net leases. It says it owns over 15,500 properties in all 50 states, the United Kingdom and eight other European countries.

    Under a net lease, the tenant pays most or all of the building's operating costs, including property taxes, insurance and maintenance. Realty Income collects contractual rent, and many leases include built-in rent increases. Its tenants are a mix of investment grade and other creditworthy businesses. It competes with other REITs and companies that buy and run similar properties.

    Realty Income pays dividends monthly. It pays for growth with long-term debt, stock sales, retained cash flow and property sales. It says it is expanding by entering new regions and property types, investing in debt as well as property, and growing its private capital business, which runs joint ventures and private funds.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Needing to keep buying properties to grow, in competition with other buyers.
    • Real estate is hard to sell quickly, so it may not be able to buy or sell when it wants.
    • Loss of REIT tax status, which would cut the dividends it can pay.
    • Taking on development projects, new property types or debt investments with extra risks.
    • Rules that limit its freedom, such as payout requirements and gaming regulation of some properties.
    From Realty Income's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 25, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
