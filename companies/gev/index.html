    What GE Vernova does

    GE Vernova makes and services equipment that generates, moves and manages electric power. Its customers include utilities, independent power producers, developers, governments and industrial companies around the world. It says the machines it has installed generate approximately 25% of the world's electricity. It was spun off from General Electric, now GE Aerospace, on April 2, 2024.

    It reports three segments. Power makes gas and steam turbines and sells nuclear technology, hydropower solutions and the service of that equipment. Wind makes onshore and offshore wind turbines and blades. Electrification supplies equipment and software to transmit, distribute, convert and store electricity. Servicing equipment it has already installed is a large part of its strategy. At the end of 2025 it had approximately $94.4 billion in remaining performance obligations, which is its measure of backlog: firm customer orders for equipment and services not yet delivered.

    Key rivals named in the 10-K include Siemens Energy and Mitsubishi Power in Power, Vestas and Siemens-Gamesa in Wind, and Hitachi Energy and ABB in Electrification. The company had approximately 75,000 employees, about 24,000 of them in Europe and 21,000 in the United States.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Quality problems in its machines that could lead to warranty claims, delays and damage to its reputation.
    • Supply chain and shipping disruptions, including limited sources for some chips and critical materials.
    • Pressure on project costs and schedules at offshore wind, including the U.S. pause on offshore wind leases.
    • Factory disruptions or capacity limits that could delay deliveries as it expands production.
    • Strong competition across its power, wind and grid businesses.
    From GE Vernova's annual report on Form 10-K, filed January 29, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
