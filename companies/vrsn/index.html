    What Verisign does

    Verisign runs part of the internet's address book. It operates the registries for the .com and .net web address endings, which means it keeps the authoritative directory that lets browsers and email systems find a site by name. It also operates two of the thirteen internet root servers and performs the Root Zone Maintainer role, which keeps the top level of the Domain Name System running.

    Verisign earns money from fees for domain name registrations, which can run from one to ten years. It sells through registrars, the companies that sell web addresses to the public, and the registrars set the retail price. Its prices for .com, .net and .name are limited by its agreements with ICANN, the body that oversees domain names, and it can raise them only within those limits. It pays ICANN a fee on each yearly registration. Revenue for .cc names and some other endings is not subject to the same pricing limits.

    Its main data centers are in Virginia. The .com registry agreement with ICANN must be renewed or extended by November 30, 2030, and Verisign says ICANN could end or refuse to renew it in certain cases.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Losing or changing its right to run the .com and .net registries.
    • Changes or challenges to the price limits in the .com registry agreement.
    • Cyberattacks, outages or errors in its systems that run the domain name directory.
    • Its dependence on registrars and their resellers to sell domain names.
    • New technologies or internet habits that reduce demand for .com and .net names.
