    What Unity Software does

    Unity sells software for making and running video games and other interactive 2D and 3D content, on mobile, PC, console and extended reality (XR) devices. Game developers use it most, but it is also used in fields such as automotive, retail, manufacturing, healthcare, robotics, architecture and construction. Its customers range from large companies and government bodies to small studios and individual developers.

    The business has two parts. Create Solutions is the toolset for building content, including Unity's real-time 3D engine, AI-assisted content tools and cloud services such as multiplayer support. Grow Solutions is mostly advertising products that help app makers find users and earn money from their content, including a mediation platform (which picks among ad sources) and its own ad network, even when the content was not built in Unity. Its Supersonic service helps small studios publish mobile games.

    Unity says it competes in Create Solutions with in-house engines at large game studios and with Godot and Epic Games' Unreal Engine. In Grow Solutions it competes with companies such as AppLovin, Moloco and Digital Turbine, while large companies such as Amazon, Meta, Google and Microsoft are both partners and competitors. It is also resetting its portfolio to focus on what it calls its Strategic Portfolio.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It has a history of losses and may not reach or keep profitability under GAAP accounting rules.
    • Its plan to reset its portfolio and right-size its investments may not work.
    • It depends on the success of customers in the gaming market and on keeping and growing its customers.
    • Phone and app store operators may change their terms or rules in ways that hurt it or its customers.
    • The use of AI in its products, or failure to use it well, creates risks.
