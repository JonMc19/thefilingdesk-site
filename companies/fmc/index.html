    What FMC does

    FMC is an agricultural sciences company. It develops, markets and sells crop protection chemicals for farmers: insecticides (to kill insects), herbicides (to kill weeds) and fungicides (to fight fungal disease), along with biologicals, crop nutrition and seed treatment products, which it groups as plant health. Its products protect crops such as soybeans, corn, fruits and vegetables, cotton, sugarcane, rice and cereals, and some are used for pest control at home and in other non-farm settings.

    FMC reports a single business segment and makes money by selling these products around the world. A key product line, Rynaxypyr, has lost its main patent protection in major markets, which affects how it competes. It is launching four new active ingredients, including Isoflex, fluindapyr, Dodhylex and rimisoxafen.

    In February 2026 its board authorized exploring strategic options, including a possible sale of the company, but the review is at an early stage and may not lead to a deal. It is also selling its commercial business in India, expected to close in 2026, and running a multi-year program, Project Foundation, to close high-cost production sites and cut costs. It has about 5,500 employees worldwide.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competition could limit its ability to hold or raise prices or keep its market position.
    • Weather and climate, and the seasonal nature of crop protection sales, can hurt results.
    • Changes to regulations in the U.S., Brazil, China, Argentina and the European Union could stop it from making or selling some products.
    • The main patents on its Rynaxypyr and Cyazypyr ingredients have expired in all major markets.
    • The strategic review and the planned sale of its India business may not be completed as expected.
    From FMC's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
