    What Raymond James Financial does

    Raymond James is a financial services firm that gives investment advice, runs brokerage and investment banking businesses, manages money and operates two banks. Its customers are individuals, companies and municipalities. It works mostly in the United States, and to a lesser extent in Canada, the United Kingdom and other parts of Europe.

    It has five segments. The largest, Private Client Group, has financial advisors who plan, advise and trade for clients; it held $1.67 trillion in client assets at September 30, 2025 and earns fees based on those assets and on transactions. Capital Markets handles investment banking, trading for institutions and research. Asset Management runs funds and portfolios for a fee. The Bank segment makes loans, mainly loans backed by clients' securities, corporate loans and home mortgages, and takes deposits, including cash swept from clients' investment accounts. The Other segment holds corporate items such as interest on the firm's debt.

    The firm had about 19,500 employees at the end of its fiscal year, plus independent advisors who work with it as contractors. As a bank holding company, it is supervised by the Federal Reserve.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Large swings in clients' cash sweep and bank deposit balances, or higher costs to attract them, could cut revenue and slow its banks.
    • Its fees depend on the value of client assets and on markets, so market declines or lower trading activity can reduce income.
    • Losing senior professionals and financial advisors, who bring in its clients.
    • Its underwriting, trading and lending put its own capital at risk.
    • Falling short of regulatory capital rules for the firm, its banks or its broker-dealers.
    From Raymond James Financial's annual report on Form 10-K, filed November 25, 2025, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
