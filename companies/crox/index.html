    What Crocs does

    Crocs designs and sells casual shoes under two brands. The Crocs brand is best known for its molded clogs, made mostly from its own Croslite material, along with sandals and Jibbitz charms that clip onto the shoes. HEYDUDE sells light, slip-on loafers, mainly the Wally and Wendy, plus sneakers, boots, sandals and some clothing. It sells worldwide to retailers and online sellers, through distributors, and directly to shoppers in its own stores and on its websites.

    The Crocs Brand and HEYDUDE Brand are its two segments. Revenues fell 1.5% in 2025, as HEYDUDE sales dropped 13.3% while Crocs Brand sales rose. Sales to retailers and other wholesale customers made up 47.9% of revenue, and digital sales 37.8%. Sales outside the US were 48.6% of Crocs Brand revenue, while HEYDUDE sells mostly in North America. It names Nike, adidas, Deckers, Birkenstock, Steven Madden, Wolverine World Wide and VF among the companies it competes with in parts of its business.

    Crocs does not make its own shoes. Outside manufacturers make them, mostly in Asia, with Vietnam the largest source for both brands. At the end of 2025 it had more than 8,010 employees, about 4,940 of them in its stores.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to protect the value and appeal of its brands, which its business depends on.
    • Its full reliance on outside factories abroad, mainly in Asia, to make its shoes.
    • Tariffs, inflation and weak economies that cut consumer spending or raise its costs.
    • Its future growth may depend partly on HEYDUDE, whose sales fell in 2025.
    • Misjudging demand, leaving it with too much stock or too little to fill orders.
    From Crocs's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
