    What EchoStar does

    EchoStar is a holding company with four business segments. Pay-TV sells satellite and streaming television under the DISH and SLING brands. Wireless sells mobile phone service and devices, mainly under the Boost Mobile brand. Broadband and Satellite Services sells satellite internet to consumers and communications services to government, enterprise and airline customers. An Other segment holds its older 5G network operations.

    The 10-K says the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) reviewed whether EchoStar was using its airwave licenses (spectrum) as required, and that the FCC viewed the spectrum as underused. EchoStar then agreed to sell licenses to AT&T for an aggregate purchase price of $22.650 billion in cash, subject to certain adjustments. It also agreed to sell other spectrum licenses to SpaceX. Both sales depend on closing conditions.

    In 2025 EchoStar chose not to pay interest on some of its senior notes on the due dates, then paid within the grace periods. It says its Wireless business is moving to a hybrid model that uses other carriers' networks.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • The AT&T and SpaceX spectrum sales may not close, or may close on different terms.
    • Regulatory action by the FCC over its spectrum licenses.
    • Operating risks to its satellites in orbit and interference from others using the same spectrum.
    • Lawsuits, especially over intellectual property, with possible large costs.
    • Rules in its charter and bylaws that may make a takeover harder.
    From EchoStar's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 2, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Income statement

    From fiscal 2016 to 2022, earnings per share grew 27.0% a year and net income 55.4% a year, as new shares raised the share count by 226% and diluted each share's earnings. EchoStar then lost money in fiscal 2023, 2024 and 2025.
