    What Enact does

    Enact is a private mortgage insurance company that has served the United States housing market since 1981 and operates in all 50 states and the District of Columbia. Its insurance covers part of the unpaid balance on low down payment loans, where the borrower puts down only a small share of the home's value. It protects lenders and investors against losses when a borrower stops paying.

    Its customers are the lenders that originate home loans and choose which mortgage insurer to use: national banks, non-bank mortgage lenders, local mortgage bankers, community banks and credit unions. It earns money from premiums paid on the insurance it writes, and from investing its assets. It competes with other private mortgage insurers on price, reputation and other factors.

    In 2025 it wrote new insurance of $51.5 billion. Loans it insures can be sold into the secondary market, including to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. It must meet capital rules set by those two government-sponsored companies and by state regulators.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Changes to the charters or practices of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac could cut use of mortgage insurance.
    • A recession or falling home prices could raise its claims.
    • Its loss models and reserves may prove wrong.
    • Fierce competition from other private mortgage insurers.
    • Alternatives to private mortgage insurance could reduce its revenue.
    From Enact's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
