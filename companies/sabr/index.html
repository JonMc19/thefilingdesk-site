    What Sabre does

    Sabre runs a business-to-business travel marketplace, called Sabre Mosaic Marketplace, that connects travel suppliers with travel buyers. The suppliers include airlines, hotels, car rental brands, rail carriers, cruise lines and tour operators. The buyers include online and offline travel agencies. Its distribution network gathers inventory, prices and availability from suppliers and delivers them to buyers so trips can be searched and booked.

    It makes money from both sides of this market. The 10-K says it receives revenue from the travel supplier and the travel agency according to the commercial arrangement with each. It also sells software to airlines, including software licensing, for which it earns fees for the on-site installation and use of its software products.

    Sabre now reports one segment, after it sold its Hospitality Solutions business during 2025. It says it competes with other global distribution systems, local distribution systems and marketplaces owned by airlines or governments, with suppliers that sell directly, and with metasearch sites. It also competes with IT providers, specialists and airlines that build their own technology. Its offices are in Southlake, Texas.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its revenue depends on travel volumes, especially air travel.
    • Competition in fast-changing markets, and pressure to innovate to keep up.
    • Travel suppliers push on prices, and may cut costs, merge or change how they distribute.
    • It depends on relationships with travel buyers and on renewing contracts with customers.
    • Acquiring or selling businesses carries risks.
    From Sabre's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 18, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
