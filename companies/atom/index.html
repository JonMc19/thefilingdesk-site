    What Atomera does

    Atomera develops and licenses a chip-making technology called Mears Silicon Technology, or MST. MST is a thin film of re-engineered silicon that the company says lets transistors be made smaller, faster and more power efficient. It does not design or make wafers or chips itself.

    Its customers and partners are foundries, chip makers that run their own factories, chip designers without factories, wafer makers, makers of the machines that lay down thin silicon films, and design-software companies. So far MST has mainly been applied to power devices, radio-frequency chips and advanced logic and memory chips.

    Atomera aims to make money from license fees and royalties. A customer pays one fee when it installs MST on a tool for research use, a larger fee when it is cleared to make MST chips for sale, and then royalties on the chips it sells. The company also licenses simulation software, MSTcad, and sells paid engineering services and joint development work to help customers test MST in their own processes. It says it has recognized only minimal revenue so far.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It has little revenue so far, and its licensing model is still unproven.
    • Its license with STMicroelectronics is on hold during the qualification phase.
    • Customers may not move from testing to paid production licenses, so royalties may not follow.
    • It has a history of large operating losses and expects more losses.
    • It may need to raise more money, and it must protect its patents.
