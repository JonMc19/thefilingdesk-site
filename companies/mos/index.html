    What Mosaic does

    Mosaic mines and sells phosphate and potash, two nutrients farmers add to soil to grow crops. It also sells phosphate-based animal feed ingredients. It mines phosphate rock in Florida, Brazil and Peru and turns it into finished products in Florida, Louisiana and Brazil. It mines potash in Saskatchewan and New Mexico. The company says it serves customers in about 40 countries, including the four biggest nutrient-using countries: China, India, the U.S. and Brazil.

    Mosaic reports three segments: Phosphate, Potash and Mosaic Fertilizantes. Mosaic Fertilizantes covers its Brazil and Paraguay business: mines, chemical plants, nutrient blending and bagging, port terminals and warehouses. It also serves as a distribution outlet for the other two segments. Mosaic says it is the second largest integrated phosphate producer in the world and typically one of the top four potash producers.

    The company has a 75% economic interest in the Miski Mayo phosphate mine in Peru. It was formed in 2004 from a combination of IMC Global and the fertilizer businesses of Cargill, and it is based in Tampa, Florida.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • U.S. tariffs on Canadian potash and retaliatory tariffs.
    • Disruptions at its mines, plants and ports, and accidents or equipment failures.
    • Price swings and supply of key inputs such as sulfur, ammonia and energy.
    • Seasonal demand for crop nutrients that can leave it holding large inventories.
    • Operating assets outside North America in countries with volatile conditions.
    From Mosaic's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
