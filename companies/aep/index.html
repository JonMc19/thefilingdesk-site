    What American Electric Power does

    American Electric Power is a holding company that owns electric utilities serving parts of Arkansas, Indiana, Kentucky, Louisiana, Michigan, Ohio, Oklahoma, Tennessee, Texas, Virginia and West Virginia. As a holding company it has no employees of its own, and its subsidiaries had a total of 17,581 employees at the end of 2025. It was incorporated in New York in 1906.

    Its utilities have traditionally provided electric service on an integrated basis, meaning generation, transmission and distribution to retail customers. In Michigan, Ohio and the ERCOT area of Texas, restructuring laws split those services into separate rates. AEP earns revenue from retail and wholesale electricity sales and from delivering electricity over its transmission and distribution lines. Its segments include Vertically Integrated Utilities, Transmission and Distribution Utilities, AEP Transmission Holdco and Generation & Marketing.

    Industrial customers include chemical, oil and gas, pipeline, metals and paper makers, and data centers and cryptocurrency operations. State and federal regulators set the rates it can charge and the return it may earn on its investments. A service company subsidiary, AEPSC, provides accounting, engineering, legal and other services to the other AEP companies at cost.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Regulators may not let it recover the cost of its planned investments in capital improvements.
    • Its plans depend partly on the continued growth of data centers and other large power users connecting to its grid.
    • Execution risks in carrying out its large capital investment plans.
    • Regulation of its rates, including review of whether its costs were prudent and challenges to its transmission rates.
    • Delays and approval problems in siting, financing and building new projects.
