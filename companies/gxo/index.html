    What GXO Logistics does

    GXO runs warehouses for other companies. It provides warehousing and distribution, order fulfillment, e-commerce and reverse logistics (handling returned goods) services. Its customers are mostly large corporations in North America and Europe that have outsourced this work to it. It says its customer base is spread across more than one thousand customers.

    GXO reports its revenue by customer industry. In 2025, 49% came from omnichannel retail, 12% from technology and consumer electronics, 12% from industrial and manufacturing, 10% from food and beverage, 10% from consumer packaged goods and 7% from other industries. Its top five customers made up about 20% of revenue. Most revenue comes from the United Kingdom, the United States, the Netherlands, France, Spain and Italy.

    At the end of 2025 GXO had about 154,000 team members, including temporary workers hired through agencies, working in 1,043 facilities worldwide. It uses robots, automated sorting systems and its own warehouse software to speed up the work. Its business is busiest in the fourth quarter, when holiday e-commerce surges.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competition from other logistics providers and failing to meet customers' changing needs.
    • Rising labor costs and difficulty hiring and keeping workers, including temporary workers.
    • Penalties if it does not meet the performance levels set in its customer contracts.
    • Acquisitions and growth that may not go as planned or be managed well.
    • Currency swings and the risks of operating in many countries, with seasonal swings in demand.
    From GXO Logistics's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 25, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
