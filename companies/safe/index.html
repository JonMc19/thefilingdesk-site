    What Safehold does

    Safehold buys, manages and finances ground leases, and reports one business segment. A ground lease is a long-term lease of the land under a commercial building. The tenant owns or operates the building and usually pays the taxes, maintenance, insurance and operating costs. When the lease ends or the tenant defaults, the land, building and improvements generally go back to the landlord.

    It earns rent on the land. It says its ground leases typically have contractual rent increases, set as a percentage or tied to the consumer price index, and in some cases a share of the property's gross revenue. It compares its business to a fixed income investment, but says it also has the chance to take ownership of the buildings at no added cost when leases end.

    Its portfolio is spread across property types and regions and includes a hotel portfolio let to Park Hotels. It focuses on ground leases because it believes they meet a need in real estate capital markets.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Too few ground lease deals that fit its growth goals.
    • Rent increases that may not keep up with inflation or with the market value of the properties.
    • Not being able to renew expiring ground leases, re-lease the land or sell on good terms.
    • Concentration in a few tenants, places and industries, with limited recourse to creditworthy counterparties.
    • Reliance on tenants' reported property income and value estimates, which may be wrong.
    From Safehold's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
