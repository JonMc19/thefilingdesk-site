    What Aflac does

    Aflac sells supplemental health and life insurance in Japan and the United States. Supplemental insurance pays cash when a policyholder gets sick or hurt, to help with costs that primary health coverage does not pay. The company is licensed in all 50 U.S. states, the District of Columbia, several U.S. territories and Japan. Its marketing features the Aflac Duck.

    It reports two segments. Aflac Japan sells cancer, medical and other policies to people in Japan and says it is the largest insurer there by cancer and medical policies in force. Aflac U.S. sells supplemental health, life, group, dental and vision products, mostly through employers and sales agents. Aflac Japan's adjusted revenues were 53% of the company's total in 2025.

    Like other insurers, Aflac collects premiums, pays claims, and invests the premiums it holds until claims come due, so its results depend on its investments as well as on its insurance business. Because Aflac Japan sells in yen, changes in the yen/dollar exchange rate matter to its reported results.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its concentration of business in Japan, and swings in the yen/dollar exchange rate.
    • Interest rate changes and trouble matching the timing and currency of its assets and its obligations.
    • Defaults and downgrades on the bonds and loans it invests in.
    • A June 2025 incident of unauthorized access to its network, and the risk of future breaches.
    • Extensive regulation and changes to the law.
    From Aflac's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 25, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
