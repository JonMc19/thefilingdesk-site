    What Halliburton does

    Halliburton provides products and services to the energy industry. Its customers are oil and natural gas producers, from the major companies to national and independent ones. It helps them locate hydrocarbons, drill and evaluate wells, build and complete wells, and get more production from them over the life of a field. It has more than 46,000 employees in more than 70 countries.

    It has two operating segments. Completion and Production covers cementing, stimulation (including hydraulic fracturing), specialty chemicals, well intervention, pressure control, artificial lift and completion products. Drilling and Evaluation covers drilling fluids, drill bits, directional drilling, wellbore placement and field and reservoir modeling and evaluation. Its revenue comes from selling these services and products to the energy industry. No single customer represented more than 10% of its consolidated revenue in any period presented.

    Halliburton's total revenue decreased 3% in 2025 compared with 2024. In 2026 it plans to focus on international growth, its electric fracturing technology in North America, and digital and automation tools. It has described demand for its services as tied to the capital spending of oil and gas companies.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Swings in oil and natural gas prices, which change how much its customers spend on exploration and production.
    • Reductions in its customers' capital spending, including because of consolidation or a shift toward other energy sources.
    • Environmental, legal and regulatory requirements that affect its operations, including hydraulic fracturing.
    • Supply constraints on raw materials such as sand, chemicals, metals and electronic parts.
    • Political, military and economic events in the many countries where it works.
    From Halliburton's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 6, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
