    What Mercury General does

    Mercury General sells insurance, mostly personal car insurance, through 12 insurance subsidiaries in 11 states, principally California. It also writes homeowners, commercial auto, commercial property, mechanical protection and umbrella insurance. Its customers are drivers, home owners and some businesses.

    It earns money from the premiums policyholders pay, minus claims and costs, and from investing the premiums it holds. Its policies are mostly sold through independent agents, who earn a commission. Private passenger auto is the largest share of its premiums, followed by homeowners and commercial auto. California accounts for most of its premiums, with Texas second. The company says its underwriting and claims handling, together with its agent relationships, give it competitive advantages.

    The company had about 4,300 employees at the end of 2025. It is a holding company, so it relies on its regulated insurance subsidiaries for cash.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Depending heavily on California for revenue and operating profit.
    • Insurance rates that need regulators' approval in most states and can be affected by politics.
    • Charging adequate premiums for the risks it takes on, and having enough loss reserves to cover claims.
    • Losing its A.M. Best rating, which could hurt the volume of policies it sells.
    • Changes in interest rates or in the value of the securities and municipal bonds it holds.
    From Mercury General's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 17, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
