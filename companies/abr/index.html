    What Arbor Realty Trust does

    Arbor Realty Trust is a real estate investment trust, or REIT, that lends money for commercial property, mostly apartment buildings. A REIT generally pays no federal income tax on income it passes on to shareholders. Arbor makes loans and services them across the United States.

    It has two segments. The Structured Business invests in loans such as bridge loans, mezzanine loans and preferred equity on apartment, single-family rental and other commercial properties. The Agency Business originates, sells and services apartment loans through Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac and the federal housing agencies Ginnie Mae, FHA and HUD. It keeps the servicing rights on nearly all the loans it sells under those programs.

    It makes money from interest on the loans it holds, from fees and gains on loans it sells, and from servicing fees. It also originates its own "Private Label" loans, which it sells or pools into securities, keeping the riskiest slice. Most operations run through its operating partnership, and some income-producing assets sit in taxable subsidiaries.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Growing lending on single-family rental and build-to-rent projects carries more credit and development risk.
    • An economic slowdown or falling property values could hurt its loans and operations.
    • Changes to the government control of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac could hurt its agency business.
    • Borrowers may fall behind, and it must advance payments to investors on many loans it services.
    • Using loan modifications and owning foreclosed property could lead to losses.
    From Arbor Realty Trust's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
