    Value Rollins yourself

    Rollins' revenue rose 11.0% to $3.8 billion in fiscal 2025. Its operating margin was 19.3%, down from 19.4% a year earlier. Free cash flow was $650 million, up 12.1%. It paid out $545 million in dividends and buybacks, 84% of free cash flow.

    Operating margin is operating income as a share of revenue. Free cash flow is cash from operations minus capital spending. Return on capital is after-tax operating income as a share of the money invested in the business: debt plus equity, minus cash.

    About these figures. Total debt is the balance sheet's long-term borrowings plus the current portion: Rollins tags its bank borrowings as a line of credit, which the default rule missed for 2019 to 2023. Rollins had no borrowings before it borrowed to buy Clark Pest Control in 2019, so its debt counts as zero in earlier years. Share counts and per-share figures for earlier years are restated for a stock split (1.5-for-1), so every year is on today's share basis.

    Value per share in US$, with the growth rates above. The highlighted cell matches the current assumptions.
    How the model works. Free cash flow grows at the first rate for five years and the second rate for five more, and each year is discounted at the discount rate. Everything after year 10 is valued as year-10 cash flow × (1 + growth after year 10) ÷ (discount rate − growth after year 10), discounted back ten years. Net cash is added and the total is divided by diluted shares. The result depends entirely on the assumptions. It is not a forecast, a price target or a recommendation.
