    What AutoZone does

    AutoZone sells replacement parts and accessories for cars, sport utility vehicles, vans and light trucks. Its stores carry new and remade hard parts, maintenance items, accessories and some non-automotive products. Customers are people who fix their own vehicles and, through a commercial program, repair garages, dealers, service stations and fleet owners. The company also sells online and sells ALLDATA, software that repair shops use for diagnostics, repair and shop management.

    AutoZone makes its money by selling these products. It says it does not earn revenue from repair or installation services. At the end of its 2026 fiscal year it ran 6,863 stores in the United States, 1,001 in Mexico and 167 in Brazil. Most stores also deliver parts quickly to commercial customers and offer them credit.

    AutoZone says it competes on service, selection, price, location and its brand. Its competitors include national, regional and local parts chains, independent parts stores, online sellers, repair shops, auto dealers and general retailers that sell car supplies. It employed about 132,000 people, around 21,000 of them outside the United States.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Tariffs and trade policy changes that could raise the cost of the merchandise it imports.
    • Slower demand for parts, which depends on how many older vehicles are in service and how much people drive.
    • Losing key executives or failing to hire and keep qualified people.
    • Problems with its suppliers or its access to products.
    • Currency exchange rate swings affecting its stores outside the United States.
    From AutoZone's annual report on Form 10-K, filed October 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
