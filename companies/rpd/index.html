    What Rapid7 does

    Rapid7 sells cybersecurity software and services that help companies find and respond to digital threats. Its customers' security teams use its platform to see weak points in their systems, detect attacks and respond to them. It describes its approach as security operations, or SecOps.

    Its offerings include managed detection and response (MDR), where Rapid7's own staff watch a customer's systems for threats and act on them, a next-generation security information and event management (SIEM) tool that collects and analyzes security data, and exposure management, which finds and ranks the weaknesses attackers could use. It says its platform uses artificial intelligence and automation. Customers pay mainly through subscriptions and recurring contracts.

    Its customers span many industries, from technology and finance to healthcare and government, in organizations of many sizes. Rapid7 says its revenue is not concentrated, and no customer made up more than 1% of revenue in 2025. It says its markets are highly competitive and fragmented, and that it competes with established and emerging security vendors, including large companies that build security into their other products.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its results depend on customers renewing and expanding their subscriptions.
    • New and improved products may not win enough market acceptance.
    • It faces intense competition and may not stay profitable.
    • Quarterly results can vary and miss expectations.
    • Its plan for international expansion adds complexity to its operations.

    Piotroski F-score: how many of nine simple tests of profitability, funding and efficiency the company passed in the year (Piotroski, 2000); 8 or 9 is strong, 0 to 2 weak. Rule of 40: revenue growth plus free cash flow margin; software investors treat 40% or more as the bar. Accruals ratio: net income minus cash from operations, over average total assets; lower means profit is backed by cash.
