    What iRhythm does

    iRhythm provides heart-rhythm monitoring services. Its main product is a wire-free patch that a patient wears on the chest. The patch is worn for up to 14 days and records the heart's electrical signals continuously. The recordings are analyzed by the company's cloud-based software, and its technicians then prepare reports for doctors. Doctors use them to diagnose arrhythmias, which are irregular heartbeats.

    iRhythm's services include long-term continuous monitoring, short-term monitoring and mobile cardiac telemetry. These are usually ordered for patients who are suspected of having infrequent, hard-to-detect or symptom-free arrhythmias, but not life-threatening ones. iRhythm provides these services from its own testing facilities, which are enrolled in Medicare.

    The services are currently its only offerings, and its revenue depends on insurers and government programs paying for them. The company says its patch and software are cleared or approved in the United States, Europe and Japan. It employs about 2,400 full-time employees.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Reimbursement for its services could be reduced or changed in the United States or in other countries.
    • It may not be able to add commercial payors or expand coverage from the ones it has.
    • Its revenue relies entirely on its iRhythm Services, which could lose market acceptance.
    • Billing is complex and highly regulated, and audits or denied claims could force it to pay money back.
    • Competition in remote cardiac monitoring is intense.
