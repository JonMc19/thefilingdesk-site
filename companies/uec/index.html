    What Uranium Energy does

    Uranium Energy (UEC) is a uranium mining and development company. Uranium is the fuel for nuclear power plants. It uses in-situ recovery (ISR), a method that pulls uranium out of the ground with solutions pumped through wells rather than digging it up. It has three ISR platforms, one in South Texas and two in Wyoming, and it runs two of them from processing plants.

    It restarted production at its Christensen Ranch project in Wyoming in August 2024 and began production at its Burke Hollow mine in South Texas in April 2026. In fiscal 2026 it produced 229,294 pounds of uranium. It says it holds licensed production capacity of about 12.1 million pounds a year across its Wyoming and South Texas platforms. It also holds land in Canada's Athabasca Basin, anchored by the Roughrider Project.

    UEC says it follows a fully unhedged uranium strategy, so it has full exposure to uranium market prices. Through a subsidiary it is also pursuing uranium refining and conversion in the United States.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Needing large amounts of new financing to keep exploring, developing and mining.
    • Failing to develop or ramp up its projects, which have no proven or probable reserves established by a final feasibility study.
    • Uncertainty in its estimates of mineral resources and in its preliminary economic assessments.
    • Not getting or keeping the permits and licenses its operations need, and challenges to them in court.
    • Reclamation costs that could far exceed its estimates, and the success of its physical uranium purchase program.
    From Uranium Energy's annual report on Form 10-K, filed September 29, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
