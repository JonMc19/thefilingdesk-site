    What Genesco does

    Genesco sells branded footwear, apparel and accessories, mostly through its own stores and websites, and also wholesale. Net sales for fiscal 2026, the year ended January 31, 2026, were $2.4 billion. At that date it ran 1,236 stores, mainly in the United States and Puerto Rico, including 63 in Canada and 118 in the United Kingdom and Ireland.

    It reports four segments. Journeys Group has the Journeys, Journeys Kidz and Little Burgundy footwear chains. Schuh Group has the Schuh footwear chain. Johnston & Murphy Group has the Johnston & Murphy stores, website and wholesale business. Genesco Brands Group handles licensed brands such as Dockers and Levi's. Its wholesale accounts include department, discount and specialty stores and online retailers.

    Its Levi's license expires in May 2026 and it is exiting that business. It signed a multi-year license with Kontoor Brands to make and sell Wrangler footwear, with the first collection expected in the fall of 2026. In fiscal 2027 it plans to open about 23 stores and close about 75.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to lift sales at existing stores while carrying high fixed costs, and fewer shoppers in malls.
    • Not keeping up with fast-changing fashion trends and consumer preferences.
    • Depending on third-party vendors and licensors for the goods it sells, including licenses that end.
    • Trade restrictions, tariffs and foreign sourcing that raise costs or limit supply.
    • Risks in opening new stores and renewing leases, and in a changing retail environment.
    From Genesco's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 25, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
