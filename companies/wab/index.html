    What Wabtec does

    Wabtec, based in Pittsburgh, makes locomotives, equipment, systems and services for freight rail and passenger transit, and for mining, marine and industrial markets. Its products include braking systems, couplers, pantographs, door mechanisms, air conditioning for transit vehicles, engines and electric motors. It says its products are on most locomotives, freight cars, passenger transit cars and buses around the world. It has about 31,000 employees and operations in over 50 countries.

    It reports two segments, Freight and Transit, which have different markets and business drivers. It sells both original equipment and aftermarket parts and services. Its results depend on the activity, finances and capital spending plans of freight railroads, passenger transit agencies, and the equipment makers that serve them.

    George Westinghouse founded the original company in 1869 when he invented the air brake. Wabtec grew through acquisitions, including Faiveley Transport in 2017 and GE Transportation in 2019, and it says it plans more.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competition in a highly competitive industry.
    • Revenue that rises and falls with railway and transit markets and with government spending.
    • A backlog of orders that may not predict future revenue.
    • Failing to predict customer demand or to keep up with changes in technology.
    • Disruption of its supply chain, and acquisitions that bring risks or may not bring the expected gains.
    From Wabtec's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 13, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
