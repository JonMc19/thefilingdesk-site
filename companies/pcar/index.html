    Paccar's revenue fell 15.5% to $28.4 billion in fiscal 2025. Its net margin was 8.3%, down from 12.4% a year earlier. Free cash flow was $3.7 billion, down 3.4%. It paid out $2.3 billion in dividends and buybacks, 63% of free cash flow.

    About these figures. PACCAR's filings tag debt separately for its truck business and its financial services arm, with no company-wide total in the SEC's structured data, so debt and debt-based ratios are left blank. PACCAR's balance sheet splits assets and liabilities into current and long-term only for its truck business, not its financial services arm, so the current and quick ratios are blank. Share counts and per-share figures for earlier years are restated for a stock split (1.5-for-1), so every year is on today's share basis.

    How the model works. Free cash flow grows at the first rate for five years and the second rate for five more, and each year is discounted at the discount rate. Everything after year 10 is valued as year-10 cash flow × (1 + growth after year 10) ÷ (discount rate − growth after year 10), discounted back ten years. Net cash is added and the total is divided by diluted shares. The result depends entirely on the assumptions. It is not a forecast, a price target or a recommendation. The last close is the consolidated US closing price, from Databento.
