    What Hartford does

    The Hartford is an insurance holding company based in Connecticut. Its subsidiaries sell property and casualty insurance, employee group benefits insurance and services, and mutual funds and exchange-traded funds to individuals and businesses, mainly in the United States and also in the United Kingdom and other countries. Its oldest subsidiary, Hartford Fire Insurance Company, dates back to 1810.

    It reports five segments: Business Insurance, Personal Insurance, Property & Casualty Other Operations, Employee Benefits and Hartford Funds. Business Insurance sells products such as workers' compensation and covers small, middle-sized and large businesses. Personal Insurance covers individuals. Employee Benefits provides group coverage through employers. Hartford Funds manages investment funds.

    The company earns money from insurance premiums and from investing them, and pays out claims. It sells mainly through independent agents, brokers and wholesalers, and also through partnerships with payroll companies and affinity organizations. It competes with large national carriers, regional carriers and direct writers, plus newer entrants that use technology.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Losses from catastrophes, including natural disasters and terrorism.
    • Claims costs turning out higher than it expected.
    • Setting prices that do not match the risks it takes on.
    • Competition and new technology that could cut its market share.
    • Weaker financial strength or credit ratings, and losses from its investments.
