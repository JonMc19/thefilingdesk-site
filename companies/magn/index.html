    What Magnera does

    Magnera makes specialty materials, such as nonwoven fabrics and films, from natural and synthetic raw ingredients. Its products go into consumer goods like wipes, healthcare items, adult incontinence products, apparel, baby and feminine care products, air filters, and food and beverage items such as tea bags and coffee filters. It also provides technical solutions for infrastructure markets. Its customers range from global and national brands to private-label makers and smaller regional businesses.

    The company has two reporting segments. Americas is the larger, at about 57% of net sales, with plants in the United States, Brazil, Mexico, Canada and Colombia. Rest of World is about 43% of net sales, with plants in Europe, China and the Philippines. It sells through its own direct sales force and says competition rests on service, innovation, quality and price.

    Magnera was formed on November 4, 2024, when Treasure Holdco, a subsidiary of Berry Global, merged with Glatfelter, which took the Magnera name. Treasure's shareholders received 90% of the combined company.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Global economic conditions, inflation and supply chain disruptions.
    • Higher raw material and energy costs, or shortages of them.
    • Not competing successfully, and customers that may stop buying its products.
    • Acquisitions, mergers or sales of businesses that could harm it.
    • Loss of a key manufacturing facility in a catastrophe.

    Income statement

    From fiscal 2015 to 2021, earnings per share fell 31.6% a year and net income 31.1% a year, as new shares raised the share count by 2% and diluted each share's earnings. Magnera then lost money in fiscal 2022, 2023 and 2025.
