    What Oklo does

    Oklo is developing small nuclear power plants, which it calls Aurora powerhouses, to produce electricity and heat. The plants are designed to run on fresh, recycled or down-blended nuclear fuel. Oklo is also working to commercialize technology that recycles used nuclear fuel into fuel for its own plants and for others. It has not yet built a commercial plant.

    Oklo plans to earn money by selling power and heat to customers under long-term power purchase agreements, in which a customer agrees to buy the output of a plant. It says it has signed a master power agreement with Switch, a data center company, and non-binding letters of intent with other companies, and that it has been tentatively selected to supply Eielson Air Force Base in Alaska. A non-binding agreement can be dropped by the other side.

    The company has a site use permit from the US Department of Energy for the Idaho National Laboratory, where it plans its first powerhouse. It targets deploying that first powerhouse in 2028. It bought a radioisotope business, Atomic Alchemy, in February 2025, and plans a fuel recycling facility in Tennessee.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its limited experience building and running commercial nuclear power plants or selling power and heat directly to customers.
    • Operating losses it expects to continue, and a possible need for more outside capital.
    • Delays or higher costs in building its powerhouses and its fuel fabrication and recycling facilities.
    • Customers backing out of non-binding agreements, and a market for recycled nuclear fuel that may never develop.
    • Regulatory approvals from the NRC and the Department of Energy not arriving on the timeline it expects.
    From Oklo's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 17, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
