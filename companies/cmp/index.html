    What Compass Minerals does

    Compass Minerals mines and sells salt and plant nutrients. It has production sites in the United States, Canada and the United Kingdom, and it also offers records management services in the UK.

    It has two segments. The Salt segment makes and sells sodium chloride, the main product, and magnesium chloride in North America, and sodium chloride in the UK. Customers use them to de-ice roads, in consumer and professional products, in chemical production and in water treatment. Salt made up about 82% of sales in fiscal 2025, which ended September 30, 2025. The Plant Nutrition segment sells nearly all of its output as sulfate of potash, a specialty potassium fertilizer that also gives crops sulfur.

    The company describes a back-to-basics strategy of cutting costs, reducing debt and focusing on salt and plant nutrition. In 2025 it exited its fire retardant business and sold substantially all of that unit's assets. It also issued new senior notes to repay its bank loans and redeem part of its older notes. It employed 1,849 people at the end of fiscal 2025, most of them in the U.S. and Canada.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A restatement and a material weakness in its internal controls over financial reporting.
    • Mining hazards and geological conditions that could shut a mine, raise costs or delay production.
    • Weather, which drives both salt and plant nutrition demand and can swing from year to year.
    • Reliance on a limited number of key production and distribution sites and on critical equipment.
    • Higher costs or supply interruptions for natural gas, electricity, diesel and other inputs.
    From Compass Minerals's annual report on Form 10-K, filed December 12, 2025, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
