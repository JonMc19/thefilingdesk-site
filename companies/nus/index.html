    What Nu Skin does

    Nu Skin develops and sells skin care, personal care and wellness products in nearly 50 markets. Its two main product categories are beauty products, sold under brands such as Nu Skin and ageLOC, and wellness products, sold under brands such as Pharmanex. It also sells connected beauty and wellness devices that work with a mobile app.

    It sells through direct selling: a sales force of independent distributors who promote and sell the products person to person, including on social and digital platforms. Revenue in 2025 was $1.5 billion, and about 26% of it came from the United States. Because most revenue is outside the United States, currency changes affect its results in dollars.

    Nu Skin also runs Rhyz, an investment arm formed in 2018 that owns consumer, technology and manufacturing companies. Rhyz companies generated $223.6 million of 2025 revenue, or 15% of the reported total, excluding sales to its core Nu Skin business.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Challenges to its network marketing system, or direct selling laws that restrict or ban it in some markets.
    • Improper actions by its sales force, or social media platforms limiting the content its sales force posts.
    • Its products and business opportunities failing to win acceptance from its sales force and customers.
    • Intense competition and adverse publicity about its business or products.
    • Its operations in Mainland China, which are subject to significant government regulation.
    From Nu Skin's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 13, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
