    What Equinix does

    Equinix runs data centers where companies place their computing equipment and connect directly to each other. It is a real estate investment trust (REIT) for federal income tax purposes. Its customers include businesses, network and cloud providers, and software companies. It says its global footprint covers 280 data centers in 77 markets in 36 countries, in the Americas, Asia-Pacific, and Europe, the Middle East and Africa.

    It earns money in two main ways. Infrastructure offerings rent customers space and power in its IBX data centers, which are neutral colocation sites, mostly billed on the space and power used under fixed-length contracts that bring in monthly recurring revenue. It also sells support services such as on-site technician help and managed platforms. Interconnection offerings, billed by the number of connections a customer has, link customers to each other inside and between its data centers, for example through its Equinix Fabric service.

    It also has xScale data centers, built for a small group of very large cloud companies and developed and run through joint ventures with partners. Equinix says it has over 10,500 customers.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Power outages, shortages or higher power costs at its data centers.
    • Failure of physical infrastructure, or damage to customer equipment in its data centers.
    • Failing to renew its data center leases on good terms.
    • Reliance on third parties for internet connectivity to its data centers.
    • Cybersecurity incidents, which it says it has had in the past.
    From Equinix's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 11, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
