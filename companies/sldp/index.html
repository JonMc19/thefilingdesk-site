    What Solid Power does

    Solid Power develops solid-state batteries. Its core product is a sulfide-based solid electrolyte, the material that carries lithium ions inside a battery cell. In a standard lithium-ion cell the electrolyte is a liquid or gel. Solid Power says its material could improve energy density, battery life and safety. It is targeting electric vehicles first.

    Its plan is to make and sell the electrolyte to large battery makers and automakers that build their own cells. It also intends to license its cell designs and manufacturing processes. It does not plan to make commercial battery cells itself, which it expects to keep its capital needs lower than those of cell makers. It says its rivals are, or plan to be, battery manufacturers.

    Today the company makes electrolyte on two pilot manufacturing lines, and uses it for samples for customers and for its own cell development. It also builds test cells on pilot cell lines. It expects to start a pilot line using a continuous manufacturing process by the end of 2026. It also gets part of its revenue from government contracts and grants.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its business plan is untested, and it may not reach commercial production or earn material revenue.
    • Its partner agreements are non-exclusive, so partners could choose other battery technologies.
    • It may need more money, and that money might not be available on reasonable terms.
    • Growth depends on consumers choosing electric vehicles.
    • Its reliance on complex equipment, a key ingredient (lithium sulfide) and government funding.
    From Solid Power's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 25, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
