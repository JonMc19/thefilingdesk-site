    What Zoetis does

    Zoetis makes and sells animal health products: medicines, vaccines, diagnostic tests, devices and genetic tests. Its products are for dogs, cats and horses, called companion animals, and for cattle, swine, poultry, fish and sheep, called livestock. Its customers are veterinarians, pet owners and livestock producers. It was a business unit of Pfizer until it became a separate company in 2012.

    Companion animal products brought in about 70% of 2025 revenue and livestock products about 29%. Its products fall into seven major categories, including parasite control, vaccines, skin conditions, antibiotics, and pain and sedation. It reports two segments, the United States and International, and sells in many other countries. Its ten top-selling products and product lines contributed about 57% of revenue.

    Zoetis also makes products under contract for other companies and sells a few human health products. Together these were a small share of revenue. It says the animal health industry is highly competitive, and that generic and other products may be seen as cheaper than its own.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Strong competition across the animal health industry.
    • Relying on a handful of top-selling products for much of its results.
    • Safety, quality or effectiveness concerns about its products, and the regulatory action that can follow.
    • Generic and other lower-cost products taking sales.
    • Animal disease outbreaks and limits on antibiotic use in food animals.
    From Zoetis's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
