    What National Health Investors does

    National Health Investors is a real estate investment trust, or REIT, a company that owns property and passes most of its income to shareholders. It owns, leases and finances senior housing communities and medical facilities, including independent living, assisted living and entrance fee communities, and skilled nursing facilities. It also lends to its tenants and operators through mortgages, construction loans and other financing.

    It reports two segments. In Real Estate Investments, it had gross investments of $2.7 billion in 176 properties in 32 states at the end of 2025, leased mostly under triple-net leases to 31 tenants. In a triple-net lease the tenant pays the property's taxes, insurance and upkeep as well as rent. Its revenue here is mainly rent plus interest on loans to operators. In the Senior Housing Operating Portfolio, it owns 26 senior housing communities in 13 states, hires outside managers to run them and pays them a management fee. Its revenue there comes from fees paid by residents.

    The company has an investment with Life Care Services in an entrance fee retirement community called Timber Ridge. A member of its board is also chairman of the board of NHC, and the annual report says his interests may differ from those of shareholders.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Depending on the success of its tenants, managers and borrowers, who may run into financial trouble or bankruptcy.
    • A few tenants account for a significant share of its rental income.
    • Its investments are concentrated in healthcare properties.
    • Changes in Medicare and Medicaid rules and payments that affect its operators' cash flows.
    • Property development and construction that may not reach the results it expects.
