    What Williams Companies does

    Williams is an energy company that moves natural gas. It gathers gas from producers' wells, treats and processes it, then transports and stores it for customers such as local gas utilities, power generators, industrial users and gas marketers. Processing separates natural gas liquids, or NGLs, which are products such as ethane and propane used in petrochemicals, heating and refining. Williams serves approximately 800 customers and says it owns an interest in and operates over 32,000 miles of pipeline in 24 states and the Gulf of America.

    It reports four segments: Transmission, Power & Gulf; Northeast G&P; West; and Gas & NGL Marketing Services. Its interstate pipelines, including Transco and Northwest Pipeline, are regulated by the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission, which oversees the rates it can charge. Most of its pipeline and storage capacity is fully contracted under long-term firm reservation contracts.

    In its gas processing business, it is mainly paid fees based on the volume processed. In 2025, approximately 93 percent of NGL production volumes were under fee-based contracts. The rest are commodity-based contracts, where Williams is paid in NGLs.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Volatile prices for natural gas, NGLs, oil and other commodities.
    • Not being able to replace, extend or add customer contracts on favorable terms.
    • Credit risk from customers and other parties it does business with.
    • Competition in the energy industry.
    • Opposition to pipelines and facilities, and costs of climate change.
