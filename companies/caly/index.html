    What Callaway Golf does

    Callaway Golf designs, makes and sells golf clubs, golf balls, apparel, bags and accessories. Its brands are Callaway Golf, Odyssey (putters), TravisMathew (apparel) and OGIO (bags and gear). It sells through golf course pro shops, off-course and sporting goods retailers, online retailers, distributors and mass merchants, and directly to consumers through its websites and some stores in Japan and Korea. It generates a large majority of its revenue from golf-related products.

    It reports two segments: Golf Equipment, which makes clubs and balls, and Apparel, Gear and Other. It assembles clubs mainly in Monterrey, Mexico, and makes balls in Chicopee, Massachusetts, and it also uses contract manufacturers in Asia.

    The company changed shape in 2025 and early 2026. It sold its Jack Wolfskin outdoor apparel business on May 31, 2025. Effective January 1, 2026, it sold a 60% ownership interest in its Topgolf and Toptracer businesses to funds managed by Leonard Green & Partners, and it kept a 40% stake in Topgolf. On January 15, 2026 it changed its name back from Topgolf Callaway Brands to Callaway Golf, and its stock ticker changed from MODG to CALY. Both sold businesses are shown as discontinued operations.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Dependence on golf-related products for most revenue, with limited room to grow sales of clubs and balls.
    • Weak consumer spending, which hurts sales of discretionary products like golf equipment.
    • Changes to the Rules of Golf, such as limits on golf ball specifications, that could hurt its products.
    • Executing the Topgolf and Jack Wolfskin sales, and having little say as a minority owner of Topgolf.
    • U.S. import tariffs and trade policy changes, since much of its production is outside the U.S.
    From Callaway Golf's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
