    What Gogo does

    Gogo provides in-flight internet and related services for business jets and for military and government aircraft. It describes itself as the only provider offering connections over multiple satellite orbits and bands built for business and military aviation. Its customers are in over 100 countries.

    It connects aircraft in two ways: its own air-to-ground network, which uses towers on the ground, and satellite service from low earth orbit and geostationary satellites owned by its network partners. It sells services, plus hardware and software, and a customer support team backs them. It sells equipment through aircraft makers and dealers, and also directly to every maker of business aircraft.

    On December 3, 2024, Gogo bought Satcom Direct, a business aviation connectivity company, for cash of about $375,000,000, 5,000,000 shares of its stock, and up to $225,000,000 more in cash or stock if performance goals are met over four years.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Not being able to keep earning revenue from its connectivity and other services.
    • Relying on aircraft makers and dealers for equipment sales.
    • Single-source third parties that make its equipment components and run parts of its network.
    • Competition that could force prices down and cost it market position.
    • Risks that come with U.S. and non-U.S. government contracts and fixed-price development contracts.
    From Gogo's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
