    What Recursion Pharmaceuticals does

    Recursion is a drug developer that uses software, machine learning and large amounts of lab data to find new medicines. It calls itself a clinical-stage "TechBio" company, meaning its drug candidates are being tested in people but none has been approved. Its in-house platform, the Recursion Operating System, is meant to map biology, design small molecules and plan clinical trials.

    Recursion has no approved products and has not earned revenue from selling a drug. Its income comes from partners that pay it for data and for progress on drug programs, mainly Roche and Genentech and Sanofi. It says it has received over $500 million in partner payments so far, and it is eligible for more milestone payments and royalties if programs advance and become medicines.

    The company runs its own pipeline of drug candidates, including one for a rare inherited condition called familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP), which causes growths in the colon. It says it is prioritizing its clinical programs and streamlining operations, and it spends heavily on research and development.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It has no approved products and has lost money since it began, so it needs to raise more funding or cut programs.
    • Its drug candidates are in early or mid-stage testing, and trials can fail, be delayed or show safety problems.
    • Its AI-based approach to finding drugs is unproven and may not produce successful medicines.
    • Raising money by selling more shares could dilute current holders and limit what it can do.
    • It depends on partners and outside providers for parts of its work, and on its computing systems.
