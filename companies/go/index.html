    What Grocery Outlet does

    Grocery Outlet is a discount grocery chain. It sells name-brand packaged goods and fresh products, mostly through stores run by independent operators. As of January 3, 2026, it had 570 stores in 16 states, from California and Washington to Pennsylvania, Georgia and Virginia. Its headquarters is in Emeryville, California.

    The company buys opportunistically, meaning a central buying team takes surplus or excess products from suppliers when they are offered. It says its prices are generally 40% to 70% below those of conventional retailers. The assortment changes often, and it also carries everyday staples and has recently added its own private label products.

    The independent operators are separate businesses that put up capital to start their store, run ordering, merchandising, local marketing and hiring, and share store profits with Grocery Outlet. Grocery Outlet plans to run some newly opened stores itself in fiscal 2026. It is carrying out a restructuring plan and an optimization plan.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Depending on suppliers to keep offering surplus products at attractive prices.
    • Not matching consumer demand with the right mix of opportunistic goods.
    • Disruption in its distribution network or at a third-party system vendor.
    • Newly opened stores that may not reach expected sales, and a revised growth plan.
    • Trouble attracting and keeping independent operators and other employees.
    From Grocery Outlet's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 4, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
