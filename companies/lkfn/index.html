    What Lakeland Financial does

    Lakeland Financial is a bank holding company based in Warsaw, Indiana. Its only operating subsidiary is Lake City Bank, a community bank founded in 1872. The bank takes deposits and makes commercial and consumer loans. It also offers treasury management for businesses, private banking, trust, wealth advisory and brokerage services.

    At the end of 2025 the company had consolidated total assets of $7.0 billion. The bank had 55 offices in fifteen counties, 46 in Northern Indiana and nine in the Indianapolis area. It opened its newest branch in Westfield, Indiana, in September 2025. Most of its assets and income are in Indiana.

    The company reports one segment. It makes money mainly from the business of banking, and it says it focuses on building broad commercial relationships and gathering deposits from retail and commercial customers. Its customers come from many industries, such as commercial real estate, manufacturing and agriculture. The holding company itself earns income mainly from dividends paid by the bank.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Interest rate changes that shrink the gap between what it earns on loans and pays on deposits.
    • Loan losses, if its credit risk controls fail or its allowance for losses proves too small.
    • Large amounts of commercial real estate, business and farm-related loans.
    • A downturn in Indiana, where its business is concentrated.
    • Worries about banks in general that can hurt confidence in individual banks.
