    What General Dynamics does

    General Dynamics is an aerospace and defense company. It builds Gulfstream business jets and services private aircraft through Jet Aviation; designs and builds nuclear submarines, destroyers and supply ships for the US Navy; makes wheeled and tracked combat vehicles, weapons and ammunition; and provides information technology services and communications and encryption equipment to the military, intelligence agencies and other government departments.

    It reports four segments. In 2025 Marine Systems, the shipyards Electric Boat, Bath Iron Works and NASSCO, made up 32% of revenue; Technologies, the IT and defense electronics business, 26%; Aerospace 25%; and Combat Systems, the vehicles and munitions business, 17%. The US government accounted for 68% of revenue. It competes with other defense contractors in the US and abroad, and Gulfstream faces several rival jet makers. Its shipyards have one main competitor, which is also its partner on submarines.

    Electric Boat leads the construction of the Navy's new Columbia-class ballistic-missile submarines and builds Virginia-class attack submarines with a partner. The company has about 117,000 employees.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its dependence on the US government, whose defense spending can change and whose funding can be delayed.
    • The government can end contracts at will, and it closely audits its contractors.
    • Cost overruns if its estimates for labor, materials and schedules on long contracts prove wrong.
    • Falling demand for business jets.
    • Its reliance on suppliers, sometimes only one or two, for key parts and materials.
    From General Dynamics's annual report on Form 10-K, filed January 30, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
