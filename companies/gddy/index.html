    What GoDaddy does

    GoDaddy sells the basic tools that small businesses and individuals need to get online: domain names (website addresses), website building and hosting, email, marketing tools, and ways to take payments. It says it has 20.4 million customers. Most are "Independents", mainly micro-businesses with fewer than five employees. It also serves web designers and developers, domain investors, and other domain registrars.

    It has two segments. Core Platform covers domain registrations and renewals, the aftermarket where people buy and sell used domain names, hosting and website security, and was about 62% of 2025 revenue. Applications and Commerce covers website builders, Managed WordPress, marketing tools, Microsoft 365 email and its payments and point-of-sale products, and was about 38%. Its payments service is available in the US and Canada.

    GoDaddy manages about 81 million domains. It also runs a registry that provides back-end technology for about 170 top-level domains (the endings such as .biz). It uses AI tools under the name Airo to help customers pick names, make logos and build sites, and it relies on Amazon Web Services to run its platform.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to keep selling to new and existing customers.
    • Heavy competition and pricing choices that could cost it customers.
    • Its dependence on Amazon Web Services and on partners such as Microsoft to deliver its products.
    • Changes in how people use the internet, including AI-based tools that may reduce the value of domain names and websites.
    • Rules from ICANN and domain registries, and fee increases they set, that it does not control.
    From GoDaddy's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 25, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
