    What Newsmax does

    Newsmax is a television broadcaster and publisher of news and opinion content. It runs the Newsmax TV news channel, the ad-supported streaming channel Newsmax2, a syndicated radio show and podcasts, the Newsmax.com website and affiliated sites, social media accounts, and more than a dozen digital and print publications that it sells by paid subscription.

    It earns money by selling advertising to marketers, from license fees paid by cable, satellite and streaming distributors that carry its channels, and from subscriptions. It says its content reaches over 100 million U.S. homes. It also licenses its channels and brands to partners in many countries, including several in Europe, Israel, Cyprus and the Dominican Republic.

    Newsmax Inc. is a holding company that owns Newsmax Media, which has subsidiaries for broadcasting, radio, insurance, publishing, health products and media strategy. The company says audience ratings and engagement are key drivers of its advertising revenue and of demand from distributors.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Past losses, and plans to keep or increase cash that may not work.
    • Changes in how people watch and which platforms distribute its content.
    • Dependence on a limited number of distributors for a significant part of its revenue, and the need to renew carriage agreements.
    • Lower advertising spending, and audience measurement that is hard to rely on.
    • Difficulty renewing programming rights on good terms.
    From Newsmax's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Piotroski F-score: how many of nine simple tests of profitability, funding and efficiency the company passed in the year (Piotroski, 2000); 8 or 9 is strong, 0 to 2 weak. Rule of 40: revenue growth plus free cash flow margin; software investors treat 40% or more as the bar. Altman Z'': working capital, retained earnings, operating income and equity weighed against assets and liabilities (Altman, 1995); Altman puts above 2.6 in the safe zone and below 1.1 in the distress zone. Accruals ratio: net income minus cash from operations, over average total assets; lower means profit is backed by cash.
