    What Donnelley Financial Solutions does

    Donnelley Financial Solutions, known as DFIN, sells software and services that help companies and investment funds meet securities regulations and file required reports with the SEC. Its clients include public and private companies, mutual funds and other regulated investment firms, mainly in North America, Europe and Asia. It became an independent public company on October 1, 2016, when R.R. Donnelley spun it off.

    It reports four segments: software and services (compliance and communications) for each of two groups of clients, capital markets companies and investment companies. Its main software products are ActiveDisclosure, used to prepare and file SEC reports; Venue, a virtual data room for confidential deal documents; and Arc Suite, for investment company reporting. The services side includes filing agent work, document preparation, and print and distribution.

    Software made up about 44% of capital markets net sales in 2025, and about 53% of investment companies net sales. DFIN says the compliance industry is highly competitive and fragmented, and that it expects more competition, including from AI-enabled or self-filing services.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Clients doing more of their own financial disclosure work, which changes the market for its services.
    • More competition from new entrants, including AI-enabled or self-filing tools.
    • Its business relies heavily on the global financial services industry, which can be uneven.
    • Changes in technology and regulation that could make its offerings less useful.
    • Exposure to currency swings and to credit markets, since it operates in many countries.
    From Donnelley Financial Solutions's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 17, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Piotroski F-score: how many of nine simple tests of profitability, funding and efficiency the company passed in the year (Piotroski, 2000); 8 or 9 is strong, 0 to 2 weak.
