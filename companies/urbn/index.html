    What Urban Outfitters does

    Urban Outfitters sells fashion apparel, accessories and home goods under the Anthropologie, Free People, FP Movement, Urban Outfitters and Nuuly brands. Anthropologie also covers Terrain and Maeve. Its first Urban Outfitters store opened in 1970. It sells through its own and franchisee-owned stores, websites, mobile apps, social media and customer contact centers. Sales were about $6.2 billion in the fiscal year ended January 31, 2026.

    It reports three segments. Retail covers its stores and digital channels and made up about 85.7% of net sales. Subscription is Nuuly, which mainly rents women's apparel for a monthly fee. Wholesale designs and sells Free People, FP Movement and Urban Outfitters products, such as the BDG and iets frans collections, to department and specialty stores, other online sellers and its own Retail segment.

    The company says its stores are meant to give each brand its own setting and that it manages the Retail segment as one omni-channel business, not store and online separately. Its fiscal year ends on January 31.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Weaker consumer spending when the economy slows, inflation rises or incomes fall.
    • Guessing wrongly about changing fashion tastes, which the company calls a material risk.
    • Its heavy reliance on production outside the United States, and tariffs and other trade measures.
    • Competition from many websites and apps, and the need for effective marketing to draw customers.
    • A failure or disruption of the information technology systems it relies on.
    From Urban Outfitters's annual report on Form 10-K, filed April 1, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
