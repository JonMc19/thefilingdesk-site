    What CoreCivic does

    CoreCivic owns and runs prisons, detention centers and residential reentry centers for government agencies, and leases correctional real estate to them. Its customers are federal, state and local governments. It says it is the nation's largest owner of partnership correctional, detention and residential reentry facilities. Its facilities also offer education, job training and treatment programs, along with health care and food service.

    It reports three segments. CoreCivic Safety operated 44 correctional and detention facilities at the end of 2025, with room for about 68,000 beds. CoreCivic Community owned and ran 20 residential reentry centers, with about 4,000 beds, and also provides electronic monitoring. CoreCivic Properties owned five properties with about 8,000 beds that it leases to government agencies.

    In Safety and Community, the company is paid a daily rate for each bed used. Contracts typically run one to five years, and most let the government agency end them at any time without cause. They also depend on yearly legislative funding. The company has operated since 1983.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Government contracts that can be ended at any time, are not renewed, or are rebid to competitors.
    • Government budget limits, shutdowns and efforts to cut the federal deficit that could reduce payments.
    • Competition from other providers, including government agencies that run facilities themselves.
    • Its ability to win new contracts depends on many factors outside its control.
    • Demand for detention beds may not grow as it expects.
    From CoreCivic's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 20, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
