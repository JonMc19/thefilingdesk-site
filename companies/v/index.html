    What Visa does

    Visa runs a payments network, VisaNet, that processes card and other payments between consumers, sellers and the banks that issue and accept cards. It handles authorization, clearing and settlement of each transaction. Visa does not lend to cardholders; banks and other clients issue the Visa-branded credit, debit and prepaid products. Visa says it had over 5 billion payment credentials, usable at more than 400 million seller locations.

    Visa earns fees from its clients for using its network. It reports service revenue and data processing revenue, and it also sells commercial payment and money movement products (such as Visa Direct) and value-added services such as risk management and fraud detection. It says total payments and cash volume was $18 trillion in fiscal 2026.

    As of September 30, 2026, Visa had approximately 34,800 employees in 90 countries and territories.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Regulation and scrutiny of card fees and network rules, which could cut what it can charge.
    • Intense competition, and sellers and processors pushing to lower the cost of accepting cards.
    • Dependence on banks, acquirers, processors and other partners that it may be costly to win and keep.
    • Its duty to cover settlement losses of clients, which could cost it significant money.
    • Failing to keep pace with new payment technologies, or a breach or outage of its networks.
    From Visa's annual report on Form 10-K, filed November 5, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
