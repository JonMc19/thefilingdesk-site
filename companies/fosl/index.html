    What Fossil does

    Fossil designs, develops, markets and distributes fashion accessories: watches, jewelry, handbags, small leather goods, belts and sunglasses. It owns the Fossil, Skagen, Michele, Relic and Zodiac brands. It also makes and sells watches and other products under licenses for brands including Armani Exchange, Diesel, Emporio Armani, Michael Kors, Skechers and Tory Burch.

    Watches are its main business. Its accessory lines, which cover everything except watches, were 16.0% of consolidated net sales in fiscal 2025, down from 19.7% in fiscal 2024. It sells to style-conscious shoppers of many ages worldwide, through wholesale customers, its own retail stores and e-commerce.

    In September 2024 it appointed a new chief executive, who started a Turnaround Plan to return the company to profitable growth. The plan has focused on its core watch business, cutting costs, which included closing underperforming retail stores and moving some smaller international markets to distributors, and strengthening its balance sheet. In 2025 it replaced its revolving credit facility and completed an exchange offer for its notes that were due in 2026.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to anticipate fashion trends and to develop products shoppers accept.
    • Losing a license for a major fashion brand name, and the sales that come with it.
    • Relying on suppliers and assembly factories, with China the source of most of its foreign supply.
    • It has no long-term contracts with customers, who control how much they buy.
    • Not carrying out its Turnaround Plan and growth strategy as intended.
    From Fossil's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
