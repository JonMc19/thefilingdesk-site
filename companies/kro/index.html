    What Kronos Worldwide does

    Kronos makes and sells titanium dioxide, or TiO2, a white pigment. TiO2 gives color, brightness and opacity to coatings, plastics and paper, and also to inks, cosmetics and pharmaceuticals. Kronos says no other white pigment has the physical properties to match it as cost-effectively, and that the extenders used alongside it cannot duplicate its opacity.

    The company sells, directly and through distributors and agents, to about 3,000 customers in 100 countries, with most sales in Europe, North America and the Asia Pacific region. It also provides technical services to those customers. It earns its money from selling pigment, so its results follow demand and prices for TiO2, which it says have generally tracked world economic output.

    In July 2024 it bought the other half of Louisiana Pigment Company, a pigment plant it had run as a joint venture with Venator, for $185 million less a working capital adjustment. The plant is now wholly owned and was merged into a Kronos subsidiary in 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Swings in demand and prices for its products, which could cut earnings or cause operating losses.
    • A concentrated, highly competitive TiO2 industry with price pressure.
    • Higher costs or limited supply of raw materials, with many contracts requiring fixed quantities.
    • The Louisiana Pigment Company purchase may not bring the benefits it expects.
    • Debt that could limit its flexibility, and currency and interest rate changes.
    From Kronos Worldwide's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 9, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
