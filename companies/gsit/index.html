    What GSI Technology does

    GSI Technology designs memory and AI chips and has them made by outside factories. Its older business is high-speed synchronous SRAM, a kind of fast memory, sold mainly for test and measurement equipment, networking and telecommunications gear, and military, defense and aerospace uses. Its newer one is the associative processing unit, or APU, a chip that does computing inside the memory to run AI and search tasks at low power at the edge, meaning close to where data is collected.

    The SRAM business pays for APU development. Revenue from the APU has not been material so far. GSI relies on a few large customers for SRAM, including KYEC, Nokia and Cadence Design Systems. It has also won four U.S. Department of Defense small business research contracts to try the APU in military and space uses. They have not yet led to follow-on production contracts.

    In its 2026 fiscal year, GSI says net revenue rose 22%, driven by SRAM sales to chip design and simulation customers. It is based in Sunnyvale, California, with operations in Taiwan and Israel.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its future depends on new associative computing chips, including Gemini-II and Plato, being developed and adopted.
    • A few large customers, including KYEC, Nokia and Cadence Design Systems, account for a significant share of sales.
    • It relies on U.S. government funding, and government shutdowns could hurt it.
    • Tariffs, export controls and trade disputes could hurt its business.
    • It has lost money and may keep losing money.
    From GSI Technology's annual report on Form 10-K, filed June 5, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
