    What Cadiz does

    Cadiz is a water company that owns land, water rights, pipelines and water filtration technology in Southern California. Its customers are public and private water systems, government agencies and businesses. Its assets sit between major water systems that serve population centers in the Southwestern United States.

    Its main asset is a property of about 46,000 acres in the Mojave Desert with groundwater that recharges naturally. The company has permits for a project, the Mojave Groundwater Bank, that would conserve water from the aquifer for use by California communities and store imported water underground. It has a long-term lease to build a pipeline along an existing railroad right-of-way to the Colorado River Aqueduct, and it owns a natural gas pipeline that runs north from the property. It has also farmed on the property for decades.

    Cadiz says its development work has not yet produced significant revenue. Its plans depend on government approvals and on raising money, and it carries debt.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its development activities have not produced significant revenue and it may never be profitable.
    • Its projects need government permits and approvals that could be denied or come with conditions.
    • It may not be able to build and fund its Mojave Groundwater Bank.
    • Missing payments on its debt, or failing to raise more money, would hurt its plans.
    • Issuing more shares and equity incentive plans would dilute current holders.
