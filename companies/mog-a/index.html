    What Moog does

    Moog designs and makes motion and fluid controls and control systems: the parts that move and steer things precisely, such as flight controls on aircraft. It also acts as a systems integrator, putting parts together into larger systems. Its customers are in aerospace and defense and in industrial markets.

    It has four operating segments: Space and Defense, Military Aircraft, Commercial Aircraft and Industrial. Space and Defense includes controls for spacecraft engines, missile steering and turreted weapons. The Industrial segment sells precision controls for industrial uses, including energy, and medical devices, which it says it sells partly through distributors.

    Moog says its twelve-month backlog, the confirmed orders it expects to count as revenue within a year, was $3.0 billion as of September 27, 2025. It sells mainly through its own technical sales staff and uses manufacturers' representatives in some domestic aerospace markets. Moog says it competes with other suppliers and with large, vertically integrated prime contractors.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competition from rivals, some larger and with more resources, in a market that has consolidated.
    • Research and development that may not succeed and that could reduce sales and earnings.
    • Failing to protect its intellectual property or to defend against infringement claims.
    • Failing to find, buy or fit in strategic acquisitions, or problems with its portfolio and footprint changes.
    From Moog's annual report on Form 10-K, filed November 26, 2025, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
