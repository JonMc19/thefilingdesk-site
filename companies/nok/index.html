    What Nokia does

    Nokia builds the network equipment and software that telecom companies, data center operators, companies and governments use to move data. Its products include optical and IP routing gear, fixed-line broadband equipment, software for running networks, and radio equipment for mobile networks. Nokia also earns money from licensing its patents.

    In 2025 Nokia ran four business groups. Network Infrastructure delivered net sales of EUR 8.0 billion. Mobile Networks delivered EUR 7.8 billion. Cloud and Network Services, which sells network software, and Nokia Technologies, which manages patents, make up the rest. Nokia Technologies delivered net sales of EUR 1.5 billion. From 2026 the company folds these into two segments, Network Infrastructure and Mobile Infrastructure.

    In February 2025 Nokia bought Infinera, an optical networking supplier, to expand its data center business. Nokia's report says it works in about 130 countries and its customers include telecom providers and large cloud companies.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to carry out its new strategy or to gain the benefits it expects from deals such as Infinera.
    • Relying on a limited number of large customers, mainly telecom operators.
    • Falling behind in fast-moving network and data center technology.
    • Disruptions in manufacturing, services or its supply chain.
    • Weakness in its patent licensing, which depends on agreements that may not cover future products.
    From Nokia's annual report on Form 20-F, filed March 5, 2026, in our words; the 20-F has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
