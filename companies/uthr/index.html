    What United Therapeutics does

    United Therapeutics is a drug company focused on rare diseases, especially pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH), a disease of high blood pressure in the lungs. In the United States it sells Tyvaso (an inhaled drug, in nebulizer and dry-powder form), Remodulin (a drug given by continuous infusion, with its Remunity pumps), Orenitram (a tablet) and Adcirca (a tablet). Tyvaso is also approved for pulmonary hypertension linked to interstitial lung disease. It also sells Unituxin, a cancer drug for high-risk neuroblastoma.

    It earns revenue by selling these drugs, and it says it relies heavily on those based on treprostinil. Outside the United States it sells Tyvaso, Remodulin and Unituxin. It has other drugs in development for pulmonary fibrosis and other diseases.

    United Therapeutics is a public benefit corporation. Its charter says it will develop new drug therapies and work on technologies that expand the supply of transplantable organs, including organ manufacturing. It relies on outside companies for parts of its work, such as Lilly, which makes Adcirca, and Sanner GmbH, which makes cartridges for a Remodulin pump.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Heavy reliance on sales of its treprostinil-based drugs.
    • Drugs in development failing in clinical trials, so they could not be sold.
    • Competition from established or newly developed drugs.
    • Insurers and government programs not covering its drugs or paying enough, including Medicaid funding cuts.
    • Manufacturing risks and its reliance on outside companies for key activities.
