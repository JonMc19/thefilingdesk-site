    What Ross Stores does

    Ross Stores runs two chains of off-price stores that sell apparel, shoes, accessories and home goods: Ross Dress for Less and dd's DISCOUNTS. Off-price means it sells name-brand and designer goods at prices below what department and specialty stores charge. As of January 31, 2026, Ross had 1,904 locations in 44 states, the District of Columbia, Guam and Puerto Rico, and it also ran 363 dd's DISCOUNTS stores in 22 states.

    Ross says its prices run 20% to 60% below department and specialty store regular prices, and that its customers are mainly from middle income households. dd's DISCOUNTS sells more moderately priced goods to households with lower to more moderate incomes. Revenue comes from selling goods in its stores. The company says it is the largest off-price apparel and home fashion chain in the United States.

    Ross buys most of its goods directly from manufacturers, often late in the buying cycle. It buys leftover or canceled orders and overruns, and takes goods straight from brands and factories. Some goods are stored in its warehouses and sold later, a practice it calls packaway. It generally does not ask vendors for promotional allowances or return privileges. New goods arrive in stores three to six times a week.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Changes in U.S. trade or tax policy, such as tariffs, on goods made in other countries.
    • Unexpected changes in consumer spending and tastes, and strong competition in retail.
    • Depending on a steady supply of attractive brand name merchandise at desirable discounts.
    • Managing inventory, markdowns and inventory losses so it can reach its planned margins.
    • Finding good new store sites and entering new markets to keep growing.
    From Ross Stores's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 31, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
