    What Alcoa does

    Alcoa is a vertically integrated aluminum company. It mines bauxite, the ore aluminum comes from, refines the ore into alumina, smelts and casts aluminum metal, and generates energy. Through direct and indirect ownership, it has 25 operating locations in eight countries, mainly Australia, Brazil, Canada, Iceland, Norway, Spain and the United States. At the end of 2025 it had approximately 14,900 employees in 16 countries.

    It reports two segments. The Alumina segment mostly holds the bauxite mines and alumina refineries and sells smelter-grade and other alumina. The Aluminum segment holds the smelting and casting operations and most of the company's energy assets. Alcoa sells to customers in North America, South America, Europe, the Middle East, Australia, China and other parts of Asia.

    Aluminum is a commodity traded on the London Metal Exchange and priced daily, and alumina is priced against an index, so both prices swing and affect Alcoa's results. Alcoa competes with other bauxite miners, alumina suppliers, aluminum producers and makers of other materials such as steel and plastics. In 2025 it formed a joint venture to keep its San Ciprián complex in Spain running and sold its stake in a Saudi Arabia joint venture.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Swings in aluminum and alumina prices, which it does not control.
    • Competition across the aluminum supply chain, including from steel, plastics and other materials.
    • Health, safety and environmental rules that could bring large claims and costs, including at waste storage sites.
    • Climate change laws and the cost of cutting emissions.
    • Debt, limits on funding capital projects, and the need to renegotiate union agreements as they expire.
    From Alcoa's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
