    What Core Scientific does

    Core Scientific designs, builds and runs large data centers that supply power and space to customers with heavy computing needs, mainly artificial intelligence and other high-performance computing. This service is called high-density colocation. It also mines digital assets, mostly bitcoin, for its own account and for some outside customers. As of December 31, 2025, it owned or leased ten data centers in seven U.S. states.

    The company began in 2017 building data centers for bitcoin mining. In 2024 it began shifting toward colocation and signed long-term contracts with CoreWeave, which later grew to roughly 590 megawatts of leased power capacity. In 2025 it still earned most of its revenue from mining digital assets for its own account, but it expects a meaningful amount of 2026 revenue to come from colocation as customer power capacity is delivered.

    Converting existing sites and building new ones requires large amounts of money and electric power. The company says its colocation business currently depends heavily on a single customer.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Delays or cost overruns in converting and building the facilities its colocation customers need.
    • Depending heavily on a single customer for its colocation business.
    • The colocation shift not succeeding, if demand for AI computing infrastructure falls.
    • Needing to raise more capital to expand, which may not be available.
    • Limited availability and high cost of the electric power its sites need.

    Piotroski F-score: how many of nine simple tests of profitability, funding and efficiency the company passed in the year (Piotroski, 2000); 8 or 9 is strong, 0 to 2 weak.
