    What Ambarella does

    Ambarella designs low-power chips, called systems-on-a-chip, and software that let cameras and other devices process images and run artificial intelligence on the device itself rather than in a data center. The company was incorporated in 2004. It is based on video processing, and it now focuses on what it calls edge and physical AI, meaning devices that can perceive their surroundings and act on them.

    Its chips go into security cameras for businesses, cities and homes, body-worn and action cameras, drones, robots, and cars. In cars they support driver-assistance cameras, cabin and driver monitoring, and, with its CV3 family, partly or fully automated driving. Its newer product families, CV7, CV3 and N1, use its own AI accelerator design.

    Ambarella does not make its chips. It relies on outside foundries, assembly and test contractors, and sells through its direct sales force and distributors worldwide. It says it depends on a limited number of customers for a significant portion of its revenue, and that it faces intense competition.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Depending on a few customers, and on those customers designing its chips into products that sell well.
    • Long, costly design-win processes that can end with a customer canceling or changing its plans.
    • Relying on outside foundries, with possible shortages or higher costs of wafers and materials.
    • Intense competition and selling prices that have typically fallen over time.
    • Cyclical swings in the semiconductor industry and customers canceling or delaying orders.
    From Ambarella's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 23, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
