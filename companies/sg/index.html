    What Sweetgreen does

    Sweetgreen runs a restaurant chain that serves salads, bowls and plates built around vegetables and other whole ingredients. At the end of 2025 it owned and operated 281 restaurants in 24 states and Washington, D.C. Customers pick from a core menu of signature items, build their own bowl or salad, or order seasonal items. It also sells through its own digital channels, including app orders and a mobile pickup lane at some restaurants.

    It makes money from the food and drinks it sells in its own restaurants; the 10-K describes no franchise business. Sweetgreen says it competes in a highly competitive restaurant industry and that it wants to be as widespread as traditional fast food. It buys from a network of farmers, manufacturers and distributors through regional distribution networks.

    The company opened 35 net new restaurants in 2025 and expects to open fewer in 2026. It had 6,486 employees at the end of 2025, most of them working in restaurants. It describes a plan with five priorities, including running its restaurants better, menu changes, and more targeted digital offers to bring customers back more often.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It has lost money since it began, expects losses to continue for the foreseeable future, and may not reach profit.
    • It may not persuade customers to pay its prices for higher-quality food.
    • Finding good sites for new restaurants, and new restaurants that may not be profitable or may cut sales at existing ones.
    • Food safety and foodborne illness concerns.
    • Higher labor, ingredient and tariff costs, and trouble hiring and keeping staff.
    From Sweetgreen's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
