    What Credit Acceptance does

    Credit Acceptance finances car purchases for people with weak or limited credit histories. It works through a nationwide network of automobile dealers. The dealer sells a car to a consumer and the loan, which the company calls a Consumer Loan, is assigned to Credit Acceptance. Legally it is an indirect lender, because the dealer makes the loan first. The company says most of the loans it takes on are to consumers with impaired or limited credit.

    It makes money from collecting on those loans. When a dealer joins, it signs a servicing agreement under which Credit Acceptance administers, services and collects the loans. It also offers dealers a non-recourse cash payment, called an advance, against the future collections on the loans it services for them. It reports to the three national credit reporting agencies, so on-time payments can help a customer's credit score.

    The company was founded in 1972 to collect loans for dealerships owned by its founder, Donald Foss, and in the 1980s it began offering the service to other dealers. It competes with traditional lenders and with non-traditional lenders.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Misjudging how much it will collect on its loans and when.
    • Competition from traditional banks and non-traditional lenders.
    • Weak conditions in the car market or among customers with poor credit.
    • Too many of its dealers located in a few states.
    • Reliance on outside companies to run its add-on products and some business functions.
    From Credit Acceptance's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 13, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
