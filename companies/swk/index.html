    What Stanley Black & Decker does

    Stanley Black & Decker, founded in 1843, makes hand tools, power tools, outdoor products and accessories, plus engineered fasteners used in manufacturing. Its 2025 revenue was $15.1 billion, of which about 62% came from the United States.

    It has two segments. Tools & Outdoor, with $13.2 billion of revenue (87% of the total), sells under brands such as DEWALT, CRAFTSMAN, STANLEY, BLACK+DECKER and CUB CADET: power tools, hand tools, storage, and lawn and garden equipment. Most sales go through retailers such as home centers and mass merchants, as well as distributors, independent dealers and a direct sales force. Engineered Fastening, with $2.0 billion (13%), sells fasteners, fittings and other parts designed for specific uses.

    The company has sold several businesses in recent years, including its security and automatic door units in 2022 and its infrastructure business in 2024. It has also agreed to sell its Consolidated Aerospace Manufacturing business and plans to use the proceeds to pay down debt. It finished a cost-cutting program at the end of 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Tariffs and other trade rules, since it makes and sources products in many countries.
    • Changes in what customers want, and its reliance on large retail customers that keep consolidating.
    • Strong global competition in tools and outdoor products.
    • Disruption at its factories or in its information systems.
    • Writing down the value of goodwill or trade names it carries on its books.
    From Stanley Black & Decker's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 24, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
