    What Eastman Kodak does

    Kodak makes products for commercial printing and advanced materials and chemicals. It was founded by George Eastman in 1880 and is based in Rochester, New York. Its customers are mainly commercial printers, and also film, industrial, pharmaceutical and other businesses.

    It reports three segments. Print sells aluminum printing plates and the equipment that images them, Prosper inkjet presses and parts, printing software, and toner-based digital printers, along with ink and service. Plates and ink are used up in printing, so they bring repeat sales. Advanced Materials and Chemicals covers industrial film and chemicals, motion picture film, pharmaceuticals, functional printing materials and licensing of patents. Brand licenses the Kodak name to other companies for products such as cameras, eyewear, batteries and apparel. Kodak Alaris, which buys still photographic film and chemicals, made up about 33% of the Advanced Materials and Chemicals segment's revenue in 2025.

    Kodak names Fuji, HP, Canon, Ricoh and Screen among its competitors in print. It says the Print segment faces higher raw material and supply chain costs, including from tariffs, price pressure and falling volume.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Print volumes and sales falling while raw material, supply chain and tariff costs rise.
    • Competition from larger rivals in printing, and new products that may not earn the expected returns.
    • Large debt and other obligations that take a share of its cash flow, and capital that may not be available when needed.
    • Dependence on Kodak Alaris for about a third of its Advanced Materials and Chemicals revenue.
    • Difficulty managing strategic deals and changes to its information systems.
    From Eastman Kodak's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
