    What Legacy Housing does

    Legacy Housing builds, sells and finances manufactured homes and "Tiny Houses." Its homes are built in factories to federal HUD construction standards and sold under the Legacy brand, mostly in the southern United States. Buyers are typically households earning less than $75,000 a year, from young families to people age 55 and older. In 2025 it sold 1,703 units, each an entire home or a single floor.

    The company sells through independent retailers, its own retail stores and directly to owners of manufactured home communities. It also makes money as a lender: it finances the inventory that its independent retailers buy, loans to consumers who buy its homes, and loans to community owners. Legacy says it competes with other manufactured home makers such as Clayton Homes, Cavco and Skyline Champion, with lenders to home buyers, and with site-built homes, apartments and condominiums.

    Legacy runs three factories, in Texas and Georgia, and had about 592 employees at the end of 2025. Sales are generally higher from March through October and slower in winter.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competition from larger manufactured home makers and lenders that have more money and wider distribution.
    • Its dependence on offering financing, which exposes it to buyers and retailers who may not repay.
    • Federal, state and local building and lending rules that can bring fines or court orders if broken.
    • Slower sales and delayed shipments in winter and harsh weather.
    • Its reliance on independent retailers and on community owners to sell its homes.
    From Legacy Housing's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
