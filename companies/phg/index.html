    What Philips does

    Philips is a Dutch health technology company. It makes medical equipment and software for hospitals and clinics, such as imaging systems, patient monitors and sleep and breathing devices, and it also sells health products that people use at home.

    It reports three segments. Diagnosis & Treatment covers diagnostic imaging, ultrasound and image-guided therapy. Connected Care covers patient monitoring, hospital information systems and sleep and respiratory care. Personal Health sells consumer products such as grooming and oral care items and infant feeding products. The six businesses inside these segments are run worldwide.

    Sales are split by region as well. In 2025, mature geographies, such as Western Europe and North America, made up 72% of revenues and growth geographies made up the remaining 28%. Philips had 65,340 employees at the end of 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • The lasting effects of the Respironics voluntary recall of sleep and breathing devices, including legal claims and settlements.
    • Patient safety, product quality and regulatory demands on medical devices.
    • Geopolitical tension, tariffs and trade limits in the US, the EU and China, where it makes and sources products.
    • Supply chain disruptions affecting parts and production.
    • Cyberattacks on Philips, its products or its suppliers.
    From Philips's annual report on Form 20-F, filed February 19, 2026, in our words; the 20-F has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
