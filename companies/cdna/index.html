    What CareDx does

    CareDx makes tests and services for people who have had, or are waiting for, a heart, kidney or lung transplant. Its main products are blood tests that look for signs that the body may be rejecting the new organ without the need for a biopsy. They include AlloSure Kidney, AlloMap Heart, AlloSure Heart, HeartCare and AlloSure Lung. It also sells laboratory products, digital health tools and patient support services.

    CareDx earns most of its revenue by running these tests in its own laboratories and billing for them. It says a substantial portion of revenue comes from Medicare. It reports a single segment. Substantially all of its revenue comes from the United States and Europe, and its operations are mainly in the United States and Sweden.

    The company is based in Brisbane, California, with other main operations in Omaha, Nebraska and Stockholm, Sweden. It says it has a history of losses and expects more net losses for the next several years.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A substantial share of its revenue comes from Medicare, so lower reimbursement would hurt it badly.
    • Its results depend largely on sales of a handful of transplant tests.
    • Insurers may drop or limit coverage of its tests, or pay too little for them.
    • It has a history of losses and expects more net losses for the next several years.
    • Clinicians and medical centers may not adopt its tests, and rivals may keep pace with new technology.
