    What Nextdoor does

    Nextdoor runs a neighborhood social network where people share local news, safety alerts, recommendations, for-sale and free listings and events. It operates in over 350,000 neighborhoods across 11 countries. Local businesses have free Business Pages, and public agencies such as fire departments and police use it to share alerts. Over 4,000 local news publishers also supply content.

    It says it had 21.0 million platform weekly active users at the end of 2025, and that it reaches 1 in 3 U.S. households. It earns substantially all of its revenue from advertising: businesses of all sizes, national brands, publishers and government agencies pay to reach people in a local area through its advertising system.

    In 2025 it launched the new Nextdoor initiative, the first phase of a multi-phase product change. The feed now includes news and alerts as well as neighbor posts, and artificial intelligence and machine learning help choose and personalize what each person sees.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Advertisers cutting or ending their spending, since it earns substantially all its revenue from advertising.
    • Failing to add or keep neighbors, or neighbors using the platform less.
    • A limited operating history at its current scale while it is still building up how it makes money.
    • Difficulty collecting and using data to deliver and measure ads.
    • Strong competition for both users and advertisers.
    From Nextdoor's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 18, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Piotroski F-score: how many of nine simple tests of profitability, funding and efficiency the company passed in the year (Piotroski, 2000); 8 or 9 is strong, 0 to 2 weak.
