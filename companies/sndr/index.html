    What Schneider National does

    Schneider National is a trucking and logistics company serving customers in the United States, Canada and Mexico. It moves freight by truck, by rail and truck combined, and through a network of outside carriers. It also offers warehousing, distribution and transloading, which means moving goods from one kind of transport to another.

    It reports its business in segments, including Truckload and Intermodal. Truckload carries freight in dry vans, tank-type bulk trailers, temperature-controlled and flatbed trailers, using company-employed drivers, independent owner-operators and contracted carriers. Some of this is dedicated service under long-term contracts. Intermodal is door-to-door container service that combines rail and short truck trips, under agreements with most major Class I railroads. Logistics arranges loads through third-party carriers and offers a Power Only service, where Schneider supplies a trailer pool and the customer's carrier supplies the truck.

    The company was founded in 1935 and trades on the New York Stock Exchange.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Weak economic conditions and intense price competition in a fragmented industry.
    • Losing one or more major customers, which provide a significant part of its revenue.
    • Difficulty hiring and keeping qualified drivers, and the risk of unionization.
    • Swings in fuel prices.
    • Its dependence on railroads and ports for intermodal service, and on outside carriers for capacity.
