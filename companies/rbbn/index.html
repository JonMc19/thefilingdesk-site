    What Ribbon Communications does

    Ribbon Communications sells software, networking hardware and services to telephone and internet service providers and to businesses. Its products carry voice and data traffic securely over networks, including internet-based voice calling, and cover optical transport and IP routing and switching. Enterprise customers include finance, education, government, utilities and transportation.

    The company grew mainly by combining Sonus Networks and GENBAND in 2017, then buying other firms, including the Israeli telecom equipment maker ECI in 2020. It sells directly and through distributors, resellers and system integrators, and says it competes with other telecommunications equipment and networking companies. Contract manufacturers build its hardware.

    Ribbon is based in Plano, Texas, and has research, sales or support locations in over 30 countries. It is also developing AI-based tools for network operations.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Quarterly revenue and results that are unpredictable and can swing widely.
    • Depending on a few large customers and on telecom carriers staying financially healthy.
    • Competition from other telecommunications equipment and networking companies.
    • Reliance on contract manufacturers and single-source suppliers for parts.
    • Conditions in Israel, where it has operations, and wider international risks.
    From Ribbon Communications's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
