    What Synopsys does

    Synopsys sells software, hardware and services that engineers use to design and test computer chips. This type of software is called electronic design automation, or EDA. Its tools cover the steps from the first chip design to verification and manufacturing, and it also sells hardware used to test systems that include chips, plus technical support and design services. Customers are chip makers and electronics companies, and, through its Ansys products, engineers in fields such as aerospace, automotive, energy and healthcare.

    It reports two segments. Design Automation includes EDA, the Ansys simulation and analysis software, and other revenue. Design IP sells pre-designed circuit blocks, such as memory and interface designs, that engineers drop into larger chips to save time. The Ansys merger added simulation software that is used to analyze designs before they are built.

    Synopsys says it faces strong competition and relies on the semiconductor and electronics industries for growth. It has 189 offices worldwide, with its headquarters in Sunnyvale, California.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its growth depends mainly on the semiconductor and electronics industries.
    • Heavy competition, and a need to give customers new technology at lower costs.
    • A relatively small number of large customers, and consolidation among customers.
    • Not gaining the expected benefits of the Ansys merger or other deals.
    • Export and import rules that could limit what it can sell and where.
    From Synopsys's annual report on Form 10-K, filed December 22, 2025, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
