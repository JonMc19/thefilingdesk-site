    What Genworth Financial does

    Genworth Financial is an insurance holding company. Its main business is private mortgage insurance in the United States, which protects lenders if a home buyer fails to repay a loan. It runs this through its subsidiary Enact Holdings, which is listed separately on Nasdaq under the ticker ACT and is controlled by Genworth.

    Genworth reports two segments: Enact, and a Closed Block of long-term care, life insurance and annuity policies sold through its older insurance companies. Those companies no longer sell these products, but they keep servicing the policies already in force. A third area, Corporate and Other, includes new businesses under the CareScout name: fee-based aging-care services and, since October 2025, a new long-term care insurance product.

    In late 2025 Genworth changed its reporting to these two segments to match how it manages the business, including its aim to keep the legacy insurance companies self-sustaining and managed on their own.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Having to add to insurance reserves if claims turn out worse than its assumptions, especially for long-term care.
    • New lines of business such as CareScout not succeeding or bringing new risks.
    • Its dependence on Enact Holdings to pay it dividends and other payments.
    • Limited sources of capital, and ratings downgrades that could cost it business.
    • Models and valuation methods that turn out to be inaccurate.
    From Genworth Financial's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
