    What Arista Networks does

    Arista Networks makes the network equipment and software that connect computers inside data centers, AI computing clusters, offices and campuses, and across wide-area links. Its products include high-speed Ethernet switches and routers, which run on its own operating system, Extensible Operating System (EOS). It also sells software for network management and monitoring, and support services.

    It groups its offerings into three categories: Core (AI, cloud and data center networking), Cognitive Adjacencies (campus and routing), and Cognitive Networks (software and services). It sells to three groups of customers. They are large cloud companies, which it calls Cloud and AI Titans, specialty and AI providers, and enterprises such as banks and government agencies. It says that a small number of customers make up a large share of its revenue.

    Arista says it competes with larger, well-established companies, and that its market has seen consolidation. It uses contract manufacturers to build its products and buys some components from a limited number of suppliers, including some sole-source ones.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A small number of large customers account for much of its revenue, so losing or delaying their purchases could hurt it.
    • Reliance on a limited number of suppliers, including some sole-source ones, for key components.
    • Tariffs and trade policies that remain uncertain.
    • Intense competition from larger, well-established companies, and from industry consolidation.
    • Revenue and margins that vary a lot from one period to the next.
    From Arista Networks's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 17, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
