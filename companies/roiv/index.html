    What Roivant Sciences does

    Roivant is a biopharmaceutical company, meaning it develops medicines. Its main candidates are brepocitinib, a pill being studied for dermatomyositis (a muscle and skin disease) and other inflammatory conditions, IMVT-1402, an antibody for several autoimmune diseases, and mosliciguat, an inhaled drug for a lung condition.

    Roivant runs its work through subsidiaries it calls "Vants," each with its own management team and focus, such as Priovant, Immunovant and Pulmovant. It builds the pipeline by licensing in or buying drug candidates from others and then developing them. Beyond medicines, it also incubates early-stage companies and health technology startups.

    Its business depends on clinical trial results, regulatory approval and, if a drug is approved, on selling it. The 10-K says its candidates are investigational and need health authority approval before they can be sold.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Drug candidates failing in clinical trials or not winning regulatory approval.
    • Operating losses for the foreseeable future, and a risk it never becomes profitable.
    • Immunovant relying on a license from HanAll for the core rights to IMVT-1402.
    • Risks tied to its Vant structure, where trouble at one Vant can hurt the whole company.
    • Difficulty finding and in-licensing new candidates that perform as expected.
