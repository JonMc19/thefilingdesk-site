    What T-Mobile US does

    T-Mobile US is a mobile phone company. It sells wireless phone and data plans and home internet through a 5G fixed wireless service that runs over its cell network and, in some places, fiber lines. Its brands are T-Mobile, Metro by T-Mobile, Mint Mobile and Ultra Mobile. It also sells phones, tablets and other devices, often paid off in monthly installments, and rents network access to other companies that resell service under their own names. Nearly all of its revenue comes from the United States.

    At the end of 2025 it served 142.4 million customers. Most of its service revenue comes from postpaid customers, who pay after using the service, at 81%; prepaid customers made up 15% and wholesale and other services 4%. Total revenues grew by $6.9 billion in 2025. T-Mobile says it is the second-largest US wireless carrier by customers; its main rivals are AT&T and Verizon, and in home internet, cable, satellite and fiber providers.

    In August 2025 it bought the wireless business of UScellular. It sells through its own stores, websites and apps, and through national retailers and dealers. Deutsche Telekom owns about 52.8% of its shares and controls the T-Mobile trademarks it uses. It had about 75,000 full-time and part-time employees at the end of 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Losing customers in a highly competitive industry, or failing to win new ones.
    • Cyberattacks and data breaches, which it has suffered before.
    • The scarcity and cost of wireless spectrum, the airwaves its network needs.
    • Folding in the UScellular business, which may bring network and customer problems.
    • Deutsche Telekom controls most of the votes and the T-Mobile name, and its interests may differ from other shareholders'.
    From T-Mobile US's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 11, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
