    What PayPal does

    PayPal runs digital payment services for shoppers and businesses in about 200 markets. People use its PayPal and Venmo apps and accounts to pay online and in stores, send money to friends and family, and buy and sell cryptocurrencies; Xoom sends money abroad. Merchants of all sizes use PayPal's checkout buttons, its unbranded card processing, buy now, pay later plans, in-store payment tools and small-business loans. It had 439 million active accounts at the end of 2025.

    PayPal reports a single business segment. Most of its money comes from fees on the payments it handles, which mostly rise with the value of those payments; it processed $1.79 trillion of payments in 2025. It also earns fees for currency conversion and instant transfers to bank accounts, interest and fees on its consumer and merchant loans, interest on money held in customer balances, and payments from partners. It says it competes with banks, card networks and processors, other digital wallets, buy now, pay later lenders, money transfer services and cash and checks.

    PayPal employed about 23,800 people at the end of 2025, about 9,600 of them in the United States. Its credit cards and some of its loans in the US are issued through partner banks. Because it moves and holds people's money, it holds money transmitter licenses in US states and is closely regulated in the other countries where it works.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Cyberattacks or system outages that could expose customer data or stop payments, harming trust in its brand.
    • Intense competition from banks, card networks, tech companies and new payment methods.
    • Fraud, misuse of its services and bad transactions that could raise its losses and drive users away.
    • Complex, changing rules on payments, lending, money laundering and data in the many countries where it works.
    • Losses on the loans it makes to shoppers and small businesses, especially if the economy weakens.
    From PayPal's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 3, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
