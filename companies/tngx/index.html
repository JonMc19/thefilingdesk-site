    What Tango Therapeutics does

    Tango Therapeutics is a drug developer focused on cancer. It looks for medicines that work against cancer cells with specific genetic changes while sparing normal cells. It has no products approved for sale and has not earned revenue from product sales.

    Its work is centered on two experimental drugs that block a protein called PRMT5 in cancers that lack a gene called MTAP. Vopimetostat (TNG462) is aimed at cancers outside the brain and spinal cord, both alone and combined with drugs that block RAS, a common cancer-driving protein. TNG456 is designed to reach the brain and is aimed at brain cancers, including glioblastoma. In October 2025 the company reported data from an ongoing Phase 1/2 trial of vopimetostat, and it plans to start a pivotal trial in second-line pancreatic cancer in 2026.

    Tango pays for its research by raising money from investors and through partnerships, not by selling medicines. It runs combination trials with drugs from Revolution Medicines, and in March 2026 it agreed to test vopimetostat alongside a drug from Erasca. It also has a discovery program that looks for new targets. At the end of 2025 it had 137 full-time employees, most of them in research and development.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It has no approved products, has never earned product revenue, and may never become profitable.
    • It has lost money since it began and will need to raise a lot more funding, which could dilute shareholders.
    • Its drugs may fail in clinical trials, show safety problems, or be delayed or not approved.
    • Early results may not hold up in later trials, and trial data it announces can change.
    • Competitors may develop rival cancer treatments first, and its approach is new and unproven.
