    What MetLife does

    MetLife is a financial services company that provides insurance, annuities, employee benefits and asset management. It operates in the United States, Asia, Latin America, Europe and the Middle East. It is also a large institutional investor: it invests the money it holds for policies mainly in fixed income securities and mortgage loans, plus real estate and other investments.

    MetLife reports six segments: Group Benefits, Retirement and Income Solutions, Asia, Latin America, Europe/Middle East/Africa and MetLife Investment Management, its institutional asset management business. It also reports other results under Corporate & Other. In late 2025 it reorganized: asset management became its own segment and MetLife Holdings stopped being one.

    It earns money from premiums and fees on its insurance and annuity products and from returns on the money it invests. Its New Frontier strategy for the next four years aims to grow in group benefits, retirement, asset management and fast-growing international markets. It describes its regulation as extensive.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Not meeting its cash needs or facing a higher cost of capital when credit markets are strained.
    • Losing business if its financial strength or credit ratings are cut.
    • Not finding affordable reinsurance to protect it from losses.
    • Changes in laws or regulation that reduce its profit or limit its growth.
    • Limits on paying dividends or buying back stock.
    From MetLife's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 19, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
