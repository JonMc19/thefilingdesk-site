    What Procter & Gamble does

    Procter & Gamble makes everyday household and personal care products sold in about 180 countries and territories. Its brands include Tide, Downy, Dawn, Swiffer, Pampers, Always, Charmin, Bounty, Gillette, Crest, Oral-B, Vicks, Pantene, Head & Shoulders, Olay and Old Spice. Its net sales were $87.0 billion in the year to June 2026.

    P&G reports five segments. Fabric & Home Care, which includes laundry detergent, dish soap and cleaning products, brought in 35% of net sales. Baby, Feminine & Family Care, with diapers, pads, paper towels and toilet paper, brought in 24%. Beauty, with shampoo, deodorant and skin care, brought in 19%; Health Care, with toothpaste, toothbrushes and cold and stomach remedies, 14%; and Grooming, mainly razors, 8%. It sells to big-box chains, online retailers, grocery, club and drug stores, and distributors, and sales to Walmart and its affiliates made up about 16% of total sales. P&G says it competes with companies large and small, including global brands and stores' own private-label products.

    P&G had about 104,000 employees at the end of June 2026, down from the year before because of a restructuring program, and operations in about 65 countries. It buys almost all its raw and packaging materials from outside suppliers, some of them the only source for an item.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • The risks of selling in many countries, including currency swings and limits on pricing or exchanging money.
    • Disruptions in its global supply chain, and swings in the cost of materials, energy and transport, including tariffs.
    • Strong competition from global and local brands and from stores' own brands.
    • Its reliance on large customers such as Walmart, and changes in how and where people shop.
    • Failing to keep innovating in products and marketing as shoppers' habits and digital selling change.
    From Procter & Gamble's annual report on Form 10-K, filed August 4, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    What Procter & Gamble says to expect

    • Fiscal 2027: sales growthP&G updated its guidance range for fiscal 2027 all-in sales growth to be in the range of two to four percent versus the prior year, reflecting the acquisition of Thorne as of October 1, 2026. The Company maintained its outlook for organic sales growth in the range of one to three percent versus the prior year.
    • Fiscal 2027: earnings per share growthP&G maintained its fiscal 2027 diluted net earnings per share growth to be in the range of one to five percent versus fiscal 2026 diluted net EPS of $6.62. P&G also maintained its fiscal 2027 core earnings per share growth to be in the range of in-line to three percent versus fiscal 2026 core EPS of $6.89.
    • Fiscal 2027: core effective tax rateP&G now expects a core effective tax rate to be in the range of 20% to 21% in fiscal 2027.
    • Fiscal 2027: cash flow, dividends and share repurchasesP&G continues to expect free cash flow productivity of 85% to 90% and expects to pay over $10 billion in dividends and to repurchase approximately $5 billion of common shares in fiscal 2027.
