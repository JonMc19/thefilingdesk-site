    What Align Technology does

    Align Technology makes Invisalign clear aligners, which are removable plastic trays that straighten teeth. Orthodontists and general dentists prescribe them to patients. It also sells Vivera retainers, iTero intraoral scanners (handheld devices that take digital images of teeth), and exocad software that dental labs and dentists use to design crowns, bridges and similar work.

    The company has two segments. Clear Aligner is the larger: it made approximately 80% of worldwide net revenues in 2025, and the Systems and Services segment, which covers scanners, software and related services, made the remaining 20%. It sells most of its products and services directly through its own sales force.

    Align describes itself as a global medical device company. Its headquarters is in Tempe, Arizona, with regional headquarters in Raleigh, North Carolina, Rotkreuz, Switzerland and Singapore. It says more than 95% of Invisalign prescription orders worldwide now come in through a digital scan.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Demand for its products may fall, including if patients or doctors resist its approach to treatment.
    • Most of its revenue depends on Invisalign aligners and iTero scanners.
    • Growing competition in the markets for aligners and scanners.
    • Reliance on third-party suppliers, some of which are the only source for key machines and materials.
    • Large dental group practices may change how they buy, which could hurt its results.
    From Align Technology's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
