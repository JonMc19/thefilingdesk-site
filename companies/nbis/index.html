    What Nebius does

    Nebius sells cloud computing built for artificial intelligence (AI). Customers rent powerful chip clusters, storage and software to train AI models and to run them for users. Those customers range from large companies and AI start-ups to research labs and individual developers. Nebius designs its own servers and racks and builds on chips from NVIDIA, and it is based in Amsterdam.

    Its main business is this AI cloud, which it describes as a full-stack platform that covers data centers, hardware and software. It runs data centers it owns, such as one in Finland, and leases space in others in several countries. Nebius says its rivals include the large cloud providers and other specialized AI clouds.

    Nebius Group also holds two smaller businesses under separate brands. Avride develops autonomous vehicles and delivery robots, and TripleTen is an education company that trains people for tech careers. The group also owns stakes in ClickHouse and Toloka, two companies it has spun out.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Not getting enough chips, power and data center sites on acceptable terms and on time to keep expanding.
    • Customers spending less on AI, or too much industry capacity being built, so new capacity goes unused.
    • Relying on a limited number of suppliers for hardware, power and network connections.
    • Avride, its self-driving unit, needs a lot of money and may not find outside financing or partners.

    Piotroski F-score: how many of nine simple tests of profitability, funding and efficiency the company passed in the year (Piotroski, 2000); 8 or 9 is strong, 0 to 2 weak. Rule of 40: revenue growth plus free cash flow margin; software investors treat 40% or more as the bar. Accruals ratio: net income minus cash from operations, over average total assets; lower means profit is backed by cash.
