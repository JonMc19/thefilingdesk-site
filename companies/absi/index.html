    What Absci does

    Absci is a drug developer that has not yet sold a product. It uses artificial intelligence models to design antibody medicines and then tests them in its own lab. It says it focuses on targets that are poorly explored, where medical need is high and competition is low.

    Its main drug candidate, ABS-201, is an antibody against the prolactin receptor. It is being tested in an early-stage trial for pattern hair loss, and the company plans a mid-stage trial for endometriosis, a chronic condition of the reproductive system. Absci also decided to look for a partner for another candidate, ABS-101, instead of taking it through late-stage development itself.

    Absci does not expect revenue from selling its own drugs unless a candidate completes clinical development and wins regulatory approval. Its clinical work may involve partners, and it competes with other companies that use technology to find and design drugs.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It has a short operating history and has lost money since it began.
    • It does not expect product sales unless a drug candidate is approved, and all of them are still in testing.
    • Clinical trials may be delayed, fail, or give early results that do not hold up.
    • FDA may not accept data from its trials run outside the United States, including in Australia.
    • Other companies are building competing AI drug design technology.
