    What American Airlines Group does

    American Airlines Group is the holding company for American Airlines and its regional airline subsidiaries. Its main business is flying passengers and cargo on a scheduled network, through hubs in Charlotte, Chicago, Dallas/Fort Worth, Los Angeles, Miami, New York, Philadelphia, Phoenix and Washington, D.C. It serves more than 350 destinations, and about 224 million passengers boarded its flights in 2025.

    At the end of 2025 it flew 1,013 mainline aircraft. Regional flights, sold as American Eagle, add 567 more aircraft. These are run by its own regional airlines (Envoy, PSA and Piedmont) and by outside carriers such as Republic and SkyWest. About 57 million passengers boarded regional flights in 2025, and about 42% of them connected to or from mainline flights.

    With its outside regional partners, American pays set fees to fly an agreed number of aircraft, whatever the passenger count. American keeps all the ticket and other revenue, and sets the schedules and prices. It also pays certain variable costs, such as landing fees and fuel. It has a cargo division that carries freight and mail, and is a founding member of the oneworld alliance of airlines.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A downturn in the economy that cuts demand for air travel.
    • Its high level of debt and other obligations, and its need for enough financing to operate.
    • Intense competition in the airline industry.
    • Strikes and other labor disruptions, and problems at the regional airlines and other outside providers it relies on.
    • Failures of the technology and automated systems it relies on to run its operations.
    From American Airlines Group's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 18, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
