    What IonQ does

    IonQ builds quantum computers, machines that use qubits instead of ordinary bits and that it hopes will one day solve problems today's computers cannot. It also sells quantum networking, sensing and security products. It sells specialized quantum hardware and full systems, either over the cloud or on a customer's premises, plus maintenance, support and professional services.

    It also sells access to its computers through Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure Quantum and Google's cloud marketplace, and to some customers through its own cloud service. Through a network of satellites it offers data products such as radar imaging, and it plans to combine them with its sensing products to offer navigation and timing services.

    IonQ says it is in the early stages of commercial growth and has lost money since it began. Its net loss attributable to IonQ was $510.4 million in 2025. It expects losses to continue while it works to raise the number and quality of its qubits. It has bought other companies and plans to keep considering deals.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It is an early-stage company with a history of operating losses, and expects more losses.
    • It has not yet built a scalable quantum computer, and its ion trap technology may prove hard to scale.
    • It may need more money, which may not be available on good terms.
    • Competitors could reach breakthroughs that make its systems obsolete, or its computers may never reach broad quantum advantage.
    • Part of its business depends on its relationships with cloud providers.
