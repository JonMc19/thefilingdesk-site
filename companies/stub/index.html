    What StubHub does

    StubHub runs an online marketplace where people resell tickets to live events such as sports, concerts and theater. It operates under two brands, StubHub and viagogo. It says it believes it runs the largest global marketplace of this kind. It does not control the box office or access to venues.

    It makes money mainly from fees it charges both buyers and sellers when a ticket sells. Fees are generally a percentage of the value of the transaction, and revenue is recorded net of the price of the tickets. It also charges buyers shipping fees. It competes for buyers on selection, brands, ease of use, and trust and safety, and for sellers on the size of its buyer base and ease of use.

    The company wants to grow beyond resale into original ticket sales, with event owners listing tickets directly on its marketplace as any other seller would. It says it is early in that effort. It had about 900 full-time employees at the end of 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its business depends on large live events continuing to take place and on demand for them.
    • It must keep attracting buyers and sellers, and may lose them to competitors.
    • Changes in search engine algorithms could reduce how many consumers find its marketplace.
    • It relies on third-party platforms to distribute its apps.
    • It faces intense competition and must keep improving its marketplace.
    From StubHub's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 5, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Piotroski F-score: how many of nine simple tests of profitability, funding and efficiency the company passed in the year (Piotroski, 2000); 8 or 9 is strong, 0 to 2 weak.
