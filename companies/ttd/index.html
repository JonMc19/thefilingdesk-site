    What The Trade Desk does

    The Trade Desk runs a self-service software platform that advertising buyers use to plan, run and optimize digital ad campaigns across formats, channels and devices. It sits on the buy side, working for advertisers and agencies, and says this lets it avoid conflicts of interest faced by competitors that serve both buyers and sellers. Clients can also use its APIs, which are software connections, to customize the platform.

    Its clients are advertising agencies, advertisers and other service providers for them. It signs ongoing master services agreements with them and says it derives substantially all of its revenue from those agreements rather than from one-off insertion orders. It earns a platform fee, generally a percentage of what clients spend through the platform, and also charges for added services and data that support campaigns.

    The platform connects to many publishers, sources of ad space and data partners, and the company sees its data and measurement offerings as a competitive advantage.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to keep and grow its client base and the spending that flows through its platform.
    • Losing agencies or advertisers as clients.
    • Less access to good advertising space from publishers and other sellers.
    • Intense competition in the advertising technology market.
    • A large increase in the supply of ad space and changing industry standards that could affect its business.
    From The Trade Desk's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
