    What Centene does

    Centene is a health insurer that mainly covers people who get care through government programs. It describes itself as the nation's largest managed care company focused on underserved populations. Members are covered under Medicaid, Medicare (including Part D drug plans) and the Health Insurance Marketplace, where individuals and families buy plans. It uses local brands and local teams in the states it serves.

    It reports four segments. Medicaid brought in 57% of external revenues in 2025, Commercial (mainly Marketplace plans) 21%, Medicare 19% and Other 3%. Other includes specialty pharmacy, vision and dental, and behavioral health services. Government programs and states pay it premiums, and it pays for members' medical care.

    Membership totaled 27.6 million at the end of 2025, and total revenues for the year were $194.8 billion. In December 2025 it signed an agreement to sell the remaining Magellan Health businesses.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Medical costs rising faster than the rates it is paid, or payments not matching the health of its members.
    • Not winning or renewing government contracts, or an adverse audit or investigation.
    • Risk-adjustment payment systems that make revenue hard to estimate and can lead to retroactive changes.
    • Pricing its Medicare and Marketplace products wrongly, or having to cut back what it offers.
    • Bids for Medicare Part D plans and the design of the risk-sharing program.
    From Centene's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 17, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Income statement

    From fiscal 2016 to 2024, earnings per share grew 17.7% a year and net income 24.8% a year, as new shares raised the share count by 60% and diluted each share's earnings. Centene then lost money in fiscal 2025.
