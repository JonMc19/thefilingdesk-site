    What GE HealthCare does

    GE HealthCare makes medical imaging machines, patient monitors and the drugs used in scans, and it sells software and services to go with them. Its customers are hospitals, health systems and researchers in more than 160 countries. Revenue comes from selling devices, consumable products, service contracts and cloud and software products. It was spun off from General Electric in January 2023.

    It reports four segments. Imaging makes CT, MR, X-ray, molecular imaging and women's health scanners. Advanced Visualization Solutions makes ultrasound systems and equipment used in heart procedures and surgery. Patient Care Solutions makes monitors, heart-test (ECG) devices, anesthesia machines and equipment for newborns, with recurring sales of supplies and services. Pharmaceutical Diagnostics supplies contrast media and radioactive tracers that make tissues show up in scans.

    The company names Siemens Healthineers, Philips, United Imaging, Mindray and Canon as its main rivals in equipment, and Bayer, Bracco, Guerbet and Curium in diagnostic agents. It has about 54,000 employees, and 32% of them are in the United States. It also employs about 11,100 engineers and scientists and about 9,700 people in sales.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Strong competition that could force price cuts or cost it market share.
    • Delays or failures in getting regulatory approval for its products, and strict rules from the FDA and other agencies.
    • Health insurers and public payers limiting what they pay, which could reduce prices and demand.
    • Supply chain problems that limit its ability to get parts and materials and make its products.
    • Costs and legal exposure if the 2023 spin-off from GE is found to be a taxable transaction.
    From GE HealthCare's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 4, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
