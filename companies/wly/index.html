    What John Wiley & Sons does

    Wiley publishes research journals, books and learning materials, and sells digital services to go with them. Its customers include libraries, universities, scientific and professional societies, corporations, researchers, students and instructors. The company was founded in 1807 and says 85% of its revenue for the year ended April 30, 2026 came from digital products and services.

    It reports two segments. Research made up about 67% of revenue. It sells journal subscriptions, usually under multi-year agreements with libraries and library groups, often through subscription agents, and customers generally pay in advance. Learning made up about 33%. It covers academic and professional books, digital courseware and assessment services.

    Wiley publishes many journals for professional societies under long-term contracts. In fiscal 2026 it began licensing content to other publishers for AI-related revenue.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • AI developments that could disrupt its markets and increase competition.
    • Cuts in federal research funding and changes to U.S. higher education policy.
    • Fewer college and university students buying its academic products.
    • Failing to protect its intellectual property, and pressure on its relationships with authors and societies.
    • Problems with its overhaul of its research publishing systems, and its dependence on subscription agents for a share of revenue.
    From John Wiley & Sons's annual report on Form 10-K, filed June 24, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
