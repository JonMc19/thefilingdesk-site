    What Sunrun does

    Sunrun designs, installs, owns and maintains solar energy systems, and often battery storage, on homes in the United States. Its main customers are homeowners. Most of them do not buy a system. They sign a lease or a power purchase agreement, which Sunrun calls Customer Agreements, and pay for the solar power the system produces, with no large upfront cost.

    Under these agreements Sunrun installs the system and typically provides the power for an initial term of 20 or 25 years, and it monitors, maintains and insures the system. After that term, customers can renew, typically at a 10% discount to then-prevailing power prices, buy the system, or have it removed. Sunrun also sells to some commercial developers through its multi-family and new homes offerings.

    Sunrun sells through a partner network and its own direct-to-consumer sales. It receives payments from customers over time and qualifies for tax and other benefits. It pays for its upfront costs partly by financing these cash flows and tax benefits through tax equity, non-recourse debt and project equity.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A solar and battery storage market that may not grow as fast as it expects.
    • Tariffs and trade measures that raise the cost of equipment.
    • Competition from traditional energy companies and other solar companies.
    • A drop in the retail price of utility electricity, which would make its offer less attractive.
    • Higher interest rates, which raise the cost of the capital it relies on.
    From Sunrun's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
