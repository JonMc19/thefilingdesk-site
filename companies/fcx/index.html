    What Freeport-McMoRan does

    Freeport-McMoRan is a mining company, based in Phoenix, Arizona, that says it aims to be foremost in copper. It digs up and processes copper, gold and molybdenum (a metal used to strengthen steel). In 2025 its mines produced 3.4 billion pounds of copper, along with gold and molybdenum, and it sells these metals to other companies.

    Its income depends on the market prices for copper, gold and, to a lesser extent, molybdenum, which it does not control. Its biggest assets are the Grasberg district in Indonesia, the Morenci mine in Arizona and other U.S. mines, and the Cerro Verde mine in Peru. In 2025 its U.S. mines produced the most copper, followed by South America and Indonesia, but Indonesia produced nearly all of its gold.

    In September 2025 a mud rush, a sudden flow of wet material into the Grasberg underground mine, hit its Indonesian operations. Seven team members died. Parts of the mine restarted in late 2025, and a phased restart of the main underground mine is expected to begin in the second quarter of 2026. It had about 29,000 employees at the end of 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Falling prices for copper, gold and molybdenum, which it cannot control.
    • Heavy reliance on its operations in Indonesia, including the mud rush at Grasberg and the terms of its mining permit there.
    • Underground mining carries higher risks than surface mining, plus the challenges of handling waste rock and tailings.
    • Environmental rules, mine closure costs and cleanup of old U.S. sites could cost more than it has set aside.
    • Estimates of ore reserves are uncertain, and development projects may cost more or return less than planned.
    From Freeport-McMoRan's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 13, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
