    What Pure Cycle does

    Pure Cycle provides wholesale water and wastewater services in the Denver, Colorado area, develops land into planned communities, and builds single-family homes that it rents out. All of its assets and operations are along the Front Range of Colorado.

    It reports three business segments. In water and wastewater services, it owns water rights, wells, pipelines, reservoirs and treatment plants, and sells mainly to local government districts that serve homes and businesses. Its largest customer is the Rangeview Metropolitan District, where it has the exclusive right to provide these services. In land development, it turns land it owns, mainly at its Sky Ranch community along the I-70 corridor, into finished lots and sells them to homebuilders. In single-family rentals, it has homebuilders build homes on lots it kept, then leases them, typically on annual leases.

    The company says owning the water supply as well as the land lets it offer a faster development timeline than a developer that must buy water or wait for a city to extend services. Its future revenue depends largely on new housing being built near its water assets. It had 44 full-time employees in August 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its assets and operations are all in Colorado's Front Range, so local economic conditions matter a great deal.
    • Future revenue depends on housing development near its water assets, which is cyclical and out of its control.
    • Rising interest rates and inflation can slow homebuilding and delay its development plans.
    • It may need more capital, and may not be able to raise it on acceptable terms.
    From Pure Cycle's annual report on Form 10-K, filed November 12, 2025, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
