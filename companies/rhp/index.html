    What Ryman Hospitality Properties does

    Ryman Hospitality Properties is a real estate investment trust, or REIT, a company that owns property and generally pays no federal corporate income tax on income it distributes to shareholders. It owns large hotels built for group meetings and conventions in cities and resort areas. Its holdings include the five Gaylord Hotels resorts and two JW Marriott resorts, with 11,869 rooms in all, and Marriott manages them.

    It reports three segments. Hospitality, which covers the hotels, made approximately 83% of 2025 revenue, and Entertainment made approximately 17%. The third, Corporate and Other, holds overhead costs not assigned elsewhere.

    The Entertainment segment is the Opry Entertainment Group, a business of entertainment and media assets in which Ryman has an approximate 70% controlling interest. The hotels are leased to taxable subsidiaries, and the 10-K says the company plans to pay dividends each year at least equal to its REIT taxable income, subject to its board's decisions.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Marriott manages its hotels, so its results depend on how well Marriott runs them.
    • Substantially all of its hotel revenue comes from a small number of hotel properties.
    • Terms in its hotel management agreements could limit what it can do with its hotels.
    • Hotels are costly to run and improve, and it may not be able to get financing for that.
    • Its properties face disasters, pandemics and other risks, and hotel managers employ the workers.
