    What Cirrus Logic does

    Cirrus Logic designs chips that process sound and other signals, mostly for smartphones and other consumer electronics. It has two product lines. Audio products include amplifiers, codecs (chips that convert between analog and digital sound), converters and digital signal processors. They go into smartphones, PCs, tablets, headsets, wearables, home theater systems and car entertainment systems. High-performance mixed-signal products include camera controllers, haptics (touch feedback), sensing, and battery and power chips.

    Cirrus is a fabless company: it does not make its own chips. It pays outside firms, including GlobalFoundries and TSMC, to make the wafers (the silicon discs chips are cut from) and to assemble and test the products. It sells directly and through distributors and contract manufacturers.

    Its sales are highly concentrated: in fiscal 2026 one end customer, Apple, accounted for about 91 percent of its total net sales. The company says its strategy is to keep its place in smartphone audio, add more high-performance mixed-signal content to phones, and expand into other markets.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Depending on a few customers, above all one, for most of its sales.
    • Changes in trade policy, such as tariffs and export limits.
    • Swings in smartphone and consumer electronics sales.
    • Strong competition from other chip makers.
    • Depending on outside manufacturers in other countries, and on other suppliers' parts reaching customers' products.
    From Cirrus Logic's annual report on Form 10-K, filed May 21, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
