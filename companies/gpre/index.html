    What Green Plains does

    Green Plains makes ethanol, a fuel additive blended into gasoline, and related co-products from corn bought near its plants. The co-products include distillers grains and a high-protein feed, both used as animal feed, and renewable corn oil, which is used as a feedstock for biodiesel and renewable diesel. It was founded in 2004 and is incorporated in Iowa.

    It reports two segments. Ethanol Production covers the production, storage and transportation of ethanol and co-products at nine plants in Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Minnesota and Nebraska; eight of them are currently operating. Agribusiness and Energy Services covers grain buying, storage and commodity marketing. It sells its ethanol through a third party and also sells its co-products, and buys and sells natural gas and other commodities.

    Green Plains says ethanol makes up about 10.1% of the U.S. gasoline supply used in surface transportation. In 2025 it sold some assets, paid down debt and changed leadership. It says it aims to benefit from low-carbon fuel markets, including through carbon capture projects and a federal production tax credit for low-carbon fuels.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its margins depend on the gap between the price of corn and natural gas and the prices of ethanol and co-products.
    • Its results are very sensitive to commodity prices.
    • Government biofuel programs and rules could change and affect ethanol demand.
    • Carbon capture projects and the 45Z production tax credit may not work out as planned.
    • It has had operating losses in the past and could have them again.
    From Green Plains's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 10, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Income statement

    From fiscal 2016 to 2018, earnings per share grew 18.0% a year and net income 21.9% a year, as new shares raised the share count by 7% and diluted each share's earnings. Green Plains then lost money in fiscal 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025.
