    What Sable Offshore does

    Sable Offshore is an oil and gas company working to restart oil production off the coast of California. It bought the Santa Ynez Unit from Exxon Mobil in February 2024. The unit has three offshore platforms, called Hondo, Harmony and Heritage, on federal leases, plus pipelines and an onshore processing and storage site at Las Flores Canyon.

    The company began life as a blank check company, a shell formed to buy a business, and became Sable when that deal closed. Since then it says it has invested significant capital to restore production safely. It restarted oil production from six wells on Platform Harmony on May 15, 2025, sending the oil through the Santa Ynez Pipeline System to its onshore facilities.

    It plans to earn money by selling the oil and natural gas it produces. Its report says it still needs clearances and permits, including from the federal Bureau of Ocean Energy Management (BOEM), to begin its planned operations, and that it does not guarantee it will have enough cash to start selling oil.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Needing federal clearances and permits, including from BOEM, before it can start planned operations.
    • Not having enough cash to begin selling oil, or costs that turn out higher than it estimated.
    • Low oil and natural gas prices that could make the business uneconomical.
    • Its oil being classed as contingent resources rather than reserves.
    • The extra risks of drilling and producing offshore.
