    What Equifax does

    Equifax is a data, analytics and technology company. It keeps large databases of consumer and business information, including credit, income, employment, education and utility payments. It uses that data to give businesses, governments and consumers reports, scores, identity checks and fraud protection. Its clients include banks and other financial institutions, companies and government agencies. It also sells products that help individuals watch their credit and protect their identity.

    It reports three segments. Workforce Solutions verifies income, employment and similar facts, and handles employer services such as unemployment claims, onboarding paperwork and tax credits. U.S. Information Solutions sells consumer and commercial information to U.S. businesses, and sells credit information to resellers. International offers similar products in Latin America, Europe, Asia Pacific and Canada, and includes joint ventures in Brazil, Cambodia, Malaysia and Singapore.

    Equifax says it works in highly competitive markets. It operates in four regions: North America, Latin America, Europe and Asia Pacific. The company was incorporated in Georgia in 1913.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A security breach or other disruption of its systems that could expose consumer and customer data.
    • Losing access to credit, employment, financial or other data from outside sources.
    • Highly competitive markets where rivals may offer better products.
    • Weaker demand if free or cheaper consumer credit information becomes more available.
    • Loss of key customers, or of government contracts.
    From Equifax's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 19, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
