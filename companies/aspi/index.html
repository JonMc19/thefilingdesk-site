    What ASP Isotopes does

    ASP Isotopes is developing technology to separate and enrich isotopes, which are forms of an element with different weights. The materials are used in nuclear medicine, advanced semiconductors and nuclear energy. It uses two proprietary methods: the Aerodynamic Separation Process and a laser-based Quantum Enrichment technology.

    Its first products are enriched Carbon-14, Silicon-28 and Ytterbium-176. The company says Carbon-14 could go into new drugs and agricultural chemicals, Silicon-28 into advanced semiconductors and quantum computing, and Ytterbium-176 into radiotherapy for cancer. It began commercial production at two enrichment plants in Pretoria, South Africa, in the first half of 2025, and is targeting its first commercial shipments in 2026. It says it has no sales of enriched isotopes yet and expects to depend on a few large customers.

    In January 2026 it acquired Renergen, a South African company that explores for natural gas onshore and produces liquid helium and liquefied natural gas. At the end of 2025 ASP Isotopes had 271 full-time employees, 189 of them in South Africa.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A very short operating history and continuing losses, and it may never generate significant revenue.
    • Its future depends on end markets such as medical imaging, and on its ability to introduce its isotopes successfully.
    • No sales of enriched isotopes yet, and likely dependence on a few large customers.
    • It may not realize the expected benefits of acquisitions, including Renergen.
    • Risks tied to expanding the Virginia Gas Project and to needing more capital.
