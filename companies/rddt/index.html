    What Reddit does

    Reddit runs a website and app made up of more than 100,000 active communities, called subreddits, each built around one topic. Users post text, links, images and videos, comment, and vote posts up or down, which decides what rises to the top. Most communities are run by volunteer moderators under their own rules as well as site-wide ones. Users do not have to reveal who they are. In the last three months of 2025 Reddit averaged 121.4 million daily active uniques, its count of distinct daily visitors, and about 57% of visitors came from outside the United States.

    Most of its revenue comes from advertising. Businesses pay to show ads to people based on the communities they visit and the conversations they are reading. The rest, which Reddit calls other revenue, comes from licensing its content, for example to companies that train AI models, and from products sold directly to users. Reddit says it competes for users with Google, YouTube, Meta, TikTok, Wikipedia, X and AI chatbots such as ChatGPT, and for ad budgets with Google, Meta, Snapchat, TikTok, Pinterest and X.

    Reddit had 2,555 full-time employees at the end of 2025. It runs its service on Amazon Web Services and Google Cloud, and relies in part on search engines such as Google to bring visitors to its site.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to grow or keep its users, or to keep them engaged, in the face of intense competition.
    • Users may stop posting, or post content that others do not find useful or appealing.
    • Changes in how search engines rank and show results could cut the traffic that reaches its site.
    • Its community-led moderation can leave offensive or harmful content on the platform.
    • It is early in making money from ads and content licensing, and may not be able to scale those businesses.
    From Reddit's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 6, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
