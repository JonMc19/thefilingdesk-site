    What Restaurant Brands International does

    Restaurant Brands International owns four fast-food chains: Tim Hortons, which sells coffee and baked goods; Burger King; Popeyes, which sells fried chicken; and Firehouse Subs. At the end of 2025 it had over 33,000 restaurants in more than 120 countries and territories. Together they had nearly $47 billion in system-wide sales, meaning the sales of all its restaurants, including those owned by franchisees.

    Franchisees, independent owners who pay the company fees, run over 95% of its restaurants. In the U.S. and Canada, royalties for standard restaurants typically range from 3.0% to 6.0% of sales. The company also leases or subleases about 4,700 properties, mostly to Tim Hortons and Burger King franchisees, who usually pay rent of 8.5% to 10.0% of monthly sales. Tim Hortons also roasts coffee and supplies its Canadian restaurants. The company reports six segments: one for each brand in the U.S. and Canada, one for International, and Restaurant Holdings, mainly for restaurants it runs itself. It says it competes with local restaurants, other chains, casual dining, convenience and grocery stores, and delivery services.

    It had about 53,500 employees at the end of 2025, about 48,900 of them in its own restaurants. Most of its own restaurants came with its purchase of Carrols, and it plans to hand most of them to franchisees. In January 2026 it closed a joint venture for Burger King in China, in which a fund managed by CPE owns about 83% and the company about 17%.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its results depend on independent franchisees, over whose operations it has limited influence.
    • Running a large group of its own restaurants, mostly from the Carrols purchase, could hurt its margins and cash flow.
    • Growing abroad depends on finding strong partners and joint ventures.
    • Rising food, equipment and commodity costs, or supply shortages, could hurt it and its franchisees.
    • Its debt and the obligations to service it.
    From Restaurant Brands International's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 20, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
