    What Northern Trust does

    Northern Trust is a financial holding company, based in Chicago, that provides wealth management, asset servicing, asset management and banking to companies, institutions, families and individuals. Its main subsidiary is The Northern Trust Company, a bank founded in 1889. It has offices in 24 U.S. states and Washington, D.C., and in 22 locations across Canada, Europe, the Middle East and the Asia-Pacific region.

    It has two reporting segments. Asset Servicing holds and administers assets for institutions such as pension funds, foundations, endowments, fund managers and insurers, with services such as custody (safekeeping), fund administration, securities lending and foreign exchange. At December 31, 2025 it had $17.4 trillion of assets under custody and administration. Wealth Management serves wealthy individuals, families and business owners with trust, investment, banking and family-office services. An asset management business supports both segments and its results are allocated to them.

    Most of its revenue is fees for managing and servicing client assets, plus interest income. It competes with other custody banks, asset managers, investment advisers and deposit-taking institutions.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Most revenue comes from fees tied to asset values and market activity, so market declines or a weak economy can reduce earnings.
    • Changes in interest rates could reduce its earnings.
    • It depends heavily on technology systems, many run by third parties, and a failure or security breach could cause losses.
    • Errors or weak controls in services for clients could lead to liability and losses.
    • Poor management of its liquidity could hurt the business.
