    What XPO does

    XPO is a freight transportation company. It moves goods for customers in North America and Europe, using its own trucks, terminals and technology. As of December 31, 2025, it had about 37,000 employees and 592 locations in 17 countries.

    It reports two segments. North American less-than-truckload (LTL) is the larger one. LTL means carrying shipments that do not fill a whole truck, combined with other customers' freight in one network. XPO says it has one of the largest LTL networks in North America. European Transportation offers truckload, LTL, brokerage, warehousing, managed transportation and last-mile delivery. XPO names Old Dominion Freight Line and Saia among its North American competitors.

    XPO also runs its own trailer manufacturing facility and truck driver schools. It says these help when equipment or drivers are hard to find. It says it may sell or otherwise divest its European business.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Less freight moving in a recession, in North America and Europe.
    • Its plan to improve its North American LTL network and cut costs may not deliver the results it expects.
    • Spending on equipment and service centers that does not match customer demand.
    • Higher driver pay, and trouble hiring and keeping drivers.
    • Fuel prices, bad weather, and debt that could weigh on its finances.
    From XPO's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 5, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
