    What Goldman Sachs does

    Goldman Sachs is an investment bank and money manager that serves companies, governments, investment funds and wealthy individuals around the world. It advises on mergers and other deals, helps clients raise money by selling stocks and bonds, trades securities, currencies and commodities with and for clients, lends to them, and manages investments. It is regulated by the Federal Reserve as a bank holding company and owns a New York-chartered bank, Goldman Sachs Bank USA.

    Goldman reports three segments. Global Banking & Markets, its deal-making, trading and lending business for large clients, had net revenues of $41.45 billion in 2025. Asset & Wealth Management, which earns fees for managing money for institutions and individuals and offers private banking and loans to wealthy clients, had $16.68 billion. Platform Solutions mainly issues the Apple Card and holds deposits from its cardholders; in December 2025 Goldman agreed to move the Apple Card program to another issuer, which it says will largely complete its pullback from consumer banking. Its competitors include other investment banks and brokers, commercial banks, card issuers, insurers, fund managers, hedge funds, private equity and private credit funds, and financial technology firms.

    Goldman had 47,400 employees at the end of 2025 and offices in over 35 countries. Of its staff, 50% were in the Americas, 20% in Europe, the Middle East and Africa, and 30% in Asia.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Weak markets or a weaker economy that cut deal-making, trading and the value of what it holds.
    • Losses if clients or trading partners fail to pay what they owe, especially where its risks are concentrated.
    • Losing access to borrowing or a cut in its credit ratings, which would raise its funding costs.
    • Heavy regulation around the world, and lawsuits or government actions that could bring large penalties.
    • Failures in its systems or those of others, staff misconduct, or cyberattacks that could cause losses.
    From Goldman Sachs's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 25, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
