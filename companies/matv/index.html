    What Mativ does

    Mativ makes specialty materials for other businesses, combining polymers, fibers and resins to improve its customers' products. It sells into markets such as filtration, healthcare and packaging. It was spun off from Kimberly-Clark in 1995 and was known as Schweitzer-Mauduit until its 2022 merger with Neenah.

    It has two reporting segments. Filtration and Advanced Materials focuses mainly on filtration media and components, advanced films, coating and converting solutions, and extruded mesh products. Sustainable and Adhesive Solutions focuses mainly on tapes, labels, liners, specialty paper, packaging and healthcare solutions. Its products are sold around the world, most of all in North America and Europe.

    The company manufactures on three continents, does business in over 100 countries and runs 34 production locations. In November 2023 it sold its Engineered Papers business, which made tobacco-related papers, and so it no longer takes part in tobacco-based product markets. That business is shown as a discontinued operation.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its technological advantages may not last, and its patents and other intellectual property may be challenged.
    • International risks from selling and making goods in many countries, including exchange rates and trade rules.
    • Further impairment of goodwill, which would reduce its reported results.
    • Trouble integrating acquisitions, and restructuring that is costly and could disrupt its business.
    • Loss or interruption of the operations of its facilities, and weaker construction and infrastructure spending.
    From Mativ's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Income statement

    From fiscal 2016 to 2021, earnings per share grew 0.7% a year and net income 1.4% a year, as new shares raised the share count by 3% and diluted each share's earnings. Mativ then lost money in fiscal 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025.
