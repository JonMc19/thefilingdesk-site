    What Copart does

    Copart runs online auctions for used and damaged vehicles. It operates in the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany, Brazil, Canada, the United Arab Emirates, Spain, Finland, Oman, Ireland and Bahrain. Sellers are mostly insurance companies, but also dealers, individuals, charities, rental car companies, banks and fleet operators. Insurance companies supplied 79% of the vehicles it processed in fiscal 2026. Many of these are cars that insurers declared total losses or recovered stolen cars.

    Buyers include licensed dismantlers, rebuilders, repair licensees, used vehicle dealers, exporters and the general public, who bid over the internet through its Virtual Bidding Third Generation platform, called VB3. In most markets, Copart sells vehicles as an agent and earns auction and sales transaction fees, plus fees for services after the auction such as delivery and storage. In the U.K., Germany and Spain it also buys some vehicles outright and resells them, and in the U.K. it sells dismantled parts.

    International buyers matter: in fiscal 2026, sales of U.S. vehicles to buyers outside the country were 38.2% of units. Copart says it was incorporated in California in 1982, became public in 1994 and now has its main offices in Dallas, Texas.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Dependence on a limited number of major vehicle sellers for much of its revenue, which can end agreements on short notice.
    • Risks of expanding into markets outside the United States and of integrating acquisitions there.
    • Not having enough storage capacity to take in more vehicles.
    • Failing to pick up and deliver vehicles on time and accurately.
    • Selling on its own account in some countries, which exposes it to inventory risk.
    From Copart's annual report on Form 10-K, filed September 29, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
