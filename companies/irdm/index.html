    What Iridium Communications does

    Iridium provides voice, data and location-timing services by satellite. Its network of 66 operational satellites in low Earth orbit gives coverage across the globe, including oceans, polar regions, airways and remote land where cell and landline networks are missing or limited.

    Iridium sells mostly through a wholesale network of about 120 service providers, about 310 value-added resellers and about 90 value-added manufacturers, which build products and services on its network and sell them to end users. Customers include businesses, the U.S. and foreign governments, aid groups and consumers, in areas such as maritime, aviation, emergency services, utilities, oil and gas, mining and transportation. It also supplies engineering and operational services to government customers such as the U.S. Space Force, and its satellites host additional payloads for other customers, such as Aireon.

    Iridium says it is one of only two networks currently approved to provide the Global Maritime Distress and Safety System, a ship safety service.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Satellites that may have operational problems or fail before the end of their lives.
    • Disruption of its ground operations, which could interrupt service.
    • Fast technological change in satellite communications that may require large new spending.
    • Collisions between its satellites and space debris or other spacecraft.
    • Cyberattacks and other security threats.
    From Iridium Communications's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
