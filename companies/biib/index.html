    What Biogen does

    Biogen is a drug company that discovers, develops and sells medicines for serious diseases, with a focus on neurology, specialized immunology and rare diseases. It sells a range of multiple sclerosis (MS) drugs, including Vumerity, Tysabri, Tecfidera, Avonex and Plegridy. It also sells Spinraza for spinal muscular atrophy, Skyclarys for Friedreich's ataxia and Qalsody for ALS.

    Biogen earns money from selling its own medicines and from rights tied to partners' products. It works with Eisai on Leqembi for Alzheimer's disease and with Supernus on Zurzuvae for postpartum depression. It has business and financial rights to Rituxan, Gazyva, Ocrevus and other drugs developed with Genentech, a member of the Roche Group. It also sells biosimilars, which are close copies of existing biologic drugs, referencing Enbrel, Humira and Remicade.

    The company builds its pipeline through its own research, collaborations and acquisitions. In November 2025 it bought Alcyone Therapeutics, which is developing a port and catheter device for delivering Spinraza. The cost was approximately $85.0 million. It says the first and only drug approved in the U.S. and E.U. for Friedreich's ataxia in adults and adolescents is one it markets.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Depending heavily on revenue from its current products.
    • Failing to develop new products and new uses for existing ones, since trial success does not ensure later approval.
    • Competition that could hurt its market position.
    • Dependence on collaborators and other third parties for revenue, development and approvals.
    • Healthcare reform and safety events that could limit use of its products.
    From Biogen's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 6, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
