    What DigitalOcean does

    DigitalOcean sells cloud computing services to growing technology companies and developers. It describes its platform as an inference cloud for building and running artificial intelligence applications, with GPU computing power, standard cloud services and tools for working with AI models. Customers range from online gaming to fintech to cybersecurity, and use it to host websites, build apps and add AI to their products.

    Its products include virtual machines called Droplets, storage and networking, managed hosting, databases and Kubernetes, and a marketplace of apps. Its AI line, called Gradient, includes GPU servers, building-block services such as large language models, and AI agents.

    DigitalOcean wins most customers through self-service sign-up, backed by inside and outside sales staff and partners. It focuses on customers it calls Digital Native Enterprises, which spend more than $500 in a month. It had about 21,000 of them at the end of 2025, and they made up 60% of that year's revenue. It says its edge is that it is simpler and easier to approach than the larger cloud providers.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Results that swing from period to period and make future results hard to predict.
    • Not being able to stay profitable.
    • Failing to grow usage by current customers or to attract and keep customers.
    • Its AI products and services may not succeed.
    • Its reliance on third-party data center providers to keep the platform running.
    From DigitalOcean's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 24, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
