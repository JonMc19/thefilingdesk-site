    What CEVA does

    CEVA licenses silicon and software designs, called intellectual property, to chip makers and equipment manufacturers. Its designs cover three areas it groups as connect, sense and infer: wireless connectivity, sensing and motion software, and processors that run AI. The company says it is the only intellectual property company with leadership across all three, and calls the combined field Physical AI.

    Customers put CEVA's designs into chips they make and sell for consumer devices, cars, industrial and infrastructure equipment, and phones and PCs. CEVA says devices using its technology have shipped in more than 20 billion units since 2003 and that its technology is in about 2 billion devices a year.

    CEVA is headquartered in Rockville, Maryland, and has development centers in France, Greece, Ireland, Israel, Serbia and the United States. It also sells spatial audio and motion sensing software, where it competes with firms such as Waves, Dolby and CyweeMotion.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competition that could mean lost sales, lower prices and lower revenue.
    • Its designs being used only if customers build them into end products that sell well.
    • Customers over which it has no control and who may not promote its technology.
    • Cyclical swings in the semiconductor industry and supply chain disruptions.
    • Changing needs of end users that it may not meet in time.
    From CEVA's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    From fiscal 2016 to 2021, earnings per share fell 49.5% a year and net income 50.2% a year, as new shares raised the share count by 8% and diluted each share's earnings. CEVA then lost money in fiscal 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025.
