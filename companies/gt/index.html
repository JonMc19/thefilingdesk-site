    What Goodyear Tire and Rubber does

    Goodyear develops, makes, distributes and sells tires for cars, trucks, buses, aircraft, motorcycles, farm equipment and other uses. It sells them to vehicle makers as original equipment and, mostly, as replacements. It also retreads truck and aircraft tires, runs about 750 retail outlets that sell tires and do repairs, and operates commercial truck service centers. Its brands include Goodyear, Cooper, Kelly, Mastercraft, Mickey Thompson, Fulda and Avon, plus private-label brands for some customers.

    Goodyear has three segments, each a regional tire business: Americas; Europe, Middle East and Africa; and Asia Pacific. About 84% of its 2025 sales were tire units. In 2025 its net sales were $18,280 million and it had a net loss of $1,721 million. It names Bridgestone and Michelin as its two major worldwide competitors.

    It finished a multi-year plan called Goodyear Forward in 2025. As part of it, Goodyear sold its off-the-road tire business, the Dunlop brand and its polymer chemicals business, for gross proceeds of about $2.2 billion, and used the money to cut debt. It makes tires at 49 factories in 19 countries and has about 63,000 full-time and temporary employees.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Not carrying out its strategic plans, or not being able to keep the savings from its Goodyear Forward plan.
    • Global competition that could cut its market share, and spending on equipment that may fall short.
    • Higher raw material, energy and transportation costs.
    • Labor union contracts that expire, and strikes or work stoppages.
    • US tariffs and other trade limits on imported tires, raw materials and equipment.
    From Goodyear Tire and Rubber's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 10, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
