    What ITT does

    ITT makes engineered parts and equipment for transportation, industrial and energy customers. These parts sit inside other companies' machines, systems and factory processes. It reports sales of $3.9 billion in about 125 countries, with 65% of revenue from outside the United States.

    It has three segments. Motion Technologies makes brake pads, shock absorbers and damping products for the car and rail markets. Industrial Process makes industrial pumps, valves, and monitoring and control systems, and provides aftermarket services for customers in energy, chemicals, drugs, mining, food and power generation, among others. Connect & Control Technologies makes connectors for harsh environments and parts that absorb energy and control flow, mainly for aerospace, defense and industrial customers.

    ITT says its engineering work leads to repeat business: ongoing customer relationships, spare parts and services after the first sale, and a place on long-lived products from original equipment makers. Its brands include KONI, Goulds Pumps, Cannon and Enidine. It has about 11,600 employees in 38 countries, and its latest 10-K describes a pending acquisition of SPX FLOW.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A significant part of its revenue comes from a single customer.
    • Raw material price swings and reliance on a limited number of suppliers.
    • Tariffs and other risks of selling and operating outside the United States.
    • Weaker spending by customers on capital investment and maintenance in cyclical markets.
    • The pending SPX FLOW acquisition may not close, or may not be integrated as planned, and adds debt.
    From ITT's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 9, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
