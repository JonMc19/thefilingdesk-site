    What Norfolk Southern does

    Norfolk Southern owns a major freight railroad, Norfolk Southern Railway. It hauls raw materials, parts and finished goods mainly across the Southeast, East and Midwest, and to the rest of the country through connections with other railroads. It also moves freight to and from several Atlantic and Gulf Coast ports. At the end of 2025 it ran about 19,100 route miles of track in 22 states and the District of Columbia.

    Railway operating revenue was $12.2 billion in 2025. Merchandise freight, such as farm and forest products, chemicals, metals, construction materials and cars and car parts, brought in 63%. Intermodal freight, which moves in shipping containers and trailers, brought in 25%. Coal brought in 12%. It sets most prices through private contracts or exempt quotes, which the federal Surface Transportation Board does not regulate. Its main rail competitor is CSX, and it also competes with trucks and barges.

    The company had about 19,300 employees at the end of 2025, and roughly 80% of its railroad employees are covered by union agreements. In July 2025 it agreed to be acquired by Union Pacific. The deal needs approval from the Surface Transportation Board, among other conditions.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • The pending merger with Union Pacific may be delayed or not completed, which could bring added costs and lost revenue.
    • Government actions and liabilities that followed the Eastern Ohio derailment incident.
    • Environmental liability and fines if it cannot comply with environmental laws, including for contamination.
    • Higher insurance costs, or losing insurance for some losses, including those from terrorism.
    From Norfolk Southern's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 9, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
