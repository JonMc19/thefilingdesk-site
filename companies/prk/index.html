    What Park National does

    Park National is a financial holding company based in Newark, Ohio. Its roots go back to Park National Bank of Ohio, founded in 1908, and it became a bank holding company in 1987. It owns and oversees its subsidiaries, which are mainly in banking. The parent sets overall policies, including lending and financial strategy, and the subsidiaries' officers run day-to-day operations.

    Banking is carried out through Park National Bank, which offers commercial banking, consumer banking and wealth management, including trust and investment services. It focuses on small and medium-sized communities in Ohio, North Carolina and South Carolina, and in northern Kentucky. A bank earns money mostly by lending out the deposits it takes in and charging fees for its services, and Park's results depend on loans repaid and on the interest it earns and pays.

    At the end of 2025 Park operated 87 financial service offices in Ohio, northern Kentucky and the Carolinas. It had 1,731 active associates, which it says equals 1,694 full-time equivalent employees.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Changes in interest rates that could hurt its earnings, cash flow and financial condition.
    • Borrowers who fail to repay, with an allowance for credit losses that may prove too small.
    • Weaker local economies and real estate values in the areas where it lends.
    • Failures or breaches in its systems or those of its service providers that could harm customers and its reputation.
    • Inflation that could hurt its business and its customers.
