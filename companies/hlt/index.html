    What Hilton Worldwide does

    Hilton is a hotel company with 9,158 properties and 1,351,351 rooms in 143 countries and territories at the end of 2025. Its brands range from luxury and lifestyle hotels to full-service, focused-service and all-suites hotels, plus timeshare (vacation ownership) brands. Hilton Grand Vacations has the exclusive right to use its timeshare brands under a license.

    Hilton reports two segments. The management and franchise segment covers hotels run for outside owners or licensed to use Hilton's brand and booking channels. It earns fees from those owners, license fees from partners such as co-branded credit card providers, and fees for managing its own hotels. The ownership segment earns money from room sales, food and drink, and other services at hotels it consolidates. It lists competitors brand by brand, including Marriott, Hyatt and Four Seasons.

    Hilton runs a guest loyalty program, Hilton Honors, which had 243 million members at the end of 2025. Members earn points that can be used for stays and with partners such as credit card providers, airlines and car rental companies. Hilton also has a development pipeline of 3,703 hotels it expects to add, nearly all of them in its management and franchise segment.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A weak economy or low travel demand cutting its revenue and slowing its growth.
    • Damage to the reputation or quality of its brands, or poor relationships with the hotel owners who sign its contracts.
    • Owners being unable to get financing or invest in their hotels, or the hotels in its development pipeline never getting built.
    • Competition from other hotel brands and from internet booking channels.
    • Problems with its reservation system, cyber-attacks or the handling of guests' personal data.
    From Hilton Worldwide's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 11, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
