    What Live Nation Entertainment does

    Live Nation promotes concerts, runs venues and sells tickets. It says it is the largest producer of live music concerts in the world, and that it owns, operates, has exclusive booking rights for or has an equity interest in 460 venues, including House of Blues clubs and The Fillmore in San Francisco. Its artist management companies represent music artists across genres.

    The company's core businesses are the promotion of live events, ticketing, and sponsorship and advertising. It earns money from ticket sales and fees, from fans spending at its venues on food, drinks and merchandise, and from sponsors and advertisers that want to reach concertgoers. Its Ticketmaster unit sells tickets through its websites, mobile apps and retail outlets for arenas, stadiums, sports teams and other event organizers, and distributed 646 million tickets in 2025. Live Nation says it connected over 805 million fans across its concerts and ticketing platforms in 55 countries in 2025.

    Live Nation says it invests nearly $15 billion a year in artist performances. It plans to expand its venues, concert business and ticketing platform, and to sell more sponsorships. It says it faces intense competition in live music and ticketing.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Changes in public tastes, and its ability to secure popular artists and live music events.
    • Intense competition in live music and ticketing that could stop it from keeping or growing its revenue.
    • Its dependence on relationships with key promoters, executives, agents, managers and artists.
    • Its ability to lease, buy and build venues on acceptable terms.
    • Accidents, injuries and bad weather at its live events, which could bring claims and lower attendance.
    From Live Nation Entertainment's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 19, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
