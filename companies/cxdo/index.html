    What Crexendo does

    Crexendo sells cloud phone and communications services to businesses of all sizes. Its unified communications service covers voice calling, video, contact center and managed IT services. It also licenses the cloud communications software behind these services to other companies, which resell them under their own brands. The company says its software supports over seven million end users worldwide, through more than 240 software subscribers and its own direct retail business.

    It reports two segments. Cloud Telecommunications sells hosted calling plans with contracts that usually run three to five years, plus broadband internet, managed IT services and the sale or lease of desk phones and other equipment. Software Solutions earns money from software licenses, maintenance and support, and professional services such as consulting, design and installation.

    Crexendo says it competes with traditional phone, wireless and cable companies, other providers of cloud phone services, and other alternative voice providers. It stopped paying a quarterly dividend in April 2023, and it expects to look at acquisitions and other changes to its capital structure to expand.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Intense competition from phone, wireless and cable companies and other cloud communications providers.
    • It may not reach or keep operating profitability.
    • Acquisitions it expects to make could distract management, raise costs and dilute shareholders.
    • It may not be able to raise financing on good terms, or at all.
    • Its uptime guarantee exposes it to customer credits, and usage-based pricing can make revenue swing.
    From Crexendo's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 3, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
