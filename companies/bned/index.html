    What Barnes & Noble Education does

    Barnes & Noble Education runs bookstores, physical and virtual, for college and university campuses and for K-12 schools across the United States. It is also a textbook wholesaler and a provider of inventory management hardware and software. As of May 2, 2026, it operated 1,116 physical and virtual bookstores selling course materials and general merchandise. Its subsidiaries include Barnes & Noble College, which runs the stores, and MBS Textbook Exchange, the wholesaler.

    It works through contracts with schools. Under its First Day programs, including First Day Complete, students get faculty-required course materials by the first day of class at below the combined retail price, billed by the school as a course charge or included in tuition. It says these programs have reversed past declines in course materials revenue at schools that adopted them. It plans to get more schools to adopt First Day Complete.

    For online sales and general merchandise, it uses Fanatics and Lids as service providers.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Reliance on capital markets, bank credit facilities and short-term vendor financing for liquidity.
    • Failing to win, keep or renew managed bookstore contracts on profitable terms.
    • Competition that it expects to grow.
    • Changes in how publishers distribute course materials, and disrupted supplier relationships.
    • Managing wholesale inventory, and dependence on shipping arrangements.
    From Barnes & Noble Education's annual report on Form 10-K, filed July 9, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
