    What AES does

    AES generates and sells electricity, and also owns two US electric utilities, AES Indiana and AES Ohio. Its power plants use a mix of technologies and fuels, including solar, wind, energy storage, natural gas and coal. It owns or operates plants in the United States and in other countries, including developing ones.

    Most of its generation businesses sell electricity under medium- or long-term contracts, called power purchase agreements, with terms of two to five years or more than five years. Others sell under short-term agreements in competitive markets. AES says its strategy is to partner with large corporations that need electricity, and it names data center companies, mainly in the US, and large mining companies outside the US as key customers. Its utilities earn money by delivering power to customers at rates set by state regulators.

    AES has a backlog of renewable projects that have signed contracts but are not yet operating, including projects already under construction. In 2025 it sold a minority interest in one of its businesses to raise cash. It says its renewables results depend on finishing construction on time and earning US renewable tax credits.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Problems that come with generating and distributing electricity and can hurt its operations and finances.
    • Uncertainty in building renewable energy and other development projects.
    • Wholesale power prices that swing sharply in the markets where it sells.
    • Doing a large share of its business outside the US, including in developing countries.
    • Relying on a few large suppliers or customers that could fail to pay or perform.
    From AES's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 2, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
