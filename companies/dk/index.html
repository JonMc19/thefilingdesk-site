    What Delek US does

    Delek US runs oil refineries and a pipeline, storage and distribution business. It calls itself an integrated downstream energy business, meaning it operates after the oil is pumped: it turns crude oil into fuels, and moves, stores and sells crude oil and refined products. It reports two segments, Refining and Logistics.

    Refining has four refineries, in Tyler and Big Spring in Texas, El Dorado in Arkansas and Krotz Springs in Louisiana, all in the Gulf Coast region. Together they have a capacity of 302,000 barrels a day, and the company also runs biodiesel facilities. Logistics owns light product terminals, pipelines, storage tanks, water disposal and gas processing capacity, and holds interests in crude oil pipeline joint ventures.

    The company earns money from the gap between what it pays for crude oil and what it sells fuels for, and from fees for moving and storing products. It says its operations are heavily regulated, and it competes with many other refiners and logistics companies. It also has to buy or generate renewable fuel credits, known as RINs, to meet federal blending rules.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A drop in refining margins, which would cut its results and cash flow.
    • Changes in global oil markets that hurt its refineries.
    • Heavy regulation, with the cost of complying with environmental and safety rules.
    • The cost and availability of the renewable fuel credits (RINs) that refiners must obtain.
    • Strong competition in refining and logistics.
    From Delek US's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
