    What BKV does

    BKV produces natural gas and generates power from natural gas. It describes four business lines: natural gas production, natural gas midstream (gathering and processing the gas), power generation, and carbon capture, utilization and sequestration, known as CCUS, which means capturing carbon dioxide and storing it underground. Its gas properties are in the Barnett Shale in Texas and in Northeast Pennsylvania.

    Its revenue comes mainly from selling the natural gas and natural gas liquids it produces. It owns a 50% share of a joint venture that owns the Temple power plants near Temple, Texas, which sell power on the Texas grid, and it runs a retail power business through another joint venture. Its first CCUS project began storing carbon dioxide in November 2023, and it expects two more to start in 2026.

    BKV says it keeps control of its gas from the well through gathering and processing, which it calls a closed-loop model. It completed its stock offering to the public in September 2024, and its majority stockholder is BNAC. It also grows by buying assets, and it has set goals to reach net-zero emissions from its own gas operations in the early 2030s.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Swings in natural gas and NGL prices that it cannot control.
    • Relying on a single third party to gather, process and move much of its gas.
    • Its power and carbon capture businesses run through joint ventures that need partners' consent.
    • Carbon capture projects that need large investment and depend on tax incentives.
    • Reserve estimates that prove wrong, and difficulties integrating assets it acquires.
    From BKV's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 6, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
