    What Liberty Global does

    Liberty Global runs and invests in telecom and media businesses, mostly in Europe. It manages three platforms. Liberty Telecom provides broadband internet, video, mobile and phone services under well-known consumer brands, with approximately 80 million fixed and mobile connections at the end of 2025. Liberty Growth invests in technology, media, sports and infrastructure businesses. Liberty Services provides technology, operational and financial services to its affiliates and, increasingly, to outside customers.

    Its main telecom operations are Telenet in Belgium, Virgin Media in Ireland and UPC in Slovakia, which it owns fully, with a sale of the Slovakia business pending. It owns half of Virgin Media O2 in the United Kingdom and half of VodafoneZiggo in the Netherlands, and accounts for those two as equity method investments. Customers can save by bundling internet, video, phone and mobile services.

    As of December 31, 2025, Liberty Growth held investments in approximately 70 companies and funds valued at approximately $3.4 billion. Liberty Services gets most of its revenue from affiliates and related parties.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competition from many service providers in broadband, video and mobile, which is increasing.
    • Technology changes that could reduce demand for its services.
    • Network upgrades and new builds that may not earn a positive return.
    • System failures or security breaches that could disrupt service and drive away customers.
    • A history of reporting losses, so it may not report net earnings.
    From Liberty Global's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 18, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Income statement

    From fiscal 2016 to 2024, earnings per share grew 11.2% a year and net income fell 0.9% a year, as buybacks cut the share count by 58%. Liberty Global then lost money in fiscal 2025.
