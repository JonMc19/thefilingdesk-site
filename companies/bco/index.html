    What Brink's does

    Brink's moves, counts and stores cash and other valuables for others. Its customers include banks, retailers, government agencies, mints and jewelers. It serves customers in more than 100 countries and has controlling ownership of companies in 51 countries. It employs about 65,400 people and runs about 1,200 facilities and 15,900 vehicles.

    Its largest line is cash and valuables management, which was 72% of 2025 revenue. It covers armored transport of cash and goods such as jewelry, precious metals and banknotes, cash processing and vaulting, and basic ATM services like refilling cash. The rest, 28% of revenue, comes from digital retail solutions (smart safes, software and analytics for retailers) and ATM managed services, in which Brink's runs ATMs for banks and retailers. Those contracts usually recur and run for several years.

    The company reports four segments: North America, Latin America, Europe, and Rest of World. Brink's was founded in 1859 and is based in Richmond, Virginia.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competition in cash handling and security services, which it describes as highly competitive.
    • Less use of cash by consumers and businesses, which would cut demand for its core services.
    • Its settlement agreements with the U.S. Department of Justice and FinCEN, and the penalties for breaking them.
    • Large operations outside the United States, and the legal and currency risks that come with them.
    • Losses of customers' valuables in its care, which could hurt earnings and cash flow.
    From Brink's's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
