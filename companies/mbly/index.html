    What Mobileye does

    Mobileye makes the technology behind advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS), such as camera-based features that warn a driver or brake for them, and is developing technology for self-driving cars. It sells a mix of software and its own EyeQ chips, which process what the car's cameras and sensors see. Its customers are automakers and the companies that supply them.

    Mobileye says it started in ADAS more than 25 years ago and is building a path toward fully autonomous vehicles, in which a human does not need to drive for long stretches. It also owns Moovit, a transit app and mobility data business. In February 2026 it completed the purchase of Mentee Robotics, a humanoid robotics company, which it says extends its AI work into robots.

    Mobileye depends on STMicroelectronics to make its EyeQ chips, and it has a 52- or 53-week fiscal year that ends on the last Saturday in December. Mobileye says it competes in a highly competitive market and that large technology companies may join it.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to develop and launch new or improved solutions in a cost-effective and timely way.
    • Research and development that does not pay off.
    • Its dependence on STMicroelectronics to make its EyeQ chips, and chip shortages like those of 2021 and 2022.
    • A highly competitive market, where large technology companies could enter.
    • Claims or losses from alleged defects in its solutions, which could harm its reputation.
    From Mobileye's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
