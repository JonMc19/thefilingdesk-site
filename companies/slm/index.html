    What SLM does

    SLM Corporation, better known as Sallie Mae, is a consumer bank focused on paying for higher education. Its main business is making and servicing Private Education Loans, which are student loans that no government makes, insures or guarantees. Students and their families use them to cover the gap between college costs and what they get from savings, scholarships, grants and federal aid.

    It lends through its wholly owned subsidiary, Sallie Mae Bank, an industrial bank, and also offers deposit accounts insured by the FDIC. The company says it serves more families than any other private student loan lender. In 2025 it made about $7.4 billion of these loans and had $20.3 billion of them on its books at year end.

    Lending peaks in the third quarter, before fall classes start, with a smaller peak before spring classes. The company was formed in late 2013 and separated from Navient in 2014, keeping the consumer banking business. It also offers free tools and scholarship resources for planning for college, and is working to build new lines of business.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its products are concentrated in Private Education Loans, in a competitive market.
    • Borrowers refinancing or consolidating existing loans.
    • Loan defaults, and a credit loss allowance that may prove too low.
    • Reliance on online deposits for funding, and exposure to changes in interest rates.
    • Failing to meet bank capital requirements set by regulators.
