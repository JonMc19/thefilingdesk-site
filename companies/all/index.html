    What Allstate does

    Allstate is a holding company whose business runs mainly through Allstate Insurance Company and other subsidiaries. It sells auto, homeowners and other personal insurance, plus select commercial property and casualty coverage. It also sells consumer product protection plans, roadside assistance, identity protection and automotive protection products, and offers analytic services built on vehicle telematics data. It reports 211 million policies in force and 53,000 employees.

    It reports four segments. Allstate Protection sells auto, homeowners, other personal lines and commercial insurance through exclusive agents, independent agents, contact centers and online, under the Allstate, National General, Direct Auto and Answer Financial brands. Protection Services covers protection plans, roadside, dealer services, identity protection and Arity. Run-off Property-Liability covers policies written from the 1960s to the mid-1980s with asbestos and environmental claims. Corporate holds holding company activities.

    Allstate's strategy is to increase market share in personal property-liability insurance and broaden its protection offerings. It also manages an investment portfolio of $83.24 billion.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Catastrophes and severe weather that can cause significant losses.
    • Price competition and changes in regulation and underwriting standards in property and casualty insurance.
    • A regulatory environment that requires rates and products to be approved and can dictate how it underwrites.
    • Market risk in its investment portfolios, including interest rates, equity prices and credit declines.
    • Operating in highly competitive markets.
    From Allstate's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 20, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
