    What Lincoln Educational Services does

    Lincoln Tech runs 22 campuses in the United States that train students for jobs in skilled trades, automotive, health sciences and information technology. Its schools operate under the names Lincoln Technical Institute, Lincoln College of Technology and Nashville Auto Diesel College. Most serve major metropolitan areas, and five are destination schools that draw students from across the country.

    It earns its revenue mainly from tuition. Its campuses are nationally accredited and can take part in federal financial aid programs, which lets students use federal student loans and other aid to pay. It reports two segments, Campus Operations and Transitional, but at the end of 2025 all of its campuses were in Campus Operations. It competes with other schools, and it says it focuses on fields that traditional colleges serve less.

    At the end of 2025 it had 17,046 students. Annual revenue was $518.2 million, up 17.8% from the year before. The company dates its history to a Newark, New Jersey school opened in 1946.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Changes to federal student aid rules or funding, which students rely on to pay tuition.
    • Fines or loss of aid eligibility if its schools break the Department of Education's rules.
    • Losing state licenses or accreditation, which would stop its schools from taking part in federal aid.
    • Less private or federal student financing, or higher costs for students.
    • Competition, and the need to keep updating its programs and recruit new students.
    From Lincoln Educational Services's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 2, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
