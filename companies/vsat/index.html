    What Viasat does

    Viasat provides satellite-based communications services and equipment. Its customers are in aviation, shipping, business, consumer, military and government markets. It runs its own fleet of satellites, which it says includes 23 in service or operational satellites as of March 31, 2026, and it also uses capacity from other operators' satellites. In 2023 it bought Inmarsat, a satellite operator, for cash and shares of its stock.

    It reports two segments. Communication Services sells broadband and narrowband connectivity to government and commercial customers, including in-flight internet for commercial airlines and business jets, and home and business internet. It also sells satellite and wireless products and terminals. Defense and Advanced Technologies makes encryption and cybersecurity products, tactical gateways, modems, antennas and other satellite communication systems for military and government customers.

    The company is building more satellites, including ViaSat-3 class satellites. It launched the second in November 2025 and the third in April 2026, with commercial service on the third expected to start by late summer 2026. It had in-flight internet systems in service on approximately 4,580 commercial aircraft as of March 31, 2026.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Satellite failures, degraded performance or a shorter useful life than planned.
    • Delays, cost overruns, launch failures and other risks in building and launching new satellites.
    • A large amount of debt that can limit what it can do and make payments harder.
    • Quarter-to-quarter results that are difficult to predict.
    • Regulatory limits and approvals needed to run its satellite and communications business.
    From Viasat's annual report on Form 10-K, filed May 29, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
