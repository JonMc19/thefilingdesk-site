    What Planet Labs does

    Planet Labs operates hundreds of small satellites that photograph all of Earth's land every day. It sells access to this imagery, and to analytics built on it, through a cloud-based platform. Customers include governments and defense and intelligence agencies, and companies in agriculture, energy, forestry, finance, insurance and mapping.

    It earns most of its revenue from licenses to its data and analytics, sold as fixed-price subscriptions or usage-based contracts. Planet says most of this revenue recurs, and that the same image can be sold many times, unlike providers that sell each image to a single buyer. It also earns revenue from long-term satellite services contracts, in which it designs and builds satellites for large government and business customers and runs launch, ground station and operations work for them. It also sells dedicated image-taking capacity on satellites it or a customer owns.

    Planet designs and builds its own satellites. As of January 31, 2026, it had approximately 1,000 employees in 29 countries. Its fiscal year ends January 31.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Operating losses, and a limited history at its current scale and under its current strategy, which make results hard to predict.
    • The market for its offerings may not grow as it expects, or customers may not adopt them.
    • Growing competition from commercial companies and governments in its markets.
    • Failing to win new customers or to get existing ones to renew and expand.
    • Uncertainty in developing, building, launching and operating its satellites and related infrastructure.
    From Planet Labs's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 23, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
