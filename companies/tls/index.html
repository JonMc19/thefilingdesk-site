    What Telos does

    Telos, based in Ashburn, Virginia, sells cybersecurity, identity and secure network technology and services. Its best-known product is Xacta, software that automates the work of assessing cyber risk and proving that systems meet security rules. It also sells security consulting and testing, secure messaging software used by the military and intelligence community, identity and biometric services, and enrollment for TSA PreCheck, the airport screening program.

    Its main customers are the U.S. federal government, large companies and international customers. It reports two segments. Security Solutions, which covers cyber, cloud, identity and messaging, made up 90.8% of 2025 revenue. Secure Networks, which builds and runs secure networks for customers, made up 9.2%.

    Telos says much of its revenue comes from contracts and subcontracts with the federal government. At the end of 2025 it had 525 employees, nearly all in the United States.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Federal agencies can end, delay or cut its contracts, and government spending changes could reduce its work.
    • Reliance on a few key customer contracts for a large part of its future revenue.
    • Customers may not renew or expand their contracts and subscriptions.
    • Competitive bidding and protests on government contracts may stop it winning or keeping work.
    • Cyberattacks on its own systems, and reliance on third parties for computing and supply.
    From Telos's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 16, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Piotroski F-score: how many of nine simple tests of profitability, funding and efficiency the company passed in the year (Piotroski, 2000); 8 or 9 is strong, 0 to 2 weak.
