    What enCore Energy does

    enCore Energy extracts uranium in the United States for use as fuel in nuclear power plants. It uses in-situ recovery (ISR), in which a solution is pumped through underground rock to dissolve the uranium, and the solution is brought up and processed. It was incorporated in British Columbia in 2009 and trades on Nasdaq and the TSX Venture Exchange.

    It runs central processing plants at the Rosita and Alta Mesa projects in South Texas. It began extracting uranium at Rosita in 2024, and at Alta Mesa in June 2024. It says it was one of only three uranium extraction operations in the United States. Its plan is to add extraction capacity in South Texas and then explore projects in South Dakota and Wyoming.

    The company is an "exploration stage issuer" under SEC mining rules because it has not established proven or probable mineral reserves for any of its uranium projects, even though it has started extracting. It said it has a history of negative operating cash flow and may need more money to carry out its plans.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Being an exploration stage company with no established mineral reserves, so its projects may never be profitable.
    • Needing more financing, and enough cash to service its debt.
    • Not getting the expected benefits from the Alta Mesa project.
    • Mining and extraction hazards, and the use of independent contractors to run its drilling rigs.
    • Buying mining assets or businesses on poor terms.
