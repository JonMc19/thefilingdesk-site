    What GoPro does

    GoPro makes action cameras that people mount on helmets, bikes, boards and bodies to record video and photos. Its cameras, mounts and accessories, lifestyle gear, apps and subscription services produce substantially all of its revenue. Its flagship camera is the HERO13 Black.

    It sells hardware through retailers, distributors and its own website, GoPro.com. Customers can buy subscriptions through GoPro.com or its Quik mobile app. In 2025 it launched an AI Training program that lets U.S. subscribers who opt in earn money from their cloud-stored content for AI model training.

    GoPro says it has lost market share to new competitors in recent years. It is investing in a new AI-driven chip, called GP3, which it expects to lead to new types of cameras, accessories and subscription offerings.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Substantial operating losses in recent years; it may not return to revenue growth or profit.
    • It must keep introducing new products on schedule, in a highly competitive digital imaging market.
    • Its products depend on the availability and cost of key components such as memory and microprocessors.
    • It may not be able to win or keep subscribers.
    • Tariffs and changes in trade policy could hurt its business.
    From GoPro's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Piotroski F-score: how many of nine simple tests of profitability, funding and efficiency the company passed in the year (Piotroski, 2000); 8 or 9 is strong, 0 to 2 weak. Rule of 40: revenue growth plus free cash flow margin; software investors treat 40% or more as the bar. Altman Z'': working capital, retained earnings, operating income and equity weighed against assets and liabilities (Altman, 1995); Altman puts above 2.6 in the safe zone and below 1.1 in the distress zone. Accruals ratio: net income minus cash from operations, over average total assets; lower means profit is backed by cash.
