    What Jackson Financial does

    Jackson Financial is a life insurance and retirement company. It sells annuities to retail investors in the U.S. An annuity is a contract in which a customer pays an insurer money and later receives income or a return. Its main product is the variable annuity, which lets customers choose among many investment options. It also sells registered index-linked, fixed index, fixed and payout annuities.

    It sells through independent broker-dealers, wirehouses, regional broker-dealers, banks, independent investment advisors, third-party platforms and insurance agents. Its retail annuity sales were $19.7 billion in 2025. According to an industry trade group, LIMRA, it ranked seventh among U.S. retail annuity companies by sales for the first nine months of 2025.

    Jackson earns fees that grow with the value of assets in customers' accounts, which are mostly invested in stocks. It administers approximately 82% of its in-force policies on its own platform, and uses hedging and reinsurance to manage its risks. It also manages investments for others.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Falling stock markets reduce the fees it earns, which depend on customers' account values.
    • Its hedging and reinsurance leave it exposed to the failure of the other side to pay.
    • A downgrade of its financial strength ratings could cost it business.
    • As a holding company, it depends on dividends from its insurance subsidiaries to meet its obligations.
    • A large share of its funding comes from the Federal Home Loan Bank of Indianapolis.
