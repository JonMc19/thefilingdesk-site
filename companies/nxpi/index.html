    What NXP Semiconductors does

    NXP Semiconductors, based in Eindhoven, the Netherlands, designs chips and sells them to makers of cars, factory and smart-home equipment, phones and communications gear. Its products include microcontrollers (small chips that control a device), application and communication processors, wireless connectivity chips, analog and power chips, radio-frequency amplifiers for cell towers, sensors and security chips used in passports, payment cards and phones.

    It reports one business segment but sells into four end markets: Automotive, Industrial & IoT (internet-connected devices), Mobile, and Communication Infrastructure & Other. Revenue in 2025 was $12,269 million. Automotive is its main focus, and it describes growth coming from driver assistance, electric vehicles and software-defined vehicles.

    NXP uses a mix of its own chip factories, a factory run jointly with another chip company, and outside foundries. It is joining two new 300 millimeter chip plants as a part-owner: one in Dresden, Germany, and one in Singapore. In 2025 it bought TTTech Auto, Aviva Links and Kinara, which add automotive software, in-car networking and AI processing chips.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A semiconductor industry that rises and falls in cycles.
    • Heavy competition and pricing pressure, especially on older technology, and having to win selection by customers.
    • Tariffs and other trade restrictions.
    • Manufacturing problems and reliance on outside suppliers and joint venture partners it does not control.
    • Product defects and security flaws that could bring warranty or liability claims.
    From NXP Semiconductors's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 19, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
