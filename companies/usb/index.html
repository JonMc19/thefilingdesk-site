    What U.S. Bancorp does

    U.S. Bancorp is a bank holding company based in Minneapolis. Through U.S. Bank, it takes deposits and makes loans to individuals, small businesses, companies, governments and other institutions. It also offers credit cards, payment and merchant processing, mortgage banking, trust and investment management, treasury and capital markets services. At the end of 2025 it held consolidated deposits of $522.2 billion. It had 2,075 branches in 26 states, mostly in the Midwest and West, and 4,428 ATMs.

    It reports four main segments plus a corporate group. Wealth, Corporate, Commercial and Institutional Banking serves wealthy individuals, mid-sized and large companies, real estate investors, governments and institutions. Consumer and Business Banking covers consumer and small business banking and consumer lending. Payment Services covers credit, debit and stored-value cards, corporate and purchasing cards, and merchant processing. Treasury and Corporate Support holds its investment portfolios, funding and corporate costs.

    In January 2026 U.S. Bancorp agreed to buy BTIG, a firm that does institutional trading, investment banking and research, for a purchase price of up to $1 billion. It expected the deal to close in the second quarter of 2026. It had 68,520 employees at the end of 2025 and competes with banks, credit unions, card companies, asset managers and technology firms.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Weak economic conditions that could reduce lending and hurt its borrowers' ability to repay.
    • Changes in interest rates that could reduce its net interest income, which it relies on heavily.
    • Cyberattacks, system failures or mistakes by employees or vendors that could disrupt its operations.
    • Changes in rules, including capital and liquidity requirements and possible credit card interest caps.
    • Changes in U.S. trade policy, including tariffs, that could hurt its customers and its business.
    From U.S. Bancorp's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 23, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
