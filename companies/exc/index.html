    What Exelon does

    Exelon is a utility holding company. Through six regulated utilities it moves electricity, and in some places natural gas, to homes and businesses. Its businesses are the transmission and distribution of energy.

    Its utilities are ComEd in northern Illinois, including Chicago; PECO in southeastern Pennsylvania, including Philadelphia; Baltimore Gas and Electric in central Maryland; and three utilities that sit under Pepco Holdings: Pepco in Washington, D.C. and parts of Maryland, Delmarva Power & Light in parts of Delaware and Maryland, and Atlantic City Electric in southern New Jersey. PECO, BGE and Delmarva also deliver natural gas. The others deliver electricity only.

    Exelon earns its money mainly from the rates its utilities charge customers, which state regulators approve. Its service company, BSC, provides legal, finance, human resources, technology and supply management help to the subsidiaries at cost.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Changes in laws or regulations, or regulators' actions, that could cut revenue and earnings.
    • Rate case decisions that are slow, contested or appealed, and may not let utilities recover their costs.
    • Higher costs and penalties from mandatory reliability standards for the power grid.
    • Large costs to meet environmental obligations, including cleanup of former gas plant sites.
    • Heavy dependence on regulators letting it recover the cost of buying, moving and delivering power and gas.
    From Exelon's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
