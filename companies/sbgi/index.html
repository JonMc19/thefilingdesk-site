    What Sinclair does

    Sinclair is a media company built around local television stations. It shows programming from third-party networks and syndicators, local news, original programming it produces, its own networks and professional sports. It distributes that content through its broadcast stations, a digital platform and third-party platforms.

    It reports two segments: local media and tennis. The tennis segment, which includes the Tennis Channel, earns fees from distributors, including streaming services that carry live TV, and advertising sold in its programming. Its risk factors point to advertising and to distribution agreements with the cable, satellite and streaming services that carry its stations as important sources of revenue for local media.

    Sinclair also owns digital media companies and holds interests in technical and software companies, research companies and other businesses and assets, including real estate, venture capital and private equity. It was formed in 2022 as the public parent of Sinclair Broadcast Group, founded in 1986.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A faster drop in the number of subscribers to cable and similar services that carry its stations.
    • Trouble renewing distribution agreements on comparable terms.
    • Distributors lobbying to change the rules for retransmission consent, which would give them more bargaining power.
    • Competition for viewers and advertisers, and changing viewing habits and technology.
    • Acquisitions and investments that could add risk and debt, and the outcome of its strategic review process.
    From Sinclair's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
