    What Kiniksa Pharmaceuticals does

    Kiniksa Pharmaceuticals develops and sells medicines for diseases with unmet needs, mainly heart-related. Its one commercial product is ARCALYST, a protein that blocks two inflammation signals, interleukin-1 alpha and beta. It licensed ARCALYST from Regeneron in 2017 with worldwide rights, excluding the Middle East and North Africa, for uses other than cancer and local treatment of the eye or ear.

    The U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved ARCALYST for recurrent pericarditis (a painful inflammation of the sac around the heart) in 2021. It is also approved in the U.S. for two rare inflammatory conditions. Kiniksa sells it in the United States through a select network of specialty pharmacies and handles all sales and distribution. It splits profits on sales evenly with Regeneron. It gave a Chinese company, Huadong, rights to develop and sell it in that company's territory.

    Regeneron began transferring its manufacturing process, and Kiniksa is working to qualify Samsung as its new contract manufacturer.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It may not keep up commercial success with ARCALYST, its sole product, or succeed with future products.
    • It relies on a select network of specialty pharmacies to market and sell ARCALYST.
    • Insurers and government payors may not cover or pay enough for its medicines, and drug-price policy could limit prices.
    • It derives substantially all of its revenue from one use in the United States.
    • Its candidates may not advance in clinical development or win regulatory approval.
