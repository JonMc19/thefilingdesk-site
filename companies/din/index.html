    What Dine Brands does

    Dine Brands owns and franchises three restaurant chains: IHOP (family dining), Applebee's Neighborhood Grill + Bar (casual dining) and Fuzzy's Taco Shop (fast-casual). As of December 28, 2025, the large majority of its 3,509 restaurants were owned and run by independent franchisees.

    It reports three segments. Franchise earns mainly royalties and advertising fees from 1,812 IHOP, 1,520 Applebee's and 105 Fuzzy's franchised restaurants, plus sales of IHOP and Fuzzy's proprietary products to franchisees. Company-owned restaurants covers 12 IHOP, 59 Applebee's and one Fuzzy's. Rental earns rent from leases and subleases on 521 IHOP franchised restaurants and one Applebee's. About 74% of revenue came from the IHOP and Applebee's segments, and the rest mostly from the company-owned and rental segments.

    Its restaurants are in 20 countries and two U.S. territories, but international operations brought in less than 2% of revenue. It plans to grow by developing its brands, including dual-branded IHOP and Applebee's restaurants, and by exploring new concepts or acquisitions.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Debt from its securitization, which could let investors take over its assets if it defaults or cannot repay.
    • Its franchise model, which leaves it dependent on how franchisees run their restaurants.
    • Risks of the restaurant industry, such as competition, food costs and consumer demand.
    • Breaches or outages in its information systems and those of outside vendors.
    • Changes in laws or regulations that affect its business and data handling.
    From Dine Brands's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 25, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
