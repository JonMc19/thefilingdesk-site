    What Marathon Petroleum does

    Marathon Petroleum refines crude oil into gasoline, diesel, asphalt and other fuels and products, and moves and stores them through pipelines, terminals, barges and trucks. It says it runs one of the largest refining systems in the United States, with about 3.0 million barrels a day of crude oil refining capacity at refineries on the Gulf Coast, in the Mid-Continent and on the West Coast.

    It reports three segments. Refining & Marketing refines crude oil and sells the products to wholesale customers, on the spot market, to independent operators of Marathon-branded outlets and to dealers who mainly use the ARCO brand. Midstream gathers, transports, stores and processes crude oil, refined products and natural gas, and mostly reflects MPLX, a partnership in which Marathon owned the general partner and about 64 percent of the units at the end of 2025. Renewable Diesel makes and sells diesel from renewable feedstocks.

    Marathon Petroleum was spun off from Marathon Oil in 2011. It bought Andeavor in 2018, which widened its reach in the West, and sold its Speedway gas stations to 7-Eleven in 2021. It says it competes with companies that make their own feedstocks, own retail sites or have more money.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Volatile refining margins, which depend on factors outside its control.
    • Falling demand for gasoline and diesel as emissions rules, fuel efficiency and alternative-fuel vehicles spread.
    • Accidents and interruptions at its plants, pipelines and terminals, which carry inherent hazards.
    • The cost and availability of renewable identification numbers and low carbon fuel credits.
    • A drop in oil and gas production in the areas served by MPLX, its pipeline and processing partnership.
    From Marathon Petroleum's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
