    What Kymera Therapeutics does

    Kymera Therapeutics is a clinical-stage biopharmaceutical company, which means it tests its medicines in people and has none approved for sale. It works on targeted protein degradation, an approach in which a small-molecule medicine uses the body's natural cellular recycling system to remove a protein that drives disease, instead of just blocking it.

    Its work is mainly aimed at immune and inflammatory diseases. Kymera wants to offer a daily pill that works like the injected biologic drugs now used for many of these patients. Its publicly disclosed programs target three proteins: STAT6, IRF5 and IRAK4. Its lead medicine in the STAT6 program, KT-621, is in Phase 2b trials in adults and adolescents with moderate to severe atopic dermatitis (a skin condition) and in adults with moderate to severe asthma. KT-579, an oral IRF5 degrader, has started a Phase 1 trial.

    Kymera says it expects to start clinical testing of at least one new molecule each year, and it hopes to become a fully integrated drug company. It was formed in 2015.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It is a clinical-stage company, so it is hard to judge its business to date or its future.
    • It has lost money since it began and expects more losses for the foreseeable future.
    • It will need substantial additional money, and raising it may dilute its shareholders.
    • Its drug candidates may fail in clinical trials or fail to win regulatory approval.
    • Targeted protein degradation is a new kind of medicine, so its development path is hard to predict.
