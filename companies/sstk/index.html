    What Shutterstock does

    Shutterstock runs an online platform where customers license images, footage, music and 3D models. Contributors upload their work and are paid royalties based on how often customers download it. Beyond content, customers use the platform for creative work from the first idea through finished production.

    Content licensing is the majority of its business. Its other offerings, called Data, Distribution, and Services, include licensing metadata about its content, distribution and advertising through its Giphy business, which supplies GIFs, production services through Shutterstock Studios, and tailored services.

    On January 6, 2025 it agreed to combine with Getty Images in a merger of equals. Under the terms, Shutterstock shareholders would receive cash and Getty Images shares.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • The Getty Images merger not closing on time, or at all, including if regulators do not approve it.
    • A termination fee, and costs paid whether or not the merger closes.
    • Losing customers, suppliers or key staff while the merger is pending.
    • Difficulty integrating the two companies and not getting the expected benefits.
    • Failing to keep attracting customers and contributors to its platform.
    From Shutterstock's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 17, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
