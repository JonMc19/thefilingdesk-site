    What PureCycle Technologies does

    PureCycle Technologies is a Florida company trying to commercialize a patented process that recycles polypropylene, a common plastic used in packaging, car parts and many other goods. The process, first developed by P&G, separates the plastic from other plastics, colors, odors and impurities to produce a resin it calls PureFive, which it says has similar properties to new polypropylene.

    The company calls itself an early commercial-stage company with minimal revenues. Its first plant is in Ironton, Ohio, designed to produce about 107 million pounds a year when fully operating. It is not yet running at that level. It also runs a facility in Denver, Pennsylvania that prepares the waste plastic. It plans further plants, including in Thailand and Belgium, with partners.

    It holds a global license for the technology from P&G, which sets deadlines for building plants and starting sales in other regions. The company reports on a consolidated basis, as a single business.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Being an early-stage company with little revenue that may never reach steady profits.
    • Needing more money for expansion, and carrying substantial debt with restrictive terms.
    • Delays in getting the Ironton plant to full, certified operation.
    • Relying on one plant for all of its operations.
    • Missing license deadlines with P&G, which could end or change its license.
