    What PriceSmart does

    PriceSmart runs membership warehouse clubs in Central America, the Caribbean and Colombia. Members, who are consumers and small businesses, pay a fee to shop there. It had 58 clubs at the end of its 2026 fiscal year, and plans to enter Chile with its first club there in the spring of 2027. It was founded in 1996 by the creators of Price Club, the original warehouse club operator.

    It makes money from selling merchandise and from membership fees. The 10-K says membership fees equaled about 1.8% of net merchandise sales and 38.6% of operating income in fiscal 2026. Consumables such as groceries and cleaning supplies were about 46% of net merchandise sales, and fresh foods about 32%. The rest was hardlines like electronics and appliances, clothing and home goods, food service and bakery, and health services.

    About half of its merchandise comes from suppliers in Latin America and the Caribbean, and the rest from the United States and elsewhere. Members can also shop through a mobile app and website, with home delivery and curbside pickup. It has over 13,000 employees.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Inflation, higher interest rates and weak economies in the countries where it operates.
    • Tariffs, trade wars and other political and economic changes.
    • A drop in money sent home from abroad to the countries where it operates, which can reduce its sales.
    • Major online retailers entering its markets, or rivals offering better online shopping.
    • Weather events, civil unrest or supply chain problems that interrupt its distribution centers and clubs.
    From PriceSmart's annual report on Form 10-K, filed October 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
