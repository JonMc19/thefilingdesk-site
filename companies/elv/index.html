    What Elevance Health does

    Elevance Health is a health insurer. It says it is one of the largest in the United States by medical membership, serving about 45.2 million medical members at the end of 2025. It sells health plans to individuals, employers, and people covered by Medicaid and Medicare. It also runs pharmacy, behavioral health and other health services, and administers the Federal Employee Program.

    It makes money in several ways: premiums paid for risk-based plans, where it pays members' medical costs; fees from employers that pay their own claims and use it to run the plan; and pharmacy and health service fees through its Carelon businesses. It reports four segments: Health Benefits, CarelonRx (pharmacy services), Carelon Services and Corporate & Other.

    Elevance is an independent licensee of the Blue Cross and Blue Shield Association. It is the Blue Cross licensee in California and the Blue Cross and Blue Shield licensee in 14 other states, mostly under the Anthem name. It also uses the Wellpoint and Carelon brands, and it is licensed to sell insurance in all 50 states, the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Misjudging, pricing for or managing healthcare costs could cut the profit on its plans.
    • Falling enrollment, especially in states where it has many members.
    • Medicare and Medicaid payment rates and rules are set by government and can change.
    • Poor relations with hospitals, doctors and pharmacy providers could hurt its business.
    • Losing its Blue Cross and Blue Shield licenses, which let it use those names in its territories.
    From Elevance Health's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 6, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
