    What Morningstar does

    Morningstar sells investment data, research, ratings and software to investors, financial advisors and institutions. Customers use its desktop and web platforms, data feeds and programming interfaces (APIs), and its artificial intelligence (AI) connections to get this information. It also sells investment management services and a range of indexes that others use as benchmarks.

    It reports five segments: Morningstar Direct Platform, PitchBook (data on private markets), Morningstar Credit (credit ratings and research), Morningstar Wealth and Morningstar Retirement. The last two manage money for individuals and advisors, and the company says they handle about $378.0 billion in assets under management and advisement.

    Morningstar says its independence and the accuracy of its research and ratings matter to its customers, so its brand and reputation are central to the business. Several of its businesses are regulated in many countries.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Harm to its brand, independence or reputation.
    • Liability if its research, ratings or published data are wrong or are misused.
    • Keeping its products fresh and up to date with technology, including AI.
    • Regulation of its ratings, advisory and investment management businesses in many countries.
    • Mistakes in its automated advisory tools that could lead to customer losses.
    From Morningstar's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 13, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
