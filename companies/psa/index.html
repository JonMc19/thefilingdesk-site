    What Public Storage does

    Public Storage is a real estate investment trust (REIT) that owns, builds and runs self-storage facilities, where people and businesses rent space to store belongings. At the end of 2025 it held interests in and consolidated 3,171 facilities under the Public Storage name, with 229 million net rentable square feet of space. It says it is the largest owner of self-storage facilities in the U.S.

    Besides renting out storage space, it has other lines of business: tenant reinsurance (it manages insurance programs through which tenants can buy cover for their stored goods from an outside insurer), management of facilities owned by others, bridge loans to other self-storage owners, and sales of locks and boxes.

    The company also holds a 35% interest in Shurgard, a separate public company that runs self-storage facilities in Western Europe. Public Storage has about 5,770 employees, most of them working at its facilities or in customer care. It faces competition from other storage operators and from other ways of storing things.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Damage to facilities or disruption from natural disasters and other events.
    • Rising operating costs, including property taxes.
    • Competition from other self-storage operators and storage alternatives.
    • Risks from buying and developing properties.
    • Higher interest rates and weaker economic conditions.
    From Public Storage's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
