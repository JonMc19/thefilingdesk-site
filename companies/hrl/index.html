    What Hormel Foods does

    Hormel Foods makes and sells packaged and processed food. It started as a meat processor in 1891 in Austin, Minnesota, and has grown through its own brands and acquisitions into a global branded food company with more than $12 billion in annual revenue. Its brands include Planters, SPAM, Jennie-O, Skippy, Applegate, Wholly and Hormel Black Label.

    It reports three segments. Retail sells through grocery stores, mass merchants, club stores, discount chains and e-commerce providers in the U.S., and includes its MegaMex Foods joint venture. Foodservice sells to distributors and operators such as restaurants, hospitals, schools and convenience stores. International sells through retail and foodservice channels abroad and includes operations in China and Brazil. Its largest customer, Walmart, accounted for 15.6 percent of gross sales less returns and allowances in fiscal 2025.

    Hormel says its markets are highly competitive and that it competes by meeting customer and consumer preferences and offering new products. At the end of fiscal 2025 it had approximately 20,000 active employees, with over 90 percent in the U.S. About 20 percent are covered by collective bargaining agreements.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Reliance on suppliers, distributors and other outside parties to supply key inputs and deliver products.
    • Food safety problems that come with making food for people to eat.
    • Swings in commodity prices and in the supply of raw materials.
    • Shoppers switching to cheaper generic or value products in some categories.
    • Ongoing government inspection of its plants and products, and the effect of losing inspectors.
    From Hormel Foods's annual report on Form 10-K, filed December 5, 2025, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
