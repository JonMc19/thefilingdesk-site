    What Mondelez International does

    Mondelēz makes and sells snacks in over 150 countries. Its main products are biscuits and baked snacks, such as Oreo, Ritz, LU, Clif Bar and Tate's Bake Shop, and chocolate, such as Cadbury Dairy Milk, Milka and Toblerone. It also sells gum and candy, powdered drinks, and cheese and grocery products in some countries. It had net revenues of $38.5 billion in 2025.

    It reports four regional segments: Latin America; Asia, the Middle East and Africa; Europe, the largest by revenue; and North America. Biscuits and baked snacks are its biggest category, followed by chocolate. Sales outside the United States made up 75.8% of net revenues in 2025. It sells to supermarkets, wholesalers, club and convenience stores, distributors and other food stores, and online, and no single customer made up 10% or more of revenue. Mondelēz says it competes with global, regional and local food and snack companies, new start-up brands, and retailers' own store brands.

    Mondelēz had about 91,000 employees at the end of 2025, about 12,000 of them in the United States. It runs 145 main factories and processing plants in 49 countries. It buys large amounts of cocoa, dairy, wheat, oils, sugar and nuts.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Swings in the price and supply of cocoa and other ingredients, packaging and energy.
    • Tariffs, trade disputes and other risks of selling in many countries, including the war in Ukraine.
    • Strong competition, including from retailers' cheaper store brands as retail chains consolidate.
    • Failing to predict and meet changing tastes with new and improved products.
    • Changes in currency rates, since most of its sales are outside the United States.
    From Mondelez International's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 4, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
