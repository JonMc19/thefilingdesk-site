    What ResMed | does

    ResMed makes cloud-connected medical devices and software for sleep and breathing conditions. It first sold CPAP therapy for obstructive sleep apnea, a condition where the airway collapses during sleep. CPAP machines blow pressurized air, usually through a mask. It now also sells masks, headgear, ventilators for life support, diagnostic products and dental devices, plus apps that let clinicians follow patients' therapy.

    It has two kinds of business: Sleep and Breathing Health products, and Residential Care Software. Its software helps home medical equipment suppliers, senior living and home care providers run their work, including billing and care management. It sells in more than 140 countries through its own subsidiaries and independent distributors, and has about 11,370 employees.

    In June 2026 it bought Noctrix Health, which makes a device for restless legs syndrome. It also agreed to sell its MatrixCare software business for $490 million in cash, to focus on sleep, breathing and home-based care. In 2025 it bought VirtuOx, which provides in-home diagnostic testing.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • New devices, drugs or other treatments, such as GLP-1 weight-loss drugs, that could make its products less needed.
    • Competition from rivals, including Philips as it returns to the U.S., and from new software and AI entrants.
    • Healthcare consolidation and payment reform that put pressure on its prices.
    • Third-party service providers that fail to protect patient and customer data.
    • Complying with healthcare, privacy and device laws in the many countries where it sells.
    From ResMed |'s annual report on Form 10-K, filed August 13, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
