    What Warner Music Group does

    Warner Music Group finds, signs and promotes musicians and songwriters and earns money from their music. Its record labels include Atlantic Records, Warner Records, Elektra Records and Parlophone Records, with artists such as Ed Sheeran, Bruno Mars, Cardi B and Dua Lipa. Its music publishing arm, Warner Chappell Music, owns or manages the rights to more than two million songs by over 190,000 songwriters and composers.

    It has two businesses. Recorded Music, which earns money mainly when people stream, buy or license its recordings, brought in $5.408 billion, or 81% of revenue, in the fiscal year that ended in September 2025. Music Publishing, which collects fees when songs are played, recorded or used in films, ads and games, brought in $1.306 billion, or 19%. It depends heavily on a small number of streaming services. Citing industry data, it says it, Universal Music Group and Sony Music Entertainment are the three largest record companies, with many smaller and independent labels competing too.

    Access Industries, which bought the company in 2011, still controls it, holding about 98% of the voting power. Warner Music had about 5,500 employees in September 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to find, sign and keep successful artists and songwriters, and depending on a few big stars.
    • Slower growth in music streaming, which drives much of its revenue.
    • Its dependence on a few large streaming services, which have strong bargaining power over it.
    • Its heavy debt, which limits its flexibility and must be repaid or refinanced.
    • Access Industries controls the company, and its interests may differ from those of other shareholders.
    From Warner Music Group's annual report on Form 10-K, filed November 20, 2025, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
