    What Stellantis does

    Stellantis is a car maker that designs, builds and sells vehicles around the world. Its brands include Jeep, Ram, Dodge and Chrysler in North America, Peugeot, Citroën, FIAT and Opel in Europe, and Alfa Romeo, DS, Lancia and Maserati at the premium and luxury end. It also sells service parts and accessories, offers dealer and customer financing and leasing, and runs used-car and mobility businesses.

    It reports six segments: North America, Enlarged Europe, Middle East & Africa, South America, China and India & Asia Pacific, and Maserati. Maserati will no longer be a separate segment from 2026. Stellantis also sells vehicles from the Chinese maker Leapmotor outside China through a company it controls. In 2025 it reported €153.5 billion in net revenues and a net loss of €22.3 billion.

    At the end of 2025 it had 258,668 employees, not counting joint arrangements, associates and unconsolidated subsidiaries.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Falling vehicle shipments, which hit profit hard because most of its costs are fixed.
    • Its North American profit depends on demand for pickup trucks and larger sport utility vehicles.
    • Misjudging demand for electric and hybrid vehicles compared with gasoline ones.
    • Tariffs, trade barriers and other economic and political shifts in its markets.
    • Emissions investigations, claims and rules in several countries.
    From Stellantis's annual report on Form 20-F, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 20-F has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Income statement

    From fiscal 2016 to 2024, earnings per share grew 5.7% a year and net income 14.9% a year, as new shares raised the share count by 95% and diluted each share's earnings. Stellantis then lost money in fiscal 2025.
