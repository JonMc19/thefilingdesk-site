    What NuScale Power does

    NuScale designs small modular nuclear reactors, which are factory-built reactor units that can be added one at a time to a power plant. Its core product is the NuScale Power Module, which can generate 77 megawatts of electricity. A plant can start with one module and grow to as many as twelve. The company has not yet delivered modules to customers.

    NuScale says its design has been approved by the US Nuclear Regulatory Commission, and its customers can refer to that approval to speed up their own licensing. Its customers are expected to be power plant owners and developers, who must still get their own approvals to build. The company expects to earn revenue from selling modules and from services that start about five years before a plant opens and continue through its life.

    The 10-K says that over $1.8 billion has been invested in developing the technology since 2007. It names ENTRA1 as its exclusive global partner for building plants that use its modules. It says it competes with other nuclear reactor designs, including companies in China and Russia that already run small reactors.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It has not yet delivered a reactor module to a customer, and early setbacks could hurt the business.
    • It has lost money since it started and may not become profitable.
    • Its plans depend heavily on its partners ENTRA1 and Fluor, who may not share its interests.
    • The market for small modular reactors is new and may grow slowly.
    • Its customers must win separate approvals before building plants, and its design is approved only in the US.
    From NuScale Power's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
