    What Adaptive Biotechnologies does

    Adaptive Biotechnologies reads the genetic code of a patient's immune system to find out how the body detects and fights disease. It uses gene-sequencing technology, a growing database and machine learning to build diagnostic tests and drug-discovery tools.

    It has two main businesses. The first, minimal residual disease (MRD) testing, uses its clonoSEQ test to find tiny amounts of cancer left in patients with blood cancers. clonoSEQ is sold to doctors as a clinical test and to drug companies as an assay for their clinical trials (called MRD Pharma). Adaptive says it was the first test authorized by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration to detect and monitor MRD in multiple myeloma, B cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia and chronic lymphocytic leukemia.

    The second, Immune Medicine, uses its ability to sequence and map immune cell receptors at scale to support new diagnostic and drug products. Adaptive reorganized the company around these two businesses in 2024.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It has lost money since it began and may not reach or keep profitability.
    • Insurers and government programs may not cover or pay enough for its clinical tests.
    • It may not be able to expand clonoSEQ to new uses or turn its immune platform into new products.
    • It relies on a limited number of suppliers, often a single one, for lab equipment and materials.
    • Its research spending on new products and services may not pay off.
    From Adaptive Biotechnologies's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
