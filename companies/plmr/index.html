    What Palomar does

    Palomar is a specialty insurance company that sells property and casualty insurance to individuals and businesses. It reports five product categories: earthquake, casualty, inland marine and other property, crop, and fronting. It writes policies in both the admitted market, where state regulators approve rates and policy forms, and the excess and surplus lines market, which is less regulated and lets it react faster. It uses its own data analytics to price risks.

    It makes money by collecting insurance premiums, and it buys reinsurance, which is insurance for insurers, to limit losses from catastrophes. It sells through retail agents, program administrators, wholesale brokers and partnerships with other insurers. Its gross written premiums, the total premiums on policies it writes, were $2.0 billion in 2025. Its business is concentrated in California.

    The company was founded in 2014. In January 2026 it bought The Gray Casualty & Surety Company, a surety carrier that issues contract bonds for mid-sized and emerging contractors, and took on unsecured credit facilities totaling $450 million to help pay for it. It will report surety and credit premium as a separate line from 2026.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Severe catastrophes, such as earthquakes and events linked to climate change, that could wipe out earnings.
    • Reinsurers that may not pay claims on time, or at all.
    • Losses that end up larger than the reserves it has set aside.
    • Models and risk controls that fail to limit its losses, and a drop in its financial strength rating.
    • Business concentrated in California, with its pricing and regulatory limits.
