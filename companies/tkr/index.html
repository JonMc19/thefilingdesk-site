    What Timken does

    Timken makes engineered bearings and industrial motion products, and provides related services. A bearing is a mechanical part that reduces friction between moving parts so a machine shaft can turn freely. Timken started in 1899 and is best known for tapered roller bearings. Its brands include Timken, GGB, Philadelphia Gear, Cone Drive, Rollon and Nadella.

    The company sells to a wide range of industries, including industrial distribution, renewable energy, automation, cars and trucks, agriculture, rail, aerospace, construction, and metals and mining. No single customer accounts for more than 5% of its net sales. Its product lines include tapered, spherical and cylindrical roller bearings, plain bearings, thrust and ball bearings, housed bearings and other industrial motion products. It competes with other bearing and industrial motion makers, and it says this brings pricing pressure.

    At the end of 2025 Timken had about 19,000 employees in 44 countries. It runs 116 manufacturing facilities and service centers, 29 technology and engineering centers, and 74 distribution centers and warehouses. It has grown partly through acquisitions.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Strong competition in bearings and industrial motion, with pricing pressure.
    • Downturns in the industries it serves, in a business that needs a lot of capital.
    • Changes in raw material prices, availability or logistics costs.
    • Acquisitions that may not deliver the results it expects or may be hard to integrate.
    • Tariffs and trade barriers, and currency swings, across its global operations.
    From Timken's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 13, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
