    What Capri Holdings does

    Capri Holdings owns two luxury fashion brands, Michael Kors and Jimmy Choo. Michael Kors sells handbags and other accessories, footwear and apparel, with collections for women and a growing men's line. Jimmy Choo is built around women's luxury shoes, with handbags, small leather goods and other accessories, and men's footwear.

    The company makes money by selling through its own retail stores and websites, through department and specialty stores that buy its goods for resale (wholesale), and through licensing partners who make some categories such as fragrance and eyewear. It has two segments. Michael Kors made up about 83% of revenue in fiscal 2026 and Jimmy Choo about 17%.

    Capri runs 673 Michael Kors stores and 211 Jimmy Choo stores, counting concessions (shops inside other retailers). It says the global luxury goods industry has lately shrunk for two years in a row. It uses mostly outside contract manufacturers in other countries to make its products.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to keep up with fast-changing fashion and retail trends.
    • Competition from other fashion and luxury brands in North America and abroad.
    • New or higher duties and tariffs on the products it imports.
    • Fewer sales through department and other wholesale customers as they consolidate, restructure or close.
    • Impairment charges if the brands it bought are judged to be worth less than their book value.
    From Capri Holdings's annual report on Form 10-K, filed May 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
