    What Honeywell Technologies does

    Honeywell makes equipment, software and services for aircraft, factories, buildings and energy plants. It sells them around the world to aircraft makers, airlines, the U.S. government, plant operators, building owners, and refining and chemical companies. Its products range from aircraft engines, avionics (flight electronics) and wheels and brakes to factory control systems, sensors, fire and security systems, and licensed technology and catalysts for refineries.

    In 2025 Honeywell's sales grew 8% to $37.4 billion. It reported four segments. Aerospace Technologies had revenue of $17,510 million, Industrial Automation $9,401 million, Building Automation $7,367 million, and Energy and Sustainability Solutions $3,134 million. The competitors it names include RTX, Safran and Thales in aerospace; Johnson Controls, Schneider Electric and Siemens in buildings; and Emerson Electric and Rockwell Automation in industrial automation.

    At the end of 2025 Honeywell employed about 101,000 people in 79 countries, about 36,000 of them in the United States. In October 2025 it spun off its Advanced Materials business as a separate public company, Solstice Advanced Materials, and in June 2025 it spent $2.2 billion to buy Sundyne. It plans to split into two public companies, Honeywell and Honeywell Aerospace, and expects to finish the separation in the third quarter of 2026.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Downturns in the industries it sells to, such as aerospace, building construction, and refining and chemical plants.
    • Its planned separation from Honeywell Aerospace may not be completed as planned or bring the benefits it expects.
    • Relying on single or sole suppliers for many parts, especially in aerospace, and swings in raw material prices.
    • Its large business outside the U.S., which exposes it to tariffs, trade limits, currency swings and political change.
    • Environmental cleanup costs and injury claims tied to its past operations and those of companies it took over.
    From Honeywell Technologies's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 17, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
