    What Cisco does

    Cisco makes the equipment and software that connect computers and networks, including routers, switches, wireless gear and optical parts for data centers, offices and factories. It also sells cybersecurity products, video meeting and calling tools, and software that monitors how networks and applications are working. Its customers are businesses of all sizes, governments, public institutions and telecom and cloud providers. It also provides support and consulting services.

    Total revenue was $63.3 billion in the fiscal year that ended in July 2026. Product sales grew, led by networking, while service revenue stayed flat. Software, sold across all of its product areas and services, brought in $23.2 billion. Large cloud companies buying its equipment to run AI made up about 6% of revenue. Cisco manages its business through three regional segments: the Americas; Europe, the Middle East and Africa; and Asia Pacific, Japan and China. It sells directly and through distributors and other partners. Rivals include Arista Networks, Broadcom, Hewlett Packard Enterprise, Huawei, Nvidia, Palo Alto Networks, Fortinet, Microsoft and Zoom.

    Cisco was incorporated in California in 1984 and is based in San Jose. It had about 82,400 employees at the end of July 2026.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Weak economies and political uncertainty that lead customers to delay buying network equipment.
    • Sales to telecom and cloud companies, including AI equipment for the largest ones, can swing sharply.
    • Intense competition, including in new markets it enters.
    • Failing to keep up with new technology or to predict what customers will need.
    • Disruption or changes in the channel partners and distributors that sell a large share of its products.
    From Cisco's annual report on Form 10-K, filed September 2, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
