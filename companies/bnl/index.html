    What Broadstone Net Lease does

    Broadstone Net Lease is a real estate investment trust (REIT), a company that owns property and pays out most of its income. It owns commercial buildings, mostly with a single tenant each, and leases them on a long-term, "net" basis. Under a net lease the tenant runs the property and covers most of its costs, while the tenant keeps control of a location that matters to its business.

    At the end of 2025 its portfolio held 771 properties, with about 41.6 million rentable square feet. Nearly all are in the United States and a few are in Canada. It earns rent from tenants in many industries and spreads its holdings across property types, regions and tenants. Most leases include built-in rent increases.

    It aims to grow in three ways: raising rents under existing leases, building properties for tenants, and buying more properties. It competes for acquisitions with other publicly traded REITs and with private investors.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Tenants may default or leave, and each property depends on a single tenant.
    • Rents rise only by the amounts written into its long leases, which limits growth.
    • Growth depends on finding and paying for more property purchases.
    • Higher interest rates could raise its borrowing costs.
    • Competition from larger REITs and private investors when buying properties.
    From Broadstone Net Lease's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 19, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
