    What Whirlpool does

    Whirlpool makes and sells home appliances: laundry machines, refrigerators and freezers, cooking appliances and dishwashers. It also sells small appliances such as the KitchenAid stand mixer, commercial laundry equipment, and InSinkErator food waste disposers and instant hot water dispensers. Its two biggest global brands are Whirlpool and KitchenAid, and it also sells under regional brands such as Maytag and Brastemp. It was founded in 1911, makes products in four countries and markets them in nearly every country. In 2025 it had approximately $16 billion in annual net sales and 41,000 employees.

    It reports three operating segments: Major Domestic Appliances (MDA) North America, MDA Latin America, and Small Domestic Appliances (SDA) Global. It sells through retailers, buying groups and builders, and a loss of sales to any key trade customer could hurt it. It competes in an intensely competitive appliance industry.

    The company has been reshaping its business. In 2024 it put its European major appliance business into a new company with Arcelik and sold its Middle East and North Africa business to Arcelik. In November 2025 it cut its stake in Whirlpool of India to approximately 40%, so India is no longer reported as a segment. It says it plans to keep looking at ways to reduce its debt in 2026.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Intense competition in the home appliance industry.
    • Losing sales to key retail customers, buying groups or builders.
    • Rising or volatile costs of raw materials and parts, and supplier delays that disrupt its factories.
    • Product liability claims and recalls.
    • Lower credit ratings, which it says were cut below investment grade in 2025, and its access to financing.
    From Whirlpool's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 11, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
