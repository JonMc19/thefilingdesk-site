    What IMAX does

    IMAX is a Canadian company that makes large-screen movie theater systems: projectors, sound systems, screens and auditorium designs. It sells or leases them to cinema operators, mostly multiplex chains, and to museums and science centers. IMAX does not run the theaters itself, apart from one. At the end of 2025 there were 1,864 IMAX systems in 91 countries and territories, and 76% of those in commercial multiplexes were outside the U.S. and Canada.

    IMAX reports two segments. Content Solutions works for studios and creators: IMAX converts films into its format and shows them across its network, typically earning about 12.5% of the box office. It also distributes documentaries, concert films and live events. Technology Products and Services sells, leases and maintains the systems for theater operators. Under some deals, called joint revenue sharing arrangements, IMAX takes a share of a theater's ticket sales instead of an upfront fee. Films in IMAX theaters took in $1.28 billion at the global box office in 2025. IMAX says it competes with theater chains' own large-format screens, with streaming and TV, and with other ways people spend their free time.

    IMAX employed 679 people at the end of 2025. It indirectly owns 71.57% of IMAX China, which trades on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its results depend on how many films and events are released in IMAX format and how well they do at the box office.
    • Its large and growing presence in China, which accounts for much of its network and backlog of planned systems.
    • Rival large-format screens and new technologies from theater chains and others.
    • Planned theater installations may not happen, so its backlog may not turn into revenue and cash.
    • Tariffs, trade barriers and the risks of doing business in many countries.
