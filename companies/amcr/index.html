    What Amcor does

    Amcor makes packaging for food, drinks, health, beauty and wellness products. Its materials include polymer resins, aluminum, fiber and paper, and it makes flexible packaging, rigid containers, closures, and dispensing and pharma delivery devices. It sells to thousands of customers, mostly through its own sales force, and competes with companies such as Crown Holdings, Ball, Sonoco and Silgan. No single customer was more than 10% of its net sales in the last three fiscal years.

    It reports two segments. Global Flexible Packaging Solutions accounted for approximately 55% of consolidated net sales in fiscal 2026. Global Rigid Packaging Solutions accounted for approximately 45%. Both sell across Europe, North America, Latin America and Asia Pacific.

    At June 30, 2026, Amcor employed about 75,000 people worldwide. It combined with Berry in a merger and says it is still working to integrate and transform the business after it.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Price swings or shortages of raw materials, energy and other inputs.
    • Losing key customers, or a drop in their orders, including through customer consolidation.
    • Significant competition in the industries and regions where it operates.
    • Shifts in consumer preferences that are not offset by growth in new or substitute products.
    • Integrating the business after the Berry merger.
    From Amcor's annual report on Form 10-K, filed August 14, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
