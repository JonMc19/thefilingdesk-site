    What Everpure does

    Everpure, formerly named Pure Storage, sells data storage systems and the software that manages them. It began by selling storage built on flash memory, which is solid-state and has no moving parts, and it now offers what it calls the Everpure Platform. The platform pulls a customer's data in company data centers, public clouds and remote sites into a single storage layer with one set of controls.

    Its customers are businesses, governments and very large cloud operators, known as hyperscalers. It says shipments began in fiscal 2026 under a design win, meaning it was chosen to supply flash storage for a major hyperscaler, and that more shipments are expected in fiscal 2027.

    It sells hardware together with its Purity software and DirectFlash technology, and is moving customers toward subscriptions, in which they pay for storage as a service instead of buying it outright. It sells through partners and uses contract manufacturers to build its products. It says it competes with established companies in data storage.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its reliance on a limited number of suppliers, some of them single-source, for parts.
    • Efforts on flash storage for hyperscalers that may not lead to meaningful revenue or more design wins.
    • Intense competition from established companies with long-standing customer relationships.
    • Trouble carrying out its move to subscription offerings.
    • Long, unpredictable sales cycles that make future sales hard to predict.
    From Everpure's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 25, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
