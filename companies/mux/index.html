    What McEwen Mining does

    McEwen is a gold and silver mining and exploration company, with a copper development project, working in the Americas. It sells doré bars, which are unrefined gold and silver bars that outside refiners turn into bullion, and ore concentrate, which goes to outside smelters.

    It owns the Froome mine and Stock mill in Ontario, Canada, the Gold Bar Mine Complex in Nevada, and El Gallo in Sinaloa, Mexico. It also holds 49% of the San José mine in Argentina, and an interest in McEwen Copper, which owns the Los Azules copper project in Argentina. Its reporting segments are Canada, United States, Mexico, MSC (the San José mine) and McEwen Copper.

    The company is building an underground mine at its Stock Property in Ontario, and plans to build a mill at El Gallo to reprocess old heap leach material. It also holds exploration properties in the United States, Canada, Mexico and Argentina.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Results that depend on gold, silver and copper prices, which can be volatile.
    • Losses in earlier years, with no assurance of steady profits.
    • Debt, including convertible debt, whose payments and covenants limit its flexibility.
    • Higher operating and capital costs, and project estimates that may prove too low.
    • Political, social and permitting risks in Argentina, Mexico and Canada.
    From McEwen Mining's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 17, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Piotroski F-score: how many of nine simple tests of profitability, funding and efficiency the company passed in the year (Piotroski, 2000); 8 or 9 is strong, 0 to 2 weak. Rule of 40: revenue growth plus free cash flow margin; software investors treat 40% or more as the bar. Accruals ratio: net income minus cash from operations, over average total assets; lower means profit is backed by cash.
