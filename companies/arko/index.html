    What ARKO does

    ARKO runs convenience stores and sells motor fuel wholesale in the United States, and says it is among the largest in both. As of December 31, 2025, it operated 1,118 convenience stores under more than 25 regional brands, in the District of Columbia and more than 30 states. It also supplied fuel to 2,099 dealer locations and ran 295 cardlock locations (unstaffed fueling sites) for its fleet fueling business, which also issues fuel cards.

    It reports four segments: retail, wholesale, fleet fueling and GPMP. Retail sells fuel and merchandise and brings in much of its revenue and most of its profit. Wholesale supplies fuel to dealers and other customers.

    It grew mostly by buying other chains, with 26 deals from 2013 through 2025. Since the second half of 2024 it has been converting many of its stores into dealer locations under a multi-year plan. In February 2026 it listed a subsidiary, ARKO Petroleum, which now runs the wholesale, fleet fueling and GPMP segments.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Weaker economic conditions, trade policy or consumer confidence in the U.S.
    • Acquisitions that cost too much, fail to be integrated or fall short of expectations.
    • Wholesale motor fuel price changes, and falling demand for fuel-based transportation.
    • Lower cigarette and tobacco use, and regulation and lawsuits about tobacco.
    • Competition in a fragmented industry, and its ability to remodel stores and build new ones.
    From ARKO's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 25, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
