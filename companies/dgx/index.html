    What Quest Diagnostics does

    Quest Diagnostics runs medical laboratories that test samples from patients, such as blood, and report the results to doctors, hospitals, health plans, employers and patients. It also sells insights drawn from its large database of de-identified lab results. It says that each year its patients make up about one-third of US adults, and that it serves about half of the physicians and half of the hospitals in the country.

    The company earns money by being paid for testing, mostly by health plans, government programs such as Medicare and Medicaid, and doctors and hospitals that send it work. In 2025 it reported net revenues of $11.0 billion. Patients can reach it through patient service centers, doctors' offices, mobile blood draws and QuestHealth.com, where people can order tests themselves with physician oversight.

    Quest is based in Secaucus, New Jersey, and has laboratories, patient service centers and offices across the United States and in a few places abroad. It says it competes in a highly competitive clinical testing business.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Cuts in what Medicare, Medicaid and health plans pay for lab tests.
    • Strong competition in clinical testing and pressure to price services low.
    • Failing to adapt as the US healthcare system and the lab testing market change.
    • Penalties or business limits from billing errors or from breaking healthcare laws.
    • Debt that may limit its financial and operating flexibility.
    From Quest Diagnostics's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
