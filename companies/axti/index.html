    What AXT does

    AXT makes wafers, called substrates, that serve as the base layer for chips that cannot use ordinary silicon. It says it does not design or make the chips themselves. It has two product lines: specialty material substrates and the raw materials used to make them.

    Its substrates are made from indium phosphide, gallium arsenide and germanium. Indium phosphide goes into fiber optic and 5G equipment and data center connections, where the company says AI is raising the need for fast data transfer. Gallium arsenide goes into phone power amplifiers, satellite communications, LEDs and lasers. Germanium goes into solar cells for space and ground use.

    Some of AXT's consolidated subsidiaries make raw materials, such as boron nitride crucibles used to grow crystals and purified gallium. It uses these in its own production and also sells them to other companies. Its customers are chip makers, and it names loss of its top-tier substrate customers as a significant risk.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Trade tariffs and export limits, such as China's requirement of export permits for indium phosphide.
    • Losing one or more of its tier one substrate customers.
    • Silicon wafers, which cost much less, replacing specialty materials in some uses.
    • Falling selling prices and a gross margin that has varied and may decline.
    • Strong competition from rivals that may develop better products.
    From AXT's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 17, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
