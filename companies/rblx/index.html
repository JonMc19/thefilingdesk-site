    Valuation

    Two ways to value Roblox yourself, both starting from its filings: a discounted cash flow model and the price-to-earnings method.

    1Discounted cash flow2P/E method

    1Discounted cash flow (DCF)

    A discounted cash flow (DCF) model values a business as the cash it may generate in future, discounted back to today. The starting figures come from Roblox's 10-K (or, for net cash and shares, its latest 10-Q) and the assumptions are yours: start from one of three example scenarios, then change any figure to see what it implies for a share.

    Value per share on three example scenarios, from Roblox's cash from operations minus depreciation in fiscal 2025 (free cash flow before spending to grow: capital spending was 2.0 times depreciation, which holds its free cash flow down) of $1.6B, net cash of $1.9B at December 31, 2025 and 690 million diluted shares, from its filings. The scenarios are fitted to Roblox: the base scenario's growth in years 1 to 5 is its revenue growth over the last 3 years, 30.0% a year, kept between 0% and 20%; the cautious and optimistic ones are lower and higher, and growth in years 6 to 10 is halfway to the long-run rate. The base scenario's discount rate is 10%: 9% for a business of moderate risk (Interactive Home Entertainment), plus 1 point for unsteady results (a loss in fiscal 2025); the cautious and optimistic ones use a point more and a point less. Select one to load it into the calculator. These are the model's arithmetic on those assumptions, not a forecast or a price target.

    How the model works. Free cash flow grows at the first rate for five years and the second rate for five more, and each year is discounted at the discount rate. Everything after year 10 is valued as year-10 cash flow × (1 + growth after year 10) ÷ (discount rate − growth after year 10), discounted back ten years. Net cash is added and the total is divided by diluted shares. The result depends entirely on the assumptions. It is not a forecast, a price target or a recommendation.

    2Price-to-earnings (P/E) method

    Another common way to value a company is to multiply its earnings per share by a price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple: the number of years of today's earnings an investor pays for a share. A higher multiple usually means investors expect earnings to grow. Choose a multiple, for example from similar companies, to see the value it gives.

    The P/E method needs positive earnings, and Roblox's latest diluted earnings per share were negative or zero.
