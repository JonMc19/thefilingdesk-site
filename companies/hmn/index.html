    What Horace Mann Educators does

    Horace Mann is an insurance holding company that sells insurance and retirement products mainly to teachers and other school employees in the United States. It began in 1945 in Springfield, Illinois, when two teachers set up an auto insurance plan for their association members. It sells auto and home insurance, life insurance, retirement plans such as 403(b) accounts, and supplemental health coverage.

    It reports three segments: Property & Casualty, Life & Retirement, and Supplemental & Group Benefits. It also offers group benefits, such as disability and life coverage, that school districts and other employers provide to staff. It earns money from the premiums customers pay, and from the investments it holds to pay future claims and benefits.

    Horace Mann says the U.S. educator market is about 14 million households. It sells through exclusive agents, broker partners who work with school districts, a call center and digital tools. It says it has local agent representation in over 60% of the territories where it operates. It competes with other insurers, mutual fund companies, independent agencies and financial planners.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Weak financial markets or changing interest rates that cut its investment income.
    • Loss reserves for property and casualty claims that may prove too small.
    • Keeping access to educators, which depends on its agents and on school and association relationships.
    • Group benefits sales that depend heavily on one distribution partner.
    • Strong competition in auto, home, retirement and supplemental insurance.
