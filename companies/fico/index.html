    What Fair Isaac does

    Fair Isaac, known as FICO, sells analytics software and the FICO Score, the measure of consumer credit risk used in most U.S. credit decisions. It serves businesses in more than 80 countries. Its customers include banks, credit card issuers, insurers, retailers, phone companies, auto lenders and government agencies. It also sells scores to consumers online through myFICO.com.

    It has two segments. The Scores segment sells credit and other scores to businesses and to consumers. Lenders and others usually pay the three U.S. credit reporting agencies, Experian, TransUnion and Equifax, a fee for each score. Those agencies then pay FICO a fee, and FICO does not usually sell scores straight to lenders. The Software segment sells ready-made analytic and decision tools for tasks such as opening accounts, fraud detection and marketing, along with professional services, and its FICO Platform. Customers can get software as a cloud service or install it themselves.

    The company was founded in 1956. The FICO Score runs from 300 to 850, and the company also offers industry-specific scores and scores for people with little credit history.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Whether its Software segment strategy works.
    • Reliance on a small number of products and services for a large part of revenue.
    • Dependence on the banking and consumer credit industries.
    • The risk that Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac stop or cut back using the FICO Score.
    • Reliance on credit reporting agencies and other third parties to distribute its scores.
    From Fair Isaac's annual report on Form 10-K, filed November 7, 2025, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
