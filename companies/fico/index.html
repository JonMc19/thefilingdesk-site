    What Fair Isaac does

    Fair Isaac, known as FICO, sells analytics software and credit scores. Its best-known product is the FICO Score, a three-digit number from 300 to 850 that lenders use to judge how likely a person is to repay a loan. Its customers are banks, credit card issuers, insurers, retailers and phone companies in more than 70 countries, and it also sells scores directly to consumers.

    FICO has two segments. Scores sells credit scores to businesses and to consumers. Software sells decision-making tools for tasks such as opening accounts, spotting fraud and marketing, built around a product called FICO Platform, as software services or installed software. Most scores reach lenders through the three U.S. credit bureaus, Experian, TransUnion and Equifax, which pay FICO a fee each time a score is generated. In fiscal 2026 the company says 92% of its revenue came from the banking industry and 20% from outside the U.S.

    FICO says it does not collect or store the consumer credit data behind its scores, and in most cases does not sell scores directly to lenders.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Competition, including lenders and mortgage buyers Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac using other credit score models.
    • Depending on a small number of products, mainly credit scores, for much of its revenue.
    • Its Software segment strategy may not succeed.
    • Demand falling if its banking and consumer credit clients face uncertainty.
    • Relying on third parties, such as the credit bureaus, to distribute and support its products.
    From Fair Isaac's annual report on Form 10-K, filed November 6, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
