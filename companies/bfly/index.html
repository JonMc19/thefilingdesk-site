    What Butterfly Network does

    Butterfly Network makes handheld ultrasound devices that fit in a pocket and connect to a phone or tablet app. Its devices are built on a semiconductor chip rather than the traditional ultrasound probe. Doctors, nurses and other healthcare workers use them for point-of-care ultrasound, which means scanning at the patient's side.

    It sells the devices and also earns revenue from software subscriptions, cloud-based software for healthcare systems, and education and professional services. It markets to healthcare systems, physicians and other providers, through its own sales force, independent distributors and an online channel, and sells in over 30 countries. Butterfly says it competes with established makers of portable ultrasound, which it views as extensions of cart-based hospital ultrasound.

    Butterfly has only a limited history of selling products and has lost money since it began. It buys a key chip component from a single supplier, TSMC, and uses a single contract manufacturer, Benchmark, to assemble its devices.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It has a short history of sales and has lost money since it began.
    • Market acceptance of its products and services may be slower than it needs.
    • Rapid technology change in medical devices could make its products obsolete.
    • It depends on a single chip supplier, TSMC, and a single manufacturer, Benchmark.
    • Conflict near Taiwan could disrupt its chip supply.
