    What Allison Transmission does

    Allison Transmission makes automatic transmissions, the gearboxes that shift gears without the driver, for commercial and military vehicles. Its products go into delivery and distribution trucks, refuse trucks, construction and fire vehicles, buses, motorhomes, off-highway equipment used in energy, mining, construction and farming, and tactical defense vehicles. Allison says it is the world's largest maker of fully automatic transmissions for medium- and heavy-duty commercial vehicles and medium and heavy tactical US defense vehicles.

    Allison sells to vehicle makers and through an independent network of about 1,500 distributor and dealer locations worldwide. In 2025 about 76% of its revenues came from North America. It is based in Indianapolis, Indiana, and was founded in 1915.

    On January 1, 2026 it completed the purchase of an off-highway drivetrain business, which broadens the products it sells to off-highway markets and gives it a bigger customer base. This description covers 2025 and does not include that business.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A recession, tariffs or trade barriers that hurt demand and its results.
    • Higher costs, supply disruption or shortages of the raw materials and parts it uses.
    • Sales concentrated among its top five vehicle-maker customers, so losing one could reduce sales.
    • Highly cyclical end markets, such as trucks and construction, that cause demand to swing.
    • Relying on independent distributors and dealers to protect its brand and service.
