    What Fortinet does

    Fortinet sells cybersecurity and networking products. Its main platform, the Fortinet Security Fabric, covers secure networking such as firewalls, switches and wireless access points, cloud-delivered secure access, and security tools that use artificial intelligence to watch for threats. The company has more than 50 products. Its end-customers are in over 100 countries and include businesses in finance, retail and healthcare, service providers and government agencies.

    Fortinet earns money by selling security hardware and software, and customers often also buy its FortiGuard security subscription services and FortiCare technical support. It sells mainly through a two-tier model: it sells to distributors such as Arrow Electronics, Ingram Micro and TD Synnex, who sell to resellers and service providers, who in turn sell to end-customers. It sells directly to some large service providers and users. Many of its appliances run on chips it designs itself.

    The company is based in Sunnyvale, California, and does its research mostly in the United States and Canada. It says it held 1,064 U.S. patents at the end of 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Results that swing from quarter to quarter, including from shipments bunched at quarter-end.
    • Intense competition in the cybersecurity and networking markets.
    • Most of its revenue comes from outside the United States, which brings trade and currency risks.
    • Dependence on its senior management and on hiring skilled staff.
    • Debt it has taken on, and the cost of building or buying data centers and offices.
    From Fortinet's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 25, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
