    What Waste Management does

    Waste Management, called WM, collects, moves, treats and disposes of waste in the United States and Canada. Its local operating units provide collection, transfer, disposal, recycling and resource recovery services. WM is a holding company, and its operations are run by subsidiaries.

    It reports several lines of business. Its solid waste business is the core. A Renewable Energy segment turns landfill gas into electricity and renewable natural gas, some of which fuels WM's own natural gas trucks. A Recycling Processing and Sales segment handles recycled materials. After buying Stericycle in 2024, WM also has a Healthcare Solutions segment, which handles regulated medical waste, compliance services and secure information destruction in the United States, Canada and Western Europe. WM says the environmental services industry is highly competitive.

    WM is based in Houston, Texas. Its results depend on volumes, fuel prices and the prices of recycled materials.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Not gaining the benefits it expects from the Stericycle acquisition.
    • Strict environmental rules, and difficulty getting or keeping permits for its landfills.
    • Operating and safety risks, including injuries to employees and others.
    • Swings in recycled commodity prices and in demand for its services.
    • Rising labor costs from union organizing or pension plan withdrawals.
    From Waste Management's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 9, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
