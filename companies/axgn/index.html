    What Axogen does

    Axogen makes products to repair damaged peripheral nerves, the nerves that carry movement and feeling signals through the body. Surgeons use them after injuries or operations that cut or damage a nerve. Its Avance nerve graft is a processed human nerve that bridges a gap between severed nerve ends. In December 2025 the FDA approved a newer version, Avance, which Axogen expects to launch in the second quarter of 2026.

    It also sells Axoguard products made from pig tissue: a connector, protectors and a cap, which join, wrap or cap nerves during repair. Axogen sells to surgeons and healthcare providers, and depends on payments from public and private insurers. Avance products make up about 60% of its revenue.

    Axogen says its business is focused specifically on the science, development and sale of technologies for peripheral nerve regeneration and repair.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A large share of revenue comes from Avance products, so an adverse FDA decision would hurt it.
    • Its revenue depends on a small number of products.
    • Its revenue depends on prompt and adequate reimbursement from insurers and health systems.
    • Problems at its processing facility could limit supply.
    • It has a history of net losses and has not consistently produced positive cash flow.
