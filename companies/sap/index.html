    What SAP SE does

    SAP, founded in 1972 and based in Walldorf, Germany, sells business software. Its programs link a company's main operations, such as finance, procurement, human resources, supply chain and customer service, in one set of applications. It is moving customers from older software they run themselves to a suite delivered in the cloud.

    It earns money from cloud revenue, software licenses and software support. It counts cloud revenue, cloud and software revenue, current cloud backlog (the cloud revenue it has contracts for over the next 12 months) and total revenue as the measures of its growth. A newer measure, subscription revenue, adds cloud revenue and time-based on-premise licenses with their support. Partners sell, install and extend its products, including on SAP BTP, its business technology platform.

    The SAP Group has a global presence and employed more than 110,000 people as at December 31, 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Cybersecurity attacks or breaches affecting its systems, cloud services or those of its partners.
    • Its reliance on the availability and reliability of cloud infrastructure, including infrastructure from third parties.
    • A decline in market share and profit from competition and new business models in software.
    • Problems in implementing its software for customers, and defects in its products.
    • Complex and sometimes conflicting data protection and privacy laws around the world.
    From SAP SE's annual report on Form 20-F, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 20-F has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
