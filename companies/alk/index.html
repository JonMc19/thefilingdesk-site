    What Alaska Air Group does

    Alaska Air Group is the parent of Alaska Airlines, Hawaiian Airlines and the regional carrier Horizon Air. The airlines fly passengers and cargo across North America, Latin America, Asia and the Pacific, with hubs in Seattle, Honolulu, Portland, Anchorage, Los Angeles, San Diego and San Francisco. Alaska bought Hawaiian's parent company in 2024, and in October 2025 the two airlines began operating under one FAA operating certificate while keeping separate brands.

    Most revenue comes from carrying passengers, with the rest from the loyalty program and from cargo and other services. The Atmos Rewards loyalty program, launched in 2025, earns the company money from co-branded credit cards issued by Bank of America, and loyalty revenue, including some counted in passenger revenue, was 16% of the total. Cargo includes flying freighter jets for Amazon for a fixed fee per aircraft plus per-flight fees.

    Regional flights on smaller Embraer jets are operated by Horizon and SkyWest, and Alaska buys all of that capacity. Alaska names Delta as its largest competitor and says 78% of its Seattle capacity competes with Delta.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • An accident or incident could hurt its reputation and results.
    • Heavy competition and fare discounting in a business with high fixed costs.
    • Its flying is concentrated in key West Coast markets.
    • Dependence on a few suppliers for aircraft and parts, and on jet fuel prices.
    • Delays in combining Hawaiian's workforce and systems with Alaska's, including union contracts.
    From Alaska Air Group's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
