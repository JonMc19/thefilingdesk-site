    What Thryv does

    Thryv sells software to small and medium-sized businesses. Its platform combines customer relationship management (a system for tracking customers and leads), marketing tools, workflow automation, messaging, website and social media tools, and payment processing. The company says it adds artificial intelligence tools to help owners run their businesses. As of December 31, 2025, it served approximately 230,000 small business clients.

    Thryv reports two segments. The SaaS segment (software sold by subscription) earns revenue from subscription plans, platform extensions, payment solutions and professional services. The Marketing Services segment is the older business that sells print directories and digital marketing services. Thryv says it is exiting that segment by the end of 2028 and moving its clients to the software platform.

    The software platform includes the CRM and automation engine of Keap, a business Thryv acquired. Thryv competes with other providers of small business software and marketing services, including companies that use pieces of its own software supplied by third parties.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Clients leaving as it moves them from digital marketing services to its software platform.
    • Competition for both its software and its marketing services, including from companies that use parts of its software.
    • The continuing decline in the use of print directories, which still brings in revenue.
    • Trouble adding new features and AI tools, or managing its use of AI.
    • Its reliance on small, local business customers and on agreements with major internet search engines.
    From Thryv's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
