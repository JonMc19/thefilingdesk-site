    What Celanese does

    Celanese is a chemical and specialty materials company. It makes engineered polymers, which are plastics designed for demanding uses, and is one of the world's largest producers of acetyl products, which are intermediate chemicals used in nearly all major industries. Its products go into cars, medical devices, consumer electronics, construction, paints and coatings, adhesives, filtration, paper and packaging, and textiles. Its customers are mainly large companies across many industries.

    It operates mainly through two segments, Engineered Materials and the Acetyl Chain. Engineered Materials is a project-based business that focuses on solutions, and the 10-K says its pricing is based mostly on the value to the customer, not on raw material costs. The segment also includes 17 strategic affiliates, which are partly owned businesses that extend the company's reach in regions such as Asia-Pacific, Europe, North America and the Middle East. The Acetyl Chain is run as an integrated business that sells across the segment and around the world.

    On February 2, 2026, Celanese completed the sale of its Micromax business to Element Solutions Inc for $492 million, subject to customary adjustments. It also acquired what it calls the M&M Business, and it says it may have difficulty and delays achieving the benefits of that deal.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Volatile prices and availability of key raw materials and energy could hurt its margins.
    • It may not get the intended benefits from the M&M Business it acquired.
    • It has recorded goodwill impairment losses and could be required to record more.
    • Its plants handle volatile and hazardous materials, and it may fall short on product quality.
    • It has put significant resources into China and other Asian countries, where growth may fall short.
    From Celanese's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 24, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Income statement

    From fiscal 2016 to 2023, earnings per share grew 16.3% a year and net income 11.6% a year, as buybacks cut the share count by 25%. Celanese then lost money in fiscal 2024 and 2025.
