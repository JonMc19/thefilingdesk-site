    What Zoom Communications does

    Zoom sells communication and collaboration software. Its main package, Zoom Workplace, brings together Zoom Meetings, Zoom Phone, Zoom Team Chat, Zoom Docs and Zoom Whiteboard, with an AI assistant called AI Companion. A second group, Zoom Business Services, is aimed at sales, marketing and customer service teams and includes Zoom Contact Center, Zoom Revenue Accelerator and Zoom Events. Its customers range from entrepreneurs to global companies.

    Zoom makes its money from subscriptions to its platform. It also sells Workvivo, an employee communication tool, as a monthly subscription with optional add-ons for an extra fee. Outside developers can build apps and integrations that use Zoom through its Developer Platform and App Marketplace.

    Zoom says it spent fiscal 2026, which ended January 31, 2026, building more AI into its products, and it is adding AI agents to AI Companion. It says it competes in markets that are competitive, and that other large established companies may also invest in video communications tools.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to attract new customers and to keep and grow business with existing ones.
    • Outages or delays in the data centers and cloud hosting services it uses to run its platform.
    • Strong competition, including from large companies that invest in video tools.
    • Slowing revenue growth, and results that can swing a lot from period to period.
    • Using generative AI in its products, which may bring operational, legal and reputation problems.
    From Zoom Communications's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
