    What Encompass Health does

    Encompass Health owns and runs inpatient rehabilitation hospitals. Patients are recovering from a major injury or illness, such as a stroke, a hip fracture or a neurological condition, and stay for intensive therapy to regain skills and independence. Nurses and physical, occupational and speech therapists provide the care under physician orders.

    The company says it is the nation's largest owner and operator of these hospitals. It ran 173 of them at the end of 2025 in 39 states and Puerto Rico, with the most in Florida and Texas. Substantially all of its patients, 92%, are admitted from acute-care hospitals after physician referrals. It gets paid for this care by Medicare, other government programs and private insurers, so it works under their payment rules.

    It reports its results as one business, inpatient rehabilitation, plus a small amount of other revenue. Its stock trades on the New York Stock Exchange, and its offices are in Birmingham, Alabama.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Cuts or changes in what Medicare, other government programs and private insurers pay.
    • Audits of its payment claims, and a slow appeals process for denied Medicare claims.
    • A change in the mix of its patients or in how sick they are, which affects what it is paid.
    • Delays in collecting, or failing to collect, what patients and payors owe.
    • Quality reporting rules that can reduce its Medicare payments.
    From Encompass Health's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 26, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
