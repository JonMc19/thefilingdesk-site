    What Avis Budget Group does

    Avis Budget Group rents cars and trucks and runs a car sharing network. Its main brands are Avis, Budget and Zipcar, and it also runs Payless, Apex and several local brands in Europe. It licenses its brand names to licensees in places where it does not operate directly. The company and its licensees operate in about 180 countries.

    Its two reportable segments are Americas and International. Each earns money from vehicle rentals, car sharing and licensee fees. Its average global rental fleet was about 684,000 vehicles in 2025, and it completed about 38 million rental transactions worldwide, with total revenues of about $11.7 billion. It says commercial renters mainly rent in the week and leisure renters on weekends and holidays.

    It has about 10,000 rental locations worldwide, including about 3,800 run by licensees. In 2025 it announced a multi-year partnership with Waymo to support self-driving ride-hailing in Dallas, and it launched a premium service, Avis First, in select markets.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Strong competition in the mobility industry.
    • The cost and availability of the vehicles it rents, and safety recalls that take cars out of service.
    • Weak travel demand, a weak economy, or sharply higher fuel costs.
    • Seasonal swings in its business.
    • Risks tied to its property leases and airport rental concessions.
    From Avis Budget Group's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 19, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Income statement

    From fiscal 2016 to 2023, earnings per share grew 57.5% a year and net income 39.0% a year, as buybacks cut the share count by 58%. Avis Budget Group then lost money in fiscal 2024 and 2025.
