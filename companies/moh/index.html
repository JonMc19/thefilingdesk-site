    What Molina Healthcare does

    Molina Healthcare is a health insurer that runs managed care plans for government-funded programs: Medicaid, Medicare and the state insurance marketplaces set up under the Affordable Care Act. It was founded in 1980 to serve low-income families in Southern California. It served about 5.5 million members at the end of 2025, in 21 states.

    It reports four segments: Medicaid, Medicare, Marketplace and Other. Medicaid is by far the largest, with most of its members, and also brings in the most premium revenue. The state and federal governments pay the company set premiums to cover its members' care. Its profit depends on keeping medical costs below those premiums. The Other segment is small.

    Molina says it wants to stay a pure government-sponsored health care business and to grow both on its own and by buying other plans, such as the ConnectiCare deal that closed in February 2025. It says conditions are tough in the short term. Medicaid enrollment has fallen as states recheck eligibility, medical costs have risen faster than the rates states pay, and Marketplace subsidies have expired.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Medicaid rates set by states may not cover rising medical costs.
    • Its plans run on very low profit margins, so small misses on medical costs matter a lot.
    • A volatile Marketplace business that changes with yearly program rules.
    • Losing or failing to win state Medicaid contracts that make up much of its premium revenue.
    • Trouble integrating acquisitions or finding new ones.
    From Molina Healthcare's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 10, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
