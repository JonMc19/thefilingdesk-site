    What Consolidated Water does

    Consolidated Water makes drinking water from seawater, using a process called reverse osmosis, and sells it, mainly in the Cayman Islands and The Bahamas. It also designs and builds water plants and makes water equipment, and it has an affiliate in the British Virgin Islands.

    It reports four kinds of operations. Retail water sells to homes, businesses and government customers in two of the three most populated areas of Grand Cayman, under an exclusive license, and made 26% of 2025 revenue. Bulk water sells to government-owned utilities in the Cayman Islands and The Bahamas and made 25%. Services, which designs, builds and operates plants for others, made 35%. Manufacturing made 14%.

    It also owns part of Ocean Conversion (BVI) Ltd., which supplies bulk water to the British Virgin Islands, and it operates water treatment plants in the United States.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its exclusive Cayman retail license is under renegotiation, and the regulator may seek terms that cut its income.
    • Its Bahamas subsidiary sometimes waits a long time to be paid by the government utility it supplies.
    • Unforeseen environmental costs could hurt its results.
    From Consolidated Water's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 16, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
