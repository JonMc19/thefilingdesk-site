    What IREN does

    IREN rents out computing power for artificial intelligence. It owns and runs the buildings and power connections that house the machines, the chips and servers inside them, and the software to manage them. Customers use this to train and run AI models. It offers both bare metal compute, where customers rent the hardware directly, and managed cloud services. Its customers include large cloud companies, AI labs, AI developers and businesses.

    At June 30, 2026 its working AI capacity was about 40MW, a measure of electric power. It says it has grid connection agreements or similar documents for about 5GW of power in the United States, Canada, Spain and Australia, and a further development pipeline. It says each operating site has run on renewable energy since it began.

    It also still mines Bitcoin. During the year ended June 30, 2026 it began decommissioning its mining hardware and moving power and data center space to AI services, and aims to finish by the end of 2026. It says the AI cloud market is highly competitive and fast moving.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • A history of operating losses, with a business that has grown quickly and keeps changing.
    • Its growth plans may take a lot of time and money and may not work.
    • Competition in AI cloud services, a fast-moving market with strong rivals.
    • Debt and other obligations that could limit the cash it has for operations.
    • Plans and projections that may not come true.
