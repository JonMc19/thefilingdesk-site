    What T. Rowe Price does

    T. Rowe Price manages money for others. It offers stock, bond, multi-asset and alternative investments to individuals, financial advisors, institutions and retirement plan sponsors around the world. It sells them through mutual funds, exchange-traded funds, collective investment trusts, separately managed accounts and other products. It also provides services such as fund accounting, shareholder services and recordkeeping for retirement plans.

    Nearly all of its revenue comes from investment advisory fees, which it earns from its funds and other clients. These fees depend mainly on the value and mix of the assets it manages. At the end of 2025 it managed $1,775.6 billion. About 55% of its advisory fees come from its U.S. mutual funds. Its investment arms include T. Rowe Price Associates and Oak Hill Advisors.

    The firm was founded in 1937. It says the industry faces a shift from active to passive investing and steady pressure to lower fees. In 2025 it cut some jobs, outsourced some technology work and decided to leave some of its own buildings.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Falling markets or client withdrawals that shrink the assets it manages and the fees it earns on them.
    • Most revenue comes from pooled funds that clients can leave without cause and on short notice.
    • Competition and fee pressure in asset management, including the shift from active to passive products.
    • Mistakes in operations or in its investment models, which could cost money and harm its reputation.
    • Relying on outside financial firms to sell its products, on terms that may change.
    From T. Rowe Price's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 13, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
