    What La-Z-Boy does

    La-Z-Boy makes, imports, distributes and sells home furniture, mainly upholstered pieces such as recliners and sofas. Its brands are La-Z-Boy, England and Joybird, plus Hammary casegoods, which are wood furniture. It began in 1927, and the company says it is the largest global producer of reclining chairs.

    It reports two segments. The Retail segment sells furniture to shoppers through stores the company owns. The Wholesale segment makes and imports furniture and sells it to La-Z-Boy Stores, to space inside other retailers' stores called Comfort Studios, and to independent dealers. In all it has a network of 378 La-Z-Boy Stores, of which the company owns 230 and independent owners run the rest. Joybird sells online and through 15 small stores.

    Its supply chain includes four major U.S. factories, 9 distribution centers, three facilities in Mexico and a trading company in Hong Kong that manages its Asian suppliers. It stopped production at its United Kingdom factory in fiscal 2026, and has sold its Kincaid and American Drew wholesale businesses while continuing to retail those products.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Weaker consumer confidence and spending on furniture.
    • Competition that could take away sales and market share.
    • Disruptions in its supply chain, or in the price and availability of raw materials.
    • Changes in the cost of foreign sourcing and political uncertainty abroad.
    • Its retail markets may not grow and make profits as it expects.
    From La-Z-Boy's annual report on Form 10-K, filed June 16, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
