    What Chubb does

    Chubb is a Swiss-incorporated holding company, based in Zurich, for a global group of insurance and reinsurance businesses. It sells property and casualty insurance (called P&C), accident and health insurance, and life insurance to businesses, individuals and other insurers. For most lines it works through insurance brokers and agents and pays them a commission.

    It earns money from three sources: underwriting income from P&C policies, investment income, and income from its life business. Chubb runs six segments. In 2025 total net premiums earned, the share of premiums counted as earned in the year, was $53.0 billion. North America Commercial P&C Insurance was the biggest, at 38 percent of that total. Overseas General Insurance was 27 percent. The others are North America Personal P&C, North America Agricultural, Global Reinsurance and Life Insurance.

    The personal insurance segment serves high-net-worth individuals and families with homeowners, collector car, fine art and travel cover. Chubb owns a majority stake of approximately 87.2 percent in Huatai Group. At the end of 2025 its total assets were $272 billion.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Large natural and man-made disasters that cause heavy claims.
    • Claims that turn out higher than its loss reserves, the money it sets aside for future claims.
    • New kinds of claim and coverage disputes that are hard to predict.
    • The methods it uses to limit losses failing to work as planned.
    • Depending on a few brokers and agents for a large share of its revenue, so losing one could hurt.
    From Chubb's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 27, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
