    What Coursera does

    Coursera runs an online learning platform where people take courses, earn credentials and, through partner universities, work toward degrees. It says it served about 197 million learners from over 230 countries and territories at the end of 2025. Its content comes from more than 200 universities and 175 industry companies, and covers business, technology, AI and data science. AI translation and dubbing let it offer some content in up to 26 languages.

    It reaches people in three ways: directly through its website, through employers, and through programs sponsored by colleges, universities and governments. Learners can also reach certain courses, Specializations and Professional Certificates through a Coursera Plus subscription. Its Enterprise offering is sold to businesses, schools and government bodies for their employees, students and citizens.

    Coursera says its offerings keep changing and that it has a limited operating history. Its content creators are its partners, and the contract terms it sets with them matter to its results.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Revenue that may not keep growing as it has in the past.
    • Results that vary from period to period, which make them hard to predict.
    • Online learning and generative AI being new, so adoption may not go as it expects.
    • Failing to keep its content creators, or changing their contract terms in ways that hurt it.
    • Failing to attract and keep learners, and to keep up with fast-changing technology.
    From Coursera's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 23, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
