    What Beam Therapeutics does

    Beam Therapeutics is a biotechnology company that develops gene-editing medicines. Its technology, called base editing, changes a single letter of DNA without cutting both strands of the DNA. The company also builds its own delivery technologies and has its own manufacturing. It has never sold a product.

    Its lead program, risto-cel, is a treatment made from a patient's own stem cells to treat sickle cell disease. The company says it could file for approval as early as the end of 2026. Another program, BEAM-302, is a one-time treatment for alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, a genetic liver and lung disease. It also has programs for phenylketonuria and glycogen storage disease type 1a.

    Beam has no product revenue and says it expects losses for the foreseeable future. It has a financing agreement with Sixth Street Lending Partners and says it will need more funding.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It has lost money since it began and may never be profitable.
    • It will need more funding, which could dilute shareholders or limit its operations.
    • It has never earned revenue from product sales.
    • Base editing is a new technology and may never lead to products it can sell.
    • Clinical trials may be delayed or fail, and early results may not predict later ones.
