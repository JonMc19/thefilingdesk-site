    What Colgate-Palmolive does

    Colgate-Palmolive makes and sells everyday household and personal care products and pet food. Its products are marketed in over 200 countries and territories. It sells toothpaste, toothbrushes and mouthwash under names such as Colgate, elmex and Tom's of Maine, and soaps, shower gels and deodorants such as Palmolive, Softsoap and Speed Stick. It also sells dishwashing liquids, household cleaners and fabric conditioners such as Ajax, Fabuloso and Suavitel.

    The company has two segments. Oral, Personal and Home Care brought in 44%, 17% and 16% of 2025 net sales. The Hill's Pet Nutrition segment, which sells Hill's Science Diet and Prescription Diet through pet stores, vets and online retailers, brought in 23%. Other customers include retailers, wholesalers and distributors. Walmart and its affiliates accounted for about 11% of net sales in 2025, and no other customer was as large.

    Colgate-Palmolive had about 33,600 employees in over 100 countries at the end of 2025. About two-thirds of its revenue comes from outside the United States. It says it competes with multinational and local companies, with store-brand products, and with new online and direct-to-consumer sellers, on price, promotions, new products and expansion into new channels.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its dependence on international sales, which exposes it to currency swings and political and economic instability.
    • Strong competition and a fast-changing retail world, including online and direct-to-consumer sellers.
    • Needing to develop and launch new products that shoppers want.
    • Swings in the cost of raw and packaging materials such as resins, oils, pulp and tallow.
    • Its growing reliance on a few key retailers and other third parties.
    From Colgate-Palmolive's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 23, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    What Colgate-Palmolive says to expect

    • Full year 2026: net salesThe Company now expects net sales to be up 4% to 5% (versus 2% to 6% previously), including a low-single-digit positive impact from foreign exchange.
    • Full year 2026: organic sales growthThe Company now expects organic sales growth to be broadly in line with its nine-month performance (versus 1% to 4% previously). This includes the impact from our exit from the private label pet food business.
    • Full year 2026: margin, advertising, earningsOn a non-GAAP (Base Business) basis, the Company now expects gross profit margin to be up (versus roughly flat previously) and still expects advertising to be up on both a dollar basis and as a percentage of net sales; it also now expects 5% to 7% earnings per share growth (versus mid-single-digit growth previously).
