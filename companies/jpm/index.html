    What JPMorgan Chase does

    JPMorgan Chase is a bank and financial services company based in the United States that works around the world under the J.P. Morgan and Chase names. It serves millions of people and small businesses, mostly in the US, as well as large companies, investors and governments. It had $4.4 trillion in assets at the end of 2025.

    It reports three business segments. Consumer & Community Banking offers checking and savings accounts, credit cards, mortgages, auto loans and investment accounts through Chase branches, ATMs, phones and online. The Commercial & Investment Bank advises companies on deals, helps them raise money by selling stocks and bonds, lends to them, moves payments for them, trades securities and derivatives for clients and holds investments for safekeeping, a service known as custody. Asset & Wealth Management, with client assets of $7.1 trillion, manages money for investors and wealthy families. The bank earns interest on its loans and investments, and fees for advice, trading, cards and money management. It says it competes with other banks, brokers, investment firms, card companies, private equity firms, insurers, and internet and financial technology companies.

    JPMorgan had 318,512 employees at the end of 2025 and 5,083 branches in 48 states and Washington, D.C. Its main bank is JPMorgan Chase Bank, and its main securities firm is J.P. Morgan Securities. As a very large bank, it is closely regulated and must pass yearly stress tests run by the Federal Reserve.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Heavy regulation, including rules on how much capital it must hold, which could raise its costs or limit what it does.
    • Lawsuits and government investigations that could bring large fines and other penalties.
    • Losses if borrowers, clients or trading partners cannot repay what they owe, especially if the economy weakens.
    • Swings in financial markets that could lower the value of what it holds and cut fees from deals and trading.
    • Failures or disruptions of its own computer systems or those of the many outside parties it is connected to.
    From JPMorgan Chase's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 13, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
