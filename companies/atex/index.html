    What Anterix does

    Anterix holds licenses for radio spectrum in the 900 MHz band that cover the contiguous United States, Hawaii, Alaska and Puerto Rico. It says it is the largest holder of this spectrum. Its customers are electric utilities and other critical infrastructure companies that want private wireless broadband networks for things like grid monitoring, remote automation and security.

    Anterix makes money by selling and leasing this spectrum. It also clears the spectrum of other users and swaps narrowband licenses for broadband ones, and it is building products and services around it, including TowerX, a tower site access service, and CatalyX, a connectivity management service, which it hopes will bring recurring revenue.

    In its 2026 fiscal year the company changed from mostly long-term leasing to a broader model in which spectrum sales are part of its ordinary business. It signed new sale agreements with CPS Energy, Texas-New Mexico Power and NorthWestern Energy, and received $127.0 million of contracted proceeds from customers. In February 2026 the FCC widened the broadband part of the band from 6 MHz to 10 MHz.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It may not succeed in selling or leasing its spectrum to utility and critical infrastructure customers as planned.
    • It depends on getting broadband licenses from the FCC and clearing other users from the band on time.
    • Its new products and services may not sell, and it needs to win new customers.
    • It has had net losses in most years since it began.
    • It may misjudge its costs and future cash proceeds and need more financing.
