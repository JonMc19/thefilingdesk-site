    What GPGI does

    GPGI, formerly CompoSecure, is a holding company that says it aims to acquire, own and grow businesses it considers well positioned in good industries. It owns two businesses. CompoSecure makes metal credit cards and secure authentication products. Husky makes injection molding equipment, and services it afterward, for the food, packaging and medical markets.

    The operations, capital allocation and strategy of these businesses are managed by Resolute Holdings Management, which was spun off to GPGI's shareholders. Resolute is paid a fee to manage them. GPGI says it applies a method called the Resolute Operating System to each business, and that it looks for more companies to buy, either as add-ons to CompoSecure or Husky or as new business segments.

    The company traces this change to September 2024, when Tungsten became its majority owner. It ended the old dual-share structure. It is a company that acquires businesses, so its results depend on the two it owns.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Failing to find, buy and integrate acquisitions successfully.
    • Relying heavily on Resolute Holdings, which manages its businesses for a fee and has substantial influence.
    • Conflicts of interest with Resolute Holdings and its affiliates.
    • U.S. tariffs and other limits on imported goods that could hurt revenue and operations.
    • Data and security breaches, or technology system failures.
    From GPGI's annual report on Form 10-K, filed March 12, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.

    Piotroski F-score: how many of nine simple tests of profitability, funding and efficiency the company passed in the year (Piotroski, 2000); 8 or 9 is strong, 0 to 2 weak.
