    What United States Antimony does

    United States Antimony Corporation mines, buys, processes and sells antimony and precious metals, mainly gold and silver, in Montana and Mexico. It also mines, processes and sells zeolite in Idaho. Antimony and zeolite are minerals used in many industrial, commercial and government applications, and the company sells them in processed forms ready for end use.

    It began operating in Montana in 1970. Antimony mining in the United States stopped in the 1980s as cheaper imported ore came in. The company kept processing ore bought from foreign suppliers into antimony trioxide, antimony metal and antimony trisulfide, and in 2025 it again mined its own antimony ore in Montana. It also processes antimony ore at two facilities in Mexico.

    In late 2024 and 2025 it acquired mining claims in Alaska, Montana and Canada that may hold antimony, tungsten, cobalt and other critical minerals. It is finishing an expansion of its Montana antimony plant that is expected to more than triple its capacity. The added supply is meant to serve two new five-year sales contracts, with the U.S. Defense Logistics Agency and an industrial customer, signed in 2025.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • It has lost money in recent years and may keep doing so.
    • U.S. trade policy changes, including tariffs and retaliatory tariffs, could hurt its business.
    • It may need more financing, which may not be available on acceptable terms.
    • A drop in metals prices, mine accidents or higher costs could force it to write down assets.
    • Its mining may not prove economically viable, and its environmental cleanup obligations may exceed what it has set aside.
