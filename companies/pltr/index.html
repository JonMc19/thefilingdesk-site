    What Palantir does

    Palantir makes software that helps large organizations pull their data together and use it to run day-to-day operations and make decisions. It sells four main platforms: Gotham, used by defense and intelligence agencies; Foundry, a data system that all of its commercial customers use; Apollo, which delivers and updates software in the cloud, on a customer's own servers or in remote field sites; and AIP, which connects large language models (the technology behind AI chatbots) to a customer's data and workflows.

    Palantir reports two segments. Of its $4.5 billion in revenue in 2025, 54% came from government customers and 46% from commercial ones, and most of it came from the United States. Palantir says it competes with large enterprise software companies, government contractors and system integrators.

    The company was founded in 2003 to build software for US intelligence agencies and later began selling to businesses. It had 954 customers at the end of 2025. It goes after large, complex projects, often runs short training sessions it calls bootcamps to show results on a customer's own data, and grows by expanding use within existing accounts. It also sells through cloud providers and partners. At the end of 2025 it had about 4,429 full-time employees.

    Its biggest risks, as it describes them

    • Its heavy reliance on government contracts, which governments can end at will or leave unfunded.
    • Long, costly and unpredictable sales cycles, and the need to keep expanding with existing customers.
    • Failing to keep up with new technology such as AI, or harm from how AI is used in its platforms.
    • Damage to its reputation from news and social media scrutiny of Palantir or its leaders.
    • Its dependence on Alexander Karp and other key staff, some of whom need security clearances.
    From Palantir's annual report on Form 10-K, filed February 17, 2026, in our words; the 10-K has the full description and every risk factor. Updated with each new annual report.
