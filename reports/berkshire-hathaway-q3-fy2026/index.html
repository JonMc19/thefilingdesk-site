    Berkshire Hathaway revenue rose 11% to $105.6 billion as net income fell 28%

    By Jon Mc Gowan, The Filing Desk

    BERKSHIRE HATHAWAY INC · NYSE: BRK-B Quarter ended September 30, 2026 Form 10-Q filed November 9, 2026 · 0001193125-26-452412 Published November 9, 2026

    Berkshire Hathaway's revenue rose 11.2% to $105.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Net income fell 28.2% to $22.1 billion. The company also bought back $9.3 billion of its shares in the quarter, after none in the third quarter of 2025.

    Revenue growth picked up for a third straight quarter

    Revenue was $105.6 billion, up from $95.0 billion a year earlier. Growth was 11.2%, after 10.0% in the second quarter and 4.4% in the first. In the first nine months of 2026, revenue was $301.1 billion.

    The company's manufacturing, service and retailing businesses led the quarter. Berkshire said after-tax earnings from these businesses "increased 11.8% in the third quarter". Its railroad, BNSF, did the same: after-tax earnings "increased 19.3% in the third quarter". Insurance underwriting went the other way: after-tax earnings "declined 42.9% in the third quarter", and the company said each of its three underwriting groups earned less than a year earlier.

    Net income fell after a large gain a year earlier

    Net income attributable to Berkshire shareholders was $22.1 billion, 28.2% lower than the $30.8 billion of the third quarter of 2025. Pre-tax income fell 28.8% to $27.1 billion, and income tax was $5.0 billion, against $7.2 billion.

    Berkshire's earnings include gains and losses on its investments, which change with market prices. The 10-Q table that breaks down the quarter shows the third quarter of 2025 held a larger investment gain than the third quarter of 2026. Berkshire says in the 10-Q that it believes such gains and losses are "often meaningless in understanding our reported periodic results". Net income was also lower than in the second quarter of 2026, when it was $25.7 billion.

    Buybacks resumed at a larger scale

    Berkshire spent $9.3 billion buying back its own shares in the third quarter, after $4.2 billion in the second and $0.2 billion in the first. It reported no buybacks from the third quarter of 2024 through the end of 2025.

    Cash from operations was $12.3 billion, down 11.1% from a year earlier. Capital spending was $5.5 billion, so free cash flow was $6.8 billion, down 17.7%. Free cash flow was 6.4% of revenue. Shareholders' equity rose to $761.4 billion.
