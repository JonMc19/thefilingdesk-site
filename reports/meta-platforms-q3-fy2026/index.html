    Meta revenue rose 26% to $64.6 billion, but a legal charge cut operating income 37%

    By Jon Mc Gowan, The Filing Desk

    Meta Platforms, Inc. · Nasdaq: META Quarter ended September 30, 2026 Form 10-Q filed October 22, 2026 · 0001628280-26-066773 Published October 22, 2026

    Meta's revenue rose 26.1% to $64.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Operating income fell 36.8% to $13.0 billion, because costs and expenses include a $9.89 billion charge for an agreement with state attorneys general. Capital spending of $33.9 billion was larger than operating cash flow of $32.8 billion.

    Advertising drove the rise in revenue

    Revenue grew 26.1% from $51.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025. Meta said advertising revenue increased $12.98 billion, due to more ad impressions delivered and a higher average price per ad. Ad impressions increased 10% and the average price per ad increased 14%, both from a year earlier. Revenue for the first nine months of the year was $181.7 billion, up 28.8% from the same period of 2025.

    The legal charge and higher costs cut the operating margin

    Operating income fell 36.8% to $13.0 billion, and the operating margin was 20.1%, down 20.0 points from 40.1% a year earlier. The second quarter of 2026 had a margin of 30.9%. In its 10-Q, Meta said general and administrative expenses rose mostly because of legal costs, including charges of $9.89 billion for the August 2026 agreement with state attorneys general in the quarter. Adding that charge back, operating income would have been $22.9 billion, a margin of 35.4%.

    Other costs also grew faster than revenue. Research and development rose 32.4% to $20.1 billion. Cost of revenue, which Meta ties to data-center and cloud infrastructure, rose 45.2%, so gross margin was 79.3%, against 82.0% a year earlier.

    Net income compares with a year of unusual taxes

    Net income was $9.9 billion and diluted earnings per share were $3.83, against $2.7 billion and $1.05 in the third quarter of 2025. The year-earlier figure was low because the provision for income taxes was $19.0 billion, 87.5% of pretax income. Meta said that came mostly from the effects of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, enacted in that quarter. This quarter's provision was $3.1 billion, a rate of 24.2%.

    Capital spending exceeded operating cash flow

    Cash from operations rose 9.3% to $32.8 billion. Cash spent on property and equipment rose 80.3% to $33.9 billion. Free cash flow, which is the first minus the second, was negative $1.2 billion, after $11.2 billion a year earlier. Meta's own release reports free cash flow of negative $2.12 billion, because it also deducts principal payments on finance leases.

    Cash and marketable securities were $82.4 billion at September 30, 2026. Debt was $83.7 billion, up from $28.8 billion a year earlier. Meta paid $1.4 billion in dividends and bought back no shares in the quarter, after $3.3 billion of buybacks in the third quarter of 2025.
