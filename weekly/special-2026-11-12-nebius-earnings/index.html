    Nebius shares jumped 15.44% on Wednesday. Its results come before Thursday's open: what to watch

    The AI cloud company reports before the market opens, after its best day in three months. It has promised a big year-end jump in revenue; analysts expect a quarter short of that pace.

    Nebius By Jon Mc Gowan, The Filing Desk · November 12, 2026

    Nebius's shareholders had a good Wednesday. The shares rose 15.44%, CNBC reported, their best day in three months, according to Asianet Newsable, after CoreWeave, a larger rival, reported strong results. On Thursday it is Nebius's turn: the Amsterdam-based company releases its third-quarter results before the market opens at 9:30 a.m. Eastern, and holds a call at 8 a.m.

    The setup is a stock that has swung hard. As of Wednesday's close the shares were up 85% this year, Asianet Newsable reported, yet still well below their 52-week high of $299.86, according to Investing.com. Early on Thursday, reports on premarket trading were mixed: Stocktwits reported the shares lower, weighed down by the turmoil at Nvidia, while TipRanks reported them inching higher.

    This is a preview, not a report. Every expectation below is someone else's, and the real figures arrive before the open. Here is what Nebius does, what it promised, what analysts expect, and what to watch.

    What Nebius sells, and why the shares move so much

    Nebius is one of the "neoclouds": companies that rent out computing power built on Nvidia's chips to businesses training and running AI models. Nvidia also holds a stake in Nebius, 24/7 Wall St. reported.

    The business grows fast and spends faster. In the second quarter, revenue rose 454% from a year earlier to $582.3 million, and its AI cloud reached a $3.0 billion annualized run-rate, the yearly revenue its current business would bring in, The Energy Mag reported. The same quarter, it spent $5.66 billion on property, equipment and intangible assets. Customers help pay for it: Nebius expects more than $9 billion of prepayments this year and says it has more than $40 billion of customer commitments. In October it added a $3.5 billion credit facility from Citi, the company said.

    That spending is why the shares swing. Neocloud shares had faced "weeks of turmoil on fears that their debt-fuelled data centre expansion could backfire if AI demand slows," Reuters reported, as published by BNN Bloomberg. Nebius's shares also fell ahead of the results on reports that Microsoft may scale back its neocloud partnerships, Investing.com reported. CoreWeave's results eased those fears, for a day at least.

    What Nebius promised, and what analysts expect

    Nebius set its own bar in August. It reaffirmed 2026 revenue of $3 billion to $3.4 billion, a year-end annualized run-rate of $7 billion to $9 billion and an adjusted EBITDA margin of about 40%, The Energy Mag reported. With $981.3 million of revenue in the first half, that means about $1.01 billion to $1.21 billion in each of the last two quarters.

    Analysts expect less than that for the third quarter. Their average forecast is revenue of $907.2 million, up 521% from a year earlier, and an adjusted loss of $0.63 a share, against $0.40 a year earlier, according to Koyfin data cited by Asianet Newsable. Of 22 analysts, 15 rate the shares buy or higher, six hold and one sell. Those are their ratings, not ours. Options prices implied a large move in either direction after the results, TipRanks and Investing.com reported.

    What to watch

    This is our analysis, not a forecast of the results or the shares.

    • The run-rate. Second quarter: $3.0 billion. The year-end goal: $7 billion to $9 billion. How far the third quarter gets, and whether Nebius keeps the range, is the clearest test of whether its new capacity is coming online on time.
    • Revenue against its own path. The $907.2 million analysts expect is short of the roughly $1.01 billion a quarter Nebius needs to reach its full-year range, so the fourth quarter would have to do more of the work. Watch whether the full-year revenue range changes.
    • Spending and funding. Capital spending, new prepayments and any new debt or share sales. Nebius raised its target for contracted power, sites with land and power lined up, to 5 gigawatts at the end of 2026. Paying for that is the main worry about the business.
    • Customers. Any word on Microsoft after this week's reports, and any new large contracts.
    • Nvidia. Nebius runs on Nvidia's chips and counts Nvidia as a shareholder. Its call comes in the middle of Nvidia's board deciding the future of its chief executive. Our story has the latest.

    After its second-quarter results in August, the shares jumped 17%, The Energy Mag reported. Thursday's results will show whether the business has kept up with that kind of expectation. The results are out: our story on them has what Nebius reported.
